# Kawakami Nobuo Looks Back on Ten Years of Nico Nico Douga: "I've Been Called 'Finished Content' More Times Than I Can Count"

![Chairman Kawakami looks back on ten years since the start of Nico Nico Douga](https://wpb.shueisha.co.jp/news/technology/2016/images/2016/11/05073563ff1bf1014a9fa11eec7112d5_f4a20bd896a2e1b6a4b0f06abed78580.jpg)

*Chairman Kawakami looks back on ten years since the start of Nico Nico Douga*

**It will soon be ten years since *Nico Nico Douga (Kari)* launched on 12 December 2006!**[^tn-kari]

**In a series of interviews, we replay the secret story of the birth of this monster platform, which produced countless idols and creators and kept viewers glued to their PCs, and the excitement of those days!**

**In part one, Nobuo Kawakami, the "father of Nico Nico Douga" who started it, looks back on the ten years!**

**■ The premium membership was just a way to deal with complaints**

**— When it started, did you think Nico Douga would last ten years?**

**Kawakami:** Back then everyone was telling me, "Nico Douga is a ridiculously fun service, but it'll definitely be gone in six months" (laughs), so my goal was to somehow keep it going for five years.

**— Why was that?**

**Kawakami:** Well, because there were all sorts of problems. Originally Nico Douga was a sort of parasite site: it pulled videos posted on YouTube and played them with comments on top. But almost all of YouTube's traffic ended up coming from Nico Douga, so they cut off our access, and so on.[^tn-youtube]

**— As a result you had to suspend the service for a while, but a month later you reopened with an account system. People had to wait months to become members!**

**Kawakami:** There were too many users trying to get in. Even when we issued accounts, the wait grew to two months, three months... and in the end I think it was about six months.

**— Is that when you felt "this service is going to work"?**

**Kawakami:** Less "felt it would work"... After YouTube cut us off, the servers we built ourselves could only handle a tenth of our estimated users.[^tn-smile] So we made it members-only, added servers every week, and let in only as many new members as we could cope with, over and over. But when new registrations hit a six-month wait, it was like, what do we do now, to hell with it (laughs).

**— No matter how many servers you added, you couldn't keep up?**

**Kawakami:** So we created the premium membership.[^tn-premium] Actually, it wasn't to make money; it was just to fend off user complaints. Existing users were saying, "If you let new people in, the videos will get slow," and users waiting to register were saying, "Let us in already!"

But if we created premium memberships, then whenever a user said "the videos are slow!" or "let me register already!", other users would fight them for us: "Pay up and go premium, you cheapskate!" (laughs). Then everyone signed up, and we figured we might even get into the black.

**"2ch's core users trashed the place as much as they liked and then left"**

![In Nico Douga's early days, Kawakami says, "We understated the PV numbers to the media, because they'd think we were lying"](https://wpb.shueisha.co.jp/news/technology/2016/images/2016/11/4b93a4682f112828e876302eea5d55fb_107e210405e95a45e2e5ccf883f1fb77.jpg)

*In Nico Douga's early days, page views (PV) grew so fast that "we understated the PV numbers to the media, because they'd think we were lying" (Chairman Kawakami)*

**— And when people think of Nico Douga, it's the comment feature.**

**Kawakami:** 2channel already had a *jikkyō* culture of posting in real time while watching TV,[^tn-jikkyo] and I thought the best way to do that with video was to make the comments flow across the screen. So at first I meant to grow Nico Douga using 2ch's users as the base.

I can say this now, but back then I was talking with Hiroyuki (Hiroyuki Nishimura), then 2ch's administrator, about whether we could build a system so that when someone posted a YouTube video link on 2ch, it would steer people to the commented version on Nico Douga. In the end it never happened; or rather, it wasn't needed, because Nico Douga got popular without it.

**— So the ones who sparked Nico Douga's popularity were 2channelers?**

**Kawakami:** Not really. 2ch's core users came to Nico Douga right after the service started, but they trashed the place as much as they liked and then left (laughs).[^tn-arashi] Once they'd gone and things settled down, casual internet users gathered and it started getting talked about, and then the core users came back, like, "Nico Douga is our colony" (laughs). That's where the internet's core users and casual users fused and a unique culture was born.

**— Nico Douga also brought all sorts of amateur creators into the spotlight.**

**Kawakami:** Japan is one of the most active countries in the world for derivative works (二次創作 *niji sōsaku*). I think the reason is that, of all the video sites in the world at the time, we were the only ones patrolling and deleting copyright-infringing videos. Users went, "If they're going to get deleted, we'll just have to make our own fun," and started making derivative works. That made Nico Douga a special site worldwide.[^tn-claim]

**— Nico Douga changed youth culture, but lately it gets treated as *owakon*...**

**Kawakami:** I've been called *owakon* more times than I can count (laughs).[^tn-owakon] But the people saying that are just users who stopped watching. Users always move on, and sites based on UGC (user-generated content) are said to have a lifespan of two or three years in the first place. Yet Nico Douga has lasted ten. That's abnormal.

**"We understated the PV numbers, because they'd think we were lying"**

*Niconico Chōkaigi, which Kawakami says he also started in a "to hell with it" mood: "I wanted to make an event that crammed in every element of Nico Douga." It started in 2012 and is held every April at Makuhari Messe. Last year it even featured a conversation with Hideaki Anno.*[^tn-chokaigi]

**— Is that thanks to management's efforts to protect the market?**

**Kawakami:** Once a market matures there's always competition, so then whoever wants that market can take it, and I'll just compete in the next one. I'm more interested in how I can change the world and culture than in defending a market.

**— When did you feel Nico Douga had established a new culture?**

**Kawakami:** When the "chorus" (合唱 *gasshō*) *danmaku* culture appeared on the music video of Remioromen's "Konayuki" posted on Nico Douga, with comments filling the screen in time with the lyrics.[^tn-konayuki] I'd never seen anything like it, and I thought, so you can play with it like this.

**— But videos like that tended to raise copyright problems.**

**Kawakami:** That's why we got permission from rights holders and worked on creating an environment where people could make derivative works openly. There are still grey areas, and you know how copyright holders sometimes don't want to give formal permission but turn a blind eye, or are even happy about the derivative works. For things like that, it's better to have a world that isn't rigid and by-the-book.

Today's world is peaceful and complicated, and people love making rules, so they want to regulate everything. But that's never going to be fun. In that sense, I think Nico Douga was able to give internet culture a story of its own.

**● Nobuo Kawakami** After about seven years as a salaryman, he went independent and founded Dwango. He moved into mobile-phone businesses such as ringtones and grew the company rapidly. He later launched Nico Nico Douga and, as the company's representative director and chairman, keeps rolling out new services.

> [!info] Cultural context and translator's notes
> An interview from *Weekly Playboy*'s news site (Shueisha), 6 November 2016, part one of a series marking Nico Nico Douga's tenth anniversary. It is a promotional retrospective: Kawakami is witty and self-mythologizing, and the timing details he gives are approximate. Compare his account of 2channel users with the contemporary view in [[Nico Nico Douga and 2channel (Part 1-1) - The Roots of Nico Users in the (Kari) Era]], written in 2007, which credits 2channelers and Hiroyuki's blog for shaping the service during the (Kari) period. For background see [[NicoNicoDouga]], [[Dwango]], [[SMILEVIDEO]], [[2ch-niconico-Relation]] and [[Hiroyuki Nishimura]]. Late 2016 was also when Niconico was losing users to YouTube and being widely called *owakon*, which is why the interviewer brings it up. Full-width digits and Latin letters in the original are rendered normally, and photo captions are kept under their images.

[^tn-kari]: (仮) (*kari*) means "provisional" or "tentative", as in a working title. The first version of the site was literally called "Nico Nico Douga (Kari)", a deliberate joke on Web 2.0's "eternal beta". It became "(β)" on 15 January 2007, "(γ)" in March and "(RC)" in June 2007 (see [[NicoNico - JP Wiki]]).
[^tn-youtube]: Early Nico Nico Douga embedded YouTube's video streams and overlaid its own comments. Around 23 February 2007 YouTube blocked access from Nico. The service was suspended the same day, partly also because of DoS attacks, and reopened on 6 March as "(γ)" with its own video hosting. Kawakami's "a month later" is a rounding of these two weeks.
[^tn-smile]: The in-house video hosting was SMILEVIDEO, launched on 6 March 2007 when Nico reopened. See [[SMILEVIDEO]].
[^tn-premium]: Paid premium membership began on 18 June 2007, together with the "(RC)" version. Premium members got priority access to the servers at busy times, which is the leverage Kawakami jokes about here.
[^tn-jikkyo]: 実況 (*jikkyō*, "live commentary") refers to 2channel's live-commentary boards (実況板), where users flooded threads with real-time posts while watching TV broadcasts. Nico's scrolling comments are often described as a video version of this.
[^tn-arashi]: 荒らす (*arasu*, "to trash, troll") is the verb behind 荒らし (*arashi*), the standard Japanese term for trolling and vandalizing a board. The developer blog records that Hiroyuki's own first test video was targeted, and comment limits were added as a result; see [[Nico Nico Douga and 2channel (Part 1-1) - The Roots of Nico Users in the (Kari) Era]].
[^tn-claim]: Kawakami's claim that Nico was the *only* video site deleting infringing videos is self-serving and hard to verify; YouTube also removed content when rights holders asked. Read it as his explanation of Nico's derivative-work culture, not as an established fact.
[^tn-owakon]: オワコン (*owakon*), short for 終わったコンテンツ (*owatta kontentsu*, "finished content"), is net slang for a service or franchise that has passed its peak. The word spread through 2channel and Niconico in the early 2010s.
[^tn-chokaigi]: ニコニコ超会議 (*Niconico Chōkaigi*, roughly "Niconico Super Meeting") is Niconico's large offline event at Makuhari Messe near Tokyo. Hideaki Anno is the director of *Neon Genesis Evangelion*. The caption's "last year" means 2015.
[^tn-konayuki]: Remioromen's "Konayuki" (粉雪, 2005) has a famous chorus line that viewers typed in unison as comments, filling the screen. 弾幕 (*danmaku*, "barrage", from bullet-hell games) is the name for comment walls that cover the whole video. It is one of Nico's best-known early memes.
