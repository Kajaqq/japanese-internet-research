# What Is Dwango Starting with Hiroyuki?

Dwango and Hiroyuki Nishimura, known as the administrator of 2channel, are about to launch a new service on the mobile industry. It takes "email, not the Web" as its angle and adopts a new business model "based on advertising revenue, not carrier billing".

The service will be run by the new company Niwango, founded with investment from Dwango, Mirai Kensaku Brazil Ltd. and CELL ([see the 14 November article](https://www.itmedia.co.jp/mobile/articles/0511/14/news043.html)).[^tn-prev] We asked Seiji Sugimoto, general manager in charge of Section 2 of Dwango's Business Development Department and now president of Niwango, about the outline and aims of the business.

*(Photo: Niwango president Seiji Sugimoto)*

## A service where you "ask" by email and get a reply

The details of Niwango's service were not made clear in the press release sent to reporters on 14 November. All it said was "a service for general users that uses mobile phone email".

![Photo](https://image.itmedia.co.jp/mobile/articles/0511/29/ms_niwango.jpg) *Niwango's logo, designed around an email motif*

Sugimoto says the key point of the service is using mobile email for "information search". "Email becomes a search window for finding things out. You send an email to the portal and a group of servers sends back the right answer. What's fresh about it is that it isn't 'Web supremacist'."

As he speaks, Sugimoto works his phone and sends Niwango's server an email with "timetable Hamachō" in the body. The reply arrives a beat later and reads:

**"Heyo~. Timetable for Hamachō Station (Toei Shinjuku Line) ■ Inbound 16:36 Local for Hashimoto, 16:40 Local for Sasazuka..."**[^tn-domo][^tn-hamacho]

"You just put the designated address in the 'to' field of a mobile email, type a word for the information you want in the body, and send it, and the information comes back in a reply." In the email above, the word "timetable" is the keyword; type a different station name after it and you get that station's timetable.

Niwango calls these keywords "commands".[^tn-command] Besides "timetable" there are several kinds, such as "transfer", "dictionary" and "weather", and depending on the word the server replies with train transfer directions or provides a dictionary function, for example. Each service is due to launch in January 2006, and many ideas have already been put forward.

For instance, typing an artist's name after a command could tell you which sites distribute that artist's songs. Entering a song title could show its lyrics. Entering an address could return information on restaurants nearby. Or there could be content like a "game novel", whose story branches according to what the user types.

Incidentally, as the "Heyo~" at the top of the reply suggests, the plan is for users to feel as if they're enjoying a conversation with Niwango's server. "A completely mechanical answer would be fine too, but to set ourselves apart, we'll have a concierge-like navigator give the answers. You can think of it as a character called 'Niwango' or not, as you like..." The company also seems to be thinking of including bonus tips along with the information itself.

## Are there drawbacks unique to email?

Exchanges by email inevitably give the impression of being "a beat slower" than a Web search service. Unlike a Web service, where you click a key and the result appears at once, you have to receive it as an email and then open it. In some cases the email might also be delayed.

Sugimoto, however, says delay is not much of a worry.[^tn-pagebreak]

Niwango uses the paid priority connection service offered by the mobile carriers. So "you can think of it as faster than a friend replying to your email." For game content and the like, though, the company may sometimes delay replies on purpose for entertainment value.

Another concern is that the email body is an input tool with an extremely high degree of freedom. Even if the company expects a command like "transfer" (乗換), users might type all kinds of words: 乗り換え, 乗換え, 乗り継ぎ and so on.[^tn-norikae] Can all of these be properly recognized as commands?

Sugimoto says the company will prepare several expected variants of each command and will also monitor user logs, checking continually which words are entered most often. Niwango does not directly look at which user wrote which message, but it does know which kinds of messages are most common. Based on this data, it will tune the servers to respond more intelligently.

"For vague messages we're also thinking of replying with an email that guides the user. If a user types 'noritsugi', for example, we'd reply, 'Did you mean "transfer"?'"

## The "Ni" in Niwango is the "Ni" of Nishimura Hiroyuki

One of the points that drew attention this time is that Hiroyuki, administrator of 2channel, Japan's largest anonymous bulletin-board site, joined the board. Sugimoto says the link between Dwango and Hiroyuki is Mirai Kensaku Brazil.

"Originally, Dwango's chairman had been talking with Mirai Kensaku Brazil about whether something could be done using search technology. Staff from Mirai Kensaku Brazil have also been working on this project, and that led to the idea of having Hiroyuki (a director of Mirai Kensaku Brazil) join the new company too."[^tn-chairman]

Sugimoto says the "Ni" in Niwango is the "ni" of Nishimura Hiroyuki. "It was the development code name, and it became the company name as it was. Sadly, it isn't the 'ni' of '2channel'."[^tn-ni] He says they are counting on Hiroyuki's planning ability, creativity, and know-how in server design and management.

Dwango is the company that drew attention with a TV commercial featuring 2channel characters, but it is not particularly stressing a connection with 2channel this time. "We'd like people to look at the good side of the service, not treat it as gossip."

## An advertising model, though the details aren't settled

Finally, the key question in discussing Niwango's service is "what kind of revenue model it has in mind". As the press release mentioned, it has been revealed that the company will adopt an advertising model.

"Dwango has mainly done ringtones until now. But from here on we need to broaden our business by paying attention to the *katte saito* side as well (which runs on an advertising model).[^tn-katte] In fact, if you look at how the mobile advertising industry is expanding, the market is doubling and doubling again."

The model the company has in mind is to combine an ad-delivery engine with Niwango's portal server and serve users the most suitable ads. There is an important question here, however.[^tn-pagebreak]

Niwango's portal server will accumulate a lot of search data from users. The first thing that comes to mind is "listing ads", shown only when a specific search keyword is entered. Or, for example, if a user keeps searching for information around a particular station, the service could deliver ads tied to that station; in other words, it could analyze the overall user profile and deliver ads accordingly.

If ads go too far in the body of an email, though, that email instantly becomes "spam". Striking the balance here is difficult. "We're afraid of ad material making users uncomfortable." At what stage and how ads come in, and whether they appear in the email reporting the search result or arrive as a separate email, all need careful consideration, he says.

The outlook on this point is still somewhat unclear, but Sugimoto explains that expectations in the advertising industry are high.

"When we discuss it with agencies, the feeling is that, thinking of the future ad market, this business is 'a go'. There's a sense of expectation; they want to give it a try."

Sugimoto admits that Niwango's service will not be finished from the start. But he also says it is something that will improve as many users use it. "Niwango has only just started walking. We'd like users to help raise it."

[^tn-prev]: See [[Hiroyuki Becomes 'Director-Administrator' of Dwango's New Company]]. 有限会社 (*yūgen gaisha*) in the original marks Mirai Kensaku Brazil as a limited-liability company, a small-company form abolished for new registrations in 2006.
[^tn-domo]: どもー (*domō*) is a very casual "hi!" or "hey~". It is unusually informal for an automated service reply, which is the point: the server is meant to feel like a chatty character.
[^tn-hamacho]: Hamachō (浜町) is a station on the Toei Shinjuku Line in central Tokyo. Trains run through onto the Keiō lines, hence destinations such as Hashimoto and Sasazuka. 各停 (*kakutei*) means a local train stopping at every station.
[^tn-command]: Nico Nico Douga later kept the idea of typed commands. Comments could carry commands that changed their colour, size or position, and Niwango's name survived in "Niwan-go" (ニワン語), the scripting commands used by uploaders. Details belong to [[NicoNicoDouga]]; the link to this 2005 service is the translator's observation, not the article's.
[^tn-pagebreak]: The ITmedia original is split over three pages here, with headings "Next page: Why bring Hiroyuki on as a director?" and "Next page: Isn't advertising email just spam?". The markers are removed so the text runs on.
[^tn-norikae]: All of these mean "change trains" or "transfer" but are written differently: 乗換, 乗り換え and 乗換え differ only in okurigana (the kana spelling of the word's ending), and 乗り継ぎ (*noritsugi*) is a near-synonym. Japanese has no single fixed spelling for many such words, which is the matching problem the article raises.
[^tn-chairman]: Dwango's chairman in 2005 was Nobuo Kawakami (川上量生), later known as the "father of Nico Nico Douga". See [[Kawakami Nobuo Looks Back on Ten Years of Nico Nico Douga]].
[^tn-ni]: The Japanese plays on に (*ni*) as the first syllable of 西村 (*Nishimura*) and as the reading of 2 in 2ちゃんねる (*Ni-channeru*). Because the name also rhymes with "Dwango", reading it as "Dwango + 2channel" is an easy assumption, and Sugimoto is heading it off.
[^tn-katte]: 勝手サイト (*katte saito*): independent mobile sites outside the carriers' official menus, usually funded by ads instead of carrier billing. See the note in [[Hiroyuki Becomes 'Director-Administrator' of Dwango's New Company]].
