**Niconico Douga** (*Nico Nico Douga*, *Niconico Video*) is a video-sharing service operated and provided by DWANGO Co., Ltd. since December 12, 2006.

---

## Overview

*(Mascot: Nico Nico Terebi-chan)*

Commonly known as **Nicodou** (ニコ動) or **Niconico** (ニコニコ). It features the unique capability of superimposing user comments directly onto the video playback screen in real-time.

Video uploading is handled directly on Niconico Douga.

To understand what kind of video service Niconico Douga is, watching a single uploaded video is vastly more effective than reading a needlessly long explanation.

As an example, see the embedded video below -> sm9

As you can see from watching, Niconico Douga has built a unique web culture around its hallmark feature: **"commenting directly across the video screen."**

At its inception, the entire service was titled "Niconico Douga." However, on May 1, 2012, the umbrella brand was renamed to **niconico**. From then on, "Niconico Douga" became both a colloquial nickname for the entire platform and the specific designation for the video-sharing section within the niconico ecosystem. This rebranding occurred because Dwango expanded heavily into non-video services, and because the word *douga* ("video" in Japanese) was considered unintuitive for overseas expansion. Following this change, the phrase "Niconico Douga" took on two simultaneous meanings: strictly the video-hosting arm of niconico, and an informal moniker for the entire platform. Consequently, Japanese online news media often report on livestreams happening on *Niconico Live* with headlines like "Streamed Live on Niconico Douga."

On October 27, 2020, the umbrella service name was rebranded again into katakana as **ニコニコ (Niconico)**, with Niconico Douga maintaining its position as the specific video-hosting service under the overarching "Niconico" banner.

On June 8, 2024, a massive cyberattack paralyzed the platform, rendering all services inaccessible for an extended period. On June 14, 2024, a stripped-down temporary recovery site called **Niconico Douga (Re:Kari)** was launched, allowing users to watch a curated selection of historically famous, high-view-count videos. On August 5, 2024, the platform made its official comeback under the release title **"Niconico Returns" (帰ってきたニコニコ)**. *(Note: All Premium membership subscription fees during the downtime from June to August were fully refunded.)*

### Origin of the Name

During an early planning meeting, a participant remarked: *"Rather than a try-hard, cool-sounding name, something completely relaxed and silly would be better. Something like 'Nico Nico Douga' (Smiling Videos), maybe?"* The name was decided in mere seconds. (At that moment, it was not intended to be permanent, serving only as a temporary working title.)

Incidentally, the very first "Nico-chuu" (Niconico addict) is recognized as engineer Akihiko Koizuka.

## Membership Announcement History

- **August 2008:** Average daily PC metrics stood at approx. 65 million page views (PV), 2.3 million unique users (UU), 19 million video plays, and 2.6 million comments posted per day.
- **July 27, 2009:** Dwango announced that Premium (paid) members surpassed 400,000. Total registered accounts reached 13.4 million, with *Niconico Douga Mobile* reaching 3.89 million. Premium signups surged much faster than prior periods, which Dwango credited to the popularity of the newly introduced "Niconico Live" service. [1]
- **September 19, 2009:** Premium members passed 500,000—adding 100,000 users in just under two months. Total registered users reached 14.1 million.
- **May 17, 2010:** Premium members passed 800,000, maintaining a pace of 100,000 new subscribers every 2.5 months.
- **August 3, 2010:** Premium members crossed 900,000. Total registered users reached 17.88 million; *Niconico Douga Mobile* reached 5.38 million.
- **August 26, 2010:** At the fan event *Nico Nico Daikaigi 2010 Summer ~The Power of Smiles~*, Dwango announced 930,000 premium members, 18.17 million total members, and 5.45 million mobile registrations.
- **October 13, 2010:** Premium members officially surpassed 1,000,000.
- **October 28, 2010:** At *Nico Nico Daikaigi 2010 Autumn ~Is That Rock?~*, figures reached 1.01 million premium members, 18.9 million total registered users, and 5.66 million mobile users.
- **June 22, 2013:** Premium membership reached 2,000,000, hitting this milestone within 1 year and 5 months of passing 1.5 million.
- **July 28, 2015:** Premium membership surpassed 2.5 million. On August 5, 2015, total registered members crossed 50 million (officially announced on August 6).
- **May 12, 2016:** In the FY 3/2016 Full-Year Earnings Briefing, Dwango reported 2.56 million premium members. This would mark the **all-time peak** of premium subscriptions, after which numbers plateaued and then began a steady decline. Total registered account IDs stood at 55.41 million.
- **February 9, 2017:** The FY 3/2017 Q3 Briefing revealed the **first-ever drop in premium subscribers** since the service launched, dipping from the 2.56 million plateau down to 2.54 million. Total accounts (including free) surpassed 62.1 million.
- **May 11, 2017:** In the FY 3/2017 Full-Year Briefing, premium members dropped below 2.5 million. However, paid monthly subscriptions to creator/channel hubs (*Niconico Channel*) grew by +140,000 year-over-year to 590,000.
- **November 8, 2018:** The FY 3/2019 Q2 Briefing showed premium members falling below 2.0 million. Meanwhile, paid Niconico Channel subscribers climbed to 840,000.
- **July 30, 2021:** The FY 3/2022 Q1 Briefing showed premium members falling below 1.5 million. Paid Channel subscriptions rose to 1.20 million.
- **November 2023:** In the FY 3/2024 Q2 Briefing, premium members dropped to 1.25 million, while paid Niconico Channel subscribers reached 1.27 million—marking the first time **channel subscriptions officially overtook site-wide premium subscriptions**.

## Issues & Problems

The primary issues surrounding Niconico Douga include:

### Copyright Infringement

As with most user-generated video-sharing platforms, substantial volumes of media are uploaded without the authorization of rights holders.

Initially, management adhered to a strict notice-and-takedown policy, removing content only after receiving formal infringement complaints from copyright owners. Consequently, the platform was inundated with unauthorized anime, music, and television broadcasts, drawing heavy outside criticism for neglecting intellectual property laws.

While copyright enforcement mechanisms have since been substantially upgraded, derivative fan mashups known as **MADs** (remix/AMV videos) built from copyrighted audio and visuals achieved immense popularity on the platform. Many rights holders adopted a stance of tacit tolerance toward these tributes. This created an ambiguous copyright ecosystem: among regular Niconico users, the prevailing sentiment became that "MADs exist in a gray zone—if it hasn't been deleted, it's fair game." Outside the community, however, Niconico users are frequently criticized for having a deficient awareness of copyright law.

Following formal negotiations between operator Niwango, Inc. and three major Japanese content trade organizations—the Association of Japanese Animations (AJA), the Japan Video Software Association (JVA), and the Motion Picture Producers Association of Japan (MPPAJ)—Niwango agreed to delete all infringing uploads hosted on the site (including MAD videos) and implement continuous monitoring to promptly purge newly posted infringing content. This formal agreement was announced on July 2, 2008, by Niwango's parent firm, Dwango.

*Related articles: Copyright; Rights Holders; The MAD Deletion Controversy; List of Rights Holders Who Deleted Videos.*

### 2channel Culture & Demeanor

*(Note: 2channel was subsequently renamed 5channel.)*

A crucial structural difference between Niconico Douga and other video platforms is that during its initial (Kari) and early (β) iterations, **virtually the entire user base was composed of 2channel users ("2ch-ers").**

According to the Nico Nico Pedia article *"History of 2channel,"* around the time Niconico launched, 2channel was engulfed in legal and administrative crises threatening an imminent shutdown. Users actively seeking alternative digital sanctuaries discovered Niconico, colonized the platform, and laid down its foundational culture, humor, and commenting norms.

As a result, Niconico's comment section still retains strong elements of 2channel culture: sarcastic wit, fast-paced anonymous banter, and informal slang. While this creates a relaxed, participatory environment where commenting is effortless, it also causes friction. Users uncomfortable with 2channel-style cynicism often avoid the site entirely, while video creators unfamiliar with the tone have experienced severe distress upon receiving harsh or blunt feedback.

Furthermore, many young internet users first learned of 2channel through Niconico and subsequently visited the textboard. However, when newcomers attempted to post on 2channel threads using the playful jokes, manners, and enthusiasm specific to Niconico (*"Niconico nori"*), fierce clashes broke out between the two communities. New users are strongly urged to exercise caution when browsing 5channel.

### Deficiencies in Anti-Trolling Measures & Passive Response

Disruptive behavior, spamming, and trolling have long been chronic issues on Niconico Douga, largely because countermeasure features were poorly designed from the ground up.

While video creators have the ability to hide comments on their own videos, external third-party analysis sites make it easy for viewers to see which comments were hidden. The act of hiding comments often triggers accusations of censorship and incites worse flame wars, discouraging uploaders from using the tool. Meanwhile, the client-side "NG Word" and "NG User" (mute/block) lists have absurdly small allowances: 40 entries for standard members and only 200 for premium members—far too low for active users.

Moreover, because Niconico accounts can be registered using disposable, free webmail addresses, malicious individuals easily create hordes of throwaway accounts. These "sockpuppets" are routinely used to manipulate rankings, views, and voting metrics.

In *Nicovideo Community* groups, trolls expelled from a community would simply spin up alternate accounts and rejoin to continue disruptions. The community feature itself had frail security: bad actors regularly engaged in "community jacking"—seizing control of public user clubs to stream unauthorized broadcasts or link offensive content—resulting in a perpetual game of cat-and-mouse. (Following the devastating cyberattack in June 2024, the *Nicovideo Community* service was officially retired and shuttered permanently.)

Automated viewing features introduced during Niconico Douga (9), such as **"New Videos of the World"** (*Sekai no Shinchaku Douga*) and **"Nico Live Cruise,"** were even mocked as **"Official Trolling"** (*kōshiki arashi*). These features automatically channeled hundreds or thousands of wandering viewers into newly submitted videos or modest amateur streams, frequently subjecting unsuspecting creators to mass spam, mockery, or instant negative voting. Demands for fundamental improvements have persisted since launch, and the platform continues to face the challenge of designing systems where all participants can genuinely "smile" (nico-nico).

## Cultural Context & Terminology Guide

This guide explains cultural phenomena, 2channel conventions, and platform-specific jargon mentioned throughout the article.

---

### 1. 2channel (2ch / 5ch) & Its Roots in Niconico

- **What is 2channel?**  
  Founded in 1999 by Hiroyuki Nishimura, **2channel (2ch)** was Japan’s largest text-based anonymous message board (rebranded to **5channel [5ch]** in 2017). It was the birthplace of modern Japanese net culture, text memes, ASCII art, and anonymous humor.
- **The "Great Migration" to Niconico:**  
  When Niconico launched its prototype in late 2006, 2channel was embroiled in legal threats, domain disputes, and rumors of server bankruptcy. 2channel users (*"2ch-ers"*) explored alternative sites and discovered Niconico. Because Niconico allowed anonymous commenting directly on videos, 2ch users treated the video screen like an animated 2channel textboard, colonizing the service and forming its early identity.
- **"Niconico Nori" (ニコニコのノリ - Niconico Etiquette/Vibe):**  
  As Niconico grew, younger mainstream internet users joined who had never posted on 2channel. When these newcomers visited 2channel and posted with childish slang, overt enthusiasm, or inside jokes from Niconico, veteran 2channel denizens fiercely ridiculed them. The clash between 2channel's hardened, cynical anonymity and Niconico's playful, meme-heavy participatory culture was a major online cultural divide in the late 2000s.

---

### 2. "Nico-chuu" (ニコ厨)

- **Etymology:**  
  The suffix **-厨 (-chū)** originates from the 2channel derogatory slur **厨房** (*chūbō*), a pun on 中坊 (*chūbō*, middle school kid). On 2ch, anyone displaying immature, obsessive, or obnoxious behavior was branded a *chū*.
- **Meaning:**  
  A **Nico-chuu** is a hardcore, obsessive Niconico addict who spends all day on the site, speaks exclusively in site memes, and floods comment sections. While originally derogatory, veteran users and Dwango staff adopted the label with self-deprecating affection. The article notes that lead backend engineer **Akihiko Koizuka** was considered "the very first Nico-chuu" because he was constantly online tinkering with the service.

---

### 3. "MAD" / "MAD Videos" (MAD動画)

- **Definition:**  
  In Western internet culture, user-edited videos are often categorized into AMVs (Anime Music Videos) or YouTube Poops (YTPs). In Japan, the umbrella term is **MAD** (or MAD videos). 
- **Style & Significance:**  
  MADs range from intricate rhythm-synced audio remixes (where sound effects and dialogue from anime or games are cut into musical instruments) to parody collages and fan animations. Because early Niconico was fueled by viral MAD series (such as remixes of *The Melancholy of Haruhi Suzumiya*, *Touhou Project*, *Death Note*, and *Donald McDonald/Ronald McDonald* commercials), the platform existed in a legal gray zone that shaped its copyright controversies.

---

### 4. Nico Nico Terebi-chan (ニコニコテレビちゃん)

- **The Mascot:**  
  *Nico Nico Terebi-chan* is Niconico’s official corporate mascot: a boxy, retro CRT television set with two rabbit-ear antennas and a simplistic smiling face `(・∀・)`. In the original wiki dump, the image of this mascot beside the article title was parsed as plain text (`テレビ`), and its caption as `ニコニコ動画のニコニコテレビ`.

---

### 5. Who is "Foo-san" (Fooさん)?

- **The Harmonic Legend:**  
  Foo-san (real name Yohei Hirose) was an early Dwango sound engineer and platform personality. In 2007, Niconico's server architecture crashed constantly under immense traffic. Whenever maintenance screens appeared, Dwango would broadcast live audio of Foo-san playing a melancholy harmonica to entertain stranded users. He became an icon of early Niconico lore.

---

### 6. Version Eras: From (仮) to "Niconico Returns"

Niconico historically named its major platform revisions after developmental phases, seasonal puns, or Tokyo train stations:

- **(仮) [Kari - Provisional]:** The December 2006 prototype that piggybacked on YouTube's servers before YouTube IP-blocked Niconico.
- **(β), (γ), (RC), (RC2), (SP1):** Early iterative builds (2007–2008) where Niconico built its own internal video server, **SMILEVIDEO**.
- **(Summer), (Autumn), (Winter):** Seasonal updates in 2008.
- **(原宿 / Harajuku) & (GINZA):** Named after major entertainment districts in Tokyo where Dwango operated physical retail shops and studios (Niconico Honsha).
- **(く / Ku or Kresko):** The 2018 update. The bracket character "く" is both Japanese hiragana and the Esperanto word *Kresko* (growth).
- **The June 2024 Ransomware Attack & (Re:Kari):**  
  On June 8, 2024, a devastating cyberattack hit KADOKAWA/Dwango’s internal servers, forcing engineers to sever all network connections. To give users a place to gather while the primary infrastructure was rebuilt from bare metal, Dwango engineers coded an emergency backup site in six days called **Niconico (Re:仮)**, serving classic pre-2014 videos. The full restoration on August 5, 2024 was named **"Kaettekita Niconico" (帰ってきたニコニコ / Niconico Returns)**, an affectionate homage to classic Showa-era tokusatsu reboots like *Return of Ultraman* (*Kaettekita Ultraman*).

---

### 7. "Official Trolling" (公式荒らし)

- **New Videos of the World (世界の新着動画) & Nico Live Cruise (ニコ生クルーズ):**  
  These were automated features that took thousands of viewers on a collective "tour." The system would automatically jump between random newly uploaded videos or low-view live streams, giving the touring mob 30 seconds to vote on whether to stay or skip. In practice, large swarms of anonymous spectators raided small, earnest creators to spam negative comments, trigger premature skip votes, and disrupt broadcasts. Because this behavior was structurally encouraged by Dwango’s own system architecture, users sardonically dubbed it **"Official Trolling."**

---

### 8. NG Settings (NG機能)

- **Japanese Net Slang:**  
  In Japan, **NG** ("No Good") is standard *wasei-eigo* (Japanese pseudo-English) meaning "forbidden," "unacceptable," or "blocked." On Niconico, an **NG Word** is a muted keyword filter, and an **NG User** is a blocked user ID whose comments will not appear on your screen.

---

### 9. Turning Profitable (黒字化 - Kurojika)

- **The Financial Meme:**  
  Throughout 2007–2009, Niconico burned through massive amounts of bandwidth and venture capital without generating profit, leading traditional tech analysts to predict Dwango's imminent collapse. Turning the company "in the black" (*kurojika*) became a running meta-joke between management and users. On April Fools' Day 2010, Dwango jokingly announced they were profitable; when they released legitimate audited earnings proving they turned profitable in May 2010, it was celebrated as a milestone in Japanese web history.

---

### 10. Shiri-P (尻P / Housuke Nojiri) & The "-P" Suffix

- **The Producer (-P) Tradition:**  
  In Vocaloid and *THE iDOLM@STER* communities on Niconico, video creators, animators, and music composers are bestowed a producer pseudonym ending with **-P** (short for "Producer"). These names are rarely chosen by the creators themselves; instead, the community crowns them based on their first hit video or an embarrassing inside joke.
- **Housuke Nojiri:**  
  A prestigious, award-winning Japanese hard science-fiction novelist (*Usurper of the Sun*, *Rocket Girls*). When he joined Niconico as a Vocaloid creator, his early 3D papercraft and animation experiments focused humorously on papercraft skirts and posterior physics. The community promptly christened this established literary figure **Shiri-P** (literally **"Butt-P"**)—a name he proudly adopted. In December 2008, he leveraged his cultural standing to demand that adult fans stop leeching free bandwidth and support the platform financially by subscribing to Premium.
