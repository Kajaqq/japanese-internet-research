**DWANGO Co., Ltd.** (株式会社ドワンゴ) is a Japanese information technology and entertainment company.

Since July 2013, its corporate headquarters have been located in the Kabukiza Tower in Ginza, Chuo-ku, Tokyo.

---

## Overview

In gaming terms, Dwango is the "Final Boss" that emerges once you defeat the mid-boss, Niwango.

Dwango Co., Ltd. was originally established in 1997 as the Japanese subsidiary of the US corporation Interactive Visual Systems (IVS). In 1998, following the shutdown of Interactive Visual Systems, the Japanese branch broke away as an independent entity. Notably, Dwango Co., Ltd. inherited all worldwide rights and trademarks to the name "DWANGO."

While the company manages diverse business sectors today, it originally made its name through technical prowess—providing technical support for Windows CE on the Sega Dreamcast and engineering video distribution pipelines for early internet content.

Before long, polyphonic ringtone services (*chakumero*)—offering 16-chord and 32-chord melodies for flip phones—became the company's primary cash cow.

Dwango ran advertisements for these ringtone services on 2channel from very early on. Through all sorts of behind-the-scenes connections arising from that relationship, the company became absurdly close with 2channel founder Hiroyuki Nishimura: hiring him to narrate Dwango's television commercials and even establishing a subsidiary, Niwango, where Hiroyuki was appointed to the board of directors. (Hiroyuki eventually resigned from the board in 2013.)

In 2008, massive public excitement surrounding the announcement of the *Niconico Douga (ββ)* upgrade drove Dwango's stock to rank 4th among the top daily gainers on the First Section of the Tokyo Stock Exchange. [1]

On October 1, 2014, Dwango and publisher KADOKAWA merged operations under a joint holding company named **KADOKAWA・DWANGO** (now simply KADOKAWA Corporation). Consequently, Dwango was delisted from the stock exchange on September 26, 2014.

In October 2015, Dwango formally absorbed its subsidiary Niwango, becoming the operator of niconico in both name and fact.

Following subsequent internal corporate restructuring within the KADOKAWA Group, Dwango is now operated as a wholly owned subsidiary of KADOKAWA.

### Operated Services & Internal Divisions

- **Niconico (ニコニコ):** Video community platform (Portal Division)
- **BOOK☆WALKER:** Comprehensive digital manga and light novel storefront
- **Dokusho Meter (読書メーター):** Reading log and book recommendation service
- **dwango.jp:** Mobile content service (Mobile Division)
- **Dwango JP Audiobook (formerly ListenGo by dwango.jp):** Digital audiobook platform
- **animelo mix:** Anime and game music download service for mobile devices
- **Dwango JP Store:** Official e-commerce merchandise shop
- **Niconico Chokaigi (ニコニコ超会議):** Mega-convention celebrating Japanese internet culture (Live Event Division)
- **Animelo Summer Live (Anisama):** The world's largest annual anime song concert festival
- **ZEN Study (formerly N-Prep / N Yobikou):** Interactive digital learning app (Education Division)
- **Netmune (formerly U&R records):** Independent music creator management and support service
- **Music Monetization Service:** Digital copyright and royalty distribution service for creators
- **IIV:** Original multimedia IP and light novel label
- **ASCII Dwango:** Technical and software engineering book publishing imprint
- **iBunko (i文庫):** Renowned e-reader software application
- **OpenToonz:** Open-source 2D animation production software suite
- **Dwango Media Village (DMV):** Applied AI and machine learning research unit (more product-oriented than the former AI Lab)
- **sheeta / Niconico Channel Plus / Sapocia:** Fan-club and paid community infrastructure platform
- **The VOCALOID Collection (Vocacolle):** Bi-annual music festival and competition dedicated to synthetic singing voice culture

#### Defunct / Terminated Services

- **DWANGO:** Dedicated dial-up and online multiplayer gaming network service
- **Dwango Kamone:** Official i-mode mobile gaming portal
- **Mobile Games 100 (formerly Dwango 7, Iromelo Games):** Comprehensive mobile gaming hub
- **Packet Radio (2005 – Nov 2007):** A service streaming digital radio broadcasts to mobile phones using packet data transmission (abbreviated as *Pake-Raji*). Users ran a dedicated app on compatible feature phones. When *Niconico Douga Mobile* launched in 2007, it initially recycled this infrastructure to stream mobile video.
- **LiveDwangoReader:** Web-based RSS feed reader (Dwango adopted and preserved the service after Livedoor Reader was slated for shutdown)
- **SmartySmile:** Mobile entertainment site
- **Ge-Maga:** Mobile companion app for *Denfaminico Game Magazine* (transferred to Vaka Inc.)
- **LOVE LOCK:** Digital padlocking app for couples and friends (closed August 2017) [2]
- **Dwango Artificial Intelligence Laboratory:** Dedicated general artificial intelligence research facility (closed March 31, 2019) [3]
- **Teku Teku Teku Teku:** Location-based mobile RPG
- **dwango.jp news:** Online entertainment news publication (closed June 2023)
- **Ncon:** Marriage matchmaking service (closed June 2024)

## Cultural Context & Terminology Guide: Dwango

- **DWANGO's Original Name & Gaming Heritage:**  
  Long before Niconico, DWANGO originated in the United States in 1994 as an acronym for **Dial-up Wide-Area Network Gaming Operation**. It was a pioneering proprietary dial-up online gaming service that allowed PC gamers to play multiplayer *DOOM*, *DOOM II*, and *Duke Nukem 3D* with low latency before the modern internet was widespread. The Japanese subsidiary survived the US company's collapse, bought the worldwide IP, and pivoted into early internet tech and cell phone services.
- **"Mid-Boss Niwango, Final Boss Dwango":**  
  A classic piece of gamer humor on Nico Nico Pedia. Early users were confused about corporate hierarchies because the public-facing operator of Niconico was Niwango, Inc., but whenever major infrastructure decisions, legal settlements, or controversial policies occurred, Dwango appeared as the real corporate authority.
- **Chakumero (着メロ) & The 16/32-Chord Goldmine:**  
  In the late 1990s and early 2000s, before smartphones, Japanese mobile phones (*garakei*) had specialized MIDI sound chips capable of playing polyphonic ringtones (first 4-chord, then 16-chord, then 32-chord synthesis). Users paid monthly subscriptions to download popular songs as synthesized ring melodies. Dwango became a financial juggernaut by dominating this market through *dwango.jp* and *animelo mix*, giving them the cash reserves required to bankroll Niconico's massive server deficits in 2007.
- **"Bettari Suguru" (べったりすぐる) & Hiroyuki:**  
  The article playfully remarks that Dwango was "in bed with Hiroyuki way too much." The word *suguru* (すぐる) is classic 2channel internet slang for *sugiru* (すぎる, "excessively / way too much"). Dwango's founder, Nobuo Kawakami, was fascinated by 2channel's organic community and formed an intimate alliance with its creator, Hiroyuki Nishimura. Dwango spent large advertising budgets on 2channel, cast Hiroyuki in national TV commercials, and appointed him as a director of Niwango.
- **Pake-Raji (Packet Radio) and Mobile Video:**  
  During the mid-2000s, Japanese mobile carrier NTT DoCoMo offered flat-rate "packet" data billing for flip phones. Dwango tried to launch digital internet radio over this network (*Pake-Raji*). While the radio service failed to catch on, its underlying streaming architecture was salvaged by Dwango engineers to launch *Niconico Douga Mobile* in 2007, making Niconico one of the earliest platforms in the world to stream video to feature phones.
- **Livedoor Reader Rescue:**  
  In 2014, when portal company Livedoor announced it was killing its beloved RSS aggregator *livedoor Reader*, Japanese internet power users erupted in mourning. Dwango stepped in, acquired the software for free, and rebranded it as *LiveDwangoReader* purely as a gesture of goodwill to the old-school Japanese web community.
- **The Controversial Job Application Fee:**  
  In 2014, Dwango made national headlines when it required prospective new university graduates applying for jobs at Dwango to pay a non-refundable "application examination fee" of several thousand yen. Dwango claimed this was to discourage frivolous mass applications and cover administrative costs, causing a fierce public debate over Japanese employment practices.
- **From Networks & FromSoftware Connection:**  
  In 2000, long before *Dark Souls* or *Elden Ring*, FromSoftware partnered with Dwango to form *From Networks*, a joint venture focused on building online networking code for console games. Dwango has maintained deep ties with the Japanese core gaming industry throughout its history, eventually acquiring legendary developer/publisher Spike Chunsoft.
