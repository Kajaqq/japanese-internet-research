*(Also written as: UTAU3人娘)*

**The Three UTAU Girls** (*UTAU Sannin Musume*) is the collective community unit name for the trio consisting of **Utane Uta (Defoko)**, **Kasane Teto**, and **Momone Momo**.

---

## Overview

*(Embedded Content)*

The "Three UTAU Girls" refers to the foundational trio comprising **Utane Uta (Defoko)**—the default voicebank bundled with the singing synthesis software *UTAU*—alongside **Kasane Teto** and **Momone Momo**, two of the earliest voice libraries publicly released for the software.  
The earliest known video to unite all three in a single musical work is considered to be *"Had UTAU Sing 1/6 no Yume Tabibito"* (*1/6の夢旅人をUTAUで歌わせた*), uploaded by producer **Inuhebi P**.

Following this, because Defoko, Teto, and Momo were present from the very infancy of the software, debuted within a very narrow window of one another, and each commanded immense individual popularity, the community frequently grouped them together as the symbolic faces of UTAU. Over time, fans universally embraced the title **"The Three UTAU Girls."**  
*(Note: In English-speaking overseas communities, they are commonly referred to as the **"UTAU 3 Girls"** or the **"UTAU Trio"**).*

Within the MikuMikuDance (MMD) community, numerous modelers have created full sets containing all three characters. Prominent examples include the early models sculpted by Nanami Watabe, Nendoroid-style chibi models by Enamel P, the "Bakumori" (stylized/busty) and standard models by Sourou P, and the distinctive CHANxCO-style minimalist chibi models by Tawashi P.

## The "Three UTAU Girls" Festival

In July 2011, a dedicated livestream event titled the **"Three UTAU Girls Festival"** (*UTAU Sannin Musume Matsuri*) was held on Niconico Live within the "Momo Lounge" community.  
Hosted by **Momoko Fujimoto** (the real-life voice provider of Momone Momo), the broadcast served as a guided tour showcasing the vast catalog of classic videos, songs, and animations produced for the trio over the years.

## Related Videos

### The Trio Singing Together

Part of what makes this trio so musically captivating is the stark, complementary contrast between their voice libraries:
- **Defoko (Utane Uta):** A purely synthetic, machine-generated voice (she has no human voice actress; her audio is generated algorithmically). Her delivery is monotone, dispassionate, and distinctly robotic.
- **Kasane Teto:** A pitch-shifted, resampled human voicebank (recorded by Mayo Oyamano). Her delivery is punchy, energetic, and bright.
- **Momone Momo:** An unmanipulated, natural human recording (voiced by Momoko Fujimoto). Her delivery is soft, breathy, gentle, and relaxed (*yukkuri*).

When all three sing in three-part harmony, this dramatic divergence in vocal character produces a wonderfully layered, textured sound.

## Trivia & Tidbits

- **Early Competing Lineup:** In the absolute earliest weeks of UTAU (Spring 2008), a few videos used the moniker "Three UTAU Girls" to refer to a different trio: **Kasane Teto**, **Kugyuroid** (an early experimental soundbank sampling voice actress Rie Kugimiya), and **Defo-imouto** (Defoko's fan-made fictional little sister).
- **Real-Life Friendship:** Kasane Teto's voice provider (Mayo Oyamano) and Momone Momo's voice provider (Momoko Fujimoto) are close personal friends in real life, a warm dynamic that was highlighted in published magazine interviews.
- **The "DTM" Coincidence:** If you take the standard romanized nicknames of the three girls and arrange their first initials in the exact chronological order their voicebanks were released:
  - **D**efoko (March 2008)
  - **T**eto (April 2008)
  - **M**omo (May 2008)  
  ...They miraculously spell out **DTM** (**D**esktop **M**usic / computer music production)!  
  → Hence the fandom unit tag: **DTM (Defoko, Teto, Momo)**.

## Cultural Context & Slang Guide: The Three UTAU Girls

### 1. Showbiz Trope: "Sannin Musume" (三人娘)
The term **Sannin Musume** (三人娘, literally *"The Three Young Daughters / Starlets"*) is an iconic archetype in Japanese entertainment history. 

It originally dates back to the 1950s golden age of Japanese cinema and music, where record labels and studios grouped three breakout teen starlets into a friendly triumvirate (most famously Hibari Misora, Chiemi Eri, and Izumi Yukimura in 1955, and later the 1970s pop idol trio of Momoe Yamaguchi, Junko Sakurada, and Masako Mori). 

In Japanese anime and vocal synthesis fandoms, whenever three distinct, complementary female characters emerge simultaneously at the dawn of a franchise, fans instinctively christen them the *Sannin Musume*.

### 2. Who Are the Three UTAU Girls?
While you already know Kasane Teto, the other two members of the trio represent the foundational history of the **UTAU** software:

1. **Utane Uta / Defoko (唄音ウタ / デフォ子):**
   - **The Name:** Her official name is *Utane Uta*, but everyone calls her **Defoko** (デフォ子, *"Default Girl"*), because she is the default soundbank pre-installed with UTAU.
   - **Her Voice:** Unlike Vocaloids or other UTAU banks, **Defoko has no human voice actor**. UTAU developer Ameya/Ayame used phoneme audio generated by **AquesTalk**—the lightweight text-to-speech software from AQUEST Corporation (famous for providing the robotic voice for **Yukkuri** memes). This gives her an unmistakable, emotionless robotic voice.
   - **Visuals:** Lavender short hair, a purple beret, and a perpetual deadpan, half-asleep expression.

2. **Kasane Teto (重音テト):**
   - The April Fool's 2008 hoax created on 2channel VIP that became a real UTAU bank. Voiced by Mayo Oyamano, with her audio heavily pitch-shifted and formant-processed, giving her a punchy, nasal, energetic pop-idol sound.

3. **Momone Momo (桃音モモ):**
   - Released in May 2008 by creator Momoko Fujimoto, Momo was the first major UTAU bank recorded to demonstrate that UTAU could handle **completely clean, natural, unprocessed human voice sampling**.
   - Her voice is sweet, gentle, breathy, and human.
   - **Visuals:** Pink hair with side buns, green eyes, and a cute pink maid uniform.

### 3. The "DTM" Miracle
In Japan, computer music production, sequencing, and home recording are universally referred to as **DTM** (an acronym for **D**esktop **M**usic, a term coined by Roland in the late 1980s). 

Fans consider it an incredible stroke of cosmic serendipity that the first three breakout heroines of the freeware music synthesizer happened to spell **D-T-M** in the exact order of their debut:
- **D**efoko (March 2008)
- **T**eto (April 2008)
- **M**omo (May 2008)

Producers regularly title group songs and collaborative albums **"DTM"** as a playful double entendre meaning both *"Desktop Music"* and *"Defoko, Teto, Momo."*

### 4. "Kugyuroid" (くぎゅロイド)
Mentioned in the trivia section, **Kugyuroid** was an early experimental UTAU soundbank created in April 2008. Anonymous users ripped voice lines spoken by superstar anime voice actress **Rie Kugimiya** (affectionately nicknamed *Kugyu* by fans—famed for playing tsundere heroines like Taiga Aisaka in *Toradora!* and Shana in *Shakugan no Shana*) and mapped them into an UTAU vocal bank. 

While it was briefly viral among early adopters, the community quickly moved away from unconsented celebrity voice ripping, establishing strict ethical guidelines that UTAU banks must only be created using original audio provided with explicit consent.

### 5. "CHANxCO-Style" (CHANxCO風)
Mentioned in the 3D model section, **CHANxCO** is a famous professional Japanese illustrator renowned in the Vocaloid community for their distinctively comical art style: characters drawn with completely round, egg-like heads, tiny dot eyes, wide derpy mouths, and stubby noodle limbs. 

CHANxCO’s designs became a massive meme on Niconico, inspiring official Good Smile Company Nendoroid figures and dedicated MMD models characterized by clumsy, adorable, wobbling dance animations.
