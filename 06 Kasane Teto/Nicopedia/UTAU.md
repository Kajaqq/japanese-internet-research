**UTAU** is a singing voice synthesis software program.

---

## Overview

Its official name is **"Singing Voice Synthesis Tool UTAU" (歌声合成ツールUTAU)**. It is essentially free singing voice synthesis software developed from a support tool for *Jinriki VOCALOID* (a utility designed for splicing and pasting WAV audio files).  
The developer is Ameya/Ayame (飴屋／菖蒲), commonly known on Niconico Douga as **Ameya-P**.

For details on the history leading up to the creation of UTAU, please refer to the article on *Rorine Com*. Although Wikipedia has somehow tagged it with a "citation needed" marker, the facts compiled in the *Rorine Com* article are completely true.

The most defining feature of UTAU is that the singing "voice" can be freely swapped and customized.  
By default, UTAU comes packaged with a female voicebank (a set of raw sound files).  
Furthermore, users can record the Japanese syllabary (*gojūon*) spoken by themselves or others, configure them as WAV audio files, and register them as custom voicebanks to be used freely.

A massive number of voicebanks (raw sound file sets) have been created by volunteers and community members, available for free download from official character sites and repositories. Commonly used voicebanks include **Kasane Teto** and **Momone Momo** (female vocals).  
*(See the "UTAU Voicebank Directory" for an extensive list; however, countless other voicebanks created both inside and outside Japan remain undocumented.)*

On August 1, 2009, a song demonstrating the newly implemented **VCV (連続音, *renzokuon*)** soundbank system was released. Due to its remarkably natural and smooth vocal output, this milestone was hailed by parts of the community as the **"August Revolution."**  
*(For more details, please see the "UTAU VCV Voicebanks" article.)*

Starting with version 0.2.60 released in January 2010, the software’s official licensing classification shifted from "freeware" to "shareware." However, there are virtually no feature restrictions or trial time limits; users can still use it comfortably without registering a license key, just as they did with the freeware version. In short, purchasing a license is merely a way to donate to development funds in exchange for a few bonus perks—functionally, it remains freeware (or donationware) as before. For details, refer to posts #1 and #2 on the developer's blog.

On May 27, 2011, **UTAU-Synth**, an official port for Mac OS X, was released (see External Links).

On September 18, 2013, a work was released utilizing the CVVC plugin *autoCVVC for Namine* alongside the compatible voicebank **Namine Ritsu Eve**. This significantly lowered the barrier to entry for CVVC voicebanks, which had previously seen limited adoption due to their complex phoneme-input workflow.  
*(For more details, please see the "CVVC Recorded Voicebanks" article.)*

> ***Note: Please submit bug reports and feature requests to the UTAU Community BBS rather than the comments section of Ameya-P's personal blog. (From the developer's blog)***

## Status on Niconico Douga

When an UTAU character shares a song alongside a VOCALOID, the video is eligible for inclusion in aggregate rankings such as the Weekly VOCALOID Ranking; however, videos featuring UTAU solo were historically excluded. In official and community livestreams covering the "Big Three" (VOCALOID, *The Idolm@ster*, and *Touhou Project*), Voc@loidM@ster events, or Touhou VOCALOID, UTAU is generally grouped together under the broader VOCALOID umbrella.

Following the category overhaul and ranking system changes introduced in **Niconico Douga (9)**, UTAU effectively became a **"category orphan"** (カテゴリ迷子). While most creators posted under the "Music" category, this caused friction during *Sekai no Shinchaku Douga* ("World's New Arrival Videos"), a live-stream discovery feature introduced in Niconico (9). If an UTAU song appeared while browsing other categories, viewers from other genres would complain: *"Take this to VOCALOID!"* Yet, when uploaders categorized their videos under VOCALOID, purists would attack them, saying: *"This is the wrong category!"* Consequently, comments on featured UTAU videos frequently devolved into flame wars, leading to the creation of the tongue-in-cheek tag **"Victims of World's New Arrival Videos"** (世界の新着動画被害者の会).  
*(※ For differences between UTAU and VOCALOID, refer to the "UTAU ≠ VOCALOID" article.)*

During the *Niconico Douga Category Conference* held on November 16, 2011, representatives from Niconico management, Yamaha, and site co-founder Hiroyuki Nishimura agreed that it was entirely acceptable for UTAU videos to be posted under the VOCALOID category. Since then, the proportion of UTAU videos residing in the VOCALOID category has grown, and complaints about "wrong category placement" have steadily diminished. (That said, final category selection rests with the uploader; viewers should bear in mind that aggressively attacking a creator simply because you disagree with their category choice is completely unwarranted.)

## Cultural Context & Japanese Internet Slang Notes

### 1. Jinriki VOCALOID (人力VOCALOID)
Literally *"Human-Powered VOCALOID."* This is a long-standing Japanese remixing culture and MAD video genre where creators manually cut up existing voice clips of real people, anime characters, or celebrities from broadcasts, pitch-correct them, and stitch each syllable together so the character appears to "sing." Because manual audio slicing in early digital audio workstations was painstaking, developer Ameya-P originally built a small tool to automate and streamline this cutting-and-pasting process. That prototype was subsequently expanded with pitch interpolation and synthesis algorithms, resulting in the creation of **UTAU**.

### 2. The "-P" Suffix (Ameya-P / 飴屋P)
In Niconico and Japanese virtual singer culture, creators (music producers, video animators, modelers) are granted the title **"P"** (short for **Producer**, a tradition borrowed from *The Idolm@ster* fandom). Once a creator becomes well-known, the community or the creator themselves adopts a producer handle (e.g., Ameya-P for the software's creator).

### 3. Rorine Com (炉利音コム)
A precursor community project originating from 2channel's `news4vip` board around early 2008. Netizens were experimenting with text-to-speech tools and slicing audio samples to create synthetic singing voices, laying the cultural groundwork that Ameya-P built upon when releasing UTAU.

### 4. The "August Revolution" (8月革命)
Early UTAU voicebanks were exclusively **CV (単独音, *tandokuon*)**, meaning each sample was a standalone syllable (e.g., "ka", "ki", "ku"). This produced a famously robotic, staccato transition between notes. On August 1, 2009, Ameya introduced native support for **VCV (連続音, *renzokuon*)**, which records vowel-consonant-vowel transitions (e.g., "- a", "a ka", "a sa"). This resulted in dramatically smoother, human-like singing, completely transforming UTAU's reputation across the internet from a "toy" into a serious competitor to commercial software.

### 5. Niconico Douga (9) and "Category Orphans" (カテゴリ迷子)
Niconico Douga denotes its major platform version eras with bracketed names (e.g., *RC*, *SP1*, *(9)*, *Harajuku*, *Q*). In late 2009, the **Niconico Douga (9)** release restructured video tags and ranking categories into rigid silos. UTAU uploaders had no dedicated category; placing videos in "Music" buried them under mainstream content, while putting them in "VOCALOID" upset vocaloid purists because UTAU is technically not Yamaha's proprietary engine. This left UTAU creators stuck as *category orphans* (カテゴリ迷子).

### 6. Sekai no Shinchaku Douga (世界の新着動画 - "World's New Arrival Videos")
A feature introduced during Niconico (9) where newly uploaded videos from specific categories were automatically broadcast into a live stream queue for users to view. Live viewers could vote whether to keep watching or skip to the next video. Because broad audiences with no interest in indie vocal synthesis were suddenly exposed to niche UTAU songs, chat rooms routinely erupted into flame wars telling uploaders to "go away to VOCALOID." Videos rejected or harassed during these automated broadcasts were sympathetically tagged as **"Victims of World's New Arrival Videos"** (世界の新着動画被害者の会).

### 7. The "Big Three" of Niconico (ニコニコ御三家, *Gosanke*)
During Niconico's golden era (roughly 2007–2012), the three most dominant user-generated content fandoms that drove the majority of the site's traffic and collaborative remix culture were dubbed the "Big Three": **VOCALOID**, **The Idolm@ster**, and **Touhou Project**.

### 8. VIPPALOID
A portmanteau of VOCALOID and VIP, referring to 2channel's notorious text board `news4vip`. On April Fools' Day 2008, 2channel users concocted a prank claiming a "new VOCALOID3" character named **Kasane Teto** had been announced by Crypton Future Media. When the prank was revealed, users took the character design and recorded a voicebank for her using the newly released UTAU software, turning an internet troll into UTAU's most famous and beloved mascot.

### 9. Oekakiko (お絵カキコ)
A native web-browser painting applet built directly into Niconico Douga's forum threads and Nico Nico Pedia articles. Readers frequently draw and submit character fanart directly to encyclopedia articles, which are then archived under the term *Oekakiko*.

### 10. Windows 100% Group (窓百組)
*Windows 100%* was a popular monthly Japanese computing print magazine. Between 2008 and 2014, its bonus CD/DVD discs frequently included exclusive professional 3D MMD models and custom UTAU voicebanks commissioned from famous illustrators and voice actors, creating a whole sub-family of characters known colloquially as the "Win100 group."
