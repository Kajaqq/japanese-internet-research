# April Fool's Day (エイプリルフール)

> Original Source: [エイプリルフール - ニコニコ大百科 (Nico Nico Pedia)](https://dic.nicovideo.jp/a/%E3%82%A8%E3%82%A4%E3%83%97%E3%83%AA%E3%83%AB%E3%83%95%E3%83%BC%E3%83%AB)

**April Fool's Day** (*エイプリルフール*) is an annual custom celebrated on April 1st where people are permitted to play harmless, playful pranks and tell white lies.

---

## Overview

In Japanese, the day is also historically referred to as **Bangu-setsu** (*万愚節*, "All Fools' Festival") or **Shigatsu-baka** (*四月馬鹿*, "April Fool"). While several theories exist regarding its historical origins, none are definitive, and exactly when and where the custom began remains uncertain.

Internationally, the British public broadcaster BBC is renowned for broadcasting deadpan April Fool's hoaxes. In Japan, companies such as **Tsuburaya Productions** (the creators of *Ultraman*) and game developer **Irem** historically built legendary reputations for preparing astonishingly elaborate, budget-heavy interactive hoaxes for this single day [1].

Across the Japanese web, corporate homepages frequently debut full-scale joke redesigns on April 1st. In otaku subcultures, visual novel (*bishōjo* / *eroge*) studios draw particular attention, and it has become an unofficial tradition for at least one studio to inexplicably rebrand its game with a "hyper-macho muscular bodybuilder" theme.

In software engineering (especially open-source circles), April 1st has long inspired playful behavioral quirks. Notable historical examples include PHP's server status output (`phpinfo()`) generating humorous easter eggs when executed on April 1st, as well as the Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF) publishing comedic "April Fools' Day RFCs" annually.

Reflecting this tech-industry heritage, modern mobile gacha and online games frequently introduce special one-day-only gameplay modes or joke campaigns. Cygames (developer of *The Idolmaster Cinderella Girls* and *Granblue Fantasy*) is particularly notorious for rolling out massive, fully voiced standalone minigames every April 1st without fail.

However, entering the 2010s, several corporate pioneers of elaborate April Fool's hoaxes withdrew from the practice. The trend largely shifted toward lightweight promotional gags: simple video sketches, joke tweets, or digitally altered teaser images. With the widespread adoption of social media, individual user hoaxes have proliferated (though occasionally irresponsible individuals have crossed legal boundaries into criminal territory, sparking police arrests and news headlines).

In recent years, fully playable custom games and high-production joke websites have dwindled. Even long-running traditions—such as tech outlet Impress's *Mado no Mori* publishing annual parody articles—have scaled back dramatically, reflecting a noticeable fatigue in novel ideas [2].

Nico Nico Douga regularly executes official April Fool's campaigns, though controversial stunts like the "Tarako-kun Navigator" and "Zwatch" were widely panned by users as irritating rather than amusing—earning the platform’s hoaxes a rather spotty reputation.

On rare occasions, a studio will announce a genuine, real-world project disguised as an April Fool's prank, only to confirm its authenticity a few days later.

*(Note: In certain regional UK traditions, pranks are strictly forbidden after 12:00 PM midday, at which point the trickster must reveal the joke. As this is a local foreign custom, it does not apply in Japan).*  
*(Linguistic Note: The Japanese term "エイプリルフール" [Eipuriru Fūru] is a loanword phrase used to denote the holiday itself; in standard English, calling someone an "April Fool" refers to the victim who was tricked).*

### Real Announcements Disguised as April Fool's

Important real-world announcements made between late March 31 and April 1 that were genuinely true, but were initially doubted because people assumed they were pranks:

- **T.M. Opera O’s Defeat (2001):** When the dominant champion racehorse unexpectedly lost the Sankei Osaka Hai, Kansai TV sports announcer Tetsuji Baba famously yelled on-air: *"This is not an April Fool's joke! It is real, Opera O has been defeated!"*
- **"Little Busters!" Anime Adaptation Announced (2012):** Officially unveiled late on March 31st; because most fans woke up to the news on the morning of April 1st, it was widely doubted until verified by Key.
- **Passing of Architect Zaha Hadid (2016):** Occurred on March 31st overseas, but broke across Japanese morning newsfeeds on April 1st.
- **"Ore Tower" Service Termination (2017):** The management explicitly had to tweet: *"This is NOT an April Fool's joke."*
- **Various Celebrity & Voice Actor Marriage Announcements:**  
  - Takaya Aoyagi & Kaede Yuasa (2021)
  - Takuya Nakashima (2021) — *"It just happened to land on this date. Please believe me!"*
  - Asuka Nishi (2021) — *"I know it's April Fool's Day, but..."*
  - Mami Yamashita (2022) — *"So that we may live days so happy they feel like a lie..."*
  - Minoru Ogino (2024) — *"It's real! Please believe me! I swear!"*
  - Karin Ogino (2024) — *"It may sound like a lie, but it is not. It is the truth."*
- **"Laid-Back Camp" (Yuru Camp) Movie Teaser Visual 2 (2022):** Dropped late on March 31st featuring an off-beat visual tone, prompting fans to mistake it for the studio's April Fool's joke.

## Footnotes

1. **Corporate Withdrawals:** Tsuburaya Productions suspended its annual April Fool's hoaxes in 2011 out of voluntary public self-restraint (*jishuku*) following the Great East Japan Earthquake; after chief coordinator Yohei Tsuburaya left the company in 2012, the tradition ended permanently. Developer Irem similarly canceled its 2011 event due to the earthquake; core staff later departed to establish developer Granzella, carrying the joke tradition over to their new company. Similarly, the official *Doraemon Channel* website historically rebranded itself as "Suneo Channel" every April 1st, ending after 2010’s "Toraemon Channel."
2. **Media Reductions:** Tech publication *Mado no Mori* historically published four illustrated fake news stories every year, but slashed its coverage in half by 2016, pointing to industry-wide concept fatigue.

## Cultural Context & Slang Guide: April Fool's Day

### 1. "Uso kara Deta Makoto" (嘘から出たまこと)
A venerable Japanese proverb meaning **"A truth spawned from a lie"** (or a reality born from a falsehood). 
In Western fandom, this is sometimes called the "Pinocchio Effect" or "Ascended Meme." In Japanese internet history, **Kasane Teto** is celebrated as the absolute golden standard of this proverb: a character created maliciously to fool gullible otaku that evolved—through community love, open-source software, and fan music—into an iconic digital diva who eventually starred in official concerts alongside Hatsune Miku.

### 2. Corporate Hoax Kings: Irem & Tsuburaya
In Western internet culture, Google was historically famous for elaborate April Fool's gags. In Japan, that throne belonged to **Irem** (creators of *R-Type* and *Steambot Chronicles*) and **Tsuburaya Productions** (*Ultraman*). 
- **Irem:** Created massive, fully navigable, multi-page parody websites complete with fake game trailers, fictional recruitment portals, and mock dating simulators that took months of internal engineering budget to build.
- **Tsuburaya:** Staged elaborate narratives where Ultraman monsters opened blogs, took over social media, or complained about workplace conditions.

### 3. Post-3/11 "Jishuku" (自粛) and the Decline of Japanese Hoaxes
Footnote #1 mentions why these elaborate corporate hoaxes vanished: the **Great East Japan Earthquake of March 11, 2011**. 
Following the disaster, Japan entered a nationwide period of **jishuku** (voluntary mourning, self-restraint, and solemnity). Staging lighthearted hoaxes or corporate pranks just three weeks after a national tragedy was viewed as deeply insensitive. By the time society recovered in 2012–2013, key creative directors had left, corporate compliance had tightened, and social media made legal liability for false announcements far riskier.

### 4. Niconico’s April Fool Stumbles: "Tarako-kun" and "Zwatch"
The article dryly notes that Niconico's own April Fool's pranks were often despised:
- **Zwatch (2013):** During the *Nico Nico Douga (Q)* era, management was heavily criticized for degrading the video player's performance. For April Fool's 2013, they launched "Zwatch"—a mock smart-watch that forcefully occupied user screens, causing widespread user annoyance.
- **Tarako-kun (タラコくん):** A caricature of 2channel and Niconico founder Hiroyuki Nishimura (nicknamed *Tarako* / "cod roe lips" due to his distinct facial features). Forced intrusive animated popups of Tarako-kun across the video player triggered widespread backlash from viewers who just wanted to watch their videos in peace.
