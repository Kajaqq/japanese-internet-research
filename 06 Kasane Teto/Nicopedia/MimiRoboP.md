**Mimi Robo P** (耳ロボP) is a music producer and video creator who produces original songs and covers utilizing a wide array of vocal synthesis technologies, including UTAU, VOCALOID, Sinsy, and Delay Lama.

## Overview

### Musical Style & Toolkit: The Ultimate DTMer
Mimi Robo P makes extensive use of diverse vocal synthesis systems: various UTAU voicebanks (most notably Kasane Teto and Utane Uta / Defoko), Macne Nana, VOCALOID (Hatsune Miku), Nagoya Institute of Technology’s experimental HMM engine **Sinsy**, AquesTone, and the cult-classic VST **Delay Lama** (affectionately dubbed "The Monk" in Japan). Beyond vocal tracks, he produces original sequenced instrumental music, covers of the pioneering techno-pop band **P-MODEL**, and tracks belonging to the niche Niconico genre known as *"Sequenced in the Style of Susumu Hirasawa."*

### Artistic Vibe: "STEM-Nerd Bait" and 80s New Wave
His compositions feature instantly memorable, lingering melodies that stick in the listener's head. Spanning quiet, introspective ballads to buoyant, upbeat tracks, his catalog is extraordinarily addictive—often catching listeners in a loop where they cannot stop listening. Several of his pieces are explicitly designed as seamless, infinite loops, demanding caution from repeat listeners.

Atop these melodies, he layers lyrics packed with rich emotion and philosophical themes, cleverly disguised in the terminology of mathematics, theoretical physics, computer science, and digital technology. Consequently, his videos frequently carry community tags such as **"STEM Bait"** (*Rikei Hoi-hoi*) and **"Philosophical Music"** (*Tetsugaku* / 哲楽). 

Far from feeling cold or clinical, however, these rows of technical jargon radiate warmth, wistfulness, and gentle melancholy. Sung through the raw, distinctly digital timbre of UTAU, his songs routinely tug at listeners' heartstrings. Many of his narratives also center around the motif of family. As his cover choices and instrumental tracks reveal, his musical roots lie partly in 1980s Japanese New Wave and early chiptune/FM video game soundtracks, though his work overflows with an idiosyncratic charm that transcends any single genre.

### Contributions to UTAU and Vocal Synthesis: A Foundational Pioneer
Mimi Robo P has directly supported the growth of the vocal synthesis community through software development and open-source sharing:
- He developed and publicly released essential software utilities that became universal standards for creating voicebanks and sequencing tracks (detailed below).
- He actively distributes MIDI files, project files, and instrumental (off-vocal) MP3s for his original songs, dramatically lowering the barrier for community covers and academic research.
- **Historic Contribution to UTAU:** In an interview conducted by ASCII.jp with UTAU’s developer, Ameya/Ayame, it was revealed that **Mimi Robo P was the person who originally proposed the concept of "VCV" (連続音 - *Renzokuon*) to Ameya**—the pivotal engineering leap that fundamentally transformed UTAU from a choppy syllable-splicer into a remarkably fluid, expressive synthesizer.
- Furthermore, as of March 2011, he was the sole producer on Nico Nico Douga actively publishing original songs using **Sinsy**, playing a central role in expanding public awareness and appreciation of the experimental academic engine.

### Chronological History: The Trailblazer
- **March 4, 2008:** Debuted on Nico Nico Douga with his original sequenced instrumental track ***TL***. Through April, he primarily posted instrumental music and covers of P-MODEL songs.
- **May 10, 2008:** Released ***Kenka Wakare (Short Ver.)*** (*けんか別れ*, "Parting Ways After a Quarrel") using UTAU’s default voice, Defoko. **This was the very first original song in UTAU history.** He released the full-length version three days later.
- **June 2008:** Following several sequenced covers of Moonriders and retro game-styled instrumentals, he released ***Mimi no Aru Robotto no Uta*** (*耳のあるロボットの唄*, "The Song of the Robot with Ears") starring Kasane Teto. Interpreted by many as a poignant self-referential meditation on the nature of artificial singing synthesis, the song achieved numerous historic milestones:
  - **First UTAU song to enter the VOCALOID Hall of Fame** (crossing 100,000 views).
  - **First UTAU song added to commercial karaoke** (JOYSOUND).
  - **First UTAU song inducted into the Nico Nico Advertising Hall of Fame.**
  - The song was later covered by professional voice actress **Haruna Ikezawa** (the voice provider of Macne Nana). It also won 1st place in listener voting on the pilot broadcast of NHK Radio 1’s vocal synthesis music program *Ele-uta!*, serving as the grand finale track of the broadcast.
- **August–October 2008:** Uploaded foundational video tutorials explaining how to install UTAU and Kasane Teto, followed by the release of custom software tools to streamline voicebank creation.
- **February 2010:** Stunned the community by releasing an original track featuring Hatsune Miku, titled ***Takarabune wa Denshi no Umi e*** (*宝舟は電子の海へ*, "The Treasure Ship Sails into the Digital Sea").
- **April 2010:** Began integrating Sinsy into his vocal lineup.
- In subsequent years, he expanded his activities into commercial music: contributing official arrangements to New Wave tribute albums produced by Kenzo Saeki, and providing original compositions to diverse vocalists.

### Origin of His Producer Name
Derived from his signature masterpiece, ***Mimi no Aru Robotto no Uta*** (*Song of the Robot with Ears*).  
His registered Niconico username is **nwp8861** (he noted in the description of his debut track *TL* that "nwp8861" was his actual automobile license plate number).

  *Vocal Engines Used:* Hatsune Miku, Sinsy.

### KarenT Digital Distribution
- On October 10, 2010, Crypton Future Media’s music label **KarenT** released the official Kasane Teto compilation album ***Graduation from Lie***, featuring Mimi Robo P’s song ***return*** (available on iTunes, Amazon MP3, etc.).

### Bandcamp Releases
- ***The Soundtrack of Φ***: A conceptual soundtrack for an imaginary 1980s retro-fantasy PC RPG titled "Φ". Produced entirely under vintage PC-8801/9801 hardware constraints (3 FM synthesis channels + 3 PSG channels = strict 6-voice polyphony; 30 tracks).
- ***e-side dishes***: A collection of off-vocal instrumental backing tracks for his 2010–2012 original vocal works (10 tracks).
- ***Zakkyoku Rakkyo (雑曲楽居)***: A curated selection of instrumental electronic pieces produced between 2008 and 2011, inspired by P-MODEL and European techno festivals like WIRE (12 tracks).

### Software Utilities Developed by Mimi Robo P

| Tool Name | Description & Impact |
| :--- | :--- |
| **OREMO** | **The global standard recording tool for UTAU.** Designed specifically to streamline recording voicebanks by displaying automated syllable prompts, pitch guides, and instant audio slicing. |
| **wavDivider** | Utility designed to automatically split long continuous recording audio files into individual phoneme WAV files. |
| **pCov** | Auditory preview utility for checking recorded samples; includes Sinsy support. |
| **scat** | UTAU sequencing plugin that automatically populates notes with nonsensical scat singing phonemes based on character constraints. |
| **setParam** | **The foundational parameter editor for UTAU.** A visual waveform editor used to configure `oto.ini` files (setting offset, consonant velocity, cutoff, pre-utterance, and overlap values with extreme precision). |
| **wavTar** | Utility to batch-archive and un-archive multiple WAV audio files. |
| **utauke** | Extension script providing enhanced keyboard shortcuts and workflow optimizations inside UTAU. |
| **utau2sinsy** | Conversion tool that translates UTAU score files (`.ust`) into MusicXML for Sinsy. |
| **prosody2utau** | Analytical tool that extracts pitch and prosody contours from human singing or Sinsy and applies them directly onto UTAU UST notes. |
| **wavSpitz** | Allows users to record voice samples directly into external digital audio workstations while maintaining UTAU-compliant file nomenclature. |

*Additional utilities published on his website include **nicoStat** (a dashboard tracking video views, comments, and MyLists), **Techno na Kagami** (a mirror visual-effect plugin for the video editor AviUtl), and **retine** (a tool that synchronizes historical Twitter timelines with radio broadcast MP3 archives).*

## Cultural Context & Slang Guide: Mimi Robo P

### 1. Who is Mimi Robo P in Vocal Synthesis History?
If you are familiar with Vocaloid history, you know figures like ryo (supercell), wowaka, or kz (livetune) who defined the early sonic identity of Hatsune Miku. 

**Mimi Robo P (nwp8861) holds that exact foundational status for UTAU.**
- **First Original Song:** On May 10, 2008, he uploaded *Kenka Wakare*, the very first original song ever composed specifically for UTAU (starring Defoko).
- **The Anthem of UTAU:** A month later, he published *Mimi no Aru Robotto no Uta* (*The Song of the Robot with Ears*), which became the emotional anthem of the entire UTAU community and earned UTAU its first 100,000-view Hall of Fame induction and its first commercial karaoke slot.
- **The Father of VCV (連続音 - *Renzokuon*):** Early UTAU sounded choppy because it used standalone CV (Consonant-Vowel, e.g., "ka", "te", "to") samples. Mimi Robo P realized that recording continuous flowing strings (e.g., "- a k a -") would capture the natural acoustic transitions between vowels and consonants. He pitched this concept to UTAU’s developer, Ameya/Ayame, who implemented it into the engine. This single innovation made UTAU famous for sounding remarkably realistic.

### 2. OREMO and setParam: The Software That Built UTAU
Almost every single UTAU voicebank created between 2009 and today—including the modern voicebanks of Kasane Teto, Defoko, Ruko, and thousands of indie fanloids worldwide—was recorded and configured using software that **Mimi Robo P programmed himself**:
- **OREMO:** A lightweight audio recording software that shows a singer a list of syllables, plays a metronome and guide tone, and automatically names and saves each WAV file the instant the singer finishes.
- **setParam:** An essential visual waveform tool that allows voicebank creators to drag colored boundary lines across phonemes to set the `oto.ini` configuration (defining where a consonant ends and where vowel stretching begins).

### 3. "STEM-Nerd Bait" (理系ホイホイ - Rikei Hoi-hoi)
*Gokiburi Hoi-hoi* ("Roach Trap") is a household brand of sticky insect traps in Japan. On Niconico, attaching the suffix **-hoi-hoi** creates a slang term meaning "irresistible bait/trap for a specific demographic."
- **Rikei Hoi-hoi** (*理系ホイホイ*): Content that acts as catnip for science, technology, engineering, and mathematics (STEM) nerds. Mimi Robo P earned this tag because his lyrics routinely weave complex mathematical and physics concepts (differential equations, phase space, digital sampling theory, wave harmonics) into tender emotional narratives.

### 4. "Delay Lama" and "The Monk" (僧侶)
In the overview, the article mentions **Delay Lama**. 
Released in 2002 by Audionerdz, Delay Lama is an infamous, cult-classic freeware VST synthesizer that features an animated, cel-shaded 3D Tibetan Buddhist monk who sways, blinks, and manipulates his hands as he sings monophonic vowel formants through a stereo delay filter. In Japanese internet culture, Delay Lama is affectionately referred to simply as **"The Monk"** (*Sōryo* / 僧侶) and is treated like an honorary vocal synthesizer alongside Vocaloids.

### 5. Susumu Hirasawa (平沢進) & 1980s New Wave
**Susumu Hirasawa** is one of the most revered figures in Japanese electronic rock. As the frontman of the 1970s–80s techno-pop band **P-MODEL** (peers of Yellow Magic Orchestra) and the solo composer behind the soundtracks for Satoshi Kon's anime (*Millennium Actress*, *Paprika*, *Paranoia Agent*) and *Berserk*, Hirasawa pioneered a sweeping, symphonic, philosophical techno-pop style. 

On Niconico, a dedicated community tag exists: **"Sequenced in the Style of Susumu Hirasawa"** (*平沢進で打ち込んでみた*). Mimi Robo P was a prominent master of this style, crafting intricate arpeggios, galloping rhythms, and grand sci-fi motifs that directly reflect Hirasawa’s musical lineage.
