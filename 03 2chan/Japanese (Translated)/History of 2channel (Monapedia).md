# History of 2channel

The **history of 2channel** is a chronological list of events related to the [anonymous bulletin board](https://wiki3.jp/mona/name/%E5%8C%BF%E5%90%8D%E6%8E%B2%E7%A4%BA%E6%9D%BF) 2channel, or that started there.

- Ayashii World, a board that influenced 2channel, opened on 21 August 1996, and [Amezou](https://wiki3.jp/mona/name/%E3%81%82%E3%82%81%E3%81%9E%E3%81%86) opened on 5 August 1997.[^tn-predecessors]

[1999](#1999) - [2000](#2000) - [2001](#2001) - [2002](#2002) - [2003](#2003) - [2004](#2004) - [2005](#2005) - [2006](#2006) - [2007](#2007) - [2008](#2008)

## 1999

- 30 May – [Hiroyuki Nishimura](https://wiki3.jp/mona/name/%E8%A5%BF%E6%9D%91%E5%8D%9A%E4%B9%8B) founds "2channel" as a refuge from Amezou, a bulletin-board site that had begun to decline because of trolling. It starts out as an anonymous board.
- 7 July – The DoCoMo Saitama board incident. In protest at posts criticizing a commercial's celebrity being deleted at once from the board of DoCoMo's Saitama branch, 2channel residents flood it. As a result, the Saitama branch's board is closed.
- 23 August – 2channel is introduced for the first time in *Weekly ASCII* and on TV, among other media.
- 17 September – The [cap](https://wiki3.jp/mona/name/%E3%82%AD%E3%83%A3%E3%83%83%E3%83%97_\(%E9%9B%BB%E5%AD%90%E6%8E%B2%E7%A4%BA%E6%9D%BF\)) system is introduced to prevent impersonation.[^tn-cap]
- 23 September – Cap passwords leak. The leaked password "[fusianasan](https://wiki3.jp/mona/name/Fusianasan)" is said to date from this incident.[^tn-fusianasan] "fusianasan" was the password of "Dengeki Shōjo", a named regular on the Lobby board. Another password, "mokorikomo", leaked at the same time; it was Hiroyuki's. "mokorikomo" disappeared around 2002 as 2ch's software was revised.
- December – The "aburanuki" system, which lets you post without bumping the thread, is introduced. The name is unpopular, and the next day it is changed to "sage".[^tn-sage]
- December onward – The "Leaf plagiarism allegation" dispute breaks out. For details, see [Leaf, Key boards](https://wiki3.jp/mona/name/Leaf,key%E6%8E%B2%E7%A4%BA%E6%9D%BF).[^tn-leaf]
- End of December – Amezou, whose boards had been forced to close one after another by rampant script trolling, finally shuts down completely, and some of its remaining residents migrate. With this, 2channel grows to a new scale.

## 2000

- 1 April – An April Fools' joke announcement, "2channel acquired by Hikari Tsūshin Inc.", is posted on 2channel's top page.[^tn-hikari]
- April – The *Gunnm* handle incident. Manga artist Yukito Kishiro unilaterally demands that the administrator of an entirely unrelated website, who happened to use the same handle as the title of Kishiro's manga *Gunnm*, change it and stop using it. Hiroyuki takes an interest, and for a while the Lobby board's title becomes "The cutting-edge turn of the era! Super Gunnm".[^tn-gunnm]
- 3 May – The Nishitetsu bus hijacking (the first case in which a crime by a 2channeler became a topic of discussion).[^tn-nishitetsu]
- 12 May – A 17-year-old second-year high-school boy in Kanagawa Prefecture is arrested in the act for attempted murder. After posting the threat "Tomorrow I'll leave the whole world speechless. I'll show you", he strikes a man with a hammer on the Negishi Line.
- 13 June – A 23-year-old third-year private-university student in Tokyo is arrested on suspicion of forcible obstruction of business for posting a threat: "I'm gonna storm Chiba University."
- August – 2channel is listed on Yahoo! JAPAN.
- September – A columnist takes a joke posted on 2channel at face value, and the weekly horse-racing magazine *Gallop* prints the hoax that Gourmet Frontier, already retired and standing at stud, will return to racing at Kasamatsu. For details, see Takahiro Ōtsuki.[^tn-gallop]
- 26 September – The top page is defaced.
- December – [Team2ch](https://wiki3.jp/mona/name/Team2ch) enters a motorcycle race at Tsukuba Circuit.
- December – "Gekisō Bakusō Team 2channel" ("Full-Throttle, Wild-Running Team 2channel"), sung by administrator Hiroyuki, is released online. It reaches number one in the download rankings of the free music site *My-Melody* (now *Muzie*).

## 2001

- February – Snow sculptures of [ASCII-art](https://wiki3.jp/mona/name/%E3%82%A2%E3%82%B9%E3%82%AD%E3%83%BC%E3%82%A2%E3%83%BC%E3%83%88) characters such as [Giko the cat](https://wiki3.jp/mona/name/%E3%82%AE%E3%82%B3%E7%8C%AB) appear at the Sapporo Snow Festival.
- 29 March – The 1,000-post limit per thread is introduced.[^tn-1000]
- 9 April – The Yoshinoya copypasta is posted on 2ch for the first time.[^tn-yoshinoya]
- May – "[Team2ch](https://wiki3.jp/mona/name/Team2ch)", an analysis team for the UD cancer research project, is set up. From then until the project ended, Team2ch always boasted the world's top analysis record.[^tn-ud]
- 14 May – The Samejima Incident uproar.[^tn-samejima]
- June – Politician Yukio Hatoyama names 2channel as one of his favourite sites.
- June – Kazuhiko Nishi of ASCII starts posting.[^tn-nishi]
- 1 June – The Kalobby board splits off from the Lobby.
- July – Politician Kōichi Katō posts on 2channel.
- 28 July – The event "2channel Festival @ 2nd Anniversary" is held at Shinjuku CODE.
- August – The [tripcode](https://wiki3.jp/mona/name/%E3%83%88%E3%83%AA%E3%83%83%E3%83%97_\(%E9%9B%BB%E5%AD%90%E6%8E%B2%E7%A4%BA%E6%9D%BF\)) system is introduced.[^tn-trip]
- 3–4 August – The 1,000-post limit breaks in the "Giga Wing 2" thread on the Arcade board. From the night of 3 August, notices that the thread had ended kept being posted. Because the broken limit also let people keep posting past 1,000, a *matsuri* broke out in the thread.[^tn-matsuri] The thread kept running wild until the evening of the next day, 4 August, reaching a total of 12,535 posts, the highest post count in 2channel's history since the 1,000-post limit was set.
- 25 August – With traffic soaring, 2channel's boards close one after another and the whole of 2channel faces the threat of closure. For details, see [the August Crisis](https://wiki3.jp/mona/name/8%E6%9C%88%E5%8D%B1%E6%A9%9F). It was the worst crisis since 2ch began.[^tn-august]
- 29 August – On the 29 August 2001 broadcast of the internet radio show "2channel Radio", Hiroyuki admits that professional right-wingers are running influence operations. This shows that 2channel is also subject to organized operations with political aims.
- 31 August – Right after the software improvements, 2channel itself is put up for auction on the internet auction site "Bidders". Bids pass ¥9 *kei* (9 × 10¹⁸ yen), but it turns out to be a prank by administrator Hiroyuki and the matter is settled. The bidders also turn out to have bid as a prank, and apologize.
- 31 August – On a petition for a provisional injunction from Nippon Life Insurance seeking the deletion of posts, the Tokyo District Court orders the posts deleted.
- 11 September – The September 11 attacks in the United States. Despite its bandwidth problems, 2channel responds to the emergency and deploys a large number of servers for exchanging information.
- 27 September – The Ibaraki and Aichi prefectural police arrest six people, including company employees in Ibaraki, for violating the Unauthorized Computer Access Law. They allegedly used other people's IDs and passwords reposted on 2channel to access Yahoo! Auctions illegally, took part in auctions posing as the victims, and proposed fake transactions to other participants so that the victims would receive negative ratings. Twenty-four people were implicated.
- October – 1ch.tv opens as a rival to 2channel, led by Value Exchange Inc., Kazuhiko Nishi (then special adviser to ASCII Corporation), the former administrator of Amezou and others. Most 2channelers visit 1ch.tv and find many vulnerabilities, and right afterwards posting is stopped and the site switches to read-only.[^tn-1ch]
- December – The official guide mook *2channel Daikōryaku Magajiso Vol. 1* goes on sale.[^tn-magajiso]
- December – A thread titled "[Breaking] Mr. Short Pants appears at the revival run of the Limited Express Aizu" is started on the Railway General board. After this, "Mr. Short Pants" threads become a series for a while. A grown man who, even in midwinter, turns up at railway events all over the country in the kind of short trousers children used to wear, "Mr. Short Pants" becomes a celebrity not only on the Railway boards but across 2channel.
- December – The Tashiro Festival breaks out in connection with Masashi Tashiro's arrest.[^tn-tashiro]
- December – A Tokyo user with the handle "Rat Killer" posts on 2channel that he'll "swing a kitchen knife around". On the 24th he is arrested in a hostage incident at the east exit of Shinjuku Station (the Rat Killer incident).

## 2002

- 7 January – The obscene song "Chinko Ondo", born on the [Festival Venue board](https://wiki3.jp/mona/name/%E3%81%8A%E7%A5%AD%E3%82%8A%E4%BC%9A%E5%A0%B4%E6%9D%BF), becomes a huge hit.[^tn-chinko]
	- Both the composer, "Chinko Utabito", and the lyricist, "Chinko Shokunin", declared that they gave up all personal rights, making it a forerunner of later freely downloadable music such as "Muneo House". Many other versions were made, including jazz and classical ones. Several versions were later included as audio tracks on the CD-ROM bundled with *2channel Official Guide 2002*; adult-video actress Shōko Mikami sang it at an AV maker's event; and in 2005 it was used in a commercial for Dwango's "Iromero Toriihōdai" ringtone service.
- February – Hiroyuki is threatened by yakuza and calls the police.
- February – The *Asahi Shimbun* runs an article saying that SAKH.COM, a portal site in Russia's Sakhalin Oblast, is running a poll on "Should the Northern Territories be returned or not?" Large numbers of 2channelers vote, and the view that "the Northern Territories should be returned" comes to dominate. SAKH.COM falsifies the vote counts and blocks access from Japan. On 20 February the *Asahi* reports that "the poll was cancelled because of a flood of access from Japan".[^tn-sakh]
- February – A thread titled 「アフガン航空相撲殺される」 ("Afghan aviation minister beaten to death") is started on the News Flash board, but one 2channeler after another misreads it as "Afghan aviation sumo" plus "killed". It later becomes a *matsuri* as the "[Afghan Aviation Sumo](https://wiki3.jp/mona/name/%E3%82%A2%E3%83%95%E3%82%AC%E3%83%B3%E8%88%AA%E7%A9%BA%E7%9B%B8%E6%92%B2)" meme.[^tn-sumo]
- Late February – "Operation Oyster" goes into full operation.[^tn-oyster]
	- To read threads that have gone into the archive after passing 1,000 posts or a certain period, so-called *dat-ochi* threads, you now have to register for a paid account and use a dedicated browser. However, threads are normally converted to HTML after a few months and can then be read for free (although, as of 2008, HTML conversion of threads has effectively stopped). This paid registration is called 2channel "●" (*maru*, "circle"), because registered users can put the ● character, which ordinary browsers can't use, in their name. Revenue from ● pays for the site's upkeep, and using the dedicated browsers also reduces load on the servers. Besides ●, threads that have fallen into the archive can be read on Mimizun Search and Nikuchannel (closed in 2007), or displayed by spending Moritapo points.
- March – "[Bisuke-tan](https://wiki3.jp/mona/name/%E3%83%93%E3%82%B9%E3%82%B1%E3%81%9F%E3%82%93)" is born in the thread "Kentucky's biscuits are delicious" on the Half-width 2D board.[^tn-bisuke]
- 3 April – 18 July – The first "2channel [All-Board Popularity Tournament](https://wiki3.jp/mona/name/%E5%85%A8%E6%9D%BF%E4%BA%BA%E6%B0%97%E3%83%88%E3%83%BC%E3%83%8A%E3%83%A1%E3%83%B3%E3%83%88)", a popularity vote between boards, is held. The Download Software board wins.
- May – Administrator Hiroyuki resigns as a director of Irregulars and Partners Inc.
- 6 May – The Dirle incident. A 2channeler (handle "Dirlewanger") who posted footage of himself abusing cats is arrested for violating the Animal Protection Law and receives a suspended sentence.
- 27 May – "Abone 01", a doujin sales event exclusively for 2channel, is held.[^tn-abone]
- 2 June – The [Giko](https://wiki3.jp/mona/name/%E3%82%AE%E3%82%B3%E7%8C%AB) trademark problem (the Takara Giko uproar).
	- It comes to light that Takara Co., Ltd. had applied on 12 March 2002 to register the name of 2channel's ASCII-art "Giko cat" as its own trademark. The protest spreads across all of 2ch: the top page becomes a picture of Giko chained by a collar, and the administrator drafts an open letter of inquiry. On 3 June, Takara withdraws the application and the problem ends.
- 26 June – In a suit in which a Tokyo animal hospital sought damages from administrator Hiroyuki Nishimura, saying its reputation had been harmed by posts made on the "Pet Lovers" board from 16 January 2001, the Tokyo District Court finds Nishimura liable and orders the posts deleted and ¥4 million paid. Nishimura appeals to the Tokyo High Court, which dismisses the appeal in December. After the High Court's dismissal, 2channel begins storing the IP addresses of posters in all threads (from January 2003). Nishimura appeals to the Supreme Court but loses.
- 5–7 July – The Shōnan beach clean-up offline meeting. It was held to protest Fuji TV's coverage of the 2002 FIFA World Cup, which could be seen as biased (for example, coverage that seemed hostile to Turkey), and was timed to take place ahead of the same station's own Shōnan beach clean-up event.[^tn-shonan]
- 5 August – *2channel Official Guide 2002* goes on sale.
- 18 August – The Mona trademark problem.
	- It comes to light that an individual had applied on 17 May 2002 to register the ASCII-art character "[Mona](https://wiki3.jp/mona/name/%E3%83%A2%E3%83%8A%E3%83%BC)" as a trademark (registered name: "Omae Mona"). It causes an uproar because the designated goods and services included "Class 38: electronic bulletin board communications". The application was later refused.[^tn-mona]
- September – A Pokémon Flash is put on the top page for a while, causing a *matsuri*. FOX ★ comments that "the aim is to reduce the number of users".[^tn-pokemon]
- 2 September – A person who had started a thread about "unfair dismissal" on the Snitching Inside Stories board and posted slander about the company that fired him is sued by that company for damages. The court grants the claim and orders the thread's author to pay the company ¥1 million.
- October – The bicycle of Steve, an American cycling around the world, is "stolen" in Kyoto (in fact it had not been stolen). The "Let's look for Steve's bicycle OFF in Kyoto" is organized, and the bicycle is safely found.[^tn-off]
- October – Administrator Hiroyuki produces the indie CD *Kuro Ijiwaru Kei*.
- 2 November – On the boards on the sports2 server, the date jumps to 4 March 2000, and large numbers of active threads drop into the archive. The date returns to normal on 6 November.
- 16 November – A one-day-only flash board, the [Hiroyuki! Happy Birthday!! board](https://wiki3.jp/mona/name/%E9%9A%A0%E3%81%97%E6%9D%BF#%E7%84%A1%E3%81%8F%E3%81%AA%E3%81%A3%E3%81%9F%E9%9A%A0%E3%81%97%E6%9D%BF%EF%BC%88%E7%8F%BE%E5%9C%A8%E3%81%AE%E7%8A%B6%E6%B3%81%EF%BC%89), suddenly appears and is filled with congratulatory posts for Hiroyuki.
- 28 November – The website of Saitama Aiseikai Hospital becomes explosively popular.
- December – A 2channeler's letter is printed in the *Asahi Shimbun* under the name "Kunimichi Akai". "Akai Kunimichi" is a pseudonym punning on *akai hōdō* ("red reporting"), satirizing the *Asahi*'s editorial stance.[^tn-akai]
- December – Venture Safenet (VSN) forces out an executive who had committed misconduct, but the employees' mobile site carrying the announcement can easily be viewed by outsiders, and its address is posted on 2channel. The company requests deletion but is refused.

## 2003

- January – Recording and storage of IP addresses for all posts begins on a trial basis. With this, 2channel ceases to be a fully anonymous board. The background is a rise in cases where 2channel was used for threats such as "I'll kill the children of XX Elementary School", for defamation of companies and others, and as a stepping stone for crimes. (It is also said that IP addresses had been recorded before, but complaints of that kind were treated as "jokes" and ignored.)
- 16 January – The campaign "Let's call *bōsōzoku* *chinsōdan*" is covered prominently by the *Sankei Shimbun* and Asahi Broadcasting.[^tn-chinsodan]
- February – Volunteers from the Models board send plastic model kits to Yasushi Chimura, a victim of abduction by North Korea. Later, the organizer receives a phone call and a thank-you letter from Chimura.
- February – A 2channel thread is shown on a Fuji TV programme as "readers' voices".
- February – The official guide mook *2channel Plus Vol. 1* goes on sale.
- March – The Metropolitan Police Department's High-Tech Crime Center arrests "Neo Oolong Tea" (24) on suspicion of forcible obstruction of business. In November 2002 he had posted a warning on 2channel that he would bomb an Odakyū Line train.[^tn-neo]
- 18 March – A temporary board is set up in preparation for the outbreak of the Iraq War.
- 2–28 March – A tour around western Japan on a Choinori scooter is planned and carried out on the Motorcycle board. Thanks to donations and support from 2channelers in western Japan, the rider safely reaches Cape Sata and completes the circuit of western Japan. The rider was "N-chibi Kozaru ◆OpwdfOSARU".[^tn-choinori]
- 21 April – The Fukuoka Prefectural Police use the word *chinsōdan* on a poster for a campaign against *bōsōzoku*.
- 25 April – The uproar over the fan vote for the professional baseball All-Star Game (the Kawasaki Festival).[^tn-kawasaki]
- Around May – 2channel blogs begin.
- May – The Char-only board split incident.[^tn-char]
- 1 May – "2channel Provider" begins operating. The provider's site had opened on 1 April that year, and there's an anecdote that it was first taken as an April Fools' joke: many users didn't think 2channel would really start an ISP business.
- 21 May – The Japan Patent Office refuses the application by Irregulars and Partners Inc. to register "2channel" as a trademark.
- 26 May – A large earthquake hits the Tōhoku region, and the news-related boards go down one after another, so a temporary Earthquake board is set up. Perhaps because large earthquakes kept occurring relatively often after that, it still exists as of 2008 as a place for exchanging information.
- 24 July – Hikitarō (-＿-) ◆HAGEs069S6, a named regular on 2channel's Shut-in board (*Hiki-ita*), dies by suicide with charcoal briquettes together with another man in the bathroom of a business hotel in Kameido, Tokyo. It is reported in the news. He had posted his plan in a thread a few days beforehand. There are still people on the Shut-in board who knew Hikitarō while he was alive, and the story is passed down there.[^tn-hikitaro]
- October – The "2ch Domain" service starts.
- October – *Tamori Club* holds the "Japan Minor Campaign Song Grand Prix", and the grand-prix winner, the "Nippon Break Kōgyō Company Song", becomes a huge craze.[^tn-break]
- November–December – The uproar on the Baseball board over B·B, the mascot of the Hokkaido Nippon-Ham Fighters (nicknamed "Erozly (Kari)").[^tn-bb]
- 6 November – A person with the handle "Gorugorumoa" posts threats to bomb U.S. military bases in Okinawa more than 30 times, is arrested for intimidation and taken to Naha police station. He is released two weeks later.
- 11 November – Tripcode display is unified across all servers.
- December – *2channel Official Guide 2004* goes on sale.

## 2004

- Around 5 January – Trackback is implemented on all 2channel servers.
- 10 January – At the "Gate-Opening Ritual: Choosing the Lucky Man" at Nishinomiya Shrine in Nishinomiya, Hyōgo, a man who had not been chosen the year before is chosen as the Lucky Man (*fukuotoko*). However, it comes out that his workplace colleagues had obstructed the other participants. Angry 2channelers protest by phone and email to the workplace where the Lucky Man works. Afterwards, he apologizes to the media, saying he "wants to return the title of Lucky Man to Nishinomiya Shrine".[^tn-fukuotoko]
- 10 January – Netizens from the Korean board NAVER and elsewhere, angered by the Korea-mocking site "The Ways of the Land of K" and by the Japanese government's plan to issue stamps of Takeshima, whose sovereignty Japan and Korea both claim, launch concentrated access through repeated reloading (an "F5 attack") to knock the servers out. 2channel is attacked more than 300 million times a day. The administrator says, "I think it's fine to just ignore the trolls. Instead of retaliating with something stupid, how about we show them how civilized we are?", asking 2channel users to hold back from counterattacking. It ends on 17 January. He then leaves the message on the top page: "Someday I hope we can communicate in a good way that isn't about attacking each other. Next round in UD." It is covered by the Japanese and Korean media. A rumour later spreads that kensuu ★ went to Korea to apologize on the administrator's behalf.[^tn-korea]
- February – In reply to the post "Is it true 2channel's closing down?", administrator [Hiroyuki](https://wiki3.jp/mona/name/%E8%A5%BF%E6%9D%91%E5%8D%9A%E4%B9%8B) posts "It's true". A flood of posts doubting the closure follows, but there is no official announcement and the details remain unclear. (The administrator plays jokes like this from time to time ("if you can't tell a lie is a lie..." and so on), and there was also word of "roughly two months later"; since two months from that day was 1 April, many regulars brushed it off as an annual ritual.) Around this time, the management side began running a personal blog separately from 2channel.[^tn-uso]
- 26 March – 2channel loses a defamation suit brought by a man living in Tokyo and is ordered to pay ¥500,000 in damages and delete the posts in question.
- 26 March – Shigotonin ★, intending to delete data on pc.2ch.net, mistakenly deletes the data on pc2.2ch.net, which hosted active boards. The logs are restored using backups from Mimizun Search and Nikuchannel, and on 29 March the boards on pc2.2ch.net move to pc5.2ch.net.
- April–May – Threats such as "I will kill pupils at an elementary school in Nagoya" and "I will blow up a Shinkansen or a passenger plane" are posted one after another on the website board of a Tokyo right-wing group that had been campaigning against 2channel.
- 7 April – The Japanese hostage crisis in Iraq. Immediately afterwards, the theory that the hostages staged it themselves spreads, mainly from 2channel.
- 9 April – The Tokyo District Court issues a provisional order to disclose poster information (IP addresses) for some posts, on a petition by journalist Yū Terasawa and others.
- 18 April – The 2channel Ninth Project offline concert is held.
	- An offline meeting to perform Beethoven's Symphony No. 9 in D minor, Op. 125, "Choral", with everyone from the conductor to the orchestra, chorus and soloists being 2channelers. All the performers gathered on 2channel. On the day, more than 600 people come to listen, and the performance is a success.
- Late April – The Iraq hostage offline meeting. Some 2channelers go to the airport where the three freed hostages arrive and criticize them, holding placards reading "personal responsibility" (*jiko sekinin*), "[nurupo](https://wiki3.jp/mona/name/%E3%81%AC%E3%82%8B%E3%81%BD)" and so on.[^tn-iraq]
- Around May – Posting on the [News Flash board](https://wiki3.jp/mona/name/%E3%83%8B%E3%83%A5%E3%83%BC%E3%82%B9%E9%80%9F%E5%A0%B1%E6%9D%BF) is stopped for about a month. The cause is never made clear, and speculation spreads: that a magazine had run an article on the end of 2channel just before, or that the management couldn't keep it under control any more. As a result, the population moves to the [News Flash (VIP) board](https://wiki3.jp/mona/name/%E3%83%8B%E3%83%A5%E3%83%BC%E3%82%B9%E9%80%9F%E5%A0%B1\(VIP\)%E6%9D%BF).[^tn-vip]
- 6 May – News of the death of singer-songwriter Ritsuko Okazaki is posted. King Records' press release came out on 11 May.
- 7 May – Having ignored the provisional order of 9 April, 2channel finally receives a formal order from the Tokyo District Court to disclose IP addresses (on 8 August it complies, creating a thread on the Restriction Information board and disclosing them).
- 10 May – The developer of Winny ("47-shi") is arrested by the Kyoto Prefectural Police on suspicion of aiding copyright infringement.[^tn-winny]
- 11 May – The Tokyo District Court orders disclosure of IP addresses, granting a claim by a software developer in Shinjuku that it had been slandered in posts.
- 18 May – A man is arrested on suspicion of damaging business credit for posting false information on the Snitching Inside Stories board under the title "Inside stories of the drugstore chain run by 17".
- 25 May – The "29th problem" crisis. When the servers moved to a new company, the DNS could not be transferred, and there was a risk that "2ch.net" would become unusable on the 29th. Thanks to the efforts of the operators it was averted on the 28th. It was 2ch's second crisis.
- 28–29 May – The whole system is moved. During the migration there is a series of problems: posting becomes impossible, processing lags and causes duplicate posts, and servers go down so that nobody can post.
- 1 June – In connection with the *Kyoto Shimbun*'s coverage of unauthorized use of images of cultural properties, 2channel's top page, which used the *Chōjū-giga*, displays "Now: super copyright infringement in progress".[^tn-chojugiga]
- 1 June – Flat-rate packet pricing for FOMA begins. Coinciding with the Sasebo killing of a sixth-grade girl by her classmate, access to 2ch viewer sites for mobile phones increases explosively, and four more servers for mobile phones are added.
- 2 June – The Sasebo killing. At an elementary school in Sasebo, Nagasaki Prefecture, a pupil kills a classmate, and many posts appear that could identify the suspected child. The management ignores deletion requests from the Legal Affairs Bureau, saying "deleting only the links is pointless". Because the child was said to have been wearing a hooded sweatshirt with University of Nevada merchandise, she was dubbed "Nevada", and the name spread within 2channel for a while. In addition, the address, login ID and password of the website the suspected child had run leaked, and some people altered her site without permission, violating the Unauthorized Computer Access Law.[^tn-nevada]
- 3 June – Saibi High School (Matsuyama, Ehime), which had won the Spring Kōshien tournament on its first appearance, demands disclosure of IP addresses and announces that it will also take other legal action. It says posts slandered the school and team members over the reasons for the baseball club manager's resignation.
- 4 June – The *Mainichi Shimbun* runs an article on a prank post claiming that typing [fusianasan](https://wiki3.jp/mona/name/Fusianasan) would let you view the website linked to the Sasebo killing, condemning it as the behaviour of "malicious regulars". From there, it comes out that Gainax staff and director Takami Akai had written things in their mixi diaries such as "Creepy otaku, die" and "2ch is an anus". Criticism floods in against Gainax, and on 27 April Takami Akai announces his resignation as director.[^tn-gainax]
- 15 May – In the Aizuwakamatsu matricide, in which a 17-year-old boy living in Aizuwakamatsu killed his mother and turned himself in to the police early that morning carrying her head, it emerges that at around 21:00 on 14 May, the day before the crime, a thread saying someone had killed their mother had been started on the [Live Commentary board](https://wiki3.jp/mona/name/%E5%AE%9F%E6%B3%81%E6%9D%BF), and the media report this post. On 21 May, administrator [Hiroyuki Nishimura](https://wiki3.jp/mona/name/%E8%A5%BF%E6%9D%91%E5%8D%9A%E4%B9%8B) publishes the IP address of the person who started the thread as a "crime announcement", but the poster turns out to be a user of the Plala line in Aichi Prefecture.
- 16 May – In connection with a sexual harassment suit by a female Self-Defense Force member at the Air Self-Defense Force's Tōbetsu sub-base (45th Warning Squadron, Northern Aircraft Control and Warning Wing), the superior being sued leaves printouts of 2channel posts about her lying around where others will see them. When the plaintiff, his subordinate, protests, he justifies it: "Anyone can look at it, so what's wrong?"
- 19 May – In a hidden-camera prank on Sarina Suzuki on *Mecha-Ike*, it is broadcast that Aki Hoshino had posted on 2channel that "Sarina Suzuki's breath smells like a dog". A reply had been posted: "That's not true." The board it was posted on was the Actresses board.
- 9 June – Personal information on more than a thousand people, agricultural-policy information and even documents apparently related to slush funds leak from a Kagoshima prefectural employee's private computer and are posted on 2channel.
- 11 June – A traffic victim who in December 2006 had been the target of vicious tailgating and a hit-and-run reported the damage to the Metropolitan Police Department's Takenotsuka police station but was ignored. So he published on his blog the footage of the whole incident recorded by the dashcam in his car at the time. 2channelers who saw it started a thread on 2channel, and for a while it became a *matsuri*. Protests flooded the car-related company where the perpetrator worked, to the point that it issued an apology, and on 14 June the perpetrator was dismissed. Then on 26 June the *Asahi Shimbun* and other news organizations reported it, and TV stations all followed. Each interviewed the perpetrator, drawing further attention; taken together, his answers about the accident to the various media contradicted one another. Tarō Kimura also criticized the Takenotsuka police station's handling on air. On 2 August the perpetrator is referred to prosecutors on suspicion of hit-and-run.
- 23 June – Media reports reveal that exercise content hinting in advance at questions actually set in the bar exam by a professor at Keiō University Law School, a member of the examination committee who sets and marks the bar exam, together with text apparently from an email he sent to all the law-school students, had been posted on 2channel. The university told J-CAST News that "two weeks ago (around 10 June 2007) we confirmed reports including information on 2channel and were in the middle of investigating, including checking with the professor".
- July – Ai Iijima, who retired from show business in March, has footage of her Niwango interview with [Hiroyuki](https://wiki3.jp/mona/name/%E8%A5%BF%E6%9D%91%E5%8D%9A%E4%B9%8B) published on Nico Nico Douga.
- 6 July – NHK's *Tokuhō Shutoken* covers 2channel and the *matsuri* that 2channelers set off, under the title "Internet 'festivals' run out of control".
- 17 July – In front of Ōmiya Station in Saitama, a person appears collecting donations to close down 2channel. He wears a sign on his body reading, "With ¥500,000 more we can close 2channel. Please help!" Some people reportedly donated game-centre tokens and Umaibō snacks to him. He never explains at all how the money would be spent to close 2channel, and it is thought to be just a threat against 2ch.
- 1 August – Major condom maker Fuji Latex announces that on 1 September it will release the "2channel Condom", a collaboration co-organized with 2channel administrator Hiroyuki Nishimura. Part of the sales will go towards 2channel's running costs. For some reason this is debated more fiercely on mixi than within 2channel.
- 7 August – Two men, including a 37-year-old on trial for fraud, are arrested by the Hiroshima Prefectural Police for violating the Anti-Drug Special Provisions Law, for dealing cannabis via 2channel between September 2006 and January 2007 and receiving ¥1.15 million from an unemployed woman in Hokkaido.
- 12 August – A message firework celebrating the "Noise Lady"'s release from prison is booked for the Nanki-Shirahama fireworks festival and cancelled at the last minute. For details, see Message fireworks.[^tn-soon]
- 18 August – A Tokyo Metro employee is found to have obtained personal information from a Suica card and posted the female owner's name and phone number on his blog, causing an uproar. The next day it is reported by major media and becomes an issue.
- 19 August – A Saitama University student reveals on mixi that he fare-dodged on the Rinkai Line to go to Comic Market and abused the station staff when caught. It comes to light and causes an uproar; his past misdeeds, such as having had sex with a junior-high-school girl and getting her pregnant, are also exposed, and he is reported to Saitama University, the company that had offered him a job, the police and others. Perhaps because it was the summer holidays, as many as 40 threads go up in flames on the [News Flash board](https://wiki3.jp/mona/name/%E3%83%8B%E3%83%A5%E3%83%BC%E3%82%B9%E9%80%9F%E5%A0%B1%E6%9D%BF) in just three days.
- 21 August – On Nico Nico Douga, a "*Utatte mita*" ("I tried singing it") video had many glowing comments, but because the posters' IDs could be displayed, it came out that most of the glowing comments were self-praise posted by the uploader. Many other self-praising videos were also dug up, drawing criticism mainly on the News Flash board. More than 30 related threads on the News Flash board were stopped. On Nico Nico Douga, some users regarded sock-puppeting as evil and flooded videos found to have used it in the past with comments such as "*jien otsu*", but users who thought a sock-puppeted video was fine as long as it was fun to watch saw this as trolling. (Nico Nico Douga) responded by making poster IDs invisible. Since then, however, some people have trolled videos they dislike that rank highly, deciding on their own that "it must be sock-puppeting".[^tn-jien]
- 24 August – A Tōkai University student confesses on mixi that he stole ¥10,000 entrusted to him by a customer at Bamiyan, where he worked part-time. Because he had posted personal information, it spreads to 2channel and becomes an uproar of reports to Tōkai University, Bamiyan and the police.
- 29 August – A 27-year-old moneylender and an unemployed 26-year-old man from Beppu, Ōita, are arrested by the Ōmuta police of the Fukuoka Prefectural Police on suspicion of violating the Investment Act, for advertising on 2channel and making loans above the legal interest rate (loan-sharking).
- 25 September – In an *Asahi Shimbun* column, Sōichirō Ishihara writes that the word "*Abe-suru*" is "in fashion". Because no such word is in fashion, criticism is concentrated on his blog and elsewhere, and as a result of this column, the word "*Asahiru*", which had been used on 2channel for some time, becomes fashionable.[^tn-asahiru]
- 28 September – An unemployed 25-year-old man who posted a threat on 2channel to bomb Chōfu Station on the Keiō Line turns himself in to the police five hours after posting and is arrested on suspicion of forcible obstruction of business.
- 28 September – "VIP's White Dragon", a fixed handle on News Flash VIP, is arrested for a death threat.
- October – A fixed handle on the Atopy board is ordered to issue a written apology and pay ¥300,000 for posting slander on the Atopy board.
- October – On the Fuji TV information programme *Hakken!!*, broadcast that day, former LDP secretary-general Tarō Asō says, "I sometimes post on 2channel." He also says, "It gets to the point better than the newspapers."
- October – The hobby9 server goes down. Many boards, including the Home Appliances, Railway General and Cars boards, become unusable. Because FOX, the server administrator, is absent, recovery is delayed until the next day or later. It comes back at about 1:30 a.m. on the 8th.
- October – Just after 22:00, all of 2channel's servers, including the top page, become unusable, coming back at around 23:30.
- October – A website asking for donations for surgery for a child called "Osamu-chan", said to have developed dilated cardiomyopathy, an incurable disease of unknown cause, is posted in transplant-donation threads on the [Married Women board](https://wiki3.jp/mona/name/%E6%97%A2%E5%A9%9A%E5%A5%B3%E6%80%A7%E6%9D%BF) and elsewhere. 2channelers examine the site's photos and find they were taken from someone else, and when they check the address and hospital listed, the child turns out not to exist. It becomes known to the mass media too, and people are warned.
- October – A 46-year-old systems engineer living in Kanagawa, who met a 21-year-old woman through 2channel posts, is arrested by the Ōme police on suspicion of abandonment resulting in death by a person responsible for protection: on 16 September he injected her with methamphetamine, and when she lost consciousness he panicked, left her in a car park and fled, and she died the next day.
- October – Lawyer Tōru Hashimoto files a criminal complaint over posts threatening to kill him and his family. On the 7th a thread titled "I'll slaughter lawyer Hashimoto's whole family" had been started.
- October – With the replacement of the operating servers (qb5/qb6), an accident occurs in which posting is impossible except on boards not subject to restrictions. (Holders of ● can get around it.)
- 11 November – "Mirai Kensaku Brazil" calls for images for 2channel's top page. From December, the candidate images are shown on the top page.
- 16 November – A death threat against actress Aya Ueto is posted on the Entertainment board.[^tn-refs] Afterwards a reply is posted: "Rather than Aya Ueto, kill a certain third-year girl at XX Municipal Junior High School in Toda, Saitama." The girl named contacts the police about it.
- 29 November – News comes out of Britain that an MP has argued the Welsh flag should be incorporated into the Union Flag. Residents of the News Flash board immediately come up with new Union Flag designs incorporating the Welsh flag, and some of them are submitted to, and published on, the page of the major British newspaper the *Daily Telegraph* that was inviting new designs. Because the published designs used Japanese anime characters, word spread among the board's residents and new designs were sent one after another from the 2channel side, until the surprised *Daily Telegraph* ran a news story about it. Later, the *Daily Telegraph* selected 20 of the new flag designs sent from around the world (four of them from 2channel) and put them to a reader vote, and one of the 2channel designs came second.
- December – A 49-year-old civil servant in Chiba Prefecture reveals on his blog that when he was single he burned kittens to death in an incinerator and threw the mother cat into a river. 2channel users identify his personal information and so on.
- December – In the case of the threatening posts that forced the cancellation of Hiromi Ikeuchi's lecture in February, the Tokyo District Court sentences the defendant to one year in prison, suspended for four years. It is the first time a court has ruled on posts on 2channel.[^tn-first]
- December – 2channel had tended to forbid reposting to blogs aimed at affiliate income, but Hiroyuki allows it for the boards in the News category only. In addition, the News Flash (Kenmo) board appears as a board forbidding reposting to affiliate blogs: the first split from the [News Flash board](https://wiki3.jp/mona/name/%E3%83%8B%E3%83%A5%E3%83%BC%E3%82%B9%E9%80%9F%E5%A0%B1%E6%9D%BF) in three years.[^tn-kenmo]
- December – Comedian Akihiro Nishino of King Kong posts a text on his blog criticizing 2channelers: "Conversely, I don't accept people who throw out opinions and walk away. People who don't take responsibility for a rebuttal. People who don't provide a way to receive one. In the internet world, the prime example is the people on the so-called '2channel' site who confirm their own existence by putting others down. It's really uncool, and to put it plainly, I think they're idiots. Don't become like that." The text is reposted on 2channel and becomes a *matsuri*.

## 2008

- February – Kumi Kōda says on a radio programme that "after 35, the amniotic fluid goes rotten when you get pregnant". The media write that criticism has arisen "mainly on 2channel", and Kōda apologizes and suspends her entertainment activities.
- February – A hotel writes on its blog in late January that "a TV station is scattering food for monkeys at Shiga Kōgen". This content is broadcast on 11 February in a TV Asahi news programme about monkey damage at Shiga Kōgen, and much criticism of TV Asahi arises on 2channel, but on 13 February TV Asahi denies the content of the post, and the hotel deletes the blog post and apologizes.
- February – In a Vegalta Sendai thread on the Domestic Football board, a post claims that "Vegalta players got drunk at the training camp and damaged a member of the public's car". At first it is thought to be a malicious prank, but when supporters who saw the post ask Vegalta, it turns out to be true, and the next day Vegalta's management holds a press conference to apologize.
- February – The Chiba Prefectural Police's Living Economy Division refers to prosecutors a 23-year-old man of no fixed address, unemployed, on suspicion of obstruction of business. At about 7:00 on the 1st he allegedly posted from a mobile phone, "I'll kill an elementary-school girl in Chiba at 15:00 on 15 February." He states, "It was fun seeing other people react to my posts." The day before his referral, elementary schools in Chiba Prefecture were on such alert that pupils went home in groups. He had also posted seven other messages, including bomb threats against TV stations, between 27 January and 4 February.
- February – In the suit in which Kirikomi Taichō sought ¥2 million in damages from Hiroyuki over defamatory posts made in July 2006, and in which Hiroyuki himself appeared at a hearing, the Tokyo District Court finds negligence in deletion and management and orders Hiroyuki to pay ¥800,000 in damages and delete the archived logs. Neither Kirikomi Taichō nor Hiroyuki appeared on the day of the ruling.[^tn-kirikomi]
- 1 March – A thread titled "I'll try killing an elementary-school kid in Fukuoka Prefecture at 15:00 on Monday 3 March" is started by an elementary-school pupil living in the prefecture. The only reply: "The killing part is for real, OK. It's suicide, though. Reporting it to the police won't do any good anyway." Elementary schools across Fukuoka Prefecture have pupils go home in groups. The Fukuoka Prefectural Police issue a statement urging restraint: "Even if meant as a prank, you can be charged with intimidation or forcible obstruction of business." Afterwards, threads threatening to kill elementary-school pupils in Fukuoka and other prefectures proliferate, and group dismissals and similar measures are taken.
- 5 March – By the evening, four posts are found threatening to "kill" unspecified junior-high-school pupils in Wakayama Prefecture. Schools and the board of education go on alert, and the Wakayama Prefectural Police begin identifying the sender.
- 12 March – Dwango begins its third round of recruiting on 2channel. It also announces that not one of the six people hired the previous year has quit.
- 21 March – Internal audit information on borrowers leaks from the private computer of an employee at the Bank of Japan's Matsue branch and is posted on 2channel; it comes to light when someone reports it to the BoJ. It includes company information such as borrowers requiring special attention. The branch requests deletion by that night. (Bank of Japan internal investigation, preliminary report.)
- 1 April – As an April Fools' joke, a link to a fictitious site, "Kuchibian.com Entrance", is put on the top image of the [News Flash (VIP) board](https://wiki3.jp/mona/name/%E3%83%8B%E3%83%A5%E3%83%BC%E3%82%B9%E9%80%9F%E5%A0%B1\(VIP\)%E6%9D%BF).
- 25 April – In the suicide on the 18th of a woman who had moved from being a TV personality to adult-video actress, the motive is said to have been slander in her blog comments and on 2channel.
- 26 April – The *Yomiuri Shimbun* takes up the 2channelers' "Nagano offline meeting, bringing Tibetan flags".[^tn-nagano]
- 28 April – It comes to light that since 21 March, threats under the same handle, of bombings, killing pupils and sexually assaulting schoolgirls, had been posted against several high schools and vocational training facilities in Osaka Prefecture and Sōka Gakkai facilities. A bomb threat had also been made against Zenkōji. After an investigation by the Osaka Prefectural Police's First Investigation Division, a Tokyo metropolitan high-school employee living in Adachi Ward is arrested on suspicion of forcible obstruction of business.
- 9 May – Internal information, including 2005 security daily reports, leaks through Winny from the private PC of an Imperial Guard officer at the Sakashita guard station of the Imperial Guard Headquarters, and is posted on 2channel, causing an uproar.
- 15 June – A 13-year-old junior-high-school boy in Niigata Prefecture posts, "On Monday 30 June at 19:30 I will set fire to Niigata Station and then commit indiscriminate murder around Niigata Station." He is taken into protective custody for the delinquent act of intimidation. Around this time, threat posts apparently imitating the Akihabara stabbing spree (the "Akihabara incident") increase explosively.[^tn-akiba]
- 27 June – In response to the continuing stream of copycat threats of the Akihabara incident, the National Police Agency emails a request to report them to the administrators of each board. It is of course sent to the owner, [Hiroyuki Nishimura](https://wiki3.jp/mona/name/%E8%A5%BF%E6%9D%91%E5%8D%9A%E4%B9%8B), too, who starts a thread publishing the full text and states that he asks 2channelers to call 110 directly.[^tn-refs]
- 28 June – The *Mainichi Shimbun* perverted reporting incident (the column "WaiWai" in *Mainichi Daily News* had for years carried English translations of articles from Japanese sex-industry magazines). On 3 July, 2channelers hold a protest demonstration in front of the *Mainichi Shimbun*'s head office. It is covered on Yomiuri TV's *Miyane-ya*.[^tn-waiwai]
- 6 July – In World Community Grid, a medical distributed-computing project aimed at developing treatments for intractable diseases, [Team2ch](https://wiki3.jp/mona/name/Team2ch), the team of 2channel users, ranks first in the world.
- 28 July – On the DTV board, users succeed in analysing a legitimate PC terrestrial digital tuner and giving it the same capabilities as Friio. In response, shipments of the product are halted, and it sells out at stores everywhere.[^tn-friio][^tn-refs]
- 1 September – At the press conference where Prime Minister Yasuo Fukuda announced his resignation, he answered a probing question from a reporter for the *Shikoku Shimbun* (who is said to have been uneasy about the Prime Minister's remarks on Hiroshima Day). ASCII art based on his remark "I'm different from you" is created on 2channel, and T-shirts of that ASCII art go on sale and become a topic.[^tn-fukuda][^tn-refs]

> [!info] Cultural context and translator's notes
> This page is a copy, on the fan wiki Monapedia (wiki3.jp/mona, last updated 2023-05-26), of the Japanese Wikipedia article "2ちゃんねるの歴史" as it stood around September 2008, when the list stops. Read it as a period document, not neutral history. It was compiled by 2channel users at the height of the site's influence, and its tone reflects that: hoaxes, pranks and harassment campaigns are often told approvingly; "2channelers" are treated as one actor; and several entries on Korea, China, comfort women and the *Asahi*/*Mainichi* newspapers repeat the board's own net-right (*netto uyoku*) framing as fact. Many entries are unsourced, and some numbers are clearly exaggerated (see the notes). Wikipedia's later versions of this article were heavily trimmed for these reasons.
>
> The list is useful as a map of what 2channel itself considered its landmark moments: technical milestones (cap, sage, 1,000-post limit, tripcodes, ●, IP logging), the legal pressure on Hiroyuki from 2002 onward, *matsuri* culture, and the 2007 events that pushed users towards Nico Nico Douga. For other accounts in this vault, see [[History of 2channel]] (Nicopedia), [[2ch History Timeline]], [[2ch History - Source Comparison]], [[2channel (2ch.net)]], [[Hiroyuki Nishimura]], [[Amezou]], [[News Flash Board]], [[News Flash Board VIP]], [[1ch.tv]] and [[A History of Japanese Internet Culture and Incidents, As I Saw It (1969–2018)]].
>
> **Editing choices.** The page's own links to other Monapedia articles are kept. The wiki's sidebar ("recently updated pages", menu, share buttons) is left out. The source's citation markers [1]–[4] point to a reference list that did not survive the copy, so they are kept only as a note. Real names of private individuals and minors do not appear in the source, and none are added. [^tn-predecessors]: Ayashii World (あやしいわーるど) and Amezou (あめぞう) were the anonymous boards 2channel grew out of. See [[Ayashii World]], [[The First Channel - Ayashii and Amezou]] and [[Amezou]].
[^tn-cap]: A キャップ (*kyappu*, "cap", from "capability") was a password that showed a verified name with a ★ after it. It was used by administrators, moderators and staff, hence names like "FOX ★" and "Shigotonin ★" later in this list.
[^tn-fusianasan]: Typing "fusianasan" in the name field made 2channel display the poster's host name instead, revealing their provider or workplace. Tricking newcomers into typing it became a classic prank, the "fusianasan trap" mentioned in 2004 and 2006.
[^tn-sage]: Typing "sage" (下げ, "lower") in the email field posts a reply without bumping the thread to the top; "age" (上げ, "raise") bumps it. "aburanuki" (油抜き, "removing the grease") was the short-lived first name.
[^tn-leaf]: Leaf and Key were visual-novel studios with huge fan followings on 2channel. The entry does not explain the dispute; it refers to a separate Monapedia page.
[^tn-hikari]: Hikari Tsūshin was a mobile-phone retailer whose share price collapsed spectacularly in 2000, making it a byword for dot-com excess. A takeover by it was a plausible-sounding joke.
[^tn-gunnm]: The Lobby title 「時代の最先ターン！超銃夢」 is a pun that doesn't survive translation; it is given as close to literally as possible.
[^tn-nishitetsu]: On 3 May 2000 a 17-year-old hijacked a Nishitetsu highway bus and killed a passenger. He had posted on 2channel under the handle ネオむぎ茶 (*Neo Mugicha*, "Neo Barley Tea"), which made 2channel nationally notorious. The 2003 handle "Neo Oolong Tea" imitates it.
[^tn-gallop]: Takahiro Ōtsuki is a folklorist and columnist; the source points to a separate article on him for details.
[^tn-1000]: Threads close automatically at 1,000 posts (the 1001st post is a system notice), and new numbered threads continue the topic ("Part 2", "20th" and so on). Closed threads go to the archive, *dat-ochi* (dat落ち, "falling into the .dat files").
[^tn-yoshinoya]: The Yoshinoya copypasta is a rant about a family crowding a Yoshinoya beef-bowl restaurant and how a true regular orders. It is one of 2channel's most famous texts and was translated and spread on English-language forums such as Something Awful and 4chan.
[^tn-ud]: The United Devices cancer research project (with Oxford University) was a distributed-computing project that ran on volunteers' idle PCs. Team2ch's ranking was a point of pride; see also the Korea entry of January 2004 ("Next round in UD").
[^tn-samejima]: The 鮫島事件 (*Samejima jiken*, "Samejima Incident") is a nonexistent "forbidden" incident. Posters pretend that everyone knows something too terrible to talk about. It is one of the oldest 2channel in-jokes.
[^tn-nishi]: Kazuhiko Nishi co-founded ASCII, the computer publisher. He later backed the rival site 1ch.tv (October 2001).
[^tn-trip]: A トリップ (*torippu*, tripcode) is a hash generated from a secret password typed after # in the name field. It shows after a ◆ and lets an anonymous poster prove they are the same person without registering, as in "◆OpwdfOSARU" (March 2003).
[^tn-matsuri]: 祭り (*matsuri*, "festival") is 2channel slang for a frenzy: a thread or board explodes with posts, often a pile-on against a target. The word appears throughout this list.
[^tn-august]: In August 2001 bandwidth costs nearly forced 2channel to close. Volunteer programmers rewrote the board software to cut server load. This is why the year and day of the week disappeared from post dates until March 2005.
[^tn-1ch]: See [[1ch.tv]].
[^tn-magajiso]: マガジソ (*magajiso*) is a deliberate misspelling of マガジン (*magajin*, "magazine"), swapping ン for the look-alike ソ in 2channel style.
[^tn-tashiro]: The 田代祭 (*Tashiro matsuri*) of December 2001 was the mass vote-stuffing of *TIME* magazine's online "Person of the Year" poll to put comedian Masashi Tashiro, who had recently been arrested, at the top. The auto-reload tool used for it became known as the 田代砲 (*Tashiro-hō*, "Tashiro Cannon"), which reappears in 2005 against Chinese and Korean sites.
[^tn-chinko]: ちんこ音頭 ("Willy Dance Song") is a deliberately crude folk-dance song. "Muneo House" was a 2002 dance remix of politician Muneo Suzuki's Diet testimony, another 2channel-born free track.
[^tn-sakh]: The Northern Territories are the southern Kuril Islands, held by Russia and claimed by Japan. SAKH.COM is a news portal in Sakhalin.
[^tn-sumo]: 航空相撲殺 can be parsed as 航空相 (aviation minister) + 撲殺 (beaten to death), the intended meaning, or as 航空 (aviation) + 相撲 (sumo) + 殺 (killed). "Afghan Aviation Sumo" became an imaginary sport with its own ASCII art.
[^tn-oyster]: ● (*maru*) accounts were 2channel's main paid service. They let you read archived threads and later also get around posting restrictions, which is why "holders of ●" could post during the October 2007 outage. Mimizun and Nikuchannel were third-party archive sites; Moritapo was a points currency run by a company linked to 2channel.
[^tn-bisuke]: Bisuke-tan is a *moe* girl character personifying KFC's biscuits, drawn by users of the Half-width 2D board, 2channel's board for adult illustrations. "-tan" is a cutesy form of "-chan".
[^tn-abone]: あぼーん (*abōn*) is the text that replaces a deleted post on 2channel, and by extension slang for "deleted" or "dead". Naming a doujin event after it is a 2channel in-joke.
[^tn-shonan]: Japan and South Korea co-hosted the 2002 World Cup, and South Korea played Turkey in the third-place match. Some Japanese viewers felt TV coverage favoured Korea; this "offline meeting" (*ofu-kai*) both protested and competed with Fuji TV's own event.
[^tn-mona]: Mona's catchphrase is オマエモナー (*omae mo nā*, "you too, pal"). Class 38 covers telecommunications services, which would have covered bulletin boards themselves.
[^tn-pokemon]: A "Pokémon Flash" is a strobing Flash animation, an allusion to the 1997 *Pokémon* episode whose flashing images caused seizures in Japanese children. FOX ★ was one of 2channel's server administrators.
[^tn-off]: OFF or オフ会 (*ofu-kai*, "offline meeting") is a real-world meetup organized online. "Sudden OFF", "Large-Scale Offline" and other boards existed to organize them.
[^tn-akai]: 邦道 is normally read *Kunimichi*, but can also be read *hōdō*, which sounds like 報道 (*hōdō*, "news reporting"). 赤い報道 (*akai hōdō*) means "red (left-wing) reporting", a common net-right jab at the *Asahi*.
[^tn-chinsodan]: 暴走族 (*bōsōzoku*, "reckless-driving tribe") was thought to sound cool to the bikers themselves. 2channel proposed 珍走団 (*chinsōdan*, roughly "the goofy-driving troupe") to make it embarrassing, and police and newspapers took it up.
[^tn-neo]: "Neo Oolong Tea" (ネオ烏龍茶) imitates the handle of the 2000 bus hijacker, "Neo Barley Tea"; see the 3 May 2000 entry.
[^tn-choinori]: The Suzuki Choinori was a very cheap 50cc scooter, not built for long trips. Cape Sata is the southern tip of Kyūshū. Here お布施 (*ofuse*, "alms") is the word used for the donations.
[^tn-kawasaki]: In the 2003 All-Star fan vote, 2channel users organized votes for Chūnichi Dragons pitcher Kenjirō Kawasaki, who had hardly pitched for years because of injury, putting him at the top of the ballot.
[^tn-char]: シャア専用板 ("Char's Custom board", after the *Gundam* villain Char Aznable's red mobile suits) was 2channel's *Gundam* board.
[^tn-hikitaro]: (-＿-) is the "shut-in" kaomoji associated with the Hikikomori board, and ◆HAGEs069S6 is his tripcode. The source treats this death as board lore; it is translated without comment.
[^tn-break]: Nippon Break Kōgyō is a real demolition company. Its corporate song, featured on the late-night TV show *Tamori Club*, became a 2channel and Flash-animation hit.
[^tn-bb]: B·B is the Fighters' bird-like mascot. The source does not explain the nickname or the uproar.
[^tn-fukuotoko]: The 福男選び (*fukuotoko erabi*) is an annual race on 10 January at Nishinomiya Shrine; the first runners to reach the main hall are that year's "Lucky Men".
[^tn-korea]: The F5 key reloads a browser page, so an "F5 attack" is a crude DoS by mass reloading. "Next round in UD" invites the Korean side to compete in distributed-computing rankings (see the May 2001 entry) instead. kensuu ★ was a member of the management side.
[^tn-uso]: Hiroyuki's best-known saying is 「嘘を嘘と見抜ける人でないと（掲示板を使うのは）難しい」: "It's hard (to use a bulletin board) unless you're someone who can tell a lie is a lie."
[^tn-iraq]: Three Japanese civilians taken hostage in Iraq in April 2004 were widely blamed in Japan under the slogan 自己責任 (*jiko sekinin*, "personal responsibility"). ぬるぽ (*nurupo*, from Java's NullPointerException) is a 2channel catchphrase that other users answer with "ｶﾞｯ" (*Ga'!*), a sound effect of hitting someone. On a placard aimed at the hostages, it was pure trolling.
[^tn-vip]: This is the origin of the News Flash (VIP) board. It began as a refuge board for News regulars and became 2channel's most influential culture board. See [[News Flash Board VIP]] and [[News Flash Board]].
[^tn-winny]: Winny was a Japanese P2P file-sharing program. Its developer, Isamu Kaneko, was called 47氏 ("Mr. 47") because he announced it as post number 47 in a Download board thread. He was convicted at first instance but finally acquitted by the Supreme Court in 2011.
[^tn-chojugiga]: The *Chōjū-giga* ("Scrolls of Frolicking Animals") are 12th–13th-century picture scrolls, long out of copyright, which makes the "super copyright infringement" notice a joke at the newspaper's expense.
[^tn-nevada]: The Sasebo killer was an 11-year-old girl. The nickname "Nevada" and images of her became a notorious meme; the source reports this without comment, and it is translated as written.
[^tn-nanashi]: 名無しさん (*nanashi-san*, "Mr./Ms. Nameless") is the default name for anonymous posts. Each board has its own variant, and changing it for elections, holidays or protests became a 2channel custom (see the Nomaneko and Saga entries).
[^tn-flash]: Flash animations made from 2channel memes and ASCII-art characters were a major part of Japanese net culture in 2001–2006.
[^tn-superfree]: Super Free was a Waseda University event club whose members were convicted of gang rapes in 2003. Being falsely named as a member was devastating; see also the 7 November 2006 ruling.
[^tn-burakura]: ブラクラ (*burakura*) originally meant a "browser crasher" link and came to mean any trap link, including shock images.
[^tn-psp]: This cross-reference to other Wikipedia articles appears under the BE@2ch entry in the source, but has nothing to do with it; it was probably displaced when the list was edited.
[^tn-bae]: Bae Yong-joon ("Yon-sama"), star of *Winter Sonata*, was at the peak of a Korean-drama boom in Japan in 2004. Puppet Mappet is a comedian who performs with frog and cow hand puppets. "Yūkorin" is gravure idol Yūko Ogura; the source does not explain her part in the vote.
[^tn-kami]: 神 (*kami*, "god") is 2channel slang for someone who delivers something amazing, such as a leak or a great post. A journalist explaining it on national TV as "God, you know" became a meme in its own right.
[^tn-tanshaotoko]: 単車男 (*tansha otoko*, "Motorbike Man") is a pun on *Densha Otoko* ("Train Man"); see the note on the 4 July 2005 entry.
[^tn-april2005]: The two names parody a far-right and a left-liberal organization's naming style, mocking 2channel's political image from both sides.
[^tn-china]: Saaya Irie was a child gravure ("junior idol") model, about 11 years old in 2005, which makes this episode uglier than the source's amused tone suggests. "Ikemen" means "handsome guy"; telling Chinese users it was an insult was a prank.
[^tn-livedoor]: Livedoor, then led by Takafumi Horie, was the hottest IT company in Japan in 2005 and hosted 2channel's domain service. The "Yamada virus" family of Winny/Share malware published the contents of infected users' drives.
[^tn-jiyuriyo]: The 自由利用マーク (*jiyū riyō māku*, "Free Use mark") was a set of labels from Japan's Agency for Cultural Affairs that let copyright holders allow free copying.
[^tn-densha]: *Densha Otoko* (電車男, "Train Man") was the 2004 story, told in 2channel posts, of an otaku who saved a woman from a drunk on a train and was coached by the board to ask her out. It became a book, film and TV drama, and the "-otoko" pattern became a template.
[^tn-tsubo]: The 壺 (*tsubo*, "pot" or "jar") illustration was 2channel's iconic top-page logo, which explains the later jokes about seizing it.
[^tn-nomaneko]: avex's Japanese release of O-Zone's "Dragostea din tei" ("Numa Numa") came with a Flash video whose cat character, "Nomaneko", was adapted from a Mona-like Flash by a 2channel user. The trademark applications and merchandise caused a large protest and threats against avex (see the "God's Proxy" entry). 八頭身 (*hattōshin*, "eight heads tall") is a lanky ASCII-art version of Mona. The figure of "more than 10 million users" is an often-repeated estimate, not a measured number.
[^tn-nomatako]: のまタコ ("Nomatako", *tako* = octopus) was Hiroyuki's mock product parodying "Nomaneko"; the open letter asking avex for permission was itself a joke.
[^tn-niwango]: Niwango later launched Nico Nico Douga. See [[Niwango]], [[Hiroyuki Becomes 'Director-Administrator' of Dwango's New Company]] and [[What Is Dwango Starting with Hiroyuki]].
[^tn-bnf]: B·N·F, later identified as day trader Takashi Kotegawa, became famous for making about ¥2 billion from the error in a single day.
[^tn-hyde]: hyde is the singer of L'Arc~en~Ciel. "156" refers to the height in centimetres that 2channel users insisted on for him.
[^tn-yamada]: Share was the successor P2P program to Winny. "Ionazun" (イオナズン) comes from a VIP board joke about a *Dragon Quest* spell.
[^tn-pov]: This entry and several others on Korea and China present a contested political view (here, about the House of Sharing, a home for former "comfort women") as plain fact. That is a feature of the source, not an established finding; see the callout.
[^tn-matome]: まとめブログ (*matome* blogs) repost and summarize 2channel threads, often with ads. Many users resented them for profiting from posts, a conflict that led to the "Kenmo" board in December 2007.
[^tn-dos]: "500 million accesses per second" is almost certainly an exaggeration or a misreading of the original report; it is translated as given.
[^tn-niisoku]: The source does not explain the board nicknamed ニー速 (*Nī-soku*); it is one of the News Flash family of boards.
[^tn-gokuraku]: Gokuraku Tonbo were a comedy duo on *Mecha-Mecha Iketeru!* (*Mecha-Ike*). In July 2006 member Kei Yamamoto was arrested, which is why videos about them were drawing attention.
[^tn-shokotan]: Shōko Nakagawa ("Shokotan") was a hugely popular blogger whose quirky slang was marketed as しょこたん語 ("Shokotan language"). VIP regulars objected that some of it was their own slang.
[^tn-cranes]: 千羽鶴 (*senbazuru*, a thousand origami cranes) is a traditional gift of prayer for recovery or remembrance.
[^tn-nangpa]: This is the Nangpa La shooting of 30 September 2006, filmed by foreign climbers near Cho Oyu.
[^tn-buraku]: 部落地名総鑑 (*buraku chimei sōkan*) were illegal directories of the addresses of buraku communities, historically used to discriminate against their residents in hiring and marriage. "Ura-2channel" ("Secret 2channel") was a fake link used as a prank.
[^tn-vipper]: VIPPER is the name for regulars of the News Flash (VIP) board.
[^tn-ps3]: 「物売るっていうレベルじゃねぇぞ！」 ("This isn't even on the level of selling stuff!") became one of the best-known 2channel catchphrases of 2006.
[^tn-choshi]: ぬれ煎餅 (*nure-senbei*, "wet rice crackers") are soft soy-soaked crackers, a local speciality that Chōshi Electric Railway sold to stay afloat. The campaign is a well-known example of 2channel collective action for a good cause.
[^tn-dqn]: DQN (*dokyun*) is 2channel slang for a loutish, uneducated delinquent, from the TV show *Mokugeki! Dokyun*.
[^tn-shinushinu]: 死ぬ死ぬ詐欺 (*shinu-shinu sagi*, "I'm-dying-I'm-dying fraud") is a derogatory 2channel term for fundraising appeals for sick children that users judged unnecessary or dishonest, as in the 28 September 2006 entry.
[^tn-seizure]: The legal pressure in 2007 is part of why Hiroyuki transferred 2channel to Packet Monster Inc. in Singapore in January 2009. Article 96 of Japan's Penal Code concerns damaging seals or seizure notices.
[^tn-nico]: 15 January 2007 is the start of Nico Nico Douga (β); the first, "(Kari)", version had opened in December 2006. See [[Nico Nico Douga and 2channel (Part 1-1) - The Roots of Nico Users in the (Kari) Era]], [[Kawakami Nobuo Looks Back on Ten Years of Nico Nico Douga]] and [[NicoNicoDouga]].
[^tn-soon]: The "Noise Lady" (騒音おばさん) of Nara was a woman convicted in 2005 of injuring a neighbour with noise harassment; TV footage of her made her a national figure of mockery. The claim that the neighbour was the real aggressor is a theory spread on 2channel, not a court finding.
[^tn-image]: ※イメージです ("*This is an image") is the small-print disclaimer used in Japanese adverts and TV for staged or illustrative footage. After this incident it became a 2channel jab at TBS.
[^tn-gainax]: Takami Akai is a Gainax co-founder. The episode was a well-known 2007 controversy about anime staff insulting fans online.
[^tn-jien]: 自演 (*jien*, short for 自作自演, "acting both parts") means sock-puppeting, and 自演乙 (*jien otsu*) is the sarcastic "nice sock-puppeting". 歌ってみた (*utatte mita*, "I tried singing it") is Nico Nico Douga's cover-song genre.
[^tn-asahiru]: アサヒる (*Asahiru*, "to Asahi") is net-right slang meaning "to fabricate", from a 1989 scandal in which an *Asahi* photographer damaged a coral reef for a story. Ishihara's column had claimed that people were saying "*Abe-suru*" ("to do an Abe", i.e. quit abruptly) after Prime Minister Shinzō Abe's sudden resignation.
[^tn-refs]: The source has bracketed citation numbers [1]–[4] here, but the reference list did not survive the copy (the "References" heading below is empty).
[^tn-first]: Civil courts had ruled on 2channel posts many times before (see 2002–2006), so "first" must mean the first *criminal* conviction for threats posted on 2channel.
[^tn-kenmo]: 嫌儲 (*kenmō*, "hating money-making") is the name of a board created for users who objected to *matome* and affiliate blogs reposting their posts for profit.
[^tn-kirikomi]: 切込隊長 (*Kirikomi Taichō*, "Raid Captain") is the handle of Ichirō Yamamoto, a blogger and commentator who was a well-known 2channel personality.
[^tn-nagano]: The 2008 Beijing Olympic torch relay passed through Nagano on 26 April 2008 and drew pro-Tibet protests.
[^tn-akiba]: On 8 June 2008 a man drove a truck into a crowd in Akihabara and stabbed passers-by, killing seven; he had narrated his plans on a mobile bulletin board. See [[A History of Japanese Internet Culture and Incidents, As I Saw It (1969–2018)]].
[^tn-waiwai]: For years, the *Mainichi*'s English-language WaiWai column had translated lurid tabloid stories about Japanese sexual behaviour, which spread abroad as fact. The 2008 exposure led to protests and an advertiser boycott, organized largely from 2channel.
[^tn-friio]: Friio was an unofficial digital TV tuner that ignored Japan's B-CAS copy-protection system.
[^tn-fukuda]: Fukuda's retort 「あなたとは違うんです」 ("I'm different from you", i.e. "unlike you, I can see myself objectively") became one of the most famous political memes of 2008.
