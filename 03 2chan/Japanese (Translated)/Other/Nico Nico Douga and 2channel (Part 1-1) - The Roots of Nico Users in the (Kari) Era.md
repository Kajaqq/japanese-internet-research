# Nico Nico Douga and 2channel (Part 1-1): The Roots of Nico Users in the (Kari) Era

#### Foreword

Hey hey, it's Sekai Amata, same as always.[^tn-amata] This time I'm planning to turn out a long piece titled "Nico Nico Douga and 2channel".
Now, why talk about this of all things? Because it seems you all don't have a good grasp of who Nico Nico Douga's users are, so I thought I'd write down what those users really are, plotted out from my memories, experience and delusions.[^1] If the story seems forced in places, *that's by design*.[^tn-shiyou] Please fill in the gaps yourselves...
Also, I often revise heavily after posting, so watch out if you're reading via RSS or an antenna.[^tn-antenna]

#### How I came to write this

id:koizuka wrote an entry called "[Looking back at the features of Nico Nico Douga (Kari)](http://d.hatena.ne.jp/koizuka/20071212/nicovideo_kari)".[^tn-koizuka]
Oh right, Nico Nico Douga started out as "(Kari)", I thought, looking back. And while I was looking back, this happened:

> Chorus festival: 650 people gather for "Nico Nico Douga", belting out 22 popular songs - Itai News
> [http://blog.livedoor.jp/dqnplus/archives/1068261.html](http://blog.livedoor.jp/dqnplus/archives/1068261.html)[^tn-itai]

After seeing that and the Hatebu comments,[^tn-hatebu] this turned up:

> Niconico and minors - What the internet really is
> [http://d.hatena.ne.jp/qaze00/20071217/1197836483](http://d.hatena.ne.jp/qaze00/20071217/1197836483)

Seeing those two, I thought, "time I wrote it down." So I did some research, and there was so little information I was in tears. Amata on the verge of dying of rage, nothing to be done.[^tn-funshi] With no other choice, I decided to combine the information there is with my memory.

#### And then, Nico Nico Douga began

##### Nico Nico Douga stumbles on day one

On 12 December 2006, Nico Nico Douga was born.[^2] It was the video-sharing site that Dwango, which [had been working on the concept since summer](http://blog.nicovideo.jp/niconews/2007/12/000684.html), had finally completed. However, [server trouble on opening day meant the service never actually ran](http://blog.nicovideo.jp/niconews/2007/12/000673.html), and posts to 2channel from inside Dwango were blocked, so they couldn't promote it either.[^tn-block] Snagged on both, it finally opened on the 13th.[^3]
On the 13th, Nico Nico Douga (Kari) stepped onto the stage for the first time. As [Old Tales, Episode 5](http://blog.nicovideo.jp/2007/08/post_162.php) explains, it got "(Kari)" because, perhaps reflecting its concept of "not trying very hard", it mocked the Web 2.0 catchphrase "eternal beta" by calling itself (Kari).[^tn-kari]
And the Nico Nico Douga (Kari) that opened on the 13th grew its users through promotion by the operators... no, it didn't. It was Hiroyuki, that hopeless administrator, who did the promotion.

##### Hiroyuki's promotion: the first stirrings

First, Hiroyuki started a thread on the News Flash board (now the Teary-Eyed News Flash board)[^tn-news] called ["What if ASIMO appeared on *8-ji da yo*"](http://amatanoyo.blog16.fc2.com/blog-entry-1390.html). That was the first time Nico Nico Douga was exposed on the internet. This is written on the Nico Nico Douga developers' blog:

> Hiroyuki introduced us
> [http://news20.2ch.net/test/read.cgi/news/1165986337/l50](http://news20.2ch.net/test/read.cgi/news/1165986337/l50)
> Because of that, traffic shot up all at once.
>
> Because the video Hiroyuki posted was trolled,
> we placed some restrictions on comment size, comment characters
> and consecutive comments from the same IP.

The video he posted was this one: footage of ASIMO falling over, with the Drifters' background music added.[^tn-asimo] > [http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z-0bhh8q8Ac](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z-0bhh8q8Ac)

A few minutes later, Hiroyuki posted the same video on his own blog on [ASKS? - the "teach-me" community](http://www.asks.jp/).[^4]

> A Yo\*\*ube you can comment on
> [http://nicovideo.jp/watch?v=utZ-0bhh8q8Ac](http://nicovideo.jp/watch?v=utZ-0bhh8q8Ac)
>
> The point is that you can comment on the video.[^tn-url]

As the [final Old Tale](http://blog.nicovideo.jp/2007/08/post_163.php) says, "more than half of our traffic came from Hiroyuki's blog": almost all of it came from there. And so Nico Nico Douga (Kari) became known to a section of internet users.

#### Nico Nico Douga inside 2channel

##### Nico Nico Douga (Kari) was part of 2channel

Thanks to Hiroyuki's blog, Nico Nico Douga (Kari) became widely known, but the people who found it were almost all 2channelers. Mind you, that was exactly what the operators themselves wanted.
The 2channelers saw Nico Nico Douga (Kari) as a new "playground" and came up with one way of playing after another. id:koizuka describes the time like this:

> On top of that, because we didn't announce the service in advance, the people who came to the site were only the really sharp-eyed ones, and few of them, so they explored all sorts of new ways to use it.

I think the "really sharp-eyed ones" were probably the News board crowd (ν速民),[^tn-nusoku] but anyway: during the (Kari) period, Nico Nico Douga (Kari) optimized itself for 2channelers.

Here is an image from the time, from [Nico Nico Douga board (Kari) - Netoneto](http://netoneto.chesuto.jp/e2496.html). I looked around a lot, but this is the only screenshot from the (Kari) era I could find. Back then, the "not trying very hard" attitude was oozing out of the top page.

##### What the (Kari) era meant

During the (Kari) era the site was able to grow rapidly as a "playground", and its features were nearly complete. Three factors produced this result:

1. Hiroyuki aimed his promotion at 2channelers.
2. The promotion was just a URL, so nobody had any idea "how to play".
3. Nobody knew who was running it.

Niwango's plan to take on 2channelers from the very start was achieved through 1. Most people reading Hiroyuki's blog were 2channelers, and because it started from Hiroyuki, even if non-2channelers had heard of it, they would surely have steered clear. The perception that "Hiroyuki's started something again" certainly did spread, though.
Then 2 was there to make the most of how 2channelers behave as newcomers. If they had clumsily explained it, at worst it would have been brushed off with "nice ad, bro".[^tn-senden] [There's actually a case of this](http://blog.nicovideo.jp/niconews/2007/12/000670.html).
Besides, Nico Nico Douga (Kari) wasn't dealing with well-behaved users. Most web services draw up guidelines beforehand and try to acquire users who fit them, but Niwango didn't take that approach and left everything to the 2channelers. By dealing with 2channelers, users whose behaviour was a complete unknown, it could investigate in advance everything that might happen. Niwango built the outline of Nico Nico Douga by making good use of these "sharp-eyed" users, and succeeded in creating a service any user could accept.
So 2 was effective for building up experience thoroughly:

1. To learn what actions 2channelers would take.
2. To take in the fun that 2channelers create.
3. To run into the technical problems early on.

Furthermore, as 3 says, nobody knew that "Niwango is running it".[^5] However much Hiroyuki promoted it, news sites won't pick something up if they don't know who or what company is behind it. This was the most important thing in making (Kari) a limited test environment aimed at 2channelers.
So it was "Nico Nico Douga, the video-sharing site Hiroyuki made", and since its users were 2channelers, in practice too "Nico Nico Douga (Kari) was part of 2channel".

#### Nico Nico Douga's first users were "2channelers"

And so Nico Nico Douga (Kari) was optimized by 2channelers, but that was obvious from the moment Nico Nico Douga was born. 2channel already had the concept of *jikkyō*, as the existence of its live-commentary boards shows,[^tn-jikkyo] and above all, it was because Nico was a place where you could speak and share that 2channelers could act and have fun there. By the way, Nico Nico Douga actually did something amazing in this respect, but I'll save that for next time.

#### The end of the matured (Kari) era and the arrival of the turbulent (β) era

The (Kari) era became complete as a "playground" for 2channelers. Through "sharp-eyed" users, the 2channelers, and (at the time) sincere operators, the prototype of a video-sharing site where you could *niko-niko* (grin) was created.[^tn-nikoniko]
Then, building on the experience accumulated in (Kari), Niwango started Nico Nico Douga (β). But (β) was turbulent from the moment it started.

#### About Nico Nico Douga (Kari)

Information about Nico Nico Douga (Kari) is extremely scarce. For a start, it was never covered by news sites, so there's no way to look it up. I searched blogs too, but there were hardly any details, and I was in tears.
What's discussed in id:koizuka's entry "[Looking back at the features of Nico Nico Douga (Kari)](http://d.hatena.ne.jp/koizuka/20071212/nicovideo_kari)", in [How Nico Nico Douga Was Made](http://info.dwango.co.jp/rd/files/itpro_challenge_20070907/itprochallenge_nicovideo_koizuka.swf) (warning: Flash) and in [interviews after (β) opened](http://internet.watch.impress.co.jp/cda/special/2007/02/09/14725.html) is mostly technical, or about the story up to the launch and the concept. At this point, Amata was on the verge of a gruesome death.
So I had to write it based on my memory that it all started with Hiroyuki writing on his blog (good thing I was an asks? user) and the [old tales posted on the Nico Nico Douga operators' blog](http://blog.nicovideo.jp/niconews/2007/12/000684.html). Even counting the whole (Kari) era, there were fewer than 100,000 comments. When the service started, a video with about 400 views and about 100 comments could be number one in the rankings. That was the scale. And that site now has millions. You've got to laugh.
[![Nico Nico Douga (Kari) top page](http://image.blog.livedoor.jp/amatanoyo/imgs/6/e/6e776aeb-s.png)](http://image.blog.livedoor.jp/amatanoyo/imgs/6/e/6e776aeb.png) [![Nico Nico Douga (Kari) rankings on 10 January 2007](http://image.blog.livedoor.jp/amatanoyo/imgs/8/3/8373b1ef-s.png)](http://image.blog.livedoor.jp/amatanoyo/imgs/8/3/8373b1ef.png)
(Kari) as it survives on the Internet Archive. These are from a few days before (β) began. The view and comment counts show that people were starting to gather toward the end of (Kari).

The [reason it was named Nico Nico Douga](http://blog.nicovideo.jp/niconews/2007/12/000678.html) is written up, but as it says, [it was really decided in five seconds](http://blog.nicovideo.jp/2007/08/post_162.php): "Not a cool service name; something laid-back is better. Like Nico Nico Douga, say." → it hit Hiroyuki's funny bone, and that settled it. So it really doesn't mean anyth(snip)[^tn-ryaku]
Only recently has [\[Nico Nico Douga 1st anniversary\] What was Nico Nico Douga (Kari)?](http://blog.nicovideo.jp/niconews/2007/12/000683.html) been published, but it still isn't about the users.

Well, that's enough for (Part 1). Next time, the story from (β) onward.

> [!info] Cultural context and translator's notes
> A Hatena Diary post by the blogger Sekai Amata, 20 December 2007, a week after Nico Nico Douga's first anniversary. It is part one of a series; the sequel is translated as [[Nico Nico Douga and 2channel (Part 1-2) - Mixing and the Spread of 2ch Culture]]. The author is upfront that it rests on memory and scattered blog posts, but it is valuable as a near-contemporary user's view of the (Kari) period (12 December 2006 – 15 January 2007), which official histories mostly skip. Its central claim, that Niwango deliberately let anonymous 2channelers stress-test the site, is the author's interpretation, partly built on the developers' "Old Tales" blog series. Kawakami's 2016 memory is less flattering to 2channelers: see [[Kawakami Nobuo Looks Back on Ten Years of Nico Nico Douga]]. See also [[NicoNicoDouga]], [[NicoNico - JP Wiki]], [[Niwango]], [[2ch-niconico-Relation]], [[News Flash Board]] and [[Hiroyuki Nishimura]].

[^tn-amata]: どもども (*domo-domo*) is a breezy "hey hey". 数多世界 (*Amata Sekai*, written surname-first in Japanese) is the blogger's handle; the blog's title is あまたの何かしら。 ("Amata's something-or-other").
[^tn-shiyou]: それは仕様です ("that's the spec / it's by design") is a classic Japanese net joke: the excuse a developer gives when a bug is reported. Here the author jokingly calls any gaps in their argument intentional.
[^tn-antenna]: "Antenna" means Hatena Antenna and similar update-tracking services that showed when a page had changed, a common way of following blogs in Japan before RSS readers became universal.
[^tn-koizuka]: koizuka is Akihiko Koizuka (戀塚昭彦), the Dwango engineer who built Nico Nico Douga's original comment system, which is why his recollections are the main technical source here. He is the "creator-god of the comment feature" in the sequel to [[Kawakami Nobuo Looks Back on Ten Years of Nico Nico Douga]].
[^tn-itai]: 痛いニュース(ﾉ∀`) (*Itai News*, "Painful News") was one of the best-known 2channel summary (*matome*) blogs, reposting threads from 2channel's news boards. The 650-person event was an offline Nico chorus gathering in late 2007.
[^tn-hatebu]: はてブ (*Hatebu*) is Hatena Bookmark, a Japanese social bookmarking service whose short comments on popular articles worked as a commentary section for the Japanese web.
[^tn-funshi]: 涙目 (*namidame*, "teary-eyed") and 憤死寸前 (*funshi sunzen*, "on the verge of dying of rage") are melodramatic net-slang complaints. The author refers to themself in the third person ("Amata") for comic effect, and later escalates to 惨死寸前 ("on the verge of a gruesome death").
[^tn-block]: 2channel blocked posts from IP ranges that had been used for spam or abuse, including many corporate networks. Staff at Dwango therefore couldn't post the link themselves, which is why Hiroyuki's posts mattered.
[^tn-kari]: (仮) (*kari*) means "provisional", as in a working title. Where Web 2.0 services called themselves "beta" for years, Nico called itself "(Kari)", playing up its lazy, unserious image. See the note in [[Kawakami Nobuo Looks Back on Ten Years of Nico Nico Douga]].
[^tn-news]: ニュース速報板 (*nyūsu sokuhō-ban*, News Flash board, "news"), commonly ニュー速 or ν速, was one of 2channel's busiest boards. By December 2007 it had been renamed 涙目ニュース速報板 ("Teary-Eyed News Flash"), one of its many joke renamings. See [[News Flash Board]].
[^tn-asimo]: ASIMO is Honda's humanoid robot; a widely shared clip from December 2006 showed it falling down stairs during a demonstration. 8時だョ!全員集合 (*Hachi-ji da yo! Zen'in shūgō*, "It's 8 o'clock! Everybody gather!") was the Drifters' hugely popular TV comedy show (1969–85), famous for slapstick pratfalls set to its music. The source spells the robot "ASHIMO".
[^tn-url]: Note the video ID: Nico reused the YouTube ID with a "ut" prefix (utZ-0bhh8q8Ac), because (Kari) only played YouTube videos with comments laid on top. "Yo\*\*ube" is Hiroyuki's coy censoring of "YouTube".
[^tn-nusoku]: ν速民 (*nyū-soku-min*, "News board people"). ν (Greek nu) is a pun on ニュー (*nyū*, "new" / "news"). Regulars of the News Flash board were known for jumping on anything new first.
[^tn-senden]: 宣伝乙 (*senden otsu*), short for 宣伝お疲れさま ("thanks for your hard work advertising"), is the sarcastic 2channel brush-off for posts that look like stealth marketing.
[^tn-jikkyo]: 実況板 (*jikkyō-ban*), 2channel's live-commentary boards, where users posted in real time while watching TV broadcasts. Nico's scrolling comments are widely described as a video version of this practice.
[^tn-nikoniko]: ニコニコ (*niko-niko*) is the onomatopoeia for smiling or grinning; the site's name is literally "Smiley-Smiley Video". The author plays on it here.
[^tn-ryaku]: （ｒｙ is short for （略 (*ryaku*, "omitted"), a 2channel convention for trailing off mid-sentence, typed by its romaji initial because it was faster than converting to kanji. "anyth(snip)" imitates the effect.
