**Nandemo Jikkyo J** is one of the boards on 5channel. It is commonly called **NanJ** (formerly also “Nanjitsu J,” among other names), and its users are called **NanJ-min**, or “NanJ residents.” Although a live-commentary board, it was unusual even on 5channel in also permitting general chat.[^tn-nanj-jikkyo]

## Overview

Its official name is **“Nandemo Jikkyo J (Jupiter)”**—“Live Commentary on Anything J (Jupiter).” Founded in 2004, it handles **all kinds of live commentary**, as its name suggests. Other boards with the same purpose include Nandemo Jikkyo S, Nandemo Jikkyo U, and Nandemo Jikkyo VIP. It was busy at first, but gradually lost momentum to Nandemo Jikkyo U, which hosted many itinerant live-commentary threads across different genres, and became sparsely populated. It then turned into a board primarily hosting surreal joke threads.

In May 2009, Baseball ch was subjected to **restrictions extraordinarily severe for a live-commentary board**, making it difficult to create team-specific commentary threads or joke threads. Large numbers of displaced Baseball ch users consequently migrated in search of somewhere easier to post commentary. From then on, baseball became the board's main focus. For a time, even signature Baseball ch threads such as the “Pitchers Getting Shelled Thread” appeared here alongside ordinary baseball commentary threads.

About a year after the migration, Baseball ch became usable again, so one might have expected people to return. However, many users were dissatisfied with Baseball ch's numerous **strong personalities**—to put it as tactfully as possible—and the commentary and shitposting elements remained on NanJ. It came to resemble a general chat board whose users were largely interested in baseball. Although it naturally lost momentum during the off-season, just as Baseball ch did, its users never went back. Instead, they stayed until the next season began, saying things like “Die, anime pigs” and “NanJ is finished.” They also began providing commentary on various sports, anime, and variety shows, with baseball broadcasts at the center. Of the four Nandemo Jikkyo boards, it became second only to Nanjitsu V in how broadly it interpreted “anything.”

Some of the original users—known as the “indigenous residents”—were bewildered, since the board had previously seen little live commentary, apart from Major League Baseball. Their original numbers were so small, however, that they were treated like coelacanths: living fossils.

On March 3, 2014, the default anonymous name became **“Fubukeba Nanashi @ Reproduction Prohibited” (風吹けば名無し＠転載禁止)**, explicitly declaring **reproduction prohibited**. Nevertheless, reposting continued through 2ch.sc, a copying site that permitted reproduction. A cat-and-mouse game followed: 2ch.net introduced measures against copying, and 2ch.sc introduced countermeasures to those measures.

NanJ maintained its momentum even after 2ch became 5ch, running far ahead at the top of 5ch's rankings by posts per board. However, a temporary closure in March 2022 caused **NanJ users to scatter**. Baseball fans—“yakiu no onii-chan,” the “baseball big brothers”—and other television-commentary users who had taken refuge on “Nandemo Jikkyo Galileo” (**NanG**) were dismayed when they saw NanJ after it returned. The board was filled with copied threads designed to provoke conflict, bearing titles such as 【Sad News】, 【Images】, and 【Urgent】, created by roundup sites seeking affiliate revenue. Live-commentary users had already endured repeated friction with general-chat users who migrated there for its activity, as well as poor conditions: a low limit on retained threads and the one-hour rule that sent threads into the dat archive. Their patience finally ran out. Meanwhile, NanG proved capable of handling professional-baseball commentary after the season opened, with more retained threads and no one-hour rule. At last, the commentary users broke with NanJ and made NanG their permanent home. Some general-chat users who stayed on NanJ triumphantly claimed they had driven out the commentary users who only got in the way of their conversations. The community thus **split completely**.

What remained on NanJ was general-chat threads from conflict-stoking roundup sites and live-commentary threads for a few virtual YouTubers. Following the move to NanG, NanJ was overtaken by a margin of almost two to one in posts, finally surrendering first place in 5ch's board rankings to NanG. Threads reaching 1,000 posts within an hour almost disappeared; commentary on virtual YouTuber Hakui Koyori, celebrated as the sole “NanJ-approved” VTuber, became the only threads regularly doing so. The board no longer had anything like its former momentum, declining until News Flash VIP—whose inactivity it had mocked—caught up with it.

On March 13, 2023, an outage affected the boards on the nova server, including NanG. NanG users consequently returned to NanJ until NanG was restored on March 23.

## Characteristics of the board

It has many characteristics unusual for a 5channel board.

**Rapid thread turnover.** Compared with currently popular boards, its maximum number of threads is extremely small. Trends therefore spread easily throughout the board. This is one reason for the rapid proliferation of the Mōko dialect and NanJ-specific turns of phrase discussed below.

**Its own language.** Users favor variations on stock joke phrases in quantities incomparable to the slang of VIP or News Flash. Their sources range from baseball anecdotes and episodes to *A Midsummer Night's Lewd Dream* (*Inmu*) references. There are simply too many for even most NanJ users to know them all, so they are maintained on a dedicated wiki (see External links). Posts apparently using NanJ language have recently appeared elsewhere, but some people dislike its distinctive atmosphere, so users should avoid bringing it indiscriminately into other communities. The pseudo-Kansai dialect used on NanJ is called **Mōko-ben**, or the **“Fierce Tiger dialect.”** Even the wiki does not clearly define whether that term includes NanJ's modified joke templates or simply names its pseudo-Kansai speech.[^tn-nanj-moko]

**The influence of professional baseball.** Because of the migration from Baseball ch, most users originally belonged to the baseball community, although their level of enthusiasm varied. Whenever a bizarre play or dramatic comeback occurred during a game, threads reporting it would multiply. The thread list would instantly fill with similar titles, pushing general-chat threads into the dat archive. However, as the user base visibly grew around the “NanJ incident of sharing emotion” and the Hasekara controversy, general-chat threads, political threads, and non-baseball commentary gained prominence, and this phenomenon became a thing of the past.[^1] It became considerably rarer after the baseball-commentary users moved to NanG.

## Growth and changes in the user base

As mentioned above, Mōko dialect and stock phrases became major characteristics of NanJ. More than ten years after the baseball influx, however, the environment was changing.

In the early days, many joke threads advanced through template responses. Threads such as “Manly Murata,”[^2] “929 McDonald's Fries,”[^3] and “Archangel Satozaki”[^4] appeared regularly. The following is a post by one VIP user from around the time other boards began recognizing NanJ's distinctive culture:

> 104 Name: VIP Sends This on Behalf of Anonymous [] Posted: Friday, 2012/09/07 12:34:20.28 ID:Gh4PJDVw0
>
> A board like NanJ, with such developed stock phrases, is convenient for people with communication difficulties.
>
> Even though they're each talking one-way, on the surface it looks like they're communicating.
>
> NanJ is a hangout for monkey-brats who've given up on mutual understanding.
>
> What they post is closer to animal cries.

Harsh, but not entirely wrong. The culture of stock phrases offered advantages that made conflict less likely, including less direct speech and presenting a barrier to newcomers.[^5] However, as the population grew and general-chat and news threads increased, those kinds of replies and threads declined relative to the past, while freely worded posts became more conspicuous.

On the other hand, some television-commentary threads reached their full post limit with nothing but the single character “草” (“grass”) repeated endlessly.[^6] NanJ users appear to be getting closer to monkeys.

## Relationship with Inmu

Because Inmu quotations are used routinely on NanJ, people often mistakenly assume that Inmu is NanJ culture. In fact, it originated on the Entertainment/Sports News board and developed on the Guidelines board, so it is not precisely a culture originating on NanJ. However, because Inmu is a genre with roots in baseball, and its nature is close to NanJ's, there is considerable overlap in users.

Nevertheless, they remain different cultures, so using their terminology interchangeably is not especially welcome. Inmu fans in particular tend to dislike being confused with NanJ, so users should avoid indiscriminately using NanJ terminology on Inmu-related videos.

> [!info] Cultural context and translator's notes
> This community encyclopedia mixes description with insider jokes, contemptuous quotations, and editorial judgments. These voices are retained rather than treated as neutral assessments. “Residents” means regular users; “migration” and “refugees” describe users moving between boards. Reaching 1,000 posts means filling a thread to its usual limit, while “dat-ochi” means falling out of the active thread list into archived thread data. “Roundup sites” collect and republish message-board posts; the source ties their activity to advertising and affiliate revenue. Inmu is retained as the name of the meme culture the article discusses, with its title translated on first mention. Offensive language is translated where it appears in the source. Dates, rankings, and the distinction between the earlier baseball-centered board and its later decline reflect the captured article's periods of description.

[^tn-nanj-jikkyo]: 実況 (*jikkyo*) here means users posting running reactions while watching a broadcast or event together. It does not mean that the board itself broadcasts the television program.
[^tn-nanj-moko]: The article calls this pseudo-Kansai speech, rather than ordinary regional conversation. “Mōko” literally means “fierce tiger”; the Japanese label is preserved to distinguish this community register from actual Kansai dialect. English prose cannot reproduce that dialect contrast without introducing a misleading English regional accent.
[^tn-nanj-grass]: The source explicitly contrasts the written laughter forms ｗ and 草. Their visual form is essential to understanding the example, so the Japanese characters remain alongside an English gloss.
