1ch.tv was a now-defunct “people-friendly group of message boards.” The “Chiyu, Age 12” site mentioned below covers its history in detail, so this article will only trace its development.

## Overview

### What prompted its creation

The impetus for 1ch.tv came in June 2001, when ASCII founder Kazuhiko Nishi posted in a 2ch thread making fun of him. Nishi strongly condemned 2channel's anonymity and, through his arguments with anonymous posters, came to believe that “2ch has professional provocateurs.” It was at this point that he first called 2ch **“toilet graffiti,”** an expression that subsequently became established as one of its derogatory nicknames.[^tn-1ch-graffiti]

An offline gathering for Nishi's thread took place in August. Incidents there included Nishi getting angry when Hiroyuki Nishimura tried to eat two bowls of beef on rice, and a female regular with a fixed handle being photographed without permission.

Following these events, on September 29, 2001, Nishi announced that he would launch a message-board service called **1ch.tv** to compete with 2channel, promoting goals including “eliminating sockpuppetry.” Contemporary news articles show that he envisaged future services such as **“allowing users to post inexpensive paid content”** and **“real-time video broadcasting” from mobile phones and PHS handsets**—services resembling what are now **Blomaga** and **Niconico Live**.

### Launch of the service

1ch.tv went live on October 5, 2001. Most posts, however, came from 2channel users, and at first it was entirely their plaything. For some reason, the administrators left the trolling in place rather than deleting it, invoking the lofty justification of “demonstrating the violence of the Internet.” A personal news site at the time wrote, “1ch.tv has gone crazy.”

Although it was supposedly a “people-friendly message board,” it inexplicably had a Netwatch board. That board became a vanguard of opposition to 2ch; even posts expressing hatred and resentment toward 2ch were not deleted. For some reason it also displayed a banner for a “Leave 2ch Webring,” and became entirely a place to post hatred of 2channel. Vague deletion criteria, under which defamatory attacks on 2channel users were not deleted, also contributed. The administrators of 2ch found the Fujimi Iota Flash animation mentioned under Related videos so hilarious that they changed the default anonymous name on their Netwatch board to “ｺﾞｰｺﾞｰｺﾞｰｺﾞｰ！” (“Go, go, go, go!”).

The original “people-friendly” concept collapsed, and the service came to find its only reason for existing as an anti-2ch message board.

Amezou, who had operated a major message board before 2channel, also participated in running 1ch.tv. Caught up in the turmoil, he ultimately disappeared from the Internet.

After a few months, perhaps reflecting 2channel users' tendency to get bored easily, the 1ch.tv thread on 2ch's Netwatch board also emptied out and turned into a general chat thread. The paid services mentioned above were never implemented either.

### Subsequently

As of the article's “present,” ten years later, the 1ch.tv domain had been bought by a commercial operator, and http://amezo.jp/top.html, which had been used since 2005, was no longer accessible.

---

Incidentally, in 2006 Nishi also caused trouble on the talk page of his own Wikipedia article, condemning its anonymity and blanking the article.

Also in 2006, on that same talk page, he stated of 1ch.tv: “**I was involved when it was being created, but the company that actually built it was Value Exchange Co., Ltd. —Nishi**.” Whether or not that was the reason, as of March 2014 **the body of his Wikipedia article contained absolutely no mention of 1ch.tv**.

ASCII art is preserved in Japanese to retain its alignment, with its spoken line translated immediately below.
