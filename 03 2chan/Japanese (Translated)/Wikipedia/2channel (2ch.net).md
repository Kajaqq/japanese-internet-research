# 2channel (2ch.net)

> [!note]
> This is a translation of the Japanese Wikipedia article *2ちゃんねる (2ch.net)*. It is the older, longer version of the article that [[5channel]] now covers, so some passages overlap. The notes in boxes like this one are additions by the translator and are not part of the original text. Where the article links to another Wikipedia page, I have linked to the matching note in this vault, and left plain text where there is none. Source citations are kept at the bottom with their titles translated. Dates and "current" statements are those of the captured article.

**2channel** is one of Japan's largest electronic bulletin boards (anonymous bulletin boards), opened in May 1999. Its domain is "**2ch.net**". [[Hiroyuki Nishimura|Hiroyuki Nishimura]] opened it as a personal site; after its administrators changed in 2014, it was renamed "[[5channel|5channel]]" in 2017. Its common names and abbreviations include "**2chan**" (*Nichan*) and "**2ch**". [^10] Its official sister sites include **8chan** (now 8kun). [^11] [^12] [^13]

"**2channel**" and "**2ch**" are trademarks that Hiroyuki Nishimura holds rights to in Japan. [^14]

Japanese media often describe it as an "anonymous bulletin board", but *CNET Japan* has reported that since January 7, 2003 it has recorded and stored the IP address of every post, so strictly speaking it is no longer an anonymous bulletin board. [^15]

> [!note]
> "2chan" in this article is the nickname for this site (2ちゃん, *Nichan*, "2-chan"). In English discussion "2chan" more often means the separate imageboard [[Futaba Channel Hub|Futaba Channel]] (2chan.net), which is a different site. The IP logging mentioned here is also dated to January 2003 in [[History of 2channel]] (which says it began "experimentally") and in [[A History of Japanese Internet Culture and Incidents, As I Saw It (1969–2018)]].

## Overview

A lineage of thread-floating anonymous bulletin boards that originated in Japan. 2channel (now 5channel), opened by Hiroyuki Nishimura in 1999, became the origin of all other chan sites, including [[4chan Hub|4chan]] and 8chan (now 8kun).

> [!note]
> This sentence is the caption of a family-tree diagram in the original (not reproduced here), and it is the strongest version of the claim in these notes that 2channel is the root of every chan site. The notes on [[4chan Foundation]] and [[4chan Pre-history - bibanon]] trace 4chan through Futaba Channel's source code instead. A *thread-floating* board (スレッドフロート型掲示板) is one where a thread that receives a reply rises to the top of the list, the "bumping" model.

2channel was a thread-floating bulletin board opened as the successor site to the [[Amezou]] bulletin board, and is a collection of multiple electronic bulletin boards. In the "2channel" era, its users were sometimes called "2channelers" (*nichanneraa*), "nerā" and "channerā". As its catchphrase "**From 'hacking' to 'tonight's side dish'**" says, topics from a wide range of fields are posted.

> [!note]
> 夜のおかず (*yoru no okazu*, "tonight's side dish") is slang for material used for masturbation, so the slogan runs from the technical underground to pornography.

On January 1, 2009, the site's administrator and registered owner moved from Hiroyuki Nishimura to a Singapore-based company, "Packet Monster Inc." ([[#Lawsuits and the transfer of ownership|see below]]). [^8]

> [!note]
> Further down this same article gives the date of the transfer as **January 2**, 2009. [[History of 2channel]] and [[Hiroyuki Nishimura]] also say January 2.

In February 2014, for reasons that are not made clear, the effective administrative authority over 2channel passed to Jim Watkins) (hereafter "Jim"), who became its effective administrator. [^16] "Hiroyuki" opposed this and opened a new "**[[2ch.sc - split from 2channel|2channel (2ch.sc)]]**".

Below, 2channel from before the transfer of administrative authority is referred to as "**2channel (2ch.net)**" where the distinction is needed.

> [!note]
> The article's naming convention: "2ch.net" means the old site before the 2014 split, and later "5ch.net" means the same site after it was renamed. "Jim" is Jim Watkins, who ran the servers and the domain through his companies (see the section on the dispute below).

Because "2channel (2ch.net)" was renamed "**5channel**" (*gochanneru*) on October 1, 2017, its domain name was also changed to 5ch.net. All web access to 2ch.net is now forwarded to 5ch.net (see [[#2ch.sc and 5ch.net|below]]). [^17]

In 2007 it was described as "the most popular online community in Japan", [^18] and the site had an influence comparable to traditional mass media such as television, radio and magazines. [^19] At that time the site earned around 100 million yen a year, [^20] [^21] and it was the largest site of its kind in the world, with about 10 million visitors [^18] and 2.5 million posts a day. [^19]

According to "Google Ad Planner", a website once provided by Google, the users of 2channel as of 2009 were analyzed as follows. [^22] The male-to-female ratio was 68:32. By age, 34% were 35–44, 17% were 25–34, 17% were 45–54 and 14% were 0–17. By education, 31% had dropped out of or not gone beyond high school, 30% were high school graduates, 34% were university graduates and 4% had completed graduate school. 46% had a household income of less than 5 million yen, while 14% were said to earn 10 million or more. [^22]

On April 11, 2014, the "2channel Official Wiki", which users can freely edit, appeared. [^23]

## History

→ For details see "History of 2channel and 5channel" ([[2ch History Timeline]] in this vault is a separate English timeline, not a translation of it).

In the early days of the Internet in 1996, general-purpose bulletin boards such as [[Ayashii World Hub|Ayashii World]] existed, but they closed in 1998. The [[Amezou]] bulletin board then took over from them and was used as a general-purpose bulletin board.

However, Amezou could not secure a stable server environment and also suffered from script-based trolling, so in May 1999 2channel was opened as a refuge for Amezou and was advertised. There is a theory that its opening date was May 30 of that year and another that it was May 16. [^24] The board at the beginning was positioned as one element within a website collecting things that Hiroyuki found amusing, such as a Kusachu-go converter and a scribble book. [^25] That board did not reuse Amezou's script either; at first it was a "Kusachu-go board" using a "Kusachu-go conversion script". [^26]

> [!note]
> A *refuge* (避難所, *hinanjo*) is a board set up to take users from a failing one, and *refugees* (難民) are the users who move, a pattern repeated throughout this history. **Kusachu-go** (クサチュー語) is a joke "language" produced by an online converter that rewrites text with deliberately mangled kana. The claim that the first 2ch board did not reuse Amezou's script conflicts with [[4chan Pre-history - bibanon]] and [[The First Channel - Ayashii and Amezou]], which say 2ch ran on Amezou's program, and the opening date of May 30 appears in [[2channel]] and [[History of Ayashii World - Part 1]]. The founder's own recollection, in footnote 24, only fixes the month and the place: a university in Arkansas.

The newly opened 2channel had 10,000 visitors a day from the start, [^25] and the number of participants increased rapidly because it gave heavy coverage to the "Toshiba complainer incident", [^27] [^28] [^29] and because of its live commentary on the Tokaimura JCO criticality accident that occurred on September 30 of the same year. [^27] [^28] [^29] After that, the Amezou board fell into malfunction in October–November of the same year and closed in 2000, the following year, so even more users moved to 2channel.

> [!note]
> **The Toshiba complainer incident** (1999): a customer posted a recording of a Toshiba support employee calling him a "*kureemaa*" (crank complainer). It spread across the web and forced a public apology from Toshiba. The article's "see also" list calls it the trigger that let 2channel rise in Amezou's place. The **Tokaimura JCO accident** was a criticality accident at a uranium-processing plant in Ibaraki Prefecture on September 30, 1999, followed live by posters. Amezou's end date varies between sources

→ See also "[[Amezou]]" and "Toshiba complainer incident".

### Growth in users

Even so, in its early days it was not influential enough to reach general society, and when celebrities or well-known people themselves posted in a thread it did not cause much of a stir. As one example, in the dialogue in chapter 9 of *The 2channel Declaration: The Provocative Medium*, Shigesato Itoi says that he had been a user since the Amezou days. [^30] Also, Fusanosuke Natsume, appearing live on NHK BS2's *BS Manga Yawa* right after "descending" [^31] onto 2channel, said, "Oh my, I got worked up on a bulletin board, at my age, and I'm short on sleep."

> [!note]
> 降臨 (*kōrin*, "descent") is the 2ch word for a famous person appearing in a thread, as though a god had come down.

However, its name recognition rose sharply after the Nishitetsu bus hijacking incident in May 2000, because the culprit had posted on 2channel. In March of the following year, 2001, in the "**Nippon Life case**", Nippon Life Insurance petitioned the Tokyo District Court for a provisional disposition ordering the deletion of posts, saying defamatory posts had been made, [^29] [^32] and in August of the same year the Tokyo District Court issued an order for deletion of the posts. [^29] [^32] Through such incidents, 2channel became widely known to general society. [^29] [^33] [^34]

> [!note]
> **The Nishitetsu bus hijacking** (May 3, 2000): a 17-year-old who had posted a warning on 2channel under the handle "Neomugicha" ("Neo Barley Tea") hijacked a bus in Fukuoka and killed a passenger. It is the first case in which a crime announced on 2ch drew attention, and it brought a flood of newcomers and police attention, as [[2004 - Japanese Find a Forum to Vent Most-Secret Feelings]] and [[History of 2channel]] describe. A *provisional disposition* (仮処分) is a court's temporary order, in effect an injunction.

In 2004 its visitors exceeded 7 million, [^35] and it ranked 34th in the world website access ranking of Alexa Internet.

In 2009 the number of 2channel users reached 11.7 million, [^36] but with the rise of other services such as blogs, social networking services, video-posting sites and mobile-only sites, its rank in the world website access ranking fell relatively, to 294th.

### Aging

In April 2010 it was reported that "young people are moving away from 2channel and its users are aging" and that "they may not look at 2channel but look at 'Itai News', that is, they may be getting the information they need efficiently by looking at 'matome sites'", [^37] and seven years later, in March 2017, similar reports were made. Among young people, SNS under real names or handle names is the mainstream, and the cause is pointed out as their finding no appeal in 2channel, which digs deeply into a single subject anonymously. [^38]

→ For 2channel's users, see "2channelers".

> [!note]
> *Matome* (まとめ, "summary") sites and blogs repost 2channel threads, often with advertising. "Itai News" ("Painful News") is one such site, and is also the name of a 2ch news board. The fights they caused are covered under the heading on matome sites below.

### Lawsuits and the transfer of ownership

→ For details see "[[Hiroyuki Nishimura|Hiroyuki Nishimura]]".

On 2channel, necessary and sufficient measures were often not taken when defamation, libel, third parties' personal information, unauthorized reposting of copyrighted works, information on drug dealing and similar things were posted, [^32] [^1] and civil lawsuits against Hiroyuki, then the administrator, never stopped.

However, Hiroyuki did not appoint an agent lawyer and did not attend the trials, so he lost most of the trials (→ fictitious confession)). Even so, he has not paid the damages. He himself has said the following about it.

> "I'm being sued from Okinawa to Hokkaido, so it's either travelling the whole of Japan at my own expense or paying over a million yen per case to hire a lawyer. But when I took the option of 'not doing (the trial)', nothing happened. That's the current situation. Whether I win or lose, if I don't pay it's the same." "If they want to make me pay, they should tell a Diet member to make a law for it." [^39]

> [!note]
> The legal point is that in a Japanese civil case, a defendant who neither appears nor answers is treated as having admitted the plaintiff's claims (擬制自白, *gisei jihaku*), so he loses by default. [[Hiroyuki Nishimura]] gives the total of unpaid damages as about 434 million yen and notes he has paid his taxes.

From 2006 to 2007, a third party filed a petition for a declaration of bankruptcy, and an application was made to provisionally seize Hiroyuki's property, including "2channel's domain (2ch.net)", [^40] [^41] and on January 2, 2009, Hiroyuki transferred the operating rights to "PACKET MONSTER INC." in Singapore and stepped down as administrator. [^42] However, the operating rights were not actually transferred, the management company merely lending its name, [^43] and in 2013 Hiroyuki was told by the Tokyo Regional Taxation Bureau of an undeclared advertising income of about 100 million yen. [^44]

> [!note]
> This is the "2007 closure scare" that [[History of 2channel]] dates to January 12, 2007, when the site's default anonymous name on the VIP board was changed to a countdown. [[2Ch_Closure]] and [[2ch-niconico-Relation]] overstate it as a court or government seizure. This article says only that a seizure application was made.

In January 2010, the royalties that had been paid to Hiroyuki for the book *I Work at an Exploitative Company, and I May Have Reached My Limit*, a book of posts from 2channel, were seized, and damages were recovered for the first time. [^45] [^46] [^47]

### 2ch.sc and 5ch.net

→ For details see "History of 2channel and 5channel § Dispute over administrative authority".

On February 19, 2014, for reasons that are not made clear, the effective administrative authority over 2channel passed to Jim, and Jim became the effective administrator of 2channel. According to the claim of a person thought to be Jim, the announcement was to the effect that "Jim Watkins, chairman of **N.T. Technology**, dismissed all of the then operating team, including Hiroyuki, on the grounds of unpaid server fees, and Watkins himself and his relatives will take up the post of administrator". [^48]

On April 1 of the same year, Hiroyuki declared that his removal from operations was an "illegal takeover", established an electronic bulletin board called 2channel ("[[2ch.sc - split from 2channel|2ch.sc]]", abbreviated "sc"), and himself became its administrator. [^16]

→ For 2ch.sc, see "[[2ch.sc - split from 2channel|2channel (2ch.sc)]]".

In response, Jim said that the "2ch.net" domain was owned by "**Race Queen Inc.**" and that the present operating structure was not illegal either, and countered that he was considering legal action against Hiroyuki. He criticized: "We intend to continue discussions with Mr. Hiroyuki's side, but it is legally mistaken to judge that it is unlawful to do business (advertising contracts and so on) with Race Queen Inc. in the absence of any judicial determination," and said, "Mr. Hiroyuki Nishimura's claim that such commercial acts are unlawful seriously damages the reputation of Race Queen Inc., and we have no choice but to consider legal action." [^49]

> [!note]
> The three company names in this dispute are easy to mix up. **N.T. Technology** is Jim Watkins's server and hosting company, **Race Queen Inc.** is the company this article says owned the 2ch.net domain, and **Loki Technology** receives the site in 2017 (below). [[Hiroyuki Nishimura]] describes Jim as "president of Race Queen". The date of 2ch.sc's founding is also given as April 11 in [[Hiroyuki Nishimura]] and [[2channel]], and as April 12 in [[2ch.sc - split from 2channel]].

Meanwhile, Hiroyuki had in January 2009 nominally transferred 2channel to Packet Monster in Singapore and, in May of that year, published the book *Why I Abandoned 2channel* (Fusōsha) and publicly stated that he had "let go of 2ch", [^50] [^51] so 2channelers bashed him, saying "Nishimura is a liar". [^52] [^53]

In September 2015, at a Q&A session on [[4chan Hub|4chan]], Hiroyuki announced that he had filed a lawsuit in Japan against Watkins over ownership of 2channel. [^54]

From May to July 2016, Hiroyuki obtained the trademark rights in Japan for "2ch" and "2channel". [^55] [^56] On the other hand, the World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO) rejected Hiroyuki's complaint that the "2ch.net" domain was being illegally occupied. [^57] In response, Race Queen Inc. commented on the top page of "2ch.net" that "the series of claims by Mr. Nishimura up to now is completely false, and Mr. Nishimura's words and actions, including 2ch.sc, are illegal acts that obstruct the operation, so we will deal with them strictly from now on". [^58] As a result, the situation was that Hiroyuki held the trademark rights for "2ch" and "2channel" in Japan, while Race Queen Inc. controlled the registration rights for the domain "2ch.net". [^59]

During this period, the Japan Patent Office rejected the request for a trial to invalidate the trademark registrations of "2ch" and "2channel" that Race Queen Inc. had filed. [^60] Following this ruling, Watkins announced on October 1 of that year that he would transfer 2channel from "Race Queen Inc." to "**Loki Technology**". Watkins said that "it had become difficult to provide users with safe and comfortable use because of interference by Hiroyuki Nishimura". [^61] "Loki Technology", named as the transferee, announced that "to avoid unnecessary disputes over rights, we will operate the bulletin board anew as '**5channel (5ch.net)**'". [^62] Incidentally, "5channel" and "5ch" are trademarks that Code Monkey★, a technical staff member of 2channel and the former administrator of 8chan (now 8kun), holds rights to in Japan. [^63] [^64] [^65]

> [!note]
> The result was a split of the pieces that make up a website: the domain and servers sat with Jim's companies, and the name "2ch" in Japan with Hiroyuki. The journalist quoted in [[5channel]] asks who "2channel" actually was, and notes that the courts later came down on Jim's side (the Tokyo Plus claims were dismissed on appeal in 2019, a later development this older article does not cover). "Code Monkey★" is the handle of Ron Watkins, Jim's son. 

### Matome sites and bans on reposting

In December 2007, matome blogs that reposted without permission, for profit and with affiliate advertising and the like, threads made on the News Flash board increased, and as the debate over whether this was acceptable grew among the threads' regulars, Hiroyuki, then the administrator of 2channel, set up the [[News Flash Board|News Flash (Kenmō) board]].

> [!note]
> The **News Flash** board (ニュース速報, "Breaking News") is one of 2channel's busiest news boards; see [[News Flash Board]]. *Kenmō* (嫌儲, "hatred of profit-making") is the name of the board and of the ethos opposed to people profiting from the community's posts. **Affiliate advertising** pays a blog when its readers click through to a shop.

In May 2012 there were still protests against matome sites that reposted 2channel posts without checking whether the information was true and so spread wrong information. [^66] There were also cases where the editing ignored the context of a thread and sometimes posts that never existed were fabricated. In response, Hiroyuki, then administrator of 2channel, named several sites regarded as especially malicious [^2] and applied a ban on reposting to them. [^67] [^68] Hiroyuki himself thought it was impossible to eradicate matome sites, but commented that he would continue to take measures against malicious ones.

In February 2014, as described above, the effective operator of 2channel changed to Jim Watkins. Then in March 2014, a vote by 2channelers made several boards reposting-prohibited, [^69] and soon after all boards were reposting-prohibited. In November 2017, after it became 5channel, the rules on matome sites were changed: because matome sites republish rather than quote, "permission is required"; unauthorized use would be reported as a DMCA violation, and legal action through civil and criminal proceedings would also be taken. [^70]

> [!note]
> The footnote to the 2012 sentence names the sites: "Hachima Kikō", "Ore-teki Game News Flash @ Jin" and "Yaraon!" among them. [[History of 2channel]] lists the five banned sites and the dates of this fight in more detail, and puts the start of the conflict between matome blogs and 2ch users in May 2005 (the first blog to be debated being the "News VIP Blog", per the 2005 entry in [[A History of Japanese Internet Culture and Incidents, As I Saw It (1969–2018)]]).

### Personal information leak incident

→ For details see "2channel personal information leak incident".

On August 25, 2013, the "2channel personal information leak incident" occurred, and the leak was confirmed of the customer information and posting history of about 40,000 users of the "2channel Viewer" (commonly called "●", now "Premium Ronin"), the administrative information of about 150,000 "●" and "trial ●" accounts, the tripcode information of users, and the cap information of people involved in operations. [^71]

> [!note]
> **●** (*maru*, "the circle") was the paid account for 2channel: it let users read archived threads, post despite IP bans, and hide their IP. A *tripcode* proves a poster's identity with a hashed password, and a *cap* is a code that marks a post as coming from an administrator or moderator, so leaking them exposed both users and staff. The date of the incident is given as August 21, 25 and 26 across the vault's notes

## Bulletin board system

→ For details see "[[5channel|5channel]] § Bulletin board system".

2channel is of the type called a thread-floating bulletin board.

Each bulletin board is divided into large units of subject called "**categories**", such as News, Food Culture and Internet-Related.

Within them, boards are divided by genre into units called "**boards**" (*ita*). Many boards exist within 2channel, but the number of users differs greatly by board. An extremely sparsely used board is called a "**kaso-ita**" (depopulated board), and conversely an extremely crowded board is called a "**kamitsu-ita**" (overcrowded board).

Each board holds finely divided **threads** (*sure*), one for each topic belonging to its field. Posting to and viewing the bulletin board is done within these threads. A piece of writing posted in a thread is called a "**res**" (response, short for "reply").

As of May 2016 there were about 905 boards, and each board has from dozens to hundreds of threads. Boards are set up from time to time, and there are also boards such as the "Bargain board" that open only in particular seasons. In May–June 2002 World Cup live-commentary boards (four boards) were created for the 2002 FIFA World Cup, and in August 2004 a dedicated board was created only for the duration of the Olympics. In the past a board was also created on the birthday of Hiroyuki, then the administrator, solely to congratulate him.

In each thread, anonymous posting is possible; if a user posts without entering a name, a provisional name set for each board is automatically attached. The provisional name is basically "Nameless-san" with variations by board, but because most users post anonymously, it can look at a glance as if a person named "Nameless-san" is posting consecutively. [^3]

> [!note]
> The default name 名無しさん (*Nanashi-san*, "Nameless") gives its name to the culture of nameless posting that 2channel inherited from Ayashii World and Amezou; on 4chan it became "Anonymous". The footnote explains that some boards, such as the dōjin board, block nameless posting as a countermeasure against a virus that posts to 2channel.

Basically anyone can post unless restricted (see [[#Restrictions]]), but posting to three boards is restricted: the Restriction Information board, the ● board, and the be category boards. The Restriction Information board lists information on providers restricted on 2channel, so only the "restriction staff" and "reporters" involved in restrictions can post to it. The ● board is a board for people who have a ● (the 2channel Viewer), so you cannot post without a ●. There is no restriction on viewing.

The three be category boards cannot be posted to unless you are logged in with a be@2ch account. There is no restriction on viewing.

Incidentally, 2channel stands out among electronic bulletin boards in general, and so there are many bulletin boards whose functions and format were created by referring to 2channel. The types are countless, ranging from those that simply show threads in the same way to those that imitate the interface and even the background images used.

### Restrictions

To prevent mass posting using programs, host restrictions are frequently carried out. At such times, users of the host concerned may be caught up in the restriction. Because arbitrary judgment by the operators is accepted for host restrictions, depending on the content even a single post can result in a long-term or indefinite host restriction. In addition to host restrictions, restriction by NG words (Rock54) and restriction of terminals by cookie are also carried out together. Since around 2018, restrictions (displays such as "please do it elsewhere") have become widespread, and the number of posts has tended to fall sharply.

> [!note]
> A *host restriction* (ホスト規制) blocks every user of an internet provider or IP range, so innocent users are caught by "collective punishment". The same restrictions are described from a user's point of view in [[5channel]]'s sister note [[2channel]]. *Rock54* is the system that blocks a post containing certain words, and "NG word" (no-good word) is the list it uses.

- On August 20, 2008, a permanent restriction on all boards was imposed on users of asahi-net's dynamic IPs. The trigger was that a single user had trolled on various boards through asahi-net, but because the IP was dynamic the host could not be identified, so all asahi-net dynamic IP users were permanently restricted. Many complaints came from 2channelers who are asahi-net users.
- From mid-March to mid-July 2009, the operators took the measure of totally banning (restricting all servers) the use of "SoftBank BB" users (bbtec.net). This was because a malicious user, who repeatedly leaked personal information, had appeared. Although it is said that the operators could have specified and restricted only the region from which the user seemed to have sent posts, they restricted all SoftBank users (it was discovered later that this user had done the same thing through other providers and had been posting from internet cafés).
- In connection with the 2009 House of Representatives election, a restriction was imposed on a host that had mass-copied and pasted articles criticizing the Democratic Party, and after that, similar copy-pasting triggered the restriction with a single post, which drew criticism. In the same way, such as obstructing restrictions ("commemorative posts") or, when a mass copy-paste that had once been blocked was posted again, restricting "as an example", the restrictions as a whole were characterized by a stance of not tolerating the public nature of the bulletin board at all, and by arbitrary management by the operators being widely accepted.
- As of August 2010, because of the restrictions on dynamic IPs described above, posting from major hosts such as KDDI, OCN and EMOBILE had remained almost impossible.
- From August 2010, "**umetateya**" ("fillers" who fill up threads) appeared, and the operators imposed a long-term restriction on them, so posting became impossible.
- In mid-March 2011, "Ninpōchō", a posting-level restriction using cookies, was introduced.
- No large-scale restrictions have been carried out since Nishimura and others were dismissed from operations in February 2014.
- From mid-2014, hostile sites (such as [[2ch.sc - split from 2channel|2ch.sc]], Logsoku and [[NicoNico - JP Wiki|Niconico Douga]]) began to be registered as NG words. At the same time, a system called CoPiPe, which prevents posts with the same content, was created.
- In early 2015, it became known that there is a function that restricts users by the number of times an NG word is posted. As a result, the providers that trolls used while changing IPs have effectively been put under large-scale restrictions.

> [!note]
> **Ninpōchō** ("ninja notebook") was a cookie-based system that limits what a new poster can do until they build up a posting record. The first bullet confirms what [[5channel]]'s companion note [[2channel]] says about asahi-net and SoftBank bans. [[History of 2channel]] dates Ninpōchō to February 9, 2011 (as "Adventure Log", renamed February 24), while this article says mid-March. An *umetateya* floods a thread to push it to its 1000-post limit, so that it dies.

## Culture

2channel has a culture that follows the earlier flourishing underground board "[[Ayashii World Hub|Ayashii World]]" and its direct source "[[Amezou]]": most participants take part without using fixed handles, and Internet slang and pictographs are heavily used. [^72] [^73] Aggressive language and tasteless posts are also frequent. [^74]

### Nameless

Unlike many web services, entering a name is not required by the system, and so people posting also more often speak as "Nameless" than use a fixed name. This is greatly due to the influence of Amezou. Perhaps for this reason, users who post with a fixed handle tend to be disliked, for example by being put on an NG list.

> The best-known 2channel ASCII-art characters are Monā (モナー), a white cat, and Giko-neko (ギコ猫), whose origin is credited to Ayashii World in [[Ayashii World]]. A trademark application on Giko-neko by Takara in 2002 is among the incidents recorded in [[History of 2channel]].

### Affinity with the online right

From 1999, at the same time as 2channel's start, neo-Nazi and far-right users carried out attacks aimed at liberal sites, and Hiroyuki Seto repeatedly posted under his real name. On boards covering current affairs, quarrel-like debates verging on trolling were fought about foreign workers and the suffrage of foreign residents. [^75]

In April 2000, the first large-scale flame war occurred on 2channel. A flood of defamatory messages was written on the "Metropolitan governor and prefectural governors board", targeting a woman who was then a city councillor of Koganei and who had called on people to send protest emails to the Tokyo Metropolitan Government over Governor Shintarō Ishihara's "*sangokujin* remark". Some contained content implying sex crimes. The bulletin board on the councillor's homepage was also trolled and was forced to close. The councillor asked the 2channel operators to delete the posts, but this earned the resentment of 2channelers and brought still fiercer attacks. Out of exhaustion she resigned as a city committee chairwoman on May 29, but the flaming did not subside. [^75]

> [!note]
> The *sangokujin* (三国人) remark was a 2000 speech in which Governor Ishihara used a term that was widely read as derogatory toward Korean and Chinese residents of Japan. *Enjō* (炎上, "going up in flames") is the Japanese term for a pile-on or flame war. This article and [[5channel]] call this the first major one on 2channel, while [[A History of Japanese Internet Culture and Incidents, As I Saw It (1969–2018)]] gives the 2003 "JOY Festival" as the first *enjō*.

## Social influence and the problems it raised

### Influence on networked society

2channel, the largest anonymous bulletin board in Japanese, has had various effects on society and individuals, including being the source of the flaming) phenomenon, and there are also sites that ban access by viewers thought to come from 2channel by using the referrer or the NG word function.

2channel's operators are also said to be mostly ignorant of security-related knowledge, with few administrators for the amount of traffic, so it can be a target of DoS attacks and zero-day attacks. [^4] Merely posting a server's URL can concentrate users' access and turn into a DDoS attack. In any case, the impact on traffic load is generally large. For this reason, 2channel's servers use motherboards made by Tyan, and in order to withstand the network load they are installed in an internet data center in San Francisco in the United States, commonly called "365 Main", which connects directly to internet exchange networks.

> [!note]
> This is the passage that places 2channel's servers in San Francisco. [[2004 - Japanese Find a Forum to Vent Most-Secret Feelings]] has Hiroyuki paying about 20,000 dollars a month to a hosting company in Palo Alto, and [[2ch History Timeline]] has N.T. Technology as San Francisco-based. [[!Title Page]] gives hosting 2ch in the United States as the reason for its long-lasting resistance to Japanese legal action. Footnote 4 adds that the traffic from attacks was sometimes used as a deliberate load test of the FreeBSD operating system that the servers ran.

### Balancing privacy and criminal acts

→ See "real-name reporting".

Because of its nature, 2channel contains some posts that expose the privacy of or defame specific individuals. Though it is not limited to 2channel, to deal with the human-rights violations that frequently occur on anonymous bulletin boards, the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications revised its "Defamation and Privacy Guidelines" and decided on a policy allowing the Human Rights Bureau of the Ministry of Justice to request deletion of posts on the victim's behalf. Based on this guideline, the human-rights divisions of the Legal Affairs Bureaus around the country have been making deletion requests for things such as the publication of the real names of juvenile offenders.

To this, Hiroyuki, then administrator, said the following [^76] and did not comply with the request (the Ministry of Justice has also not given a clear answer to this).

> "I think that the privacy of unrelated ordinary people being made public should be deleted unconditionally, but I cannot think that suspects strongly suspected of being criminals should be guaranteed the same level of rights as ordinary citizens. Also, if the same level of privacy as ordinary citizens is to be guaranteed, then besides the culprit in this case there are victims whose privacy was also made public, and I think the same remedial measures should be given to them too. If the Legal Affairs Bureau's thinking is that the remedies for suspects should take priority over the remedies for ordinary people, I am prepared to comply, but I would like a clear answer on this matter." — Hiroyuki

### Crime announcements and monitoring of posts

After the Nishitetsu bus hijacking, as if induced by it, there appeared people who were arrested for writing crime announcements, and people who put them into action and were arrested. Because arrests have also resulted from bombing and murder announcements, it is said that when a major incident occurs, the relevant agencies check the relation between the murder and attack announcements frequently written on various boards and the incident, and the National Police Agency's Cyber Force also searches regularly and monitors posts. Even for trivial crime announcements that seem clearly meant as pranks, other users sometimes report them to the operators, leading to arrests, and for many users crime announcements are a taboo. A crime announcement alone does not meet 2channel's deletion criteria and is treated as something to be preserved as evidence, so a deletion request for a crime announcement will almost never be granted. However, on some boards that are monitored frequently, the administrator takes the measure of forcibly stopping a thread that he judges to be a crime announcement.

Afterwards, following the Akihabara massacre, reports and automatic reports to "Yokoku.in", a service started in 2008, have made it possible to suppress and arrest to some extent, but the situation remains far from complete eradication.

In addition, companies, mainly large companies, are said to be increasing in number that set up departments specializing in monitoring posts so as not to suffer losses from false rumors and malicious posts, or outsource such monitoring; indeed the major advertising agency Dentsu has partnered with Gala [^5] and provides for a fee, to client companies, an ASP service "Dentsu Buzz Research" that monitors and reports posts about the company on bulletin boards (100,000 yen a month, excluding consumption tax, per client). [^77] [^78] Profiting from such services that use 2channel was regarded as a problem by users and the operators from the outset, and around 2005 there was a statement on the lower part of the top page calling attention to it. [^79]

Also, in the PC remote-control incident, in which computers were remotely operated and four people were wrongly arrested, 2channel was the trigger for infection with the remote-control virus. On November 26, 2012, a joint investigation headquarters including the Tokyo Metropolitan Police Department searched a company said to be affiliated with 2channel, seizing data on paid members and posting manuals. The search came after, on August 9, 2012, a murder announcement, "mass murder at Comiket", was posted on 2channel concerning an event held in Koto Ward, Tokyo, which led to stronger security at the venue. Anonymizing software had been used so that the source of the series of posts to 2channel could not be identified, but it came to light that on one occasion the software may not have been used. [^80]

> [!note]
> The *Akihabara massacre* is the June 2008 attack in Tokyo's Akihabara district in which a man drove into a crowd and stabbed people; he had announced his plans beforehand on a mobile bulletin board (see [[A History of Japanese Internet Culture and Incidents, As I Saw It (1969–2018)]]). A *Cyber Force* is a Japanese police unit for cybercrime. *Comiket* is Tokyo's huge twice-yearly comic market.

### Educational problems for boys and children

On some search sites for children, such as Kids goo), it is filtered, and in filtering software it is in the "bulletin board" category.

According to a survey announced in February 2007 by the web-filtering company Netstar, "12.2% of elementary and junior high school students use 2channel", [^81] and there is a statistic saying that already more than one in ten elementary and junior high school students are "2channelers".

### Contribution to the integrity of academic research

Since around 2000, suspicions of fabrication in academic papers such as in medicine have been continually pointed out on the Biology board. Some literature evaluates many of the posts as getting to the heart of the problem. [^82] There are many university teachers who have been disciplined, for example dismissed, with the points raised on 2channel as the trigger (see "11jigen" and the "mass paper-fraud allegations by Anonymous A case" for details).

## Social reactions

### Favorable uses

On 2channel boards on every kind of theme exist regardless of genre, and the information exchanged is extremely diverse. There are boards used as CGM (consumer-generated media) sites where consumers and engineers exchange information on products, services and technology, and academic categories used for scholarly discussion. However, it is hard to say that these function accurately as "places for fair and truthful exchange of information" or "places for academic discussion with a clear purpose" in the strict sense, and are left to users' good sense.

As an example of academic contribution, it contributed together with Korean bulletin boards to uncovering the fabrication of human embryonic stem cells (ES cells) by Hwang Woo-suk of Korea (December 2005), and it has also contributed to the disclosure and resolution of the problem of access concentrating on the NTP server of Fukuoka University (January 2005). [^83]

In times such as disasters it is also used as a site for accumulating and providing information; in the October 2004 Chūetsu earthquake in Niigata Prefecture it was used, mainly at the individual level, to provide information together with Yahoo! bulletin boards and earthquake-information matome sites. [^84] Dedicated earthquake boards such as the Earthquake News Flash board are also always open.

→ For details see "2channelers § Volunteering and support".

### Negative and critical evaluations

2channel is often called "**toilet graffiti**" (*benjo no rakugaki*). [^74] For example, on August 12, 2001, at an offline meeting of the thread "ASCII's Mr. Nishi resigns as a director", held at the head office of ASCII Corporation in Shibuya, Tokyo, Kazuhiko Nishi said in front of Hiroyuki, then the administrator, who was attending the offline meeting, "*2channel is like graffiti in a toilet*". [^85] This nickname is also sometimes used within 2channel.

> [!note]
> Nishi's remark is also the starting point of [[1ch.tv]]'s story: [[1ch.tv]] says he launched a rival board with the aim of eliminating sockpuppetry and "creating a kind Internet" soon after, and that Amezou joined him, which is also in [[Amezou]]. An *offline meeting* (オフ会, *ofukai*) is a real-world gathering of online users.

2channel tends to be disliked by users of existing boards of the same kind such as "[[Ayashii World Hub|Ayashii World]]" and "[[Amezou]]", [^86] and is sometimes called "*tantsubo*" (spittoon). Hiroyuki, the founder, treated this as black humor and displayed a "pot" on 2channel's top page. [^7]

> [!note]
> The "pot" (壷, *tsubo*) is the image on the top page of 2channel. The footnote explains that it changed with events. Why it was chosen is also explained differently elsewhere in the vault: [[Amezou]] gives a second theory that it refers to fraudulent spiritual goods sold on 2ch, and [[4chan Pre-history - bibanon]] ties it to the claim that Hiroyuki had a hand in Amezou's collapse. The antagonism with Ayashii World users, which turned into an attack on 2ch's servers in August 2000, is described in [[Ayashii World]] and [[History of Ayashii World - Part 2]].

### Politics and 2channel

At the political-party level, on May 10, 2000, Naoto Kan of the Democratic Party sent a notice through a lawyer to 2channel asking for the deletion of a post, "A request to everyone from Naoto Kan of the Democratic Party", thought to be by someone impersonating him. [^87]

After the 2007 Liberal Democratic Party presidential election, Tarō Asō said, "I sometimes post on 2channel."

Every time an election season comes, many posts supporting Jesus Matayoshi and Mac Akasaka are made, but because most of the posts are half in fun they have not led to votes at all, and they continue to lose, never having been elected once. Also, intentional rumors aimed at damaging the image of particular politicians or companies are frequently posted, and many libelous anonymous-document-like posts that slander parties, whether in government or opposition, are also made. This tendency is stronger in the election season.
