# 5channel

For individual boards within 5channel, see 'List of 2channel and 5channel Boards.'

**5channel** is a Japanese electronic bulletin-board website (an anonymous board). Founded in May 1999 by [[Hiroyuki Nishimura|Hiroyuki Nishimura]] as a personal site called '[[2channel Hub|2channel]],' it changed administrators in 2014 and was renamed 5channel in 2017. As of March 6, 2026, its domain is **5ch.io**. Common names and abbreviations include **5-chan** and **5ch**. [^13]

Genealogy of anonymous thread-bumping boards originating in Japan. 2channel (now 5channel), founded by Hiroyuki Nishimura in 1999, became the origin of all other chan sites, including 4chan and 8chan (now 8kun).

It is a collection of multiple electronic bulletin boards using a thread-bumping system. As its slogan, '**From hacking to tonight's side dish**,' suggests, posts cover a broad range of subjects. In 2021, it ranked 29th among popular Internet sites in Japan by visitor numbers. [^14] [^1]

## History

For details, see '[[2ch History Timeline|History of 2channel and 5channel]].'

### 2ch.sc and 5ch.net

For details, see 'History of 2channel and 5channel § Administrative Control Dispute.'

On February 19, 2014, effective administrative control of 2channel transferred to Jim for reasons not specified, making him its de facto administrator. According to a statement by someone believed to be Jim, **N.T. Technology** chairman Jim Watkins had dismissed Hiroyuki and all other existing operators for unpaid server fees, with Watkins himself and his relatives taking over administration. [^16]

On April 1 that year, Hiroyuki declared his removal from operations 'an illegal takeover,' established another electronic board called 2channel ('[[2ch.sc - split from 2channel|2ch.sc]],' abbreviated 'sc'), and became its administrator. [^17]

For 2ch.sc, see '[[2ch.sc - split from 2channel|2channel (2ch.sc)]].'

Jim responded that **Race Queen Inc.** owned the 2ch.net domain and that the existing administration was lawful, while saying he would consider legal action against Hiroyuki. He criticized the claim: 'We intend to continue discussions with Mr. Hiroyuki's side, but in the absence of any judicial determination, it is legally mistaken to say that doing business with Race Queen Inc., including advertising contracts, is unlawful.' He added, 'Hiroyuki Nishimura's assertion that such commercial dealings are unlawful seriously damages Race Queen Inc.'s reputation, and we have no choice but to consider legal action.' [^18]

However, Hiroyuki had nominally transferred 2channel to Singapore's Packet Monster in January 2009 and publicly said he had relinquished it, publishing *Why I Abandoned 2channel* through Fusōsha that May. [^19] [^20] 2channel users consequently attacked him as a liar. [^21] [^22]

In September 2015, Hiroyuki announced during a [Q&A session](https://archive.is/AWqj2#selection-1483.0-1483.25) on [[4chan Hub|4chan]] that he had sued Watkins in Japan over ownership of 2channel. [^23]

Between May and July 2016, Hiroyuki acquired Japanese trademark rights to '2ch' and '2ちゃんねる.' [^24] [^25] Meanwhile, the World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO) rejected his complaint alleging unlawful occupation of the 2ch.net domain. [^26] Race Queen Inc. responded on the 2ch.net homepage that Nishimura's claims were entirely false, that his actions including 2ch.sc illegally obstructed operations, and that it would respond firmly. [^27] The resulting situation was that Hiroyuki controlled Japanese trademark rights to the names, while Race Queen Inc. controlled registration of 2ch.net. [^28]

During this period, the Japan Patent Office rejected Race Queen Inc.'s requests to invalidate the registrations of '2ch' and '2ちゃんねる.' [^29] Following that decision, Watkins announced on October 1 of the same year that 2channel would transfer from Race Queen Inc. to **Loki Technology**. He said interference by Hiroyuki Nishimura had made it difficult to provide safe and comfortable use. [^30] Loki Technology announced that it would operate the board anew as **5channel (5ch.net)** to avoid unnecessary disputes over rights. [^31] '5ちゃんねる' and '5ch' are Japanese trademarks held by Code Monkey★, a 2channel technical staff member and former administrator of 8chan (now 8kun). [^32] [^33] [^34]

On June 22, 2018, in a civil suit brought by Hiroyuki (Tokyo Plus Co., Ltd.) against **N.T. Technology** over 2channel's ownership, the Tokyo District Court found a breach of contract and ordered damages and repayment of prepaid server fees. [^35] On April 25, 2019, 8chan's 8ch.net Twitter account (@infinitechan) announced that the Tokyo High Court had reversed the first-instance judgment and dismissed all Tokyo Plus claims. [^36] [^37] On December 4, 2019, the Twitter account '5channel (5ch.net)' (@5chan_nel) announced that the Supreme Court's rejection of an appeal had finalized the High Court ruling dismissing all Tokyo Plus claims. [^38]

On October 27, 2020, 8chan (now 8kun) founder and former Watkins business partner Fredrick Brennan offered the view that the name and domain had changed to 5channel because Hiroyuki Nishimura was planning to seize 2channel under the UFMJRA (Uniform Foreign Money-Judgments Recognition Act, a US law), and the change was intended to avoid this. [^39]

Journalist Yoshiaki Sei argued that 2channel had been operated like a business exploiting legal loopholes by taking advantage of ambiguity over website ownership. Based on the litigation over administrative control, he summarized the question of 'who 2channel is' as follows:

> Is 2channel the data stored on its servers, its domain, its trademarks, or Hiroyuki Nishimura himself? If a company operates it, is that the shell company Packet Monster? Ambiguity over who a website is enabled various businesses exploiting legal loopholes. Nobody knows the correct answer. But in the 2channel takeover case that went all the way to the Supreme Court, Jim, who owned the servers and held the domain, prevailed. At present, that precedent is all there is. — Yoshiaki Sei (March 26, 2021). '[QAnon and Japanese Anonymous-Board Culture [2]: Hiroyuki Nishimura and Jim Watkins' Bitter 2channel Feud, Part 2](https://web.archive.org/web/20210326154407/https://webronza.asahi.com/national/articles/2021030500012.html).' *RONZA*. Asahi Shimbun Publications. Archived from the [original](https://webronza.asahi.com/national/articles/2021030500012.html) on March 26, 2021. Accessed September 22, 2022.

Separately from ownership litigation, Hiroyuki brought a trademark damages claim against Watkins (Race Queen Inc.). Alleging that Race Queen Inc. improperly infringed his '2ch' and '2ちゃんねる' trademarks from the 2014 takeover until the 2017 renaming, he demanded ¥175 million in damages plus ¥5 million per month until use of 2ch.net ceased. [^40]

On December 24, 2019, Race Queen Inc.'s Twitter account (@RQueeninc) announced that Nishimura's claims had been dismissed and that its prior-use rights under trademark law had been recognized. [^41]

In 2020, a judgment prohibited Race Queen Inc. from using the name 2channel, but dismissed the other damages claims. Hiroyuki appealed immediately. As of 2023, the case remained contested. Witnesses associated with 2channel testified for Jim about its actual operations. For example, Yoshihiro Nakao, known as FOX★, said in a written statement that the familiar story of the 2001 August Crisis—UNIX board residents saving 2channel when rising traffic nearly brought down its servers—contained many inaccuracies. He said the servers he and Jim provided did not earn enough advertising revenue to justify the traffic, and they had actually shut the servers down. [^40]

### Compilation sites and reproduction bans

In December 2007, increasing numbers of compilation blogs were reproducing threads from the Breaking News board without permission for profit, including through affiliate advertising. As residents debated the practice, then-administrator Hiroyuki established the Newsoku (Kenmō) board.[^tn-5ch-matome]

In May 2012, protests continued against compilation sites that reproduced 2channel posts without checking their truth and spread misinformation. [^42] Some edited posts without regard for thread context or even fabricated posts that never existed. Hiroyuki singled out several sites considered especially malicious [^2] and prohibited them from reproducing content. [^43] [^44] Although he believed eradicating compilation sites was impossible, he said measures against malicious sites would continue.

As noted above, Jim Watkins became 2channel's effective operator in February 2014. In March 2014, user votes made several boards reproduction-prohibited, [^45] and all boards soon followed. After the renaming to 5channel, rules for compilation sites changed in November 2017. Such sites were deemed to perform reproduction rather than quotation, requiring permission. Unauthorized use would lead to DMCA complaints and legal action through civil and criminal proceedings. [^46]

Original administrator [[Hiroyuki Nishimura|Hiroyuki Nishimura]] famously told an interviewer during the Nishitetsu bus hijacking that '(using a bulletin board) is difficult for people who cannot recognize lies as lies.' [^47] Asking 'Where is the source?' had become a common means of self-protection on 2channel. He argued that after smartphones spread and social networking services displaced bulletin boards, more people circulated information without checking sources and public Internet literacy declined. [^48]

Responding to the suggestion that 2channel had created a culture of insulting media personalities, Nishimura argued: 'That demand already existed; after 2channel declined, people simply started writing it on Twitter.' He continued: 'In today's online society, including Twitter, nobody criticizes people for freely spreading things without sources. Wasn't 2channel's culture more sensible? In many cases, people who have been deceived are simply left deceived. They do not even know it themselves. And nobody says, “We should help them.” Sometimes I wonder whether people in trouble will just remain in trouble forever.' [^49]

Hiroyuki cited litigation as a reason for 2channel's decline: 'It is a question of demand and who provides it. At the time, I was the only provider. Operating in Japan causes so much trouble that services used here now, such as Twitter and Facebook, are all made abroad. In Japan people complain and obstruct you from every direction, so people stopped doing it here and everyone went to foreign services.' [^49]

Meanwhile, in November 2017, Mynavi Teens, a media outlet for teenage girls, surveyed 1,006 females aged 13–19. 'Ngo,' originating on 5channel's Nanj ([[nanJ Board|Anything Goes Live Commentary J board]]), ranked third in its words category of the year's buzzwords chosen by teenage girls. [^50] It became a sentence ending in expressions such as 'warota-ngo' ('I laughed'), 'naita-ngo' ('I cried'), 'otsukare-ngo' ('good work'), and 'oishikatta-ngo' ('it was delicious'). Netlab reported that users liked the sound rather than the original self-mocking sense, which arose from posts ridiculing former Tohoku Rakuten Golden Eagles pitcher Domingo Guzmán's failed relief appearance in the 2008 season opener. [^51]

5channel's 'culture of cynical mockery' influenced social-media culture as of 2023. Chuo University lecturer and journalist Tetsuya Shibui argued: 'Its roots go back to 2channel, founded in 1999. Expressions that seem to convey people's true feelings are well received there. A characteristic is the exhilaration of posting anonymously and seeing replies multiply.' He added: '2channel has many unwritten rules, so posting is intimidating, but many people simply read it. Contemporary cynical behavior online can be said to have been reinforced by 2channel and similar spaces.' He maintained that Hiroyuki had not imposed this culture: 'It reflects society. It arose spontaneously and, simply put, is a festival. People act cynically or post serious replies to enliven it. People join real festivals without knowing which deity they honor, too. Similarly, whether what is causing a stir is true is unimportant; it only needs to provide entertaining material. That structure is unchanged between 2channel and social media.' [^52]

For roughly ten years after predecessor 2ch.net opened, posting volume was very high. [^53] As of 2025 it was declining as users split across other boards, including Open 2ch, Edge, and Talk. [^54]

On March 5, 2026, registrar Epik revoked the domains for 8chan, 8kun, 5ch.net, and bbspink.com. The stated reason was that boards containing animal abuse had been left unattended and judged to host content violating ethical standards. 5ch.net continued as 5ch.io. Both 5ch.net and bbspink.com indicated they would seek restoration. [^55][^tn-5ch-dates]

### Aging users

In April 2022, a thread claimed that people born in 1985 (Shōwa 60; the Pressure Generation) had experienced the greatest evolution in gaming. Many older users disagreed, roughly spanning the late Shirake Generation to the early Bubble Generation, the cohort known as Shinjinrui. This prompted the comment: 'Quite a few posts come from people who still remember gaming before the Famicom. **5channel really is a den of middle-aged men.**' [^56]

For 2channel users, see '2channelers.'

### Domain suspension

On March 9, 2026, US time (March 10 in Japan), United States registrar Epik permanently suspended 5channel's domain, **5ch.net**, and the related BBSPINK domain, **bbspink.com**, because animal-abuse content violated its terms of service. [^57] Epik said responsibility for content management lay with the domain owners and was not being fulfilled. [^57] Jim Watkins objected that this was 'the end of free speech' and that 'ICANN does not authorize permanent locks like this,' calling on users to help by emailing Epik. [^57]

Since March 6, 2026, 5channel has operated at the alternative domain **5ch.io**.

## Bulletin-board system

5channel uses the format known as a thread-bumping bulletin board.

Its boards are grouped into broad **categories**, including News, Food Culture, and Internet-Related Topics.

Within these, subject genres are divided into **boards** (*ita*). The many boards on 2channel differ greatly in user numbers. Extremely quiet boards are called **depopulated boards** (*kaso-ita*); extremely busy ones are **crowded boards** (*kamitsu-ita*).

Each board contains **threads** (*sure*) divided by particular subjects. Reading and posting occur within these threads. A posted message is called a **resu**, short for 'response,' meaning a reply.

As of May 2016, during the 2channel period, there were about 905 boards, each with tens to hundreds of threads. Boards are occasionally added; some, such as Bargains, open only seasonally. Four 2002 FIFA World Cup live-commentary boards opened in May–June 2002, and dedicated Olympics boards operated only during August 2004. A board was once created solely to congratulate then-administrator Hiroyuki on his birthday.

Users can post anonymously. If they leave the name field empty, a board-specific provisional name is assigned automatically. Most are variations on 'Nanashi-san' ('Anonymous'). Since most users post anonymously, it can appear that one person named Nanashi-san is posting repeatedly. For this reason, the site was formerly sometimes called 'Anonymous World.' [^3]

Basically anyone can post unless restricted (see Restrictions), but posting is limited on the Restriction Information board, the ● board, and the three be-category boards. Restriction Information lists restricted providers, so only restriction staff and reporters may post. The ● board is for holders of ● (2channel Viewer) accounts, which are required for posting. Reading is unrestricted.

Posting on the three be-category boards requires a be@2ch account and login. Reading is unrestricted.

2channel's prominence among electronic boards inspired many others to imitate its functions and format. The variants are countless, ranging from similar threaded displays to copies of its interface and background images.

### Threads

#### Overview

On 5channel, a single thread is limited to 1,000 successful posts or about 500 KB (kilobytes) of data. [^4]

Numbered successor threads often use counters related to their subject: 'period X' for school threads, 'case X' for incidents, or 'home run No. X' for home-run threads on various boards. Numerical wordplay may also be used.

When rising participation or posting heavily loads a particular server, a condition called '人大杉' ('too many people') may make it inaccessible through ordinary web browsers. Access then requires a dedicated 2channel browser.

The capacity limit can also be exploited to destroy threads. [^5]

There are also advertising-style threads in which posting is entirely disabled. They appear on all boards and rise again after sinking a certain distance. [^6]

#### After a thread ends

The 1,001st entry of a thread with 1,000 posts, or the next entry after data exceeds about 500 KB, displays a board-specific notice that posting is closed. A few hours later the thread disappears from the board's thread list.

The following explains 2channel-specific terminology concerning thread endings.

##### Dat-ochi

A thread has been removed from the board's thread list after a compression or immediate-death determination.

A dat-ochi thread displays 'This thread has been placed in the past-log archive.' Posting is impossible, but it can be read in a web browser.

Other viewing methods include external sites such as Mimizun Search and [Mirror Converter](https://web.archive.org/web/20050728152714/http://www.geocities.jp/mirrorhenkan/). Waiting for HTML conversion is another option, although it takes time.

##### Compression determination

When the number of threads reaches a board-specific threshold, such as 400, 500, or 600, a specified number, such as 100, enter dat-ochi in order of oldest last posting.

##### Immediate-death determination

This applies when a thread has at least 980 posts or its data size is below a board-specific threshold, such as 5 KB or 30 KB. Threads with no post for a full 24 hours enter dat-ochi. Some busy boards use stricter limits of 12 or six hours. Live-commentary boards also have rules moving threads into dat-ochi after a set time regardless of post count.

##### The n-day rule

A rule moving a thread into dat-ochi n days after creation, mainly used on live-commentary boards.

##### HTML conversion

A dat-ochi thread becomes viewable again through a web browser—that is, an HTML file is served. Whether this happened was once largely a matter of luck. Since dat-ochi threads can now continue to be read in browsers, the term is almost obsolete.

### Anonymous names

When the name field is left blank, '**Nanashi-san**' or a name appropriate to that [[#Bulletin-board system|board]] is inserted automatically.

This anonymous-name system reduced personal interaction between users, producing the following characteristics:

- The email-address field is often left without an actual email address.

(One reason is the thread-bumping system, in which entering 'sage'—read as the Japanese word for 'lower'—in the email field prevents the thread from rising to the top.)

- Many users do not use honorific language, including respectful, humble, or polite forms, although tendencies vary by board.
- Many threads favor concise exchanges and avoid complicated content.
- People using a handle, called fixed-handle users or *kotehan*, are often disliked. [^7]

(This varies by board and is often used as a pretext for provocative disruption.)

Then-administrator [[Hiroyuki Nishimura]] explained anonymity: 'For example, if Prime Minister Abe posted under his real name on an online board, discussion would become impossible. For pure discussion, it is easier without a personal identity.' [^58] He also said, 'Other diary and blog sites are built for individual communication, but 2ch aims to accumulate information.' [^59]

The anonymous-name system was inherited from [[Amezou]].

### ID

See also 'Tripcode (Electronic Bulletin Boards).'

Some 5channel boards use an ID feature. It has a limited effect in preventing sockpuppetry exploiting anonymous names.

There are three kinds of boards: mandatory IDs; optional IDs, where entering text in the email field yields 'ID:???' or entering !id in the name field displays one; and no IDs. IDs mainly use encrypted IP-address and date information. The feature is not intended to identify users personally or link individual posts to real identities.

Although timing varied by board, around the end of 2024 ID assignment shifted toward device information rather than IP addresses. If recognized as the same device, an ID remains unchanged despite a changed IP. This responds to widespread impersonation through disconnecting and reconnecting to change IDs and alter threads' direction or atmosphere. Benefits include targeted restrictions, rather than provider-wide bans, against disruption or defamatory posts about the government. By recognizing a device associated with disruption, the system can infer that posts through entirely different IPs, including Wi-Fi, come from the same person. Such users may see notices such as 'Please do this elsewhere' or '**We are currently rejecting your posts**.'

IDs prevent sockpuppetry and reduce thread consumption by meaningless replies. Consequently, operators do not accept requests for IDs on a quiet board solely because a resident troublemaker posting irrelevant material has rendered a thread nonfunctional.

Taking such issues into account, operators experimentally introduced the **be@2ch board** system around November 2004 to reduce irresponsible posts. It cannot directly identify individuals, but allows some recognition of which posts were made by a logged-in user.

Some users employ fixed handles (kotehan) and may become central to a thread's discussion. Most use an anti-impersonation feature called a tripcode, showing that a series of posts comes from the same person. Some 'anonymous' fixed-handle users use tripcodes without a handle.

As i-mode spread and mobile-phone support made it easy to hold separate IDs for a phone and a computer, ID endings distinguished them: **O** (letter O) for phone posts and **0** (zero) for computer posts, depending on board settings. Posts through the official 2channel viewer [p2](https://web.archive.org/web/20060810172353/http://p2.2ch.net/) ended in **P**. Under the new administration, p2 was discontinued, while smartphones, MVNOs, and public wireless LANs using Wi-Fi made multiple IDs even easier to obtain. ID suffixes were subdivided by connection type, and **BBS_SLIP** was introduced to display carrier identifiers and an additional hashed ID called KOROKORO derived from ISP, browser, and other information. [^60] Even with these measures, sockpuppetry using multiple devices or connections is frequently alleged.

### Cap ★

For details, see 'Cap (Electronic Bulletin Boards).'

Various operational volunteers on 5channel may be issued a ★, an anti-impersonation star.

Commonly called a cap, this could be obtained easily by email before tripcodes were introduced. Types vary by role, such as 'Deletion Moderator★,' 'Guide★,' and 'Reporter★.' Operators managing caps occasionally recruit applicants by email. Unlike ◆-marked fixed-handle users using tripcodes within threads, cap holders mainly work in operational areas less visible to users outside those threads.

Question threads sometimes ask, 'How can I become a deletion moderator?' Details remain unclear. The stock answer is 'Start by not asking that question,' apparently to exclude people who only demand things and find people able to investigate independently. The basic **procedure** is to serve an apprenticeship as a guide, or formerly a restoration worker, then respond to recruitment, with recommendations from other deletion moderators.

Cap holders are strictly forbidden to disclose their credentials. If a mistake while starting a thread or similar mishap exposes them, they must immediately report it to a superior administrator. Penalties include demotion to the second team.

On January 6, 2011, a hacking tool caused one of the largest-ever cap credential leaks, rather than an individual accidentally exposing theirs. [^61]

#### Types of cap

##### Deletion moderator

Also called a deletion worker; authorized to delete posts within 2channel.

##### Restoration worker

Eligible to apply to become deletion moderators, they advised users on deletion-related boards such as Deletion Organization and Deletion Requests and restored broken thread lists. Improved fault tolerance made their role less relevant, and introduction of guides ended recruitment.

##### Guide

Eligible to apply to become deletion moderators, they advise users on boards such as Deletion Organization and Deletion Requests. Their position resembles restoration workers without restoration powers, although some can inexplicably restore boards. Recruitment occurs periodically.

##### Restoration assistant

In 2004, when restoration powers were given to some fixed-handle users on Operations Information and Restriction Discussion, this designation was given to three who lacked caps. They are outside the deletion supervisor's jurisdiction, and the position does not confer eligibility to apply as a deletion moderator. [^8]

##### Reporter

Authorized to create threads in the News category. Their names end in 'φ★,' combining the star with φ, often used in kaomoji as a hand holding a pen.

##### Otomegumi

An organization established in summer 2004, authorized to stop threads conducting live commentary to prevent server load.

There are also senior operators able to perform various actions freely. For example, 'root▲▲ ★,' as the name suggests, handles server system tuning using administrator privileges.

## Culture

2channel's culture follows earlier underground board [[Ayashii World Hub|Ayashii World]] and its direct predecessor [[Amezou]]. Most participants do not use fixed handles, and Internet slang and pictorial characters are widespread. [^62] [^63] Aggressive language and tasteless posts are also common. [^64]

### Anonymous names

Unlike most web services, it does not require a name, so users more often post anonymously than under fixed names. Amezou was a major influence. Perhaps for this reason, users posting with fixed handles tend to be disliked or added to ignore lists.

### Affinity with the online right

From 1999, around 2channel's founding, neo-Nazi and far-right users attacked liberal sites, and Hiroyuki Seto posted frequently under his real name. Current-affairs boards hosted debates resembling disruptive posting about foreign workers and voting rights for foreign residents. [^65]

In April 2000, 2channel experienced its first major online pile-on. A female Koganei city councilor had called for protest emails to the Tokyo Metropolitan Government over Governor Shintarō Ishihara's 'sangokujin' remarks. Large numbers of defamatory messages targeted her on the Tokyo/Prefectural Governors board, including content suggesting sexual crimes. Her website's board was also disrupted and forced to close. She asked 2channel's operators to delete posts, provoking users and intensifying the attacks. Under the strain, she resigned her municipal committee chairmanship on May 29, but the pile-on continued. [^65][^tn-5ch-sangokujin]

Genealogy of anonymous-board sites originating in Japan. The source calls the site founded by Hiroyuki Nishimura in 1999 '5channel (now 5channel)' [sic]; it became the origin of all other chan sites, including 4chan and 8chan (now 8kun).

5channel also became the origin of overseas anonymous-board culture, **chan culture**, including [[4chan Hub|4chan]] and 8chan. Explaining why this Japanese-origin culture became popular in Japan and the United States, a US news site summarized the background as socioeconomic stagnation since the 1990s and the rise of otaku communities: [^66]

> If the name 2channel sounds vaguely familiar, that may be because it resembles the controversial American imageboard [[4chan Hub|4chan]] and its spiritual successor 8chan, now renamed 8kun. […]
> 
> According to Hiroki Azuma, the rise of otaku communities was a byproduct of Japan's particular political and economic circumstances in the 1990s, when the Internet became widely commercialized. Rapid postwar reconstruction and growth created a fantasy of endless capitalist development. […] This arrogant optimism vanished with the bursting of the bubble, and real wages fell during the resulting severe recession and stagnation of the 'Lost Decade'—or two decades. Young people suffered most, and the lifetime-employment model collapsed. […]
> 
> In 1995, the two tragedies of Aum Shinrikyo's Tokyo subway sarin attack and the devastating Great Hanshin-Awaji Earthquake further depressed national morale. In online spaces occupied by otaku, **snide irony and cynicism** prevailed, and users became absorbed in the communal aspects of boards. […]
> 
> **To fill the void left by the collapse of the narrative of endless progress, they took refuge in spaces built from fiction, Internet memes, and insider jokes.** (The article's annotator identifies this narrative with the **grand narrative** proposed by Jean-François Lyotard in *The Postmodern Condition* in 1979: unified values shared across society, supported by postwar democracy and rapid economic growth.)

The article identifies anti-liberalism as a feature of contemporary Japanese subculture behind 5channel. Military subculture, including weapon-model kits popular from the 1960s, continued through works such as Space Battleship Yamato, Mobile Suit Gundam, and Neon Genesis Evangelion. Partly in reaction against Japan Teachers' Union educators, young people became fascinated with subcultures depicting war through values different from postwar democracy, including Nazi elements. Manga artists such as Yoshinori Kobayashi and Sharin Yamano achieved hits criticizing postwar democracy, bringing subculture closer to the right. [^67] Tsunehira Furuya, once prominent in conservative commentary as an 'anime-otaku mainstream conservative' and later ideologically transformed, said his enthusiasm for alternate-history war fiction led him into conservative commentary. [^68]

Liberals failed to channel these people's energy for social reform. [^69] By contrast, Liberal Democratic Party politician Tarō Asō, although his own otaku credentials were doubtful, successfully associated himself with otaku, including 2channel users, through media strategy in the 2000s. Through the 2010s, the article argues, subculture's rightward shift became established: anime-style illustrations appeared in the nationalist right-wing magazine *Japanism* (Seirindō), and otaku-industry cultural figures expressing conservative opinions on social media became prominent. Even the perception of Akihabara as a right-wing district became conspicuous. [^67] [^70] [^71]

![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4f/Demonstration_on_17_October_2010_at_Akihabara01.jpg/500px-Demonstration_on_17_October_2010_at_Akihabara01.jpg)

During the same period, in the 2010s, the **alt-right**, described as an American version of Japan's online right, rose on the politically incorrect **/pol/** boards of [[4chan Hub|4chan]] and 8chan, offshoots of 5channel. #### Language viruses in the post-truth era, and the turn toward neoreaction

![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4f/2021_storming_of_the_United_States_Capitol_DSC09254-2_%2850820534063%29_%28retouched%29.jpg/500px-2021_storming_of_the_United_States_Capitol_DSC09254-2_%2850820534063%29_%28retouched%29.jpg)

Hyakurō Murasaki, both founder and embodiment of kichiku-kei, took a skeptical stance toward 2channel in his final interview, drawing on William Burroughs' theory of the language virus. [^10]

> I think things typified by 2ch are precisely the bases where Burroughs' 'language virus' actively works with malice. The language virus only needs to make you consume language. There is nothing but exhaustion there, so I cannot get very involved. It feels as though your remaining lifetime is simply being shaved away, doesn't it? If a fool uses the Internet, they just end up being used by it. [^82]

Writer Satoshi Kizawa also quoted Burroughs' motto '**Nothing is true. Everything is permitted**' together with language-virus theory [^10] when discussing the spread of fake news in the post-truth era and the rise of neoreaction = accelerationism = the Dark Enlightenment, [^9] presented as an antithesis to Enlightenment and democracy:

> Neoreaction, accelerationism, and memes are all merely hype. But hype intervenes in the world as hyperstition, changing reality. […] In that case, William Burroughs can be called the king of hype—no, of hyperstition. […] According to Burroughs, language is a virus sent from beyond Earth, parasitizing human hosts and placing them under its control. Of course he sought liberation from it. […] He also recognized that language viruses appropriated through technology could serve as frontline weapons for starting and escalating riots.
> 
> […] '**Nothing is true. Everything is permitted**'—Burroughs' motto resonates with greater significance in the present post-truth age. Could he have foreseen the rise of fake news and memes now proliferating online? We know, of course, that those who seized the hegemony of 'control' were not necessarily fighters for freedom.
> 
> The alt-right now manipulates fake news and spreads ideological memes like viruses. No revolution came; instead, a new reactionary era under Trump arrived. — Satoshi Kizawa, '[The Enemy Called Control: The Lemurs Burroughs Loved](https://s-scrap.com/3033),' Shōbunsha Scrapbook, June 4, 2019.

The article identifies the faltering of liberal democracy through political dysfunction as a background to these developments. [^83] Hostility toward liberals became rejection of liberal values and elites such as established media and academics, contributing in the late 2010s to the rise of xenophobic right-wing populists and the alt-right. [^84] This further accelerated a cynical relativism, also advocated in the kichiku-kei magazine *Abunai 1-gō*, that '**justice, truth, and universal values do not really exist**.' [^85] A social condition in which appeals to personal emotions and beliefs influence public opinion more than objective facts is called **post-truth**. [^86]

German philosopher Markus Gabriel states that viruses include natural viruses and mental viruses—fictions or delusions, or language viruses—and both possess enough reality to affect society. [^87] He sharply criticized post-truth, which denies even obvious truths on the basis of rumors, and its underlying postmodern relativism, as 'not only wrong, but extremely dangerous for democracy,' arguing that relativism's claim that many truths exist merely excuses avoiding facts. [^88] [^85] From a Marxist position, political philosopher Kōhei Saitō similarly dismissed relativism: 'According to it, we cannot understand one another; each of us inhabits a divided world.' He added, 'Relativists invent otherness—cultural and value differences, foreign traditions—to see only what they want to see.' [^85]

On January 6, 2021, alt-right supporters, Trump supporters, and QAnon conspiracy theorists nurtured by Japanese-origin anonymous-board culture (chan culture), including spreaders of election-fraud misinformation such as the Italygate conspiracy theory, attacked and occupied the United States Capitol. ## Cultural context and translator's notes

> [!info] Cultural context and translator's notes
> **Names, domains, and control.** The article distinguishes the original 2ch.net service, the separate 2ch.sc service, and the later 5channel name. Trademark rights, server ownership, domain registration, and operational control are different issues. Statements by parties and their social-media accounts remain attributed to those parties; the translation does not treat every announced legal interpretation as an independently established finding.
>
> **Board vocabulary.** *Ita* means board; *sure* abbreviates thread; *resu* abbreviates response. *Nanashi* means nameless, *kotehan* a fixed handle, and *sage* requests a reply without bumping a thread. “Residents,” “festivals,” and “depopulated boards” are community metaphors. A tripcode marks continuity of posting identity rather than a real-world identity.
>
> **Cynicism and political argument.** *Reishō* here means derisive or detached mockery, translated according to context as cynicism or cynical mockery. *Netto uyoku* identifies Japan's online right; it is not automatically interchangeable with the American alt-right. The source presents arguments connecting these cultures, alongside differing views about liberalism and Hiroyuki. Its quoted claims, polemical language, and generalizations are preserved as the article's historical material.
>
> **Literary metaphor.** Burroughs' “language virus” belongs to the literary and philosophical discussion quoted here. The article's equation of several political-intellectual movements reflects its own framing; the translation retains it rather than introducing a new classification.
>
> **Source perspective and imperfections.** Statistics, access rules, litigation status, and statements described as current retain their stated dates. The source contains conflicting March 2026 domain-event dates and a caption that calls the 1999 service “5channel (now 5channel).” These are flagged rather than silently repaired. English glosses of Japanese publication titles are descriptive, not asserted official editions.
>
> **Related notes:** [[Ayashii World|Ayashii World]] · [[Futaba Channel|Futaba Channel]]

[^tn-5ch-matome]: *Matome* sites compile selected posts into standalone articles, often monetized through advertising. *Kenmō* (嫌儲) signals opposition to profiting from such reposting. The source's quotation/reproduction distinction is its account of the site's rules, not legal advice.
[^tn-5ch-dates]: The source gives March 5, 2026 for domain revocation, March 6 for operation under 5ch.io, and March 9 US time/March 10 Japan time for permanent suspension. Its citation [^57] also records access on March 6. This translation preserves the discrepancy.
