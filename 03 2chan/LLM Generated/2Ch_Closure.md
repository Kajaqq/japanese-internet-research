> **Cultural Context: The 2007 Domain Seizure & Migration**
> 
> **Why was 2channel shutting down?**
> At the time, 2channel was plagued by lawsuits regarding defamation and libel posted by anonymous users. The founder and administrator, Hiroyuki Nishimura (who later also became a director at Nico Nico Douga's parent company, Dwango), famously ignored countless court orders to pay damages or remove offensive posts. In early 2007, the Japanese courts finally moved to forcibly seize the `2ch.net` domain as an asset to pay off these legal debts, threatening to shut the massive site down completely.
> 
> **"Default Anonymous Names" (名無し - Nanashi)**
> 2channel does not use accounts. If you leave the "Name" field blank when posting, the site gives you a default anonymous name depending on the specific board you are posting in. To troll the users and emphasize the impending doom, the 2channel administrators changed the default name on the highly populated VIP Board to a literal countdown timer (*"XX days, XX hours until closure"*). This threw the users into an absolute frenzy.
> 
> **The Mass Migration (移住 - Iju)**
> In 2000s Japanese internet culture, when a major platform went down, users became "refugees" (難民) and would organize massive digital migrations to new websites. Nico Nico Douga had launched its temporary version in December 2006, but it officially launched its Beta version on **January 15, 2007**—the exact week millions of 2channel users were looking for a new home. 
> 
> *Note for Vocaloid Fans:* If you have ever wondered why early Niconico and Vocaloid culture (like early Hatsune Miku memes) was so heavily steeped in cynical, anonymous text-board slang and inside jokes, this event is the exact reason why. 2channel users essentially hijacked the brand-new video site and made its culture their own.
