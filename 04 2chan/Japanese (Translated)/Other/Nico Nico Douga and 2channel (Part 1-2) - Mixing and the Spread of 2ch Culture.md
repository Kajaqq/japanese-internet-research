# Nico Nico Douga and 2channel (Part 1-2): Mixing and the Spread of 2ch Culture

#### Foreword

In [Part 1-1](http://d.hatena.ne.jp/amatanoyo/20071220/1198156827) I explained the (Kari) era in detail; in this entry I'll go through the period from (β) to (γ) in chronological order.[^tn-part1]
As with Part 1, this series is cluttered, because it packs in lots of background knowledge and also sums up the Nico-related articles out there so far. That's by design, of course.[^tn-shiyou] Please send in your objections early on, otherwise they won't be reflected in the next entry I write.
By the way, as for why ~~the middle part~~ Part 1 is split into 1-1 and 1-2...
It's because of [circumstances](http://d.hatena.ne.jp/amatanoyo/20071222/1198310443) along the lines of: "I'll wrap it up in beginning, middle and end!" "The beginning's done, next the middle!" "Huh? Isn't the middle way too long? Wait, what, I need an interlude?!" And so it's been decided that this is a long series (sigh).[^tn-utsu]

#### Synopsis of Part 1-1

Just as the operators planned, Nico Nico Douga (Kari), its environment shaped by 2channelers, moved to (β) and set out as a general web service. But (β) would turn out to be the most turbulent period in Nico Nico Douga's history.

#### Nico Nico Douga tries to break away from 2channel

News comes out that Nico Nico Douga is a service run by Niwango.

- [Niwango beta-tests "Nico Nico Douga", which lets you put comments on videos - Internet Watch](http://internet.watch.impress.co.jp/cda/news/2007/01/15/14460.html)
- [Hiroyuki supervises the video service "Nico Nico Douga" - ITmedia](http://www.itmedia.co.jp/news/articles/0701/16/news092.html)

This spread the understanding that "Nico Nico Douga is not part of 2channel", and it was able to attract the interest of non-2channelers. And since 2006 was the Web 2.0 bubble, it must have been a perfect target for people who love whatever is trendy.[^1]
But these weren't much of an advertisement. Or rather, they were supposed to become one.

#### Once again, it came from 2channel

On 12 January 2007, a piece of news swept the internet: the [2ch closure scare](http://softbank.s231.xrea.com/) caused by the ["seizure of the 2ch.net domain"](http://www.zakzak.co.jp/top/2007_01/t2007011201.html).[^2][^tn-seizure] This uproar didn't stay within 2channel; it drew the interest of a great many internet users.[^3]
And that night, Hiroyuki posted the following on his blog:

> Uh-oh. Uh-oh.
> ![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7Yd0Dx01v_c)

\# What it looked like at the time [survives on the Internet Archive](http://web.archive.org/web/20070218184800/http://hiro.asks.jp/18745.html). The video was on Nico Nico Douga (Kari), but for convenience I've embedded the original YouTube video.

Even though he was a party to the uproar, Hiroyuki used it as material for a gag and showed it off on Nico Nico Douga. This was still three days before (β) started, and it brought in users all at once. Most people would probably say, "When I checked Hiroyuki's blog during the closure scare, there was a link to Nico Nico Douga, and..."

> Just when we'd decided it was about time to advertise properly and make our move, the 2channel seizure uproar happened.
> To ride on it, Hiroyuki uploaded a video of a toy car being seized, and it got so much traffic that it came second worldwide in YouTube's daily rankings. The YouTube comment section was full of complaints from American YouTube users asking why such a damn boring video was number two.
>
> It was the first time Nico Nico Douga made a stir in the wider world.[^tn-toycar]

Then Hiroyuki, thinking ["it's more fun to ride the wave"](http://www.itmedia.co.jp/news/articles/0701/30/news038.html), posted an [emergency interview](http://image.itmedia.co.jp/l/im/news/articles/0701/30/l_yuo_niwango_01.jpg) on Nico Nico Douga on 17 January.
\# The video from then is on Ameba Vision. > [http://vision.ameba.jp/watch.do?movie=118155](http://vision.ameba.jp/watch.do?movie=118155)
\# The video itself was distributed for mobile phones by Niwango. > [http://niwango.jp/mobile/event/hiroyuki/interview.php](http://niwango.jp/mobile/event/hiroyuki/interview.php)
\# For operational reasons, since the maintenance of 1 October 2007, Ameba Vision videos can no longer be posted to Nico Nico Douga, and existing ones show the so-called "deleted video" screen saying the operators have stopped playback, but [the comments and tags still work](http://www.nicovideo.jp/watch?v=am118155).[^tn-ameba]

#### 2channelers and non-2channelers

##### The spread of 2channel culture

And so Nico Nico Douga (β) gained a huge number of users all at once without going through any "proper" advertising by Niwango. But since the (Kari) era, Nico Nico Douga had been a colony of 2channelers ([see Part 1-1](http://d.hatena.ne.jp/amatanoyo/20071220/1198156827)). So ordinary users were required to:

1. Know the in-jokes and jargon.
2. Be prepared for, and resistant to, trolling and abuse.
3. Be anonymous.

Yet these were accepted without a fuss. The reasons are probably these:

1. Things were judged fairly.
2. The service worked on feeling.
3. It was an environment that was easy to blend into.

From (Kari) to (β), there weren't many in-jokes or jargon. Comments flowed past, so even if you wrote something long, nobody could read it. Apt comments were preferred instead.[^6][^tn-kusa] Videos were limited to what was on YouTube, so the material for jokes was limited too. However optimized it was for 2channelers, the environment itself let anyone stay a beginner.
In that respect it was intuitive and easy to use, and since the thing being commented on was a video, you couldn't judge it without watching it. You couldn't casually make fun of things.[^7] Furthermore, since you were placed in an anonymous, impersonal[^8] world, you had no particular need to be considerate of others, which made it easier to blend in.

##### Remarks

Back then (before RC), you could enter a name as well as comments and commands. But perhaps because of the groundwork of 2channel culture, almost nobody entered a name, and when anyone did, it was mostly for trolling.
As an aside, I once saw exactly one person posting under a name. The video I saw it on was VIP Mario, and there were lots of oddly critical comments like "this is a cheat". Sometimes they changed colour, sometimes font size, and they showed up every time a new one was posted. But I no longer remember the name. This was in the (γ) era, by the way.[^tn-vipmario]

It's fair to say that today's light-blue comments are a symbolic remnant of those critical comments. Because of that history, light-blue comments are now posted as the standard colour for criticism, abuse and sarcasm, and have now become a beloved running joke.[^tn-mizuiro]

##### Nico Nico Douga (β) as a buffer zone

After that, it was word of mouth.[^9] Popularity attracted more popularity and it kept growing. Once something is popular, it gets attention and becomes news. Once it's news, it attracts people, and so on, over and over.
That's how Nico Nico Douga gathered ordinary users. Each time ordinary users increased, 2channel culture was accepted and spread that much further, until before long it became normal, and Nico became a buffer zone where 2channel and non-2channel mixed. 2channel-ish things and non-2channel-ish things appeared side by side, and each was judged on its own terms. I think that was probably its most ideal form.

#### The end of (β) and the birth cry of (γ)

##### Access denied by YouTube, and a DDoS attack

On 23 February 2007, the service was suspended because a DDoS attack that had been going on for three days was also affecting Dwango's mobile sites, and around the same time it was also denied access by YouTube.[^10]
By this time Nico Nico Douga had a lot of users. Until then it had taken requests and complaints in the comment section of the operators' blog, but from 24 February it introduced a bulletin board.[^11] Since then, the blog's comment section has been closed.

##### Aside: Nico Nico Douga was already a business

[Paying cash back on 17 February for points in Ameba's uploader ranking](http://blog.nicovideo.jp/2007/02/post_27.php) is probably the first such thing to be made public. [By 13 March it had quadrupled](http://blog.nicovideo.jp/2007/03/2_4.php).

##### Work behind the scenes

The operators announced (γ) right away. It used accounts, and at first it was a closed service that only 100,000 people could use. Accounts began to be issued on 3 March 2007, and the next day they passed 100,000. Plenty of people probably got an account then so they could keep watching Nico, but some must have been optimistic and not got one, thinking "eventually, like (β), we'll be able to watch whether we have an account or not". Well, they ended up teary-eyed later.[^tn-wait]

By the way, here's something trivial about accounts. Did you know that Dwango was recruiting staff on 2channel?

- [Dwango recruits from 2channelers: job ad in a dedicated thread, "a bold experiment" - ITmedia](http://www.itmedia.co.jp/news/articles/0702/19/news045.html)
- [【Full-time staff wanted】 Dwango hiring engineers, non-graduates only 【academic credentials emphasized】 - Programming board (already archived)](http://pc10.2ch.net/test/read.cgi/prog/1171868043)[^tn-hiring]

Apparently accounts (and young ones at that, IDs around the 100s) were handed out at that time, but that really is trivial.

#### (γ) begins quietly

After all, only 100,000 people were allowed in. The servers didn't get very slow, and with few people there were few comments; if anything, it was just the right amount.[^12]
Small numbers, and being shut off from the outside by the account system: what happened in (γ), with those two conditions in place? Let me write about how (γ) became the foundation of Nico Nico Douga from then on.

#### The birth of the Nico Nico Douga user

(Kari) was a part of 2channel, a colony.
(β) was a mixture of 2channel and non-2channel.
(Kari) was built by 2channelers, and (β) was formed by the mixing and harmony of 2channelers and non-2channelers. But there was no such thing as a "Nico Nico Douga user"; it didn't exist.
In (γ), because it was a closed environment, its users, 2channelers or not, were users *inside* Nico Nico Douga, and so they were inevitably described as "Nico Nico Douga users".
Here, for the first time, the "Nico Nico Douga user" was born as an entity in its own right.

##### A very simple analogy

The natives, the 2channelers, built a civilization on a land called Nico Nico Douga. Foreign peoples poured in, but the two didn't fight; they accepted each other and lived together.
But a cataclysm struck Nico Nico Douga, and nobody could live there any more.
The people who rebuilt on the land that remained founded "the Nation of Nico Nico Douga" with only those who had gathered there.
