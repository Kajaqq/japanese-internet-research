# Futaba Channel

**Futaba☆Channel** (Futaba Channel) is a group of electronic bulletin boards in Japan, primarily imageboards. [^7] It is also written **Futaba-chan♪** (双葉ちゃん♪) [^8] or **2chan**, [^9] and abbreviated **Futaba** (ふたば or 双葉). [^9] Originally anonymous, it partially introduced user IDs in 2013. [^10] Its unofficial sister site is **[[4chan Hub|4chan]]** (Yotsuba Channel), the largest group of anonymous boards in the English-speaking world. [^11]

It is among the most active electronic imageboards, supplying the Internet with catchphrases, newly created images, collages, and other joke material. [^7] Although it has existed for a double-digit number of years, as [[#Brief history|described below]], its homepage still calls it a '**newborn bulletin board**.'[^tn-futaba-name]

## Overview

![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/ff/Chan_sites_family_tree.png/500px-Chan_sites_family_tree.png)

Genealogy of anonymous thread-bumping boards originating in Japan. Futaba's format became the prototype for 4chan, 8chan, and all other chan sites.

It is a collection of electronic bulletin boards/Web BBSs, classified as a general-purpose BBS or 'mega BBS' with boards divided into various categories, but it centers particularly on imageboards represented by Nijiura. [^7]

All boards except the uploaders described below use a thread-bumping structure. In addition to conventional text-based BBSs, the collection includes 'imageboards' accepting images, 'uploaders' for files other than images or text, and drawing boards where users draw directly in the browser and post their work. Activity is greatest on imageboards organized by category.

The imageboards use a modified script based on ' imgboard,' imageboard software with replies developed by the [1998 Tokuhō Club CGI Download Center](https://www.big.or.jp/~talk/t-club/soft/index.html). Since it is released as open-source software, it is now widely used on imageboards beyond Futaba. [^12]

The domain owner was formerly Marionette Corporation. (As of December 2019, the marionette.jp domain had passed into other hands.)

A [[2channel Hub|2channel]] offshoot, it was established as a refuge during the threatened shutdown of 2channel (the August Crisis), when that site faced bandwidth limits. [^13] Subsequently, **[[4chan Hub|4chan]]** was established in October 2003 as an unofficial sister site using Futaba☆Channel's source code. [^14] [^11]

## Principal boards

### Nijigen Ura

Officially the 'Nijigen Ura @Futaba' board, commonly 'Nijiura' (二次裏 / 虹裏). One of Futaba☆Channel's imageboards, its activity and achievements made the wider world aware of Futaba, and it is the site's representative board in both name and substance. [^7] People sometimes use 'Futaba' to mean Nijiura specifically. Since its creation, its lively activity has generated numerous cultural practices and catchphrases, making it a symbol of Futaba☆Channel.[^tn-futaba-niji]

It began around March 2002 as a board for isolating disruptive users. The 'Nijigen Gazō Keijiban @Futaba' board ('Nijiomote'), originally intended for two-dimensional images such as drawings, had been disrupted by an influx of users posting outside its purpose. Moving them to a separate board was an improvised response that created a buffer zone.

Originally a single board, it underwent additions and mergers and now comprises five boards bearing the name Nijigen Ura @Futaba: img, dat, may, jun, and dec. (The cgi board [^1] and Mobile Nijigen Ura board [^2] also formerly existed.) Nijigen ID, Nijigen Neta, Test jun, and dec Test are often discussed as Nijiura boards despite lacking the name. The may, nov, jun, and dec servers are said to be named after the English names of their opening months. [^3] May has the most residents, followed by img.

Although all share the name 'Nijigen Ura,' differences in founding dates, systems, administration, and user numbers have produced cultural differences between servers. Besides differences in posted images and tendencies, some servers have their own mascots or original characters. The may server is often used to test features and is called a 'testing ground' by some residents.

Its most distinctive feature is that threads are deleted after a certain period and not retained. (The source adds a parenthetical remark that logs elsewhere are not deleted.) Each board has a retention limit, such as 6,000 or 10,000 replies; once posts exceed that limit, old threads are automatically deleted in order. This makes compilation sites and compilation blogs difficult to sustain, limiting the formation of surrounding Internet culture and encouraging an emphasis on the interactive excitement of people actually present. It also contributes to a distinctive insular atmosphere that excludes those not directly involved. External 'archives' of logs, images, and text about major topics or popular jokes have become common in recent years, making developments easier to follow, but its distinctive culture remains extremely difficult for nonresidents to understand.

Despite the 'two-dimensional' name, images are not restricted to a particular genre: anything amusing or well received is acceptable. The main users' interests lead to frequent posting of screenshots from existing media, magazine scans, and character collages. Activity continues around the clock. Regular mini-events include '[[#Festivals|festivals]]' involving large quantities of images or discussion on one subject, and posting the same image or starting the same topic at a set time ('scheduled time' or 'scheduled posting'). Because of the volume, images disappear within tens of minutes or a few hours, as noted above. General chat also takes place on these boards because they have the most viewers and the largest reply-retention limits.

### Drawing boards

Futaba☆Channel has several oekaki drawing boards. Their residents differ from those of Nijiura, often treated as synonymous with Futaba, and have developed a distinctive culture. Many users overlap, of course, and exchange and circulation of joke material are active. Many contributors tend to choose a particular territory. Except on OeSQL, consecutive contributions by the same artist tend to be disliked, and the current rough convention is one picture per day. Some artists therefore post across several boards.

#### Oekaki

Judging by its name and position in the list, this should be the principal drawing board, but many users avoid its abundance of 'trash drawings'—doodles in the literal sense, on the level of bathroom graffiti—and it has become peripheral. Skilled artists also reside there, however, producing an odd atmosphere: in a phrase, a mixture of gems and stones. It moves slowest among the drawing boards; without unusual activity, drawings remain for over a week.

#### OeSQL

A test board for a script using MySQL to manage threads. Its proportion of fixed-handle users is higher than on other drawing boards. Fast-moving and the most active, its motto is 'Just draw; drawing is fun.' Drawing something matters more than its quality or humor. Consecutive image posts are permitted, and posting festivals by a single person occur frequently.

There are 'SQL Iron Rules,' but they are not absolute:

- Basically combative, occasionally friendly. After a fight, smile and join hands.
- Basically use a fixed handle. Accept any troublemaker from other boards. Do not draw genitals.

#### Rakugaki (Doodles)

This has the largest number of contributors and reply-only participants among Futaba☆Channel's drawing boards. Contrary to its name, submitted works are of high quality. Its popularity has also lowered the overall user standard: annoying replies such as 'Where's your archive?' appear in almost every thread. Many artists therefore put their website/archive address in the email field, although such replies often appear regardless. 'Festivals' in which multiple artists draw on the same theme are frequent, and normal activity is second only to OeSQL.

#### Rakugaki Ura

As the adult connotations of a 'reverse side' to Rakugaki suggest, this board contains many drawings unsuitable for under-18s. Its relatively leisurely, calm pace may explain why it has more series and serial works than Rakugaki. Occasionally, however, a festival causes a surge of posts and rapidly displaces logs. An uncensored 'Shiokara version' is often posted alongside a self-censored image.

In early May 2008, the administrator removed disruptive drawings en masse and restricted some IP addresses. Some artists appear to have been caught in the restrictions, but relative peace seems to have returned. Rumor attributes the action to appeals made by some artists to people involved at 'Futaba Gakuensai,' but this remains unconfirmed.

### Military

Primarily for military images, but its scope extends to related subjects ranging from rations to police, firefighting, the Japan Coast Guard (coast guards), and disaster preparedness. It formerly also covered politics and world affairs. Many users also frequent the Railway board and the external site 101 Freeway. It has particularly friendly relations with Railway and sometimes cooperates against disruptive users. Unlike other boards, it expects a certain manzai comedy sensibility: users post an image with a deliberately foolish comment, and others reply with a corrective retort. Historically valuable photographs of the former Japanese military are sometimes reproduced from albums of family members who served.

### Military Ura

Newly established on November 28, 2008. Unlike Military, it did not display IP addresses. After IP display and the del feature were added to Military, off-topic and two-dimensional joke material almost disappeared there, whereas Military Ura tended to tolerate them. (It has since been changed to display IP addresses.)

### Politics

For political subjects. Site policy specifies throughout Futaba that 'politics belongs on the Politics board,' so political discussions are directed here.

### Insects

Covers not only insects but 'insects, arthropods, fish, amphibians, reptiles, mollusks, aquatic organisms, microorganisms, and other mysterious creatures.' Keeping animals and pest control are also active topics. Since the subjects are real organisms, residents frequently upload photographs. Many have specialist knowledge, and photographs of unidentified bugs are quickly identified.

### Supigura

A promotional board for the anime Speed Grapher. Initially it had a background image. Although intended to promote and discuss the series, it quickly acquired Nijiura's atmosphere, with unrelated subjects forced into discussion under labels such as 'X is Speed Grapher.' Apparently intended as a temporary board tied to the broadcast, it inexplicably remained after the series ended. It now resembles the former Television board, with television captures and gravure photographs.

## Former boards

### Three-Dimensional

A board for gravure-idol and nude images.

### Girls

Disappeared around 2004. A board for photographs of girls rather than drawn characters. Near its end, posted images were only 5 KB, most posts came from adult sites, and activity declined. The article describes online enforcement at the time as lax, with pre-restriction child sexual abuse images largely tolerated and the board flourishing.

### Television

Deleted on February 1, 2007. It originated by splitting from the 'Sanjigen Neta' board. It was one of Futaba☆Channel's boards without a clearly defined community. Television captures and images of female idols predominated, but images and topics were essentially unrestricted by genre. Disruption was rampant, with some users disliking it and others enjoying or tolerating it. Frequent server outages were another characteristic. Many users feared that the constant presence of obscene and copyright-infringing images endangered the board and even Futaba☆Channel itself. Until deletion, administrators intervened rarely, leaving the situation to develop into a distinctive culture.

### Nijigen Live Commentary

Disappeared on May 7, 2014. Its format of posting while watching individual television programs placed heavy loads on the server. It apparently was deleted after people mechanically posting large quantities of television screenshots, and people requesting them, escalated their demands until administrators lost patience.

### Shopping

A casual discussion board for Gothic Lolita fashion. After closure, users moved to various refuge sites.

## Terminology

### Residents

Although Futaba☆Channel contains many boards besides Nijiura, 'Futaba residents' usually means Nijiura residents. Its origins as a refuge during the 2channel crisis meant that early residents reflected that site's user base. After developing its own culture and undergoing several splits and mergers, its users came to consist mainly of university students and working adults. Younger users, including middle- and high-school students, are said to have been drawn in and increased their share.

It continues to appeal to people with varied interests. As commonly happens in longstanding communities, however, differences in values and generational attitudes cause friction. Division across multiple servers also means Nijiura has no unified consensus or outlook. Attempts to discuss unification are rare, and moves to establish a consensus instead meet strong resistance.

### Administrator

![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c0/2chan_administrator.png/500px-2chan_administrator.png)

An imagined appearance of Futaba☆Channel's administrator, affectionately called 'ｸﾝﾘﾆﾝｻﾝ' (Kunrinin-san) by users.

Futaba☆Channel's administrator has no particular nickname or fixed handle and has never appeared publicly in an official capacity as its administrator. [^4] Their gender is unknown. They rarely respond to residents' posts and seldom sought user opinions about site policy. Earlier management decisions were notably whimsical, often amusing or confusing residents. Administrative posts display the name '[Administrator]' in red.

With no publicly recognized name, residents simply called the administrator 'Kanrinin-san' or 'ｸﾝﾘﾆﾝｻﾝ.' Their low profile was unusual among anonymous-board administrators, who often clash with users over policies, opinions, and posts. Since they never showed their face, outside rumors about their behavior were also rare. This inspired jokes portraying the administrator as a beautiful woman, a beautiful girl, a dog, an organization, or even a nonexistent person represented by a program, producing many collages and illustrations. Administrative posts were once treated as rare events: a single post prompted threads across Nijiura boards and excited observation. The photographs and character images posted as portraits of the administrator are modeled on Tokyo Castella, a legendary Internet idol active in the Internet's early days.

From late 2007 through around 2008, the administrator became more active than before, introducing affiliate advertising, adding the del feature, opening and closing servers and boards, restricting Internet providers, and deleting criticism of management. Residents consequently speculated that the administrator had changed or management policy had shifted considerably. The administrator responded, 'I have been the administrator from the beginning; that has not changed.' Still unidentified, they are now less often addressed with the honorific '-san,' and the earlier excited observation has also disappeared.

### Festivals

A large number of residents posting many pictures of a particular character, collages, or scripts in the sense of written scenarios. It can also mean quoting a particular reply and starting threads along a shared theme. For example, after quoting 'Is the current trend X?', users upload images associated with X. Threads on that subject naturally proliferate, producing an 'X festival.'

Nijiura tends to dislike unembellished personal stories and chat. By guiding chat toward jokes, particular obsessions, or self-mockery, residents search for subjects while affirming festivals that involve the whole board. Simply approving consecutive posts would invite exploitation by disruptive users; deliberately including character-based jokes also distinguishes festivals from disruptive flooding.

### Toshiaki

'Toshiaki' was formerly the default posting name—the name used when a poster leaves the name field blank—on the Nijigen Ura board.

Its original default was 'Nanashi-san' ('Anonymous'). One day a person calling himself Toshiaki repeatedly disrupted the board, including demands for uploads of dōjinshi such as *Kaiten Sommelier 15 Kaiten*. Since the board was anonymous, other residents adopted the handle Toshiaki to join the disruption or mock the original poster. At one point Toshiakis multiplied beyond control, causing turmoil.

In a playful response, Futaba☆Channel's administrator adopted 'Toshiaki' as Nijiura's default name. This established the equivalence 'Nijiura resident = Toshiaki.' Terms such as 'sureaki' for the person starting a new thread and 'joshiaki' for a female poster emerged spontaneously and soon became established.

Having acquired a name, the Toshiakis developed an identity, strengthening their solidarity and sense of belonging. This energized activity but also produced undesirable consequences, including irresponsible behavior behind a shared mask, making the name widely known online for both good and ill.

As Nijiura expanded, the Toshiakis of the board where the name originated became increasingly aggressive, disrupting their own community and showing signs of harming others. Perhaps to restrain this, the administrator removed the default name Toshiaki and the default subject 'Munen' ('Regret') on July 15, 2004. The solidarity of the Toshiaki era was inseparable from activities resembling disruption; residents who remember it recall a nostalgic yet embarrassing period with mixed approval and disapproval.

After losing their name, Nijiura residents began referring to posters as '「」'—a pair of Japanese quotation marks indicating namelessness or silence. This became established and continued for nearly ten months, but on May 27, 2005, Toshiaki returned on the may server. The subsequently opened nov and jun servers also adopted it as their default name. Dat and img continued using 「」. Some boards said to have split from early Nijiura—including Eroge, Yaoi, and Refuge—had retained Toshiaki as their default throughout the 「」 period.

Other variants include 'Yuriaki,' the default name on the Yuri board for girls' love images, where all conversation uses refined young-lady speech, and 'Shimonkin,' which suddenly became dat's default name for about one day on February 5, 2005.

### The del feature

A feature added to Futaba☆Channel boards beginning September 27, 2008, for reporting prohibited images and requesting deletion. A 'del' hyperlink appears at the upper right of threads and replies; clicking opens a deletion-request form. Reasons are selected using radio buttons and include obscene images, child sexual abuse material, personal information, defamation, threats, consecutive posting, and harassment.

Using reports from the form, the administrator or deletion moderators decide whether to delete threads, replies, or images and impose access bans. In cases judged especially malicious, they may publish the poster's IP address in the reported thread or reply; this is called 'red text' because it appears in red. According to administrative comments, records also appear to be kept of people submitting del requests.

In July 2013, certain boards without IDs gained a feature that forcibly displays an ID when deletion requests pass a threshold.

### Thread isolation

On some boards, once a thread receives a certain number of del reports, it disappears from the normal catalog and is 'isolated.' It can then be displayed only by entering its address directly or through the History tab for residents who posted before isolation. This helps prevent more people gathering in an already disrupted or contentious thread and escalating the conflict. Volunteer investigations suggest the threshold is fewer than 1% of residents.

Threads once moved to a special isolation board where they could be viewed, but that board no longer exists. Consequently, as described above, an isolated thread cannot be viewed without knowing its URL.

### Deletion moderators

People apparently introduced around the same time as del, who delete images, threads, and replies on the administrator's behalf. When they delete a reply, its email field and body change to the single word 'nā' (なー).

The administrator said there were seven or eight, but no subsequent announcement clarified their selection criteria, deletion standards, exact powers, or even whether they really exist.

### Mobile Futaba

A viewing and posting section for mobile-phone users. Except for its introduction page and main menu, it is accessible only from phones. Deletion keys, del, and ID/IP viewing are not currently available, and some boards are unsupported. Users can post through dedicated forms or email. Forms have only name, email address, and body fields, so they cannot specify a subject or deletion key or attach images. Email allows image attachments and subjects, but not a posting name or email-address field. Replies by email are sent to **board name and thread number@server name.2chan.net**; for example, abc.2chan.net/xyz/res/777.htm corresponds to xyz0000000777@abc.2chan.net. New threads can be created by emailing boardname0000000000@[server name].2chan.net.

## Issues

### Relationship with 2channel

Because it was established as a refuge for [[2channel Hub|2channel]], its earliest board organization followed that site and users tended to reflect its population. Slang, ASCII art, and joke material were frequently exchanged.

Marionette Corporation, which held the domain, also acted as an advertising agency for 2channel and Gazō Channel, but this only meant they shared an agency and did not establish a connection. See [Advertising Space Information♪](https://web.archive.org/web/20070202204446/http://www.2chan.net/ad/ad.html).

### The Netrunner dispute

The magazine *Netrunner* inevitably enters discussions of Futaba. It published methods for using Futaba efficiently and content using Nijiura-created characters, but its methods were highly aggressive. It promoted Futaba-originated material as its own, sometimes effectively disregarded copyright, and affected Futaba's administration. Opposition to Netrunner consequently became the normal stance on and around Futaba, with anti-Netrunner banners frequently visible. This had little effect on its behavior, however. Suggested reasons include the difficulty of asserting rights over works created in a practically near-copyright-free manner and Futaba's own relaxed attitude to copyright. [^5]

### Collages and character alteration

Perhaps because its image-centered system makes visual communication easier than on text-centered boards, existing characters are very often reinterpreted through collage and similar techniques. Nijiura characters are almost entirely different from their originals except for visual resemblance; to varying degrees, they occupy an intermediate position between derivative work and original creation.

The resulting distinctive Futaba culture is incomprehensible to nonusers, but people naturally become familiar with it through participation. Original characters have also emerged outside Nijiura. The site's Rakugaki board hosts serial comics including 'Doraemo,' a parody of Doraemon, 'Mushiatsui kara Nugu' ('It Is Muggy, So I Am Taking My Clothes Off'), and 'Japa-Hora.,' based on characters from Japanese horror films.

### Etiquette problems

As with large boards such as [[2channel Hub|2channel]] and The BBS, participants' manners worsened as Futaba☆Channel's user base grew. In Futaba, where interaction tends to revolve around an insider atmosphere, disruption stands out. Residents' attempts to repel it provoke further disruption, creating a vicious circle and frequent threads so chaotic that conversation becomes impossible.

Examples include repeatedly posting images or messages wholly unrelated to a board's purpose; posting repellent images and persistently bumping them; exposing and attacking particular individuals, including residents; using special software to flood the server with the same image until it crashes; sexism; persistent hostile threads about insider jokes; and exploiting anonymity to stage conflicts through sockpuppetry. These are particularly common on some boards without displayed IDs. Some communities have been reduced to empty shells by such behavior.

Although more active than at one time, the administrator remains notably reluctant among major-board administrators to address disruption. One could call this tolerant or irresponsible; some servers are entirely unattended. Since del was introduced, self-governance has advanced according to each board's culture, but disagreements over self-governance and administrative policy have led to conflicts and access bans. Criticism appears in various places, including 'Why punish us for discussing the issue rather than the people disrupting things?' As noted above, the administrator rarely responds.

## Influence overseas

See also '[[#Major boards derived from 4chan|§ Major Boards Derived from 4chan]].'

Comparable imageboards, particularly ones like Nijiura, had not previously existed overseas and made an impression on foreigners interested in Japanese culture. This prompted the creation of [[4chan Hub|4chan]] (*yotsuba-channel*), a similar collection of imageboards. [^15] Its administrator calls it Futaba's unofficial sister site, a one-sided designation not made by Futaba. It reused Futaba's imageboard script and was designed mainly for English-speaking users. [^6]

Besides [[4chan Hub|4chan]], there was krautchan, a similar imageboard collection originating in Germany. Its international board had active exchanges among users from Eastern Europe, Northern Europe, and the Russian-speaking world, using mainly English. Other examples include Russian-language IIchan and Taiwan's Komica.

Futaba☆Channel itself allows overseas visitors to read but not post. Some overseas users obtain information about Japanese otaku culture through Futaba.

## Brief history

### 2001

- August 30: Futaba☆Channel established as a refuge during the threatened shutdown of [[2channel Hub|2channel]], which faced bandwidth limits. It is no longer treated as a refuge.

### 2002

- March: Activity on the Two-Dimensional Images board (Nijiomote) increased, with repeated posting of captures, manga, and dōjinshi. Nijigen Ura (Nijiura) was separated from it.
- March 14: A Gore Images board was established because disruptive users were posting grotesque images on imageboards for amusement.
- March 16: Frequent disputes over two-dimensional and photographic images led to the separation of the Two-Dimensional Images and Three-Dimensional Images boards.

### 2003

- January: Early Nijiura jokes such as Heika and Wahā flourished.
- April 12: Toshiaki, a name used by a disruptive user, became Nijiura's default posting name.
- June: *Netrunner* was found to have used without permission the idea and project for 'Pakkuriman Stickers,' previously exchanged on Nijiura, as a magazine supplement. This became the origin of the later dispute with Netrunner.
- August 6: Me-tan was first posted.

### 2004

- April 18: A Trouble Windows Flash movie was released and attracted attention within and beyond Futaba☆Channel for its rich content and quality.
- April 21: Medoi-san created.
- July 15: A specification change removed Toshiaki as Nijiura's default posting name.

### 2005

- January 3: Nijiura split into img and dat; the homepage linked to dat.
- May 27: A new server, may, was introduced, and the homepage's Nijiura link changed to may.
  - Toshiaki returned as the default posting name on the new may Nijiura board.
- August 9: The URL of Nijiura may changed after heavy loads caused by a version of the Kintama virus, a Trojan horse targeting Winny users, that had appeared the previous day, August 8, and targeted the board.
- November 10: Image-collection tools heavily loaded Nijiura may, prompting creation of Nijiura nov; may remained. A Nijigen Live Commentary Ura board also opened. Initially named Sanjigen Live Commentary, it was renamed within a day.

### 2006

- June: The Nijiura jun server was introduced. As before, adding a server did not remove existing servers. By the end of June, two Nijiura boards were linked from the homepage and five called themselves Nijiura: img, dat, may, nov, and jun.
- July 8: Nijiura was hacked, and everyone's hostname appeared in red.
- December 6: The img board moved from the img server to nov. The img server thereafter operated only text boards, leaving img as a board address and informal name. Four servers—dat, may, nov, jun—now hosted five Nijiura boards: img, dat, may, nov, jun.
- December 11: The Bara board closed, and the Yaoi board became inaccessible.

### 2007

- February 1: Television closed.
- Around April 11: Preparation Test, Lounge, and other boards that accepted deletion and access-ban requests disappeared. Administration itself nevertheless appeared to continue.
- Around July 8: Most text boards disappeared, apparently because declining activity and disruption had reduced user numbers. Half-Width, Anime, and others remained.

### 2008

- September 27: The del feature was added.
- Around October: Politics @Futaba, Economics @Futaba, and Korean Economy @Futaba opened. These subjects, including current news, were to move there from existing boards.
- October 15: Military introduced IP display.
- November 28: Military temporarily closed, reopening the next day. Military Ura @Futaba opened the same day.
- Around November: Touhou @Futaba opened on may, without ID or IP display.
- December 10: Sanjigen Live Commentary @Futaba opened. A new server, dec, was introduced, hosting Nijigen Ura @Futaba dec. An administrative announcement said dec could withstand heavier loads than existing servers.

### 2009

- Date unknown: 'Deletion through accumulated del reports' became functional on Nijiura may. After a specified number of deletion requests, red text appeared immediately below a thread's initial post or above a reply body: 'There are deletion requests for this thread (reply).' Since implementation, posts could be deleted after sufficient reports. Although report accumulation leading to deletion was conspicuous on the first day, the number of accumulated requests was no longer displayed in subsequent days.
- December 15: Threads about the Democratic Party, politics, and ethnicity were deleted en masse on Nijiura may, with access bans. This was reportedly a response to the continual proliferation of political threads. Some dissatisfied users reacted aggressively by creating still more, including 'political threads disguised with anime/game images' and threads asking how many seconds it took to be deleted or banned after posting 'Ozawa.' A Politics board already existed, however, and Futaba had never declared that it guaranteed freedom of speech, freedom of expression, or neutrality. Notices such as 'Politics belongs on Politics' and 'No politics' were later added to Nijiura boards, clarifying the rule.

### 2010

- January 21: Nijiura may experimentally gave thread starters ('sureaki') permission to delete replies in their threads, even if the reply's author had set a password. The exact reason was unknown. Since users could now delete replies opposing their views, some threads prominently displayed 'This reply was deleted by the person who started the thread.'
- January 22: Nijiura may tested ID display triggered by particular text in the email field. If a thread starter entered it, all subsequent replies displayed IDs. Some interpreted it as prevention of sockpuppetry or disruption, but the exact reason was unknown. Together, these features allowed users to delete disliked replies and omit IDs to conceal sockpuppetry, dividing opinion between supporters and opponents.
- February 5: Someone exposed in a Nijiura jun thread that anyone using Internet Explorer could delete threads and replies. The flaw was immediately exploited, resulting in mass deletions across Nijiura except dat and on Touhou. Damage was particularly severe on jun and Touhou.
- February 6: Only catalog images suddenly disappeared on Nijiura may. Disruptive users were exploiting an administrative bug allowing people other than thread starters to delete threads using a particular procedure. Similar disruption occurred on nov.
- March 26: Nijiura may began testing a handwriting feature that let users draw in the normal posting field. Its catalog immediately filled with threads containing handwritten images.
- April 8: A voting thread on Preparation @Futaba asked whether the long-debated political threads should exist, but administrators deleted it. 'Politics, religion, ethnicity' was added to del reasons that day. Deletion requests posted on Preparation also became subject to deletion.
- April 14: Nijiura nov's retention limit changed from 5,000 to 1,000. No administrative announcement explained it, but it was considered an anti-disruption measure. Perhaps in response to residents' appeals, it was relaxed to 3,000 on April 16.
- May 12: Infectious Diseases @Futaba appeared in the menu. An outbreak of foot-and-mouth disease was occurring, and its content overlapped Politics, but the reason for making it independent was unknown.
- May 26: Politics Live Commentary @Futaba was renamed Sanjigen Live Commentary @Futaba. Many television commentary threads moved from Nijigen Ura @Futaba dec, previously used for live commentary.
- July 7: Links to 'Terms' appeared on Nijiura boards. Their content matched del reasons. This was Futaba☆Channel's first explicit written set of terms. Later, the link was moved to the homepage only.
- July 31: Nijigen Gore Ura @Futaba opened. Some threads moved from may, jun, and elsewhere.
- August 18: The nov server of Nijigen Ura @Futaba was discontinued without a public explanation. Nov's scheduled threads dispersed across Nijiura, mainly to dec.
- August 28: Infectious Diseases @Futaba became Aquarium @Futaba, and Advice @Futaba became General Chat @Futaba, treated as new boards. Reply numbers continued without resetting.
- December 13: Nijiura catalogs gained a history feature showing threads in which the user had posted.

### 2011

- January 15: Following residents' discussions and proposals, the front and reverse Nijigen Live Commentary boards merged. The front board remained and the reverse board was discontinued.
- March 15: Following the Great East Japan Earthquake, a corresponding board opened with a retention limit of 10,000.
- March 16: Following the Fukushima Daiichi nuclear accident, Nuclear Power @Futaba opened with a retention limit of 10,000.
- June 16: Thread isolation was introduced on Nijigen Ura @Futaba img. After suspension and reintroduction, it was ultimately abandoned.
- August 26: Nijigen Ura @Futaba img's reply-retention limit increased to 8,000.
- September 2: At residents' request for self-governance, Original Artwork Ura @Futaba began always displaying posters' IP addresses.
- Around October: Touhou @Futaba began displaying IDs.

### 2012

- March 16: The Senkaku Islands dispute prompted a proliferation of political threads on Nijiura may. 'No politics' was added to the usage rules of all Nijiura boards.

### 2013

- February 4: At residents' request, Touhou @Futaba switched from IDs to IP display, and Touhou Ura @Futaba opened on dec, allowing thread starters to choose ID/IP display.
- June 4: At Nijiura may residents' request, the maximum image size rose from 500 KB to 8,000 KB, but the server load immediately forced a reduction to 2,000 KB. After several further changes, all boards settled at 2,000 KB.
- June 26: Social Games @Futaba opened on dec, with a retention limit of 6,000.
- June 29–July 7: Del and ID-display specifications changed several times. Ultimately, IDs were shown after enough del reports, and 'wrong board' was removed from reasons on Nijiura deletion-request forms.

### 2014

- April 17: The Korean Ferry Sinking board opened following the Sewol sinking.
- April 18: Nijigen Ura No Reproduction and Nijigen Ura Reproduction Permitted opened, reportedly addressing unauthorized copying by affiliate blogs.
- April 29: Nijigen Live Commentary restricted consecutive image posts, reduced retention from 40,000 to 20,000, enforced 'IPHONE' display [as written in the source], suspended del, and changed the default name from Toshiaki to Anonymous.
- May 7: Nijigen Live Commentary closed. Its homepage link was removed, but the URL was still valid that day.
- July 10: KanColle opened with a retention limit of 10,000.

### 2015

- December 6: Touken Ranbu and Fortune-Telling opened.
- December 21: Fashion, Travel, and Parenting opened.
- December 29: Webm opened.

### 2016

- February 23: Moai opened.
- July 15: New Board Proposals opened.
- August 27–September 3: Intermittent DDoS attacks made all boards inaccessible.
- August 30: Refuge opened to respond to these attacks, but proved ineffective and became inaccessible under attack like the other boards.
- October 5: WebM attachments, previously available only on Webm, became possible on some boards including Nijiura.
- October 25: A dice feature was implemented on some boards including Nijiura.
- November 12: HTTPS support added.

## Cultural context and translator's notes

> [!info] Cultural context and translator's notes
> **Shared names and local identities.** Toshiaki is a collective default identity, not the real name of everyone posting. Empty brackets 「」 likewise function as a community name. The article distinguishes individual servers because their conventions and cultures differ.
>
> **Ephemeral participation.** Automatic thread deletion favors people present at the time. “Festivals” are coordinated waves of jokes or images; “archives” and reproduction disputes concern attempts to preserve or redistribute that ephemeral material.
>
> **Creative vocabulary.** *Oekaki* means drawing, *rakugaki* means doodles, and *ura* means the reverse or back side. The article uses “two-dimensional” mainly for drawn/anime characters, contrasting them with photographs of real people. Dōjinshi are independently produced publications; the article's discussion of permissive fan practice does not establish that works lack copyright.
>
> **Tone and historical perspective.** The source sometimes uses harsh judgments about users and rival sites. Its uncertainty, changing technical arrangements, and period-specific “current” statements are retained as part of the captured article. The unusual “IPHONE display” entry is reproduced as written rather than silently corrected. No present-day access or moderation policy is asserted by this translation.
>
> **Related notes:** [[Ayashii World|Ayashii World]] · [[5channel|5channel]]

[^tn-futaba-name]: *Futaba* means a pair of young leaves, so “newborn bulletin board” plays on the site's seedling name. *Yotsuba*, used for 4chan, means four leaves.
[^tn-futaba-niji]: *Nijigen* means “two-dimensional”; *ura* means “back” or “reverse.” The abbreviation *Nijiura* also appears as 虹裏, replacing 二次 with the identically pronounced word 虹 (“rainbow”). These are community labels, not separate literal descriptions of every posted image.
