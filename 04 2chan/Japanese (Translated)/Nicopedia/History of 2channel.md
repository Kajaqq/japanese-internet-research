“History of 2channel” is an article about exactly what its title says.

## Overview

This lists controversies and events on 2channel—including *matsuri*, news, and the founding dates of boards with Niconico Pedia articles—alongside their connections with Niconico, including the encyclopedia and Dwango. Events after the site's renaming to 5channel are also included.[^tn-history-matsuri]

> [!info] Cultural context and translator's notes
> This is a community-written chronology with an explicitly Niconico-centered third column, not a neutral or comprehensive history. Its accusations, uncertain explanations, jokes, and period-specific judgments are retained. Dates and figures are those of the captured source; events described as “ongoing” or as records “as of May 2012” have not been updated. Repeated blank table headings have been normalized into one header per table, without dropping chronology rows or their contents. Copypasta blogs are blogs republishing board posts; the article's conflicts concern their relationship with users, advertising, and reproduction permission. “VIPPER” means a VIP-board user, while ν速 is a stylized abbreviation for News Flash. Unavailable embedded community content remains identified rather than reconstructed.

[^tn-history-matsuri]: 祭り (*matsuri*) literally means “festival.” Here it labels a burst of collective online activity: a joke campaign, voting raid, scandal discussion, or mass reaction to serious news. It does not itself imply a happy occasion; the source explicitly questions the word's appropriateness for the Fukushima accident.
[^tn-history-dates]: The chronology contains apparent date inconsistencies, retained rather than silently repaired. For example, it dates 1ch.tv's launch October 15, whereas the vault's separate 1ch.tv account gives October 5; it lists the Sasebo killing under June 2; and places Chōsuke Ikariya's death in its 2005 table. The Ōtsu entry similarly conflates the incident and the later controversy. These entries should be treated as source claims, not independently verified dates.
[^tn-history-taste]: “YES Lolita, NO Touch” is the source's tasteless refrain in entries involving sexual misconduct toward minors. It is preserved as evidence of the article's register, not an endorsement or description of lawful conduct.
