Nico Nico Douga (NND), 2channel (2ch), and Futaba Channel (often called 2chan) essentially form the **"Holy Trinity" of Japanese 2000s otaku internet culture**. 

While they are separate websites, their user bases, histories, and meme cultures are so deeply intertwined that it is impossible to talk about the history of one without the others. If you want to understand how a meme, a Vocaloid character, or an internet trend was born in Japan during that era, it almost always traveled through these three sites.

Here is the breakdown of their relationship:

### 1. 2channel (2ch) — The "Father" (The Text Foundation)
2channel (now 5channel) was Japan’s largest anonymous text-board. As you saw in the translated articles, NND was essentially built *by* and *for* 2channers. 

*   **The Founder Link:** 2channel was founded by Hiroyuki Nishimura. He later joined Dwango (NND's parent company) and was a core director in building Nico Nico Douga. 
*   **The Culture Transfer:** In early 2007, when 2channel was at risk of being shut down by the government, millions of 2ch users (specifically from the VIP and News boards) fled to the newly opened NND. 
*   **The UI Design:** The entire concept of NND—having comments scroll horizontally across the video—was designed specifically to mimic the chaotic, rapid-fire feeling of an anonymous 2channel text thread. NND inherited all of 2ch’s cynical, text-based slang (like using "www" for grass/laughter).
*   **The Rivalry:** As NND became mainstream and younger kids ("Nico-chus") started using 2ch slang without knowing the unwritten rules, a massive rivalry formed. Older 2ch users (VIPPERs) grew to resent NND users, despite the fact that they created NND's culture in the first place.

### 2. Futaba Channel (2chan) — The "Visual Cousin"
Futaba Channel is an anonymous Japanese **imageboard**. (Fun fact: Christopher "moot" Poole created 4chan in 2003 specifically as an English clone of Futaba Channel, right down to the site layout). 

Ironically, Futaba Channel was *also* created as an emergency refuge for 2channel users during a major server crash in 2001. However, because Futaba allowed users to upload images, it developed a completely different, highly visual culture.

*   **The Visual Foundation of NND:** If 2ch gave NND its *text slang*, Futaba gave NND its *visual memes*. Futaba was heavily populated by artists, Photoshop ("kora") trolls, and Flash animators.
*   **The Birth of MADs:** When NND launched, Futaba users realized they now had a platform to upload high-quality video edits. The legendary "MAD" (parody/remix) video culture on NND was largely driven by Futaba creators animating inside jokes from their imageboards.

### 3. The Meme Pipeline (How they interacted)
Because users freely browsed all three sites, an unofficial "pipeline" formed for Japanese internet culture. Generally, a meme would follow this path:
1.  **2channel:** A funny phrase, troll, or text-based concept is born here.
2.  **Futaba Channel:** Artists on Futaba draw it, photoshop it, and turn it into a visual meme.
3.  **Nico Nico Douga:** Someone takes the Futaba images/lore, sets it to a Vocaloid song or remixes it into a MAD video, and it goes viral to the mainstream public.

### How this relates to Vocaloid
Because Vocaloid exploded in 2007—the exact same year this cultural cross-pollination was peaking—early Vocaloid history is deeply tied to this trinity. 

*   **Kasane Teto:** Created entirely on **2channel's VIP Board** as a text/lore prank to fool NND users on April Fool's Day.
*   **Yukkuri ("Take it easy!"):** The massive Touhou/Vocaloid meme of the floating, disembodied heads originated from a bad ASCII art drawing on **2ch**, was visually refined and drawn by artists on **Futaba**, and then turned into thousands of videos on **NND**.
*   **Mikudayo:** The horrific, big-headed Hatsune Miku mascot suit was initially mocked mercilessly on **Futaba Channel**. Futaba users photoshopped her into horror scenes and yakuza settings, giving her a sociopathic personality. This lore then leaked onto **NND**, where it became so popular that SEGA and Crypton officially embraced the meme. 
*   **Derivatives (Haku, Neru, etc.):** The "Fanon" Vocaloid derivatives (like Akita Neru, who was born to mock anti-Miku trolls, and Yowane Haku, the depressed Vocaloid for people who sucked at making music) were collaboratively brainstormed across 2ch threads and Futaba imageboards before making their debut in NND videos.
