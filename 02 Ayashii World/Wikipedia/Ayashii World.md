# Ayashii World

![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3e/Ayashii_World_style_BBS.svg/250px-Ayashii_World_style_BBS.svg.png?utm_source=ja.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)

Illustration of an Ayashii World-style bulletin board

**Ayashii World** (Strange World) is a group of [underground](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%82%A2%E3%83%B3%E3%83%80%E3%83%BC%E3%82%B0%E3%83%A9%E3%82%A6%E3%83%B3%E3%83%89_\(%E6%96%87%E5%8C%96\) "アンダーグラウンド (文化)") sites on the [Internet](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%82%A4%E3%83%B3%E3%82%BF%E3%83%BC%E3%83%8D%E3%83%83%E3%83%88 "インターネット") ([World Wide Web](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wide_Web "World Wide Web")) that was Japan's largest of its kind from the late [1990s](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990%E5%B9%B4%E4%BB%A3 "1990年代") to the early [2000s](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000%E5%B9%B4%E4%BB%A3 "2000年代"). [^21] Unlike the closed bulletin-board communities of [dial-up online services](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%83%91%E3%82%BD%E3%82%B3%E3%83%B3%E9%80%9A%E4%BF%A1 "パソコン通信"), which required users to register, it is considered a [pioneer](https://ja.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%9A%86%E7%9F%A2 "wikt:嚆矢") of large underground communities openly established on the Internet. [^22]

Its original founder was Masayuki Shiba ([handle](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%83%8F%E3%83%B3%E3%83%89%E3%83%AB%E3%83%8D%E3%83%BC%E3%83%A0 "ハンドルネーム"): **Shiba**). [^1] The name is also frequently written **ぁゃιぃわーるど**. [^2] [^23][^tn-ayashii-name]

Originally, the name referred to the Internet bulletin board run by **Masayuki Shiba**, [^24] which opened on [August 21](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/8%E6%9C%8821%E6%97%A5 "8月21日"), [1996](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996%E5%B9%B4 "1996年"), and closed on [September 3](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/9%E6%9C%883%E6%97%A5 "9月3日"), [1998](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998%E5%B9%B4 "1998年"). It also refers to the group of sites that inherited its culture and format after its closure.

In its early days, when **Shiba** was its sole administrator, it was a single site. After his retirement, numerous administrators who did not know one another personally established boards, chats, and websites based on their own interpretations of the existing **Ayashii World** format. It gradually developed into an autonomous, distributed network. Consequently, there is no administrator overseeing the whole, and obtaining a complete picture of the Ayashii World milieu (**ぁ界**, the 'a-world') is difficult.

## Overview

Ayashii World consists primarily of multiple electronic bulletin boards. Many retain a blackboard-like appearance, with a dark-green background and white text. [^25] Subjects span many fields, with an emphasis on underground material shunned by mainstream society. [^3] Although individual boards have founders, no administrator oversees the whole. Even **Shiba**, the first board's administrator, is said not to have grasped its entirety; comprehending everything after he stepped down and boards proliferated is difficult.

In the central gathering places known as the **main** boards, many subjects are discussed simultaneously on a single board. They function as social spaces where regulars spend their time, with an atmosphere that ordinary outsiders cannot readily enter. [^23] Because someone who stays there is imagined as living on the Ayashii World board, regular participants are called its [residents](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E4%BD%8F%E6%B0%91 "住民") (jūmin, jūnin, or ぁ民).

When **Shiba**, the charismatic [^23] founder and administrator of the original board, closed Ayashii World on September 3, 1998, many participants became 'refugees.' [^26] They moved to sites including [Amezou](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%81%82%E3%82%81%E3%81%9E%E3%81%86 "あめぞう"), which implemented a [thread-bumping bulletin-board system](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%82%B9%E3%83%AC%E3%83%83%E3%83%89%E3%83%95%E3%83%AD%E3%83%BC%E3%83%88%E5%9E%8B%E6%8E%B2%E7%A4%BA%E6%9D%BF "スレッドフロート型掲示板") shortly afterward on September 6, carrying Ayashii culture outside the community. [^21] [^27] That culture subsequently passed from Amezou to [2channel](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/2%E3%81%A1%E3%82%83%E3%82%93%E3%81%AD%E3%82%8B "2ちゃんねる"), so early 2ch culture owed much to Ayashii World. [^4] For example, the design of [Giko the Cat](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%82%AE%E3%82%B3%E7%8C%AB "ギコ猫") originated there, [^28] [^29] as did a considerable number of other examples of Japanese [Internet slang](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%82%A4%E3%83%B3%E3%82%BF%E3%83%BC%E3%83%8D%E3%83%83%E3%83%88%E3%82%B9%E3%83%A9%E3%83%B3%E3%82%B0 "インターネットスラング"), including [DQN](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/DQN "DQN"). [^30]

### Ayashii World in the narrow sense

#### Origins

Some time after the [Tokyo subway sarin attack](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%9C%B0%E4%B8%8B%E9%89%84%E3%82%B5%E3%83%AA%E3%83%B3%E4%BA%8B%E4%BB%B6 "地下鉄サリン事件") carried out by [Aum Shinrikyo](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%82%AA%E3%82%A6%E3%83%A0%E7%9C%9F%E7%90%86%E6%95%99 "オウム真理教") on March 20, 1995, a [tasteless game](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E4%B8%8D%E8%AC%B9%E6%85%8E%E3%82%B2%E3%83%BC%E3%83%A0 "不謹慎ゲーム") about the attack, titled *Kasumigaseki*, circulated on some grassroots BBSs. An article in the evening edition of the Asahi Shimbun on October 26, 1995, attracted considerable interest, but the game was unavailable on major dial-up services such as NIFTY, leading to repeated requests for it. **Shiba**, who happened to own it, uploaded it several times in response, but administrators immediately deleted it and eventually sent him a warning email. He established 'Ayashii World' to distribute it somewhere those service administrators could not intervene. [^5] [^6]

In the narrow sense, Ayashii World means the board during the period of **Shiba's** administration that began with these events. [^31] Purists argue that only this period supported social interaction worthy of the underground, but opinions differ over what counts as legitimate and whether the 'true' Ayashii World has ended. [^32] [^7] The only statement attracting agreement is: 'As long as you believe in it in your heart, Ayashii will keep existing ヽ(´ー｀)ノ.' [^33] [^35]

### Ayashii World in the broad sense

In the broad sense, Ayashii World encompasses the boards that proliferated after the 1998 closure, as well as various other [communities](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%82%B3%E3%83%9F%E3%83%A5%E3%83%8B%E3%83%86%E3%82%A3 "コミュニティ") bearing the **Ayashii** name. Major examples include [iRC](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%82%A4%E3%83%B3%E3%82%BF%E3%83%BC%E3%83%8D%E3%83%83%E3%83%88%E3%83%BB%E3%83%AA%E3%83%AC%E3%83%BC%E3%83%BB%E3%83%81%E3%83%A3%E3%83%83%E3%83%88 "インターネット・リレー・チャット") (Ayashii World @iRC) and [mailing lists](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%83%A1%E3%83%BC%E3%83%AA%E3%83%B3%E3%82%B0%E3%83%AA%E3%82%B9%E3%83%88 "メーリングリスト"). Since anyone can basically use the Ayashii name freely, many people establish and operate communities under it. No rules concerning site management govern permission to use the 'Ayashii World' brand. One could say it continues to develop culturally through varied interpretations that change with the times, or that it has lost its coherence as a community and effectively disintegrated. Although many well-known Ayashii World boards have closed, some remain.

The most distinctive feature of communities bearing the Ayashii name is their lack of discipline. In Japanese online gathering places, including bulletin boards, users establish numerous rules and constraints, ranging from posting etiquette to language, and mutual observance of these rules itself becomes one of the community's purposes. [^8] Ayashii World basically has no such restrictions: provocation and impersonation, normally prohibited or avoided elsewhere, occur openly, and everyone does as they please.

Nevertheless, while no explicit rules govern Ayashii as a whole, individual spaces have unwritten codes of conduct imposed by administrators and veteran participants, notably those with fixed handles. [^9] Violators are, at best, ignored, and otherwise severely criticized. If an administrator is involved, some boards may prohibit posting or even viewing. [^10] Participants are advised to 'ROM for six months' to learn the local culture; even afterward, it is usual to participate with a blank name field rather than a handle. [^36][^tn-ayashii-rom]

### Decline

Bulletin boards once formed the core of online interaction, but partly because of the rise of [social networking services](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%82%BD%E3%83%BC%E3%82%B7%E3%83%A3%E3%83%AB%E3%83%BB%E3%83%8D%E3%83%83%E3%83%88%E3%83%AF%E3%83%BC%E3%82%AD%E3%83%B3%E3%82%B0%E3%83%BB%E3%82%B5%E3%83%BC%E3%83%93%E3%82%B9 "ソーシャル・ネットワーキング・サービス"), user numbers have fallen considerably since the peak around 2000. New sites continued to open in the 2010s, but in far smaller numbers than at the peak. Existing sites have increasingly closed because their administrators are absent, so the overall tendency can be described as contraction.

## Bulletin-board format

Initially, the community used [Home Party](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%83%8B%E3%83%95%E3%83%86%E3%82%A3%E3%82%B5%E3%83%BC%E3%83%96#%E3%83%9B%E3%83%BC%E3%83%A0%E3%83%91%E3%83%BC%E3%83%86%E3%82%A3%EF%BC%88HP%EF%BC%89 "ニフティサーブ") on [NIFTY-Serve](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%83%8B%E3%83%95%E3%83%86%E3%82%A3%E3%82%B5%E3%83%BC%E3%83%96 "ニフティサーブ"). After moving onto the Internet, it used existing bulletin-board hosting services such as Digital Eden and Kenjinkai. [^11] Those boards closed, partly because Ayashii World's enormous posting volume imposed a heavy burden. [^12] In [1997](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997%E5%B9%B4 "1997年"), it began operating independently by installing a modified version of Netsurf Rescue's [^13] Minibbs (Ver. 3) on a rented server. [^14] With no threads, there is essentially a single posting page, making simultaneous discussion of multiple subjects difficult. [^37]

The original script had not been designed with extensibility in mind and gradually grew heavier as quoted-reply and anti-disruption features were added. **Kuzuha Script**, independently developed around 1998–2000 and refined during conflicts with disruptive users, subsequently became the de facto standard. In either case, the basic format remained the same. [^38] [^25]

By design, all subjects are posted in the same place on the main board, where all participants read and respond to them. This makes the flow rapid and sustained discussion of diverse subjects difficult. A board-creation system called **@Remix** was therefore developed and became widely used, enabling small communities to form, operate, pursue their activities, and disappear smoothly. REMIX applies a topic-based board system to Ayashii World: participants freely create boards for subjects they like; boards with increasing activity remain available on REMIX, while unpopular boards eventually disappear. [^15]

[Ayashii World @Strawberry](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%81%82%E3%82%84%E3%81%97%E3%81%84%E3%82%8F%E3%83%BC%E3%82%8B%E3%81%A9@%E3%82%B9%E3%83%88%E3%83%AD%E3%83%99%E3%83%AA%E3%83%BC?action=edit&redlink=1 "あやしいわーるど@ストロベリー (存在しないページ)") uses the tree-style display implemented at [Ayashii World @Zantei (Zantei Taihi)](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%81%82%E3%82%84%E3%81%97%E3%81%84%E3%82%8F%E3%83%BC%E3%82%8B%E3%81%A9@%E6%9A%AB%E5%AE%9A_\(%E6%9A%AB%E5%AE%9A%E9%80%80%E9%81%BF\) "あやしいわーるど@暫定 (暫定退避)"). Since a tree can be extended from any point, the flow of conversation remains easy to follow even when several subjects develop.

## Culture

### Expressing emotion on Ayashii World

Alongside half-width katakana and platform-dependent characters, Ayashii World frequently uses particular [kaomoji](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%A1%94%E6%96%87%E5%AD%97 "顔文字"), such as 'ヽ(´ー｀)ノ'. These have appeared almost invariably at the ends of posts expressing joy or pleasure. Recently recovered logs suggest an origin in the 'Guest's One-Word Corner No. 2' section of a [grassroots BBS](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%8D%89%E3%81%AE%E6%A0%B9BBS "草の根BBS") called [Center Net](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%82%BB%E3%83%B3%E3%82%BF%E3%83%BC%E3%83%8D%E3%83%83%E3%83%88 "センターネット"), where ヽ( ´ー\`)ﾉ was posted on [August 26](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/8%E6%9C%8826%E6%97%A5 "8月26日"), [1995](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995%E5%B9%B4 "1995年"). A guest using the [handle](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%83%8F%E3%83%B3%E3%83%89%E3%83%AB%E3%83%8D%E3%83%BC%E3%83%A0 "ハンドルネーム") 'Arere,' who appeared the following month, used it repeatedly, prompting speculation that he made the first post, but there is no longer any way to prove this.

As of 2008, however, '(;´Д\`)' had become more common than 'ヽ(´ー｀)ノ'. [^16]

### Ayashii World's comfort food

Few would dispute that the restaurant chain [Gyoza no Ohsho](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%A4%83%E5%AD%90%E3%81%AE%E7%8E%8B%E5%B0%86 "餃子の王将") has become a shared [comfort food](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%82%BD%E3%82%A6%E3%83%AB%E3%83%95%E3%83%BC%E3%83%89 "ソウルフード") for Ayashii World residents. It became a frequent topic around 2000, especially on Ayashii World II. During quiet periods with few posts, such as late at night, one pastime involves posting the characters '将', '王', 'の', '子', and '餃' one at a time; if the same user's posts appear consecutively without interruption, the mission is complete. Also indispensable is [Kyabetsu Tarō](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%82%AD%E3%83%A3%E3%83%99%E3%83%84%E5%A4%AA%E9%83%8E "キャベツ太郎"), nicknamed Kyabeshi Tarō and famous as Ayashii World's official snack. Its reasonable price, quality and quantity, comical character, and the subcultural atmosphere of cheap sweets combined to make it appealing joke material and explosively popular. Besides appearing in joke posts, it often appears in uploaded photographs as a humorous accent.

### Friction with 2channel

#### Conflict

[2channel](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/2%E3%81%A1%E3%82%83%E3%82%93%E3%81%AD%E3%82%8B "2ちゃんねる") lowered the barriers to participating in underground anonymous boards even further and was structured to make entry easy for newcomers. Following the [Nishitetsu bus hijacking](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%89%84%E3%83%90%E3%82%B9%E3%82%B8%E3%83%A3%E3%83%83%E3%82%AF%E4%BA%8B%E4%BB%B6 "西鉄バスジャック事件") by a teenage 2channel user on May 3, 2000, extensive mass-media coverage brought in large numbers of newcomers unfamiliar with Internet culture or history, who then absorbed underground board culture. [^17] Consequently, many mistakenly believed that mascots such as [Giko the Cat](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%82%AE%E3%82%B3%E7%8C%AB "ギコ猫"), various forms of [ASCII art](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%82%A2%E3%82%B9%E3%82%AD%E3%83%BC%E3%82%A2%E3%83%BC%E3%83%88 "アスキーアート"), [slang](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%82%A4%E3%83%B3%E3%82%BF%E3%83%BC%E3%83%8D%E3%83%83%E3%83%88%E3%82%B9%E3%83%A9%E3%83%B3%E3%82%B0 "インターネットスラング"), and rules such as 'read only for six months' ([ROM](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%83%AA%E3%83%BC%E3%83%89%E3%82%AA%E3%83%B3%E3%83%AA%E3%83%BC%E3%83%A1%E3%83%B3%E3%83%90%E3%83%BC "リードオンリーメンバー")) had originated on 2channel, although they came from Ayashii World. [^18] In August 2000, people convinced that Ayashii World had copied these things created a thread on 2channel's Hack/Crack board calling for an attack on it, and indiscriminate disruption of Ayashii World boards actually followed.

Ayashii World users already disliked 2channel, which they saw as built on [copying](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%83%91%E3%82%AF%E3%83%AA "パクリ") everything from its board system to its culture. Its inflated self-regard further antagonized them. In particular, attackers disrupted **Hajimete no Ayashii World**, where courteous interaction had been maintained even amid the provocation and disruption prevalent elsewhere in Ayashii World. Users of 2channel had also repeatedly appropriated uploaders provided for Ayashii residents. When an '@2ch Annihilation' topic appeared on Remix, anti-2channel feeling erupted. Ayashii residents and Gesu attacked servers using tools including the retaliatory script Phaser; several 2ch servers went down and logs were lost. Further escalation was being prepared, but 2channel administrator [Hiroyuki](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%81%B2%E3%82%8D%E3%82%86%E3%81%8D "ひろゆき") visited the topic and Ayashii World boards to apologize, [^19] bringing a provisional calm. Even afterward, anti-2channel sentiment remained strong, and using its slang or mentioning it became [taboo](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%82%BF%E3%83%96%E3%83%BC "タブー") on many boards. [^25] [^39]

#### Differences from 2channel

Centralized boards typified by 2channel are subject to controls imposed by central administrators, topic restrictions through self-governance, and retrospective deletion. In the article's account, such censorship means they cannot be called places for free expression; if their center stops functioning for any reason, the whole system becomes paralyzed. [^20] Ayashii World, by contrast, especially after original administrator Shiba's retirement, became a loose collection of independently administered boards. Users could choose boards or chats reflecting each administrator's personality according to their preferences. [^23] Viewed positively, this was [anarchy](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%84%A1%E6%94%BF%E5%BA%9C%E7%8A%B6%E6%85%8B "無政府状態"): a distributed form able to withstand intervention by the authorities and localized overload attacks. Ayashii World can therefore be described as less susceptible than other [anonymous boards](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%8C%BF%E5%90%8D%E6%8E%B2%E7%A4%BA%E6%9D%BF "匿名掲示板") to collapsing all at once.

Also, unlike 2ch, which placed advertisements directly in its social spaces and earned enormous profits, [^40] Ayashii World's administrators have generally provided boards and other spaces as unpaid volunteers since Shiba's time. [^41]

## The underground and Ayashii World

### Warez

[Warez](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warez "Warez") generally refers to Internet users illegally exchanging or obtaining commercial software, music files, and similar material over networks. In Japan's early Internet era, when the word arrived from abroad, Ayashii World was known as the leading underground board, with everyday discussions of hacking and related topics. Warez was also frequently discussed as a form of underground activity.

After legal restrictions increased, however, warez activity on Ayashii World was limited to a small scale, using [WinMX](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/WinMX "WinMX") or [FTP](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ftp "Ftp") servers set up by some users. Although users still reacted more tolerantly to warez posts than on other boards, it was seldom discussed as routinely as before. This was not limited to Ayashii World: with the development of legislation and police enforcement in twenty-first-century Japan, warez culture was no longer conducted openly as before, and compared with the underground's heyday, had diminished almost to nothing.

### Disruptive posting

Around 1997, Ayashii World frequently discussed board [disruption](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%8D%92%E3%82%89%E3%81%97 "荒らし") as well as hacking and related subjects. A group calling itself 'Gesu' emerged from people particularly interested in disrupting boards and sometimes went around attacking numerous boards, including Ayashii World itself.

## Ayashii World communities known to have been active in the past

### Direct Shiba lineage

**strange (April 15, 2006–2013)**

Run by [Shiba](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%81%97%E3%81%B0?action=edit&redlink=1 "しば (存在しないページ)"). After various changes, it took the form of a personal blog on [Hatena Diary](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%81%AF%E3%81%A6%E3%81%AA%E3%83%80%E3%82%A4%E3%82%A2%E3%83%AA%E3%83%BC "はてなダイアリー"), accepting comments from Hatena users outside the community as well. It closed in 2013.

### Honten lineage

[Ayashii World @Honten](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%81%82%E3%82%84%E3%81%97%E3%81%84%E3%82%8F%E3%83%BC%E3%82%8B%E3%81%A9@%E6%9C%AC%E5%BA%97 "あやしいわーるど@本店") (January 1999–2015)

The largest community in the Honten lineage. Along with @Shanghai and @Zantei (Zantei Taihi) of the Reverse lineage described below, it ranked among the busiest by user activity. A considerable number of users dated back to the dial-up era; its conservative character led it to be regarded as the mainstream continuation of the 'Shiba era.' It was generally recognized as the Ayashii World board with the highest average age and highest proportion of employed users. Its [administrator](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%AE%A1%E7%90%86%E4%BA%BA "管理人") was ds. Administration had changed hands several times, and some called the then-current @Honten 'ds Honten,' assessing it separately from earlier Honten boards. Its administrator became absent in [October](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/10%E6%9C%88 "10月") [2015](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E5%B9%B4 "2015年"), and all services stopped. As of 2018, only Honten Taihi barely survived.

Ayashii World Nihon @Honten (March 2003–December 2005)

Yamato Nihon, a fixed-handle Honten user, established 'Ayashii World Nihon @Honten.' It became very busy on April Fools' Day that year.

Ayashii World @Shiten (September 26, 2006– )

Established as @Taihi Honten after a major disruptive attack targeting @Honten on September 25, 2006. After @Honten recovered, it continued under the name @Shiten. Some [residents](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E4%BD%8F%E6%B0%91 "住民") dissatisfied with @Honten moved there, attracting hostility from some Honten purists. Its administrator is Okayu (ォヵュ).

Honten Taihi Custom (October 8, 2006– )

Established as a new refuge board for Honten after @Taihi Honten became @Shiten. Unlike @Honten and @Shiten, its administrator has advanced technical skills, and many original Web 2.0-style features have been added. Perhaps because its name explicitly identifies it as a refuge, it attracts little negative criticism. A distinctive feature is the absence of 'Ayashii World' from its name. The administrator is Az.

Ayashii World @Sakura Honten (November 6–December 6, 2007)

Established after a major disruptive attack on the Honten-lineage boards above in early November 2007, with numerous anti-disruption measures. Some residents dissatisfied with Honten's and Shiten's weak resistance to attacks expressed interest in moving, but few settled there, and the administrator closed it after a very short period. The administrator was Nanami.

### Reverse lineage

[Ayashii World II](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%81%82%E3%82%84%E3%81%97%E3%81%84%E3%82%8F%E3%83%BC%E3%82%8B%E3%81%A9II "あやしいわーるどII") (July 1999–2015)

Commonly called 'ぁぃぁぃ' (Ai-ai). Formerly the largest Reverse-lineage community, it ranked behind @Honten, @Shanghai, and @Zantei (Zantei Taihi) as of 2014. After a prolonged server outage in autumn 2015, a refuge was established on October 28, 2015.

Ayashii World @Extra (July 1999–July 2000; March 2005– )

Founded around the same time as Ayashii World II. It competed with II and others to be Ayashii World's main board, but closed a year after opening. After a period without major developments, it fully reopened in 2005.

[Ayashii World @Christmas Island](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%81%82%E3%82%84%E3%81%97%E3%81%84%E3%82%8F%E3%83%BC%E3%82%8B%E3%81%A9@%E3%82%AF%E3%83%AA%E3%82%B9%E3%83%9E%E3%82%B9%E5%B3%B6 "あやしいわーるど@クリスマス島") (September 1999– )

Commonly called 'Kurishima.' Established by residents opposed to Ayashii World II's administration. It has used its present name since November 1999.

Ayashii World NG (October 2000–April 2002)

Established to accommodate the many refugees denied access to Ayashii World II, and later became independent. It was very active for a time.

Mattari World (December 2000– )

A board inheriting the tradition of the former Ayashii World @Kinsan. Like @Kinsan, its core users prefer a relaxed, gentle atmosphere, making it somewhat unusual within Ayashii World.

Ayashii World @Nozomi (February 2002– )

The effective successor to the closed Ayashii World NG. Many former NG residents moved to @Nozomi, which retains a certain following even after its activity subsided.

#### Mirai lineage

[Ayashii World @Mirai](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%81%82%E3%82%84%E3%81%97%E3%81%84%E3%82%8F%E3%83%BC%E3%82%8B%E3%81%A9@%E3%81%BF%E3%82%89%E3%81%84 "あやしいわーるど@みらい") (February 2000–March 2005)

Established following widespread access restrictions on Ayashii World II. Initially regarded as a refuge for II, it grew to surpass it and remained the largest Reverse-lineage community for a long period until closing in March 2005.

Ura Mirai (December 2001– )

Created as a refuge board for @Mirai. It survived @Mirai's closure.

[Ayashii World @Shanghai](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%81%82%E3%82%84%E3%81%97%E3%81%84%E3%82%8F%E3%83%BC%E3%82%8B%E3%81%A9@%E4%B8%8A%E6%B5%B7 "あやしいわーるど@上海") (March 2005–December 24, 2016)

Established as the successor to Ayashii World @Mirai. Initially operating as @qwerty, it changed its name to @Shanghai. It was one of the largest Reverse-lineage communities after @Mirai closed.

It closed on Christmas Eve 2016, as announced in advance.

Ayashii World @Hinata (November 2005– )

A new board established for refugees from @Shanghai.

It also contains dangerous features and traps, such as a button exposing remote host information.

Ayashii World @Misao (August 27, 2016– )

A board created as a refuge for [Ayashii World @Shanghai](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%81%82%E3%82%84%E3%81%97%E3%81%84%E3%82%8F%E3%83%BC%E3%82%8B%E3%81%A9@%E4%B8%8A%E6%B5%B7 "あやしいわーるど@上海"). It also distributes an Android application and Greasemonkey scripts for the board. The current main board of the Mirai lineage.

#### Zantei lineage

[Ayashii World @Zantei](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%81%82%E3%82%84%E3%81%97%E3%81%84%E3%82%8F%E3%83%BC%E3%82%8B%E3%81%A9@%E6%9A%AB%E5%AE%9A "あやしいわーるど@暫定") (January 2002–April 2008; March 2018–July 31, 2025)

Created by people rebelling against Ayashii World II's administration. It attracted attention for its unprecedented system of multiple administrators, but internal divisions over management policies and other issues left it nonfunctional, yielding its position to Ayashii World @Zantei (Zantei Taihi). It subsequently pursued an independent course and closed in April 2008. Support operations continued until April 2010, followed by a move to [Twitter](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter "Twitter"). It formally reopened in March 2018, but closed on July 31, 2025, when xdomain server services ended. [^42] After that, [the source sentence ends here].

[Ayashii World @Zantei (Zantei Taihi)](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%81%82%E3%82%84%E3%81%97%E3%81%84%E3%82%8F%E3%83%BC%E3%82%8B%E3%81%A9@%E6%9A%AB%E5%AE%9A_\(%E6%9A%AB%E5%AE%9A%E9%80%80%E9%81%BF\) "あやしいわーるど@暫定 (暫定退避)") (March 2003–September 28, 2018)

Established when Ayashii World @Zantei (January 2002– ) temporarily stopped functioning. @Zantei recovered almost a year later, but @Zantei (Zantei Taihi) continued to be used as the main board. It was one of the largest Reverse-lineage communities.

Ayashii World @Okome.Zanzantei (October 1, 2018– )

At the time described, the successor to [Ayashii World @Zantei (Zantei Taihi)](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%81%82%E3%82%84%E3%81%97%E3%81%84%E3%82%8F%E3%83%BC%E3%82%8B%E3%81%A9@%E6%9A%AB%E5%AE%9A_\(%E6%9A%AB%E5%AE%9A%E9%80%80%E9%81%BF\) "あやしいわーるど@暫定 (暫定退避)"). The current main board of the Zantei lineage.

@Okome.Zanzantei contains a Notogawa (cat) section. This is Gikoneko's @Zantei, where posting continues.

Ayashii World @Ryofu

One of the main boards.

Nazo Board

[Ayashii World @Strawberry](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%81%82%E3%82%84%E3%81%97%E3%81%84%E3%82%8F%E3%83%BC%E3%82%8B%E3%81%A9@%E3%82%B9%E3%83%88%E3%83%AD%E3%83%99%E3%83%AA%E3%83%BC?action=edit&redlink=1 "あやしいわーるど@ストロベリー (存在しないページ)") (June 2011– )

Ayashii World's first board specializing in a tree-style display.

Renamed 'Ayashii World @Nagomi' in 2014.

Nagomi and its associated board 'Jōbu Kōzō' ('Superstructure') are now integrated.

Abunai Board

A gathering place for the most aggressively minded users.

**[Ayashii World @Kenashiya](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%81%82%E3%82%84%E3%81%97%E3%81%84%E3%82%8F%E3%83%BC%E3%82%8B%E3%81%A9@%E3%81%91%E3%81%AA%E3%81%97%E5%B1%8B?action=edit&redlink=1 "あやしいわーるど@けなし屋 (存在しないページ)") (May 2025– )**

## Cultural context and translator's notes

> [!info] Cultural context and translator's notes
> **A decentralized family of communities.** The article distinguishes Shiba's original board from later communities sharing its name and conventions. Labels such as Honten (main shop), Shiten (branch shop), Mirai (future), Zantei (provisional), and Taihi (refuge) identify local lineages and migration spaces; they do not imply a single organization.
>
> **Community language.** “Residents” and “refugees” describe regular users and migration after closures or bans. *Arashi* covers disruptive posting, flooding, and attacks; the translation uses the specific action where the source supplies it. “Underground” is the community's historical label for taboo material, irreverence, and sometimes illegal activity.
>
> **Names and visual expression.** Distinctive Japanese spellings, emoticons, vulgar examples, and quoted handles are retained where their form matters. The snack and restaurant rituals illustrate shared in-jokes rather than official institutional endorsements.
>
> **Source perspective.** This is a translation of a captured Wikipedia article, including its varying “current” dates, disputed origin stories, evaluative descriptions of rival communities, and incomplete sentence after the 2025 closure. Those claims have not been silently updated to 2026. Bibliographic English glosses are descriptive translations, not asserted published English titles.
>

[^tn-ayashii-name]: *Ayashii* can mean strange, suspicious, or dubious. The stylized form ぁゃιぃ mixes small kana and the Greek iota to imitate the sound and appearance of あやしい; source note [^2] identifies it as Kusachū-go. Its visual form is therefore preserved.
