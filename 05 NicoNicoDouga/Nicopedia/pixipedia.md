**Pixiv Encyclopedia** (ピクシブ百科事典, *Pikushibu Hyakkajiten*; formerly **pixpedia**) is an online crowdsourced encyclopedia provided by the Pixiv Encyclopedia Production Committee.

---

## Overview

Pixiv Encyclopedia is a collaborative wiki designed to explain tags attached to illustrations on pixiv, provide background details on depicted subjects, and document community art events and contests (*kikaku*). It supports standard wiki syntax, allowing registered users to compose and format articles much like any conventional wiki website. While editing requires an active pixiv user account, a paid pixiv Premium subscription is not required.

Because the encyclopedia operates as an auxiliary service to an illustration-sharing community, it features extensive, detailed articles on visual concepts: character compositions, poses, artistic tropes, and situational themes. Articles tend to be concise, with many entries consisting of just a single explanatory line. The content primarily focuses on how a given word (tag) is perceived and used within the pixiv ecosystem, a tendency that is especially pronounced for everyday vocabulary that would not normally qualify as specialized jargon. On the other hand, several articles are substantially longer, more exhaustive, and more intricately detailed than their counterparts on Nico Nico Pedia.

Because pixiv explicitly permits R-18 (adult) content, the encyclopedia features a substantial volume of sexually suggestive, innuendo-heavy, and explicit articles. (To be frank, the parent pixiv platform itself is overflowing with sexually charged illustration tags to begin with.)

On June 30, 2011, a major platform renewal overhauled the site design, and the operational entity was reorganized as the "Pixiv Encyclopedia Production Committee." The exact corporate makeup and membership of this committee remain undisclosed. Aside from bugs accompanying the redesign, several legacy features were removed—such as custom article icons and video embedding—drawing numerous complaints from veteran editors.

As an aside, Nico Nico Pedia developer **Gunyarakun ★** once met with pixiv staff at a Denny's diner and proposed embedding links to Nico Nico Pedia directly beside pixiv's illustration tags, but his pitch was politely ignored. It is widely speculated that pixiv already harbored plans to develop its own independent encyclopedia platform at the time.

## Specifications & Features

Similar to Nico Nico Pedia, articles are generated directly from community tags, and an icon displayed next to a tag indicates whether an article exists. Users can embed illustrations hosted on pixiv directly into the article body or set one as the "Main Image." Additionally, YouTube videos can be embedded by inputting a video ID.

Each article includes a "Comments" section functioning as a discussion board. Because comments are tied to the user's public pixiv handle, it is not an anonymous message board. While an edit history log exists, users cannot currently revert to previous revisions directly from the history log. Furthermore, the editor's username merely links to their public pixiv profile page, making it exceedingly difficult to view an editor's complete contribution history across other articles.

The internal search engine does not differentiate between article titles and article bodies; a search term matching either will return a hit. Search results are sorted chronologically by the most recently updated articles. Additionally, when searching for multiple keywords separated by spaces, the engine performs an **AND search** (requiring all words to be present), having transitioned from an earlier OR-based search model.

Users can embed both Niconico Douga and YouTube videos directly into articles.

### Legacy Specifications & Retired Features
- **Tag-Restricted Creation (Beta Era):** During the beta period, articles could only be created for tags that were already actively attached to at least one published illustration on pixiv. To create an article for a phrase that did not exist as a tag, the user first had to tag an artwork with that term. When the service graduated to its official release on July 8, 2010, users gained the ability to create articles for words not used as tags. Following the June 30, 2011 renewal, setting a "Main Image" illustration became mandatory when publishing a new article.
- **Custom Pixel Icons:** Users previously could design custom article icons on the spot using a built-in pixel art editor (*dot-art tool*) or by uploading their own illustrations. The June 30, 2011 redesign abolished these icons in favor of full-sized Main Images.
- **BBS Comments & Deletions:** The comment section was originally named "Bulletin Board" (*Keijiban*), and users were permitted to delete their own past comments—a holdover from pixiv’s native profile comments. The June 30, 2011 overhaul implemented the current, non-deletable comment system.

## Major Differences from Nico Nico Pedia

Because Pixiv Encyclopedia is an online encyclopedia intimately tethered to a specific User-Created Content (UCC) hub, it is frequently compared to its predecessor, Nico Nico Pedia. This comparison is natural given that pixiv and Niconico share an overlapping otaku demographic (and, as mentioned, Gunyarakun ★ initially proposed cross-linking the two platforms). However, several profound cultural and systemic differences separate them:

---

### Article Tendencies & Community Tone

- **Jokes and Gag Entries Are Strictly Taboo:**  
  On Nico Nico Pedia, editors frequently inject self-deprecating jokes, memes, "dark history" (*kuro-rekishi*), and comedic sarcasm throughout articles. On Pixiv Encyclopedia, however, **comedic "joke" articles (*neta kiji*) are heavily frowned upon**. In fact, pixiv's Terms of Service explicitly **prohibit posting false or fabricated information**. The editor toolbar does not even provide a strikethrough function (a staple of sarcastic commentary on Nico Nico Pedia). Nevertheless, because the barrier to editing on pixiv is so low, newcomers unfamiliar with site culture frequently attempt to insert jokes and memes, sparking bitter rollback/edit wars (*sashimodoshi gassen*).
- **Subjective, Emotion-Driven Writing is Tolerated:**  
  In stark contrast to Nico Nico Pedia, which encourages an objective, encyclopedic viewpoint, Pixiv Encyclopedia is dominated by **overtly subjective, sentimental, and emotional commentary**. This is openly permitted by the community: the platform is fundamentally treated as "a sanctuary to express love for characters and works." As long as the writing demonstrates genuine affection, it is generally accepted. Conversely, writing harsh anti-fandom criticism or tarnishing a character's public image triggers vicious flame wars; **content that unjustifiably disparages the creative value of an artwork or character is a direct violation of the Terms of Service**.
- **Articles That Cannot Function as Tags Are Discouraged:**  
  On pixiv, typing a half-width space inside a tag splits it into two separate tags. Therefore, article titles containing spaces often replace them with underscores (`_`) or omit spaces entirely. While non-tag titles are not strictly banned, titles that cannot function smoothly as searchable illustration tags are discouraged for utility reasons.
- **Character-Centric Over Lore-Centric:**  
  Articles place far more emphasis on individual characters than on overarching plot narratives (an obvious byproduct of being an art community). Consequently, the wiki maintains close ties to Virtual YouTuber (VTuber) culture.
- **Extreme Fervor for "Nichiasa" Superhero Shows:**  
  Articles dedicated to *Nichiasa Kids Time* broadcasts (particularly the *Kamen Rider* and *Pretty Cure* franchises) display an eccentric, almost unhinged level of exhaustive passion that frequently eclipses Nico Nico Pedia.
- **Demographics & Subject Matter:**  
  Owing to pixiv's significantly larger female userbase compared to Niconico, the encyclopedia features extensive coverage of fashion aesthetics, botany, fine art, and human psychological traits (fueled by pixiv’s thriving fan-fiction novel platform).
- **Strict Prohibition on 18+ Content Within Articles:**  
  **Writing articles unsuitable for minors (under 18) is strictly prohibited by the Terms of Service**. Violations result in immediate article deletion and, in egregious cases, permanent account termination. On Nico Nico Pedia, adult topics are fully permissible as long as an R-18 age-gate warning banner is displayed at the top of the article.

---

### Platform Guidelines & Administrative Philosophy

Beyond its binding Terms of Service, Nico Nico Pedia’s operators publish self-authored community manifestos—such as *"Differences Between Nico Nico Pedia and Wikipedia"* and *"To Enjoy Your Time on Nico Nico Pedia"*—which actively shape the site's culture. Furthermore, Dwango operators regularly provide direct, transparent replies on user request boards, establishing clear behavioral benchmarks. When edit wars or trolling erupt on Nico Nico Pedia, management frequently intervenes directly to revert edits, scrub offensive posts, or place controversial articles under temporary editing freezes to enforce a cooling-off period.

By contrast, Pixiv Encyclopedia provides virtually zero editorial guidance beyond its bare-bones legal Terms of Service, leaving article standards entirely to the whim of the userbase. While this sounds hands-off and libertarian on paper, it undeniably fosters chronic vandalism and "hit-and-run" article creation (*tatenige*).

Most notoriously, many editors format entire articles using exhaustive **bullet-point lists**—a formatting habit considered bad practice in professional encyclopedias. This frequently degrades well-written articles into unreadable, childish lists of trivia.

## Issues & Criticisms

### Non-Existent Articles Ranking High on Search Engines
Search engines like Google frequently place Pixiv Encyclopedia entries near the very top of search results, only for users to click through and find a blank page reading: *"This article has not been written yet."*  
This occurs because pixiv's backend fails to output a `noindex` meta-tag on uncreated article URLs, allowing search crawler bots to index blank stubs. In contrast, platforms like Wikipedia and Nico Nico Pedia properly serve `noindex` headers on empty entries. This issue is exacerbated by search engine algorithms that rank pages based purely on pixiv's high overall domain authority without verifying whether meaningful text actually exists.

### Low Editing Barriers Encouraging Problematic Behavior
Because any free pixiv account can edit articles, the platform is plagued by vandalism, personal slander, and reckless edit-spamming that strains server loads. On July 16, 2010, an editor who published an article specifically defaming and provoking pixiv's management was permanently banned from the platform. [4]

Similarly, the site is overrun with half-baked, poorly researched entries, or articles created for subjects that possess zero encyclopedic notability. While typos can be corrected by subsequent editors, unnotable junk articles **cannot be deleted by regular users**, leaving them permanently stranded on the servers. In recent years, malicious users have frequently engaged in **"hit-and-run article creation" (*tatenige*)** to mock, harass, or publicly dox rival users. Other users repeatedly trigger server errors by bypassing maximum character limits, trapping popular anime, tokusatsu, and VTuber articles in endless cycles of administrative rollbacks. Naturally, arbitrary personal opinions and self-serving headcanons run rampant.

### Direct Plagiarism from Wikipedia, Nico Nico Pedia, and Official Websites
Countless articles are generated via shameless copy-pasting. While certain sources permit reproduction under proper attribution licenses (such as Creative Commons), virtually no editors adhere to these licensing requirements. Comically, the reverse has also occurred in recent years: lazy Nico Nico Pedia contributors have copy-pasted articles from Pixiv Encyclopedia without removing pixiv-specific formatting, confusing readers on both sides.

### Opaque & Arbitrary Administrative Censorship
**Whenever an article documents controversies that reflect poorly on pixiv’s management, administrators step in, delete the article, and permanently blacklist the title to prevent recreation.**
- *Examples of blacklisted titles:* `現代アート` (*Contemporary Art*), `現代あーと` (*Contemporary Art [hiragana]*), `アート犬` (*Art Dog*).
- System searches for specific sensitive keywords were deliberately altered to return fatal server errors (e.g., querying the word `現代` [*contemporary/modern*]).  
- *(For historical context regarding this censorship, see the Nico Nico Pedia articles on the **pixiv Controversy** and **Chaos\*Lounge**).*

Within Pixiv Encyclopedia, this censorship is chronicled under the article title **"nopage"**, where users document their grievances and cynical commentary regarding administrative suppression. While pixiv published a vague statement claiming *"certain tag pages on Pixiv Encyclopedia have been temporarily suspended as a provisional measure,"* community consensus agrees that management's explanations are utterly insufficient.

## Protocol When Discovering Plagiarism from Nico Nico Pedia

All articles authored on Nico Nico Pedia legally assign their intellectual property rights to DWANGO Co., Ltd. upon publication (excluding infringing material). Consequently, Dwango is the legal copyright holder of all Nico Nico Pedia text.

Therefore, if you discover an article on Pixiv Encyclopedia that has been plagiarized word-for-word from Nico Nico Pedia:
1. Cross-reference the revision histories and timestamps of both articles to confirm beyond doubt that the text appeared on Nico Nico Pedia first.
2. File a formal report in the dedicated thread on the *Nico Nico Pedia Deletion Request Board* (*"Thread for Reporting Plagiarism from Nico Nico Pedia"*).
3. Dwango's legal operations will review the claim and issue a formal takedown request to Pixiv Encyclopedia.

The following is a quote from comment >>79 on this article's discussion board, posted by lead engineer Gunyarakun when Nico Nico Pedia was managed by Mirai Kensaku Brazil:

> **79 ： Gunyarakun ◆e5sgKA2q7. ： 2010/09/18 (Sat) 13:15:23 ID: Bit9ewwraB**  
> *"If an article you contributed to Nico Nico Pedia is reproduced on pixpedia without permission, please notify us in the Deletion Request BBS thread. Mirai Kensaku Brazil will submit a formal copyright takedown request on your behalf."*

## Cultural Context & Terminology Guide: Pixiv Encyclopedia

- **The Rivalry: "Nico-Hyaku" (ニコ百) vs. "Pixiv Dic":**  
  *Nico Nico Pedia* (affectionately shortened to **Nico-Hyaku**) launched in May 2008 as a community-driven wiki for Niconico's subcultures. In November 2009, pixiv launched its clone, **pixpedia** (later renamed *Pixiv Encyclopedia* / *Pixiv Dic*). Because both wikis document the same anime, Vocaloid, gaming, and VTuber topics, an intense cultural rivalry developed. This article—written by Nico Nico Pedia contributors—reflects Nico-Hyaku’s pride in its perceived intellectual superiority, objective standards, and witty humor compared to pixiv's more emotional, messy wiki.
- **Gunyarakun ★ and the Denny's Meeting:**  
  Gunyarakun ★ was the eccentric lead engineer from Mirai Kensaku Brazil who single-handedly developed Nico Nico Pedia. Early in pixiv's history, he met pixiv's management at a Denny's diner in Tokyo to propose a partnership: whenever a user hovered over an illustration tag on pixiv, it would link directly to the corresponding Nico Nico Pedia article. Pixiv politely stalled and ignored the proposal, only to release their own competing encyclopedia (*pixpedia*) a year later.
- **"Neta" (ネタ) vs. Serious Documentation:**  
  In Japanese internet slang, **neta** refers to jokes, memes, comedy tropes, parodies, or satirical tall tales.  
  - On Nico Nico Pedia, inserting *neta*, tongue-in-cheek sarcasm, and self-deprecating humor is considered the highest art form of editing.  
  - On Pixiv Encyclopedia, inserting *neta* or joke descriptions is strictly prohibited under the platform's anti-misinformation rules. Users trying to write satirical articles are routinely reported, sparking **"rollback wars" (*sashimodoshi gassen*)**.
- **"Tatenige" (立て逃げ - Hit-and-Run Article Creation):**  
  Originating on 2channel, *tatenige* originally described someone who creates a discussion thread (*tateru*) and immediately abandons it (*nigeru*). On Japanese wikis, it refers to malicious or lazy users who publish a one-sentence stub article—often to complain about an annoying player, harass an artist, or spark a flame war—and leave other editors to deal with the fallout. Because neither wiki allows normal users to delete pages once created, these stub articles clutter search engines permanently.
- **Nichiasa Kids Time (ニチアサキッズタイム):**  
  A legendary Sunday morning children's television programming block broadcast on TV Asahi, consisting of the latest *Super Sentai*, *Kamen Rider*, and *Pretty Cure* (*PreCure*) series. While intended for children, the block has a massive, hyper-dedicated adult otaku fanbase. In Japanese wiki culture, "Nichiasa" editors are famous for their terrifyingly obsessive attention to detail, cataloging every monster-of-the-week, weapon variant, and catchphrase within seconds of an episode airing.
- **The Chaos\*Lounge Controversy (カオス\*ラウンジ問題) & Administrative Censorship:**  
  In 2011, a modern art group called **Chaos\*Lounge** triggered one of the largest internet scandals in Japanese otaku history. The collective printed out original illustrations uploaded by amateur pixiv artists without permission, collaged them, walked over them, glued them onto toilet paper, and displayed them in art galleries as "contemporary art" (*gendai art*).  
  - When outraged pixiv users discovered that pixiv’s top executives were close personal friends with Chaos\*Lounge and had actively sponsored their exhibits, the userbase revolted.  
  - Rather than addressing the intellectual property theft, pixiv management went on a censorship spree: banning critical users, deleting encyclopedia articles explaining the controversy, blacklisting article titles like `現代アート` (*Contemporary Art*), and hardcoding internal search engines to return errors whenever someone searched for the word "contemporary."  
  - This incident led to mass boycotts of pixiv in 2011 and permanently stained the reputation of Pixiv Encyclopedia's moderation team.
- **"nopage":**  
  When pixiv administratively deleted and blacklisted the Chaos\*Lounge articles, navigating to their URLs returned a minimalist system error screen displaying the string `nopage`. Outraged users circumvented the ban by creating a new article titled **"nopage"**, writing a detailed, satirical chronicle of the company's censorship practices within it.
- **"Epigonen" (エピゴーネン):**  
  A German loanword commonly used in Japanese literary and internet critique (*epigone*), meaning a shallow imitator, derivative copycat, or uninspired disciple. Veteran Niconico users frequently mocked Pixiv Encyclopedia as an "epigone" of Nico Nico Pedia.
- **"Up-nushi" (うｐ主) and "Ok" (おk):**  
  The article uses standard 2channel shorthand: **おk** (spelling "ok" on a Japanese IME keyboard) and references 2channel discussion boards where wiki disputes are coordinated.
