**niconico** is the **former umbrella brand name** used to collectively refer to the suite of web services operated by DWANGO Co., Ltd., including Niconico Douga, Niconico Live, and Nico Nico Seiga.

## Overview

"niconico" was the comprehensive master brand name utilized for eight years, from 2012 to 2020. 

Prior to 2012, the entire service ecosystem was collectively called "Niconico Douga." However, alongside the major version update to *Niconico Douga: Zero*, the corporate hierarchy was reorganized: "Niconico Douga" was repositioned as a single video-hosting sub-service contained within the larger "niconico" umbrella. The brand rollout was originally scheduled to begin on April 29, 2012, immediately following the close of *Niconico Chokaigi 2012*, but was postponed slightly due to unforeseen technical delays (launching on May 1). The decision to adopt an all-lowercase English Latin-alphabet spelling was made *"with a view toward expanding international access in the future."*

On October 27, 2020, coinciding with a comprehensive redesign of the browser portal top page, the umbrella brand and corporate logo were switched to the Japanese katakana **ニコニコ (Niconico)**. The Latin-alphabet "niconico" moniker was officially retired after eight years of use. From this point forward, major platform version codenames (e.g., *Niconico (Re)*) apply across the entire multi-service ecosystem rather than solely to the video-hosting platform.

## New Features & Services Introduced During the "Zero" Era

### New niconico Portal Top Page
- Upgraded to a more modern, contemporary visual design.
- Re-themed with an overarching dark, black-toned aesthetic.

### Niconico Douga: Zero
- **ZeroWatch:** Video playback canvas resolution expanded significantly from 640×368 to 864×486 pixels.
- Real-time tag synchronization: edits made to video tags reflect instantly on the page without requiring a manual refresh.
- Newly established video playback queue / playlists.
- New video selection pop-up window during playback.
- New user review and critique section beneath videos.

### Niconico Live: Zero
- Broadcast viewing canvas expanded to 810×450 pixels via the ZeroWatch interface.
- Related programs and upcoming stream directory added.
- Live genre categories reorganized under a dynamic "Recent" tab.
- Instant, real-time notifications when a broadcast breaks into the platform rankings.

### "Nicoru" (ニコる)

> *"Hilarious videos, witty comments, stylish tags, exciting NicoRepo activity updates, clever Ichiba shopping picks... Niconico Douga is overflowing with creative contributions that deserve far more recognition. The feature created to praise and validate them is 'Nicoru.'"*

- A social appreciation button modeled after Facebook's "Like" button, allowing users to send a positive reaction ("Nicoru") to individual comments, tags, user activity logs (*NicoRepo*), videos, and Nico Nico Ichiba product links.

### Redesigned My Page
- Overhauled user dashboard with an expanded suite of personalized tracking tools.

### Interactive Request Broadcast Station: "Nsen"
- A 24/7 collaborative radio livestreaming feature where users gather to request and listen to Niconico music tracks collectively in real time.
- Featured dedicated genre channels: **VOCALOID**, **Niconi Indies**, **Utattemita (Vocal Covers)**, **Ensou-shitemita (Instrumental Covers)**, **Touhou Project**, **PV (Music Videos)**, **Hotaru no Hikari (Ambient / BGM)**, and **All-Genre**.
- Broadcast format: Alternated continuously between 30-minute user request blocks and automated system-curated spotlight playlists.

### NicoSound
- An official audio extraction feature allowing users to download the soundtrack of uploaded videos for free directly to their PC browser or Android device (strictly limited to videos where the original uploader explicitly authorized audio downloads).

### Infrastructure & Network Upgrades

During a press-only presentation on April 26, 2012, Dwango announced that its total network backbone bandwidth across the entire Niconico network had been dramatically upgraded to a maximum capacity of **400 Gbps**. According to historical figures shared at the event, Niconico Douga’s bandwidth at the start of the (γ) version era in 2007 was a mere 2 Gbps.

> *"As user numbers, video uploads, and live broadcasts grow, network bandwidth becomes an existential problem. Takeshi Natsuno explained: 'Users scolded us, saying: "We can't even connect! Stop building flashy concert halls like Nicofarre and upgrade your server lines!" Since around this time last year, we have aggressively expanded our infrastructure. We have now reinforced our network to a maximum of 400 Gbps. I believe we are the only private enterprise in Japan pulling 400 Gbps on this scale. People might look at Niconico Douga and assume anyone could build it just by scrolling text over video, but because the infrastructure investments are so punishingly expensive, competitors can't make a profit trying it. That infrastructure cost serves as our barrier to entry.' Dwango Chairman Nobuo Kawakami also reflected: 'Five years ago, we were forced to restart the entire service with just 2 Gbps and a hard cap of 100,000 users.'"*  
> — *Niconico Douga Evolves into "niconico" on May 1* (AV Watch)

## Cultural Context & Terminology Guide: niconico (Zero Era)

- **The "niconico" Rebrand (2012–2020):**  
  Between 2006 and 2012, the entire company platform was synonymous with *Niconico Douga*. However, by 2012, Dwango was streaming Japanese parliamentary debates, running massive concert halls, hosting digital manga, and operating e-commerce hubs. To reflect that it was no longer just a "video site" (*douga*), they introduced the lowercase Latin brand **niconico**. In 2020, acknowledging that Japanese users still universally referred to it in katakana, they officially rebranded the umbrella identity to **ニコニコ (Niconico)**.
- **The "ZeroWatch" Backlash:**  
  While the article summarizes the technical upgrades of *Niconico Douga: Zero* neutrally, the launch of the new video player—codenamed **ZeroWatch**—was arguably the greatest public relations crisis in Niconico’s history.  
  - The player was heavy, bloated, broke third-party user scripts, lagged on average PCs, and forced an unpopular pitch-black UI.  
  - Thousands of users protested, flooding comment sections with fury. The backlash was so severe that Dwango had to publicly apologize, allow users to revert to the legacy *Harajuku* interface, and rush out the next version (*Niconico Douga: Q*) just five months later to fix performance issues.
- **"Nicoru" (ニコる) & Comment Culture:**  
  Formed by taking the brand name *Nico* (smiling) and conjugating it into a Japanese verb with *-ru* (similar to how "to Google" became *Guguru* in Japanese slang).  
  - While intended as a wholesome way to praise funny comments or helpful timestamps, viewers quickly found mischievous ways to use it—such as sarcastically "Nicoru-ing" cringe comments, spam, or flame bait.  
  - The feature was quietly discontinued in 2015 due to server maintenance overhead, but community nostalgia was so intense that Dwango rebuilt and restored it to the video player in 2019.
- **Nsen (The Internet Radio Hangout):**  
  *Nsen* was one of the most beloved features of the 2010s Niconico community. It functioned like a collaborative 24/7 radio station: users joined a genre room (such as the *VOCALOID Channel*), queued up songs using video IDs, and listened together while chatting in the synchronized scrolling comment feed. It served as a massive discovery engine for newly uploaded Vocaloid and indie music.
- **NicoSound & Vocaloid Distribution:**  
  In the early 2010s, before Spotify or Apple Music were established, digital music distribution in Japan was heavily restricted by DRM. With **NicoSound**, Vocaloid producers could tick a checkbox allowing listeners to download an MP3 audio rip of their song directly from the player legally and for free. For years, NicoSound was the primary way Japanese middle and high school students loaded new Vocaloid songs onto their portable digital audio players.
- **Nicofarre (ニコファーレ) vs. Server Bandwidth:**  
  In 2011, Dwango constructed a lavish, multi-million-dollar digital nightclub in Roppongi called *Nicofarre*, outfitting every wall and the ceiling with full-surround LED screens that rendered rolling Niconico comments during live idol concerts and events. Regular users—plagued by constant video buffering, stream disconnections, and low bitrates—were outraged, creating the popular meme: *"Why are you wasting our Premium subscription money on a disco in Roppongi instead of buying more bandwidth?!"* Takeshi Natsuno’s quoted defense in the article was a direct response to this community meme.
- **"NicoRepo" (ニコレポ):**  
  Niconico’s automated user feed (short for "Nico Report"), similar to a Twitter timeline or Facebook activity feed, alerting followers when a creator uploaded a new video, posted an illustration, or began a livestream.
