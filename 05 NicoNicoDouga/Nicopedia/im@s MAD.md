# [The Idolmaster MAD (iM@S MAD)](https://dic.nicovideo.jp/a/%E3%82%A2%E3%82%A4%E3%83%9E%E3%82%B9mad)
- [[#Overview]]
- [[#Related Tags]]
- [[#See Also]]

**iM@S MAD** (アイマスMAD) refers to fan-made MAD videos that use assets and materials from *The Idolmaster* franchise.

However, because the sheer volume of *The Idolmaster* videos on the platform is staggering, viewers typically find videos by querying specific artist names, anime titles, or game titles, or by searching standardized "Collaboration" tags. The general tag **"iM@S MAD"** tends to be reserved for works that do not fit neatly into genre-specific tags—such as MADs using anime footage or still-image MADs.  
Therefore, if you want to search through a broad collection of videos, it is recommended to consult the **List of Idolmaster Tags** and related indexes.

---

## Overview

Compared to conventional anime and video games previously used as MAD source material, the original *The Idolmaster* game had one decisive difference: **an exceptionally high degree of freedom as visual source footage.**

Taking the first home console release on the Xbox 360 as an example, when setting up a stage music video (PV), players had access to:

- **16 Songs** (each with unique choreography)
- **Selection of up to 3 idols** out of 10 available characters (+ Brown-haired Miki)
- **36 Costumes** (base game, excluding DLC)
- **144 Accessories** in total (48 head, 36 body, 36 hand/arm, 24 leg/foot)

Because a stage performance could be customized using any combination of the above, it is no exaggeration to say that **virtually infinite** PV variations were possible. (When you factor in customizable camera work and vocal part assignments, a single person could not possibly watch every permutation in a lifetime.)

Such abundant source material enabled creators to produce an equally diverse range of visual direction. Beyond that, the footage itself was remarkably high-quality for its time, and the format of "idols dancing on a stage" had an organic, powerful synergy with MAD video editing.

As Nico Nico Douga grew, *The Idolmaster* MADs exploded in popularity alongside the platform. However, due to the unique production nature described above, two distinct tags originally came to define these uploads:

1. **iM@S Normal PV** (iM@SノーマルPV): For unedited, direct captures of in-game stage performances.  
   *(Embedded content)*

2. **iM@S Collab PV** (iM@SコラボPV): For edited stage performances synchronized to third-party, non-Idolmaster songs.  
   *(Embedded content)*

While it is no longer clear which tag originated first (though "Normal PV" was likely earlier), one can easily see that the two served as counterpoints to each other. (Note: The videos referenced above were the earliest uploads returned when sorting each tag by "Oldest First.")

During the dawn of the **Nicomas** subculture in early 2007, stage dance videos were the overwhelming mainstream format of iM@S MAD creation. Thanks to the established pairing of these two tags, the generic tag "iM@S MAD" was largely unnecessary and unused.  
Meanwhile, traditional-style MADs—such as dialogue redubs, parodies, and comedic skits—adopted even more granular tags. Creative subgenres that blurred the lines between MAD and gameplay video—such as the *iM@S Fictional War Chronicles Series* (*iM@S Kakuu Senki*) or *iM@S Liberal Arts Lectures* (*iM@S Kyouyou Kouza*)—were also cataloged under their own individual tags.

As Nicomas continued to flourish, tags multiplied and specialized, leaving the umbrella term "iM@S MAD" mostly neglected.

In later years, the franchise expanded dramatically with the broadcast of the television anime adaptation, followed by social mobile games such as *Cinderella Girls*, *Million Live!*, *SideM*, and *Shiny Colors*. With these came an influx of works exploring styles that did not fit vintage 3D dance PV tags—such as "still-image MADs" using 2D card illustrations. Consequently, the broad tag **"iM@S MAD"** has seen a steady, gradual resurgence in use.

## Cultural Context & Slang Guide

### 1. What is a "MAD" (MAD動画)?
In Japanese internet culture, a **MAD** (or *MAD video*) is an umbrella term for fan-made derivative video remixes. While Western fandom differentiates strictly between AMVs (Anime Music Videos), YTPMVs (YouTube Poop Music Videos), and meme edits, the Japanese term "MAD" encompasses all of these:
- **Anime/Game MADs (AMV style):** Synced visual cuts highlighting drama or action.
- **Still-Image MADs (静止画MAD):** Dynamic motion-graphic videos made by animating 2D illustrations, manga panels, or game card art with typography and 3D camera effects in editing suites (like AviUtl or After Effects).
- **OtoMAD (音声MAD):** Splicing dialogue, sound effects, and character lines to pitch-shift them into musical notes playing a melody over a rhythm track (the direct Japanese counterpart to YTPMVs).

### 2. "Nicomas" (ニコマス) and the Nico Nico Big Three
**Nicomas** is a portmanteau of *Nico Nico Douga* and *Idolmaster*. 

If you are familiar with early Vocaloid history (around 2007–2010), you may know that Nico Nico Douga's golden age was built upon what users called the **Nico Nico Gosanke** (ニコニコ御三家, *The Big Three of Niconico*):
1. **Vocaloid** (Hatsune Miku, Kagamine Rin/Len, etc.)
2. **Touhou Project** (bullet hell games, remixes, fan anime)
3. **The Idolmaster** (Xbox 360 captures, PVs, and community MAD culture)

Nicomas video editors were often referred to as **"P" (Producer)**, mimicking the in-game role of talent producers managing idols.

### 3. "Collab PV" (コラボPV)
In English internet spaces, a "collaboration" usually means two human creators making something together. On Nico Nico Douga, however, **Collab PV** (*Collaboration PV*) refers to **collaborating footage from one work with music from an entirely different franchise**. 

Because early *Idolmaster* games allowed creators to record clean, high-definition 3D dancing models without on-screen UI, video editors treated it like a 3D animation sandbox (very similar to how creators later used *MikuMikuDance / MMD* to make Vocaloid dance videos for non-Vocaloid pop songs).

### 4. "Brown-Haired Miki" (茶髪美希)
In the 2007 Xbox 360 game, character **Miki Hoshii** is famously a blonde, laid-back idol. However, during specific late-game story branches ("Awakened Miki"), she cuts her hair short and dyes it back to her natural dark/brown color, taking her idol career with fierce seriousness. Because her character model, personality lines, and aura completely change, fans treated "Brown-haired Miki" as an entirely separate 11th idol when calculating video permutation counts.

### 5. Tag Culture & Deep Nicomas Subgenres
Early Niconico was famous for its hyper-specific taxonomy tags:
- **iM@S Normal PV:** Straight gameplay footage without custom music or cuts.
- **iM@S Kakuu Senki Series (iM@S架空戦記シリーズ):** *"Fictional War Chronicles"*. A massive subgenre where creators hacked or edited strategy games (*Nobunaga's Ambition*, *Romance of the Three Kingdoms*, *Civilization*, *Fire Emblem*) or RPGs to feature Idolmaster characters in epic, dramatic narrative campaigns told through custom visual novel dialogue boxes.
- **NovelsM@ster:** Narrative fan-fiction dramas staged entirely within the *Idolmaster* engine using character animations and text overlays.
