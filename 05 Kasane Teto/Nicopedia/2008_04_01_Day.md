## Current Events (Real World)

- The Revised Part-Time Employment Act takes effect in Japan.
- Nationwide operation of Japan’s **Earthquake Early Warning** (*Kinkyū Jishin Sokuhō*) system officially commences.
- Nippon TV revives its iconic station ident broadcast, **"Dove's Day Off"** (*Hato no Kyūjitsu*), for the first time in seven years.

## Nico Nico Douga Milestones

- **Historic Agreement with JASRAC:** Dwango signs an official copyright licensing agreement with the Japanese Society for Rights of Authors, Composers and Publishers (JASRAC). With this agreement, user uploads of **"Played It"** (*Ensouしてみた*, instrumental covers) and **"Sang It"** (*Utattemita*, vocal covers) to SMILEVIDEO are officially legalized and recognized by copyright authorities.
- The "Nico Nico External Player" adds embed support for gaming news outlet *GameSpot Japan*.
- **Rankings at 5:00 AM:** The viral MAD video *"Is M.C. Donald Addicted to Dancing? Final Savage Clown Donald M"* knocks *"Sang the Melancholy of Haruhi Suzumiya Medley on Impulse"* down to take 21st place in all-time MyList bookmarks.
- **Rankings at 5:00 AM the following day:** The video *"[Hatsune Miku] 'Hungry Song' from The Tower of Druaga"* surges from 57th to 53rd place in all-time MyLists.
- **Launch of "Nico Nico Douga 2.0 (lol)":** To celebrate April Fool's Day, Niconico rolls out a joke version update titled *Nico Nico Douga 2.0 (Warai)*, packed with prank features:
  - The development team claims to have "successfully achieved mastery over the space-time continuum," allowing user comments to scroll backwards from left to right (later formally implemented as the `@逆` command).
  - Introduction of the `@pizza` command, which jokingly promises to have a hot Domino's pizza delivered to your doorstep if you comment on a video.
  - The midnight time-chime broadcast (*Nicowari*) drops a shocking announcement: *"As of today, Nicowari services have been permanently terminated."*
  - Synthetic news anchor **Bōyomiko** abruptly announces her immediate retirement on *NiwaNews*.
- **The Announcement of "Gackpoid" (がくっぽいど):** Internet Co., Ltd. announces they are developing a new VOCALOID voiced by legendary visual kei rock star **GACKT**. *(Due to the sheer absurdity of the concept, the entire internet universally dismissed it as an obvious April Fool's joke—until it was later confirmed to be 100% real).*

## Other April Fool's Events

Outside of official administration pranks, the community was awash with hoaxes:

- **The Birth of Kasane Teto:** VIPPERs on 2channel create an imaginary fake VOCALOID: **Character Vocal Series 03 "Kasane Teto."** Armed with fabricated box art, custom-recorded audio samples, rigged comment filters, and coordinated upvoting, they successfully bamboozle countless *Niko-chū* (Niconico addicts) into believing Crypton had announced the successor to Hatsune Miku and the Kagamine twins.
- The *Weekly VOCALOID Ranking* jokingly announces its permanent discontinuation, citing the sudden cultural invasion of "Gachimuchi Pants Wrestling" (Billy Herrington memes) and the rising global cost of crude oil.

# Cultural Context & Slang Guide: April 1, 2008

### 1. The JASRAC Agreement: A Historic Turning Point for Vocaloid Culture
The April 1, 2008 JASRAC agreement cannot be overstated in its importance. Prior to this date, uploading cover songs or playing copyrighted melodies on Niconico carried constant risk of copyright strikes. 

When Dwango signed this comprehensive blanket license with JASRAC, amateur musicians gained the legal right to upload their own vocal and instrumental performances of thousands of commercial pop and anime songs. This paved the way for the golden age of **Utaite** (singers like Mafumafu, Soraru, and 96Neko who built their careers covering Vocaloid songs) and allowed Vocaloid producers who signed their original works to JASRAC to let fans freely cover their music on the platform.

### 2. "Gackpoid Was Real?!"
When software maker Internet Co., Ltd. announced on April 1, 2008, that international rock icon **GACKT** was providing his vocal cords to become a commercial Vocaloid ("Gackpoid" / Camui Gackpo), the Japanese internet laughed it off as the day's silliest corporate gag. In 2008, Vocaloid was still seen by the mainstream as an underground otaku niche; the idea that a mainstream celebrity of Gackt’s stature would participate sounded impossible. When Internet Co. clarified on April 2 that the contract had indeed been signed, the shockwave swept across both the music industry and otaku culture.

### 3. "Niko-chū" (ニコ厨)
*Chū* is short for *chūbō* (middle schooler), used across Japanese forums as a derogatory suffix for immature, obsessive, or gullible users. **Niko-chū** refers to hardcore Nico Nico Douga addicts who spent their entire days glued to the site's rankings, making them prime targets for the Kasane Teto VIP prank.

### 4. Gachimuchi Pants Wrestling (ガチムチパンツレスリング)
A massive, long-running meme subculture on early Niconico centered around heavily spliced, sound-manipulated MAD videos of Western adult fitness videos starring bodybuilder **Billy Herrington** (dubbed "Aniki" / Big Brother by Japanese netizens). The meme was so dominant in early 2008 that it regularly hijacked Niconico’s overall top video rankings.

### 5. "Nicowari" (ニコ割) & "Bōyomiko" (棒読子)
- **Nicowari:** A system Dwango used in the late 2000s where all videos across the entire website were forcefully split or interrupted at specific scheduled times (like midnight) to display a synchronized interactive banner or news announcement to every connected user simultaneously.
- **Bōyomiko:** Literally *"Monotone Reading Girl"*. The robotic, synthesized female text-to-speech engine utilized by Niwango for system announcements and audio news flashes on Niconico.
