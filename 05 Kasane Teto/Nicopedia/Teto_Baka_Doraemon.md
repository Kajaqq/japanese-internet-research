# "You Really Are an Idiot, Aren't You?" (きみはじつにばかだな)

- [[#Your Overview Really Is an Idiot]]

**"You really are an idiot, aren't you?"** (*Kimi wa jitsuni baka dana*) is an iconic quote spoken by Doraemon.

---

## Your Overview Really Is an Idiot

The quote originates from the short manga chapter **"Ultra Super Battery"** (*Urutora Sūpā Denchi*), written and illustrated by Fujiko F. Fujio (collected in *Doraemon Color Masterpiece Collection, Vol. 6*).

After Nobita shatters an electric lightbulb, he attempts to make do by screwing a solid rubber baseball into the empty ceiling socket instead. Staring at Nobita's sheer, unadulterated foolishness, an utterly exasperated Doraemon delivers this legendary assessment.

While *Doraemon* is globally renowned as the gold standard of gentle, wholesome children's entertainment, the original manga frequently featured blisteringly harsh, venomous zingers from the titular robotic cat:
- *"You bastard, I'll murder you!"* (*野郎、ぶっ殺してやる*)
- *"If the entire nation of Japan sank to your intellectual level, it would be the end of the world!!"* (*日本じゅうがきみのレベルにおちたら、この世の終わりだぞ!!*)

This quote stands as one of his most cutting remarks. Thanks to its sublime, deadpan cynicism, it has been embraced since the dawn of the Japanese internet as a ubiquitous Shift-JIS ASCII Art reaction image across online communities.

### The Television Anime and "Educational Considerations"

While Fujiko F. Fujio's unvarnished dialogue faithfully reflected the real-life teasing of Japanese elementary schoolers, the first TV Asahi animated adaptation (the "Nobuyo Dora" era, 1979–2005) softened these sharp edges for broadcast television.

Some hardcore manga purists initially felt let down, viewing this as an unnecessary sanitization of the original work. However, voice actress Nobuyo Oyama later explained the creative reasoning: while these abrasive lines were present in the earliest scripts, the cast concluded during rehearsals that *"a child-care robot would never be programmed with foul language."* Consequently, they ad-libbed gentler phrasing right in the recording booth.

During the Showa era, broadcast television held immense, unrivaled cultural influence over children. With this educational responsibility in mind, Doraemon's speech patterns were polished into polite, gentle language—typified by his famous catchphrase greeting: **"Hello, I'm Doraemon!"** (*Konnichiwa, boku Doraemon desu*). Rather than treating Nobita as an equal childhood peer, the anime firmly cast Doraemon into the role of a generous, protective guardian.

Other cast members followed suit. Most famously, Gian’s voice actor, Kazuya Tatekabe, struggled to find a milder way to express Gian's bullying, replacing harsh slurs like *"Bakkyarou!"* (idiot) and *"I'm gonna beat you to a pulp"* with the legendary, softer catchphrase:  
**"Pretty cheeky for someone who's just Nobita!"** (*Nobita no kuse ni namaiki da!*).

## Cultural Context & Slang Guide: "You Really Are an Idiot"

### 1. Why Is This Connected to Kasane Teto?
If you look at the official profile of **Kasane Teto** on the TwinDrill website or in her UTAU/Synthesizer V software manuals, her official catchphrase/slogan is literally:  
> **「君はじつに馬鹿だな」** (*"You really are an idiot, aren't you?"*)

Because Teto was created as an April Fool's hoax designed to mock gullible fans who fell for the prank, and because she was conceptually hybridized with Doraemon ("Doriemon"), the VIPPERs selected this exact classic Doraemon zinger as her official motto. When Teto sings sarcastic or unhinged songs, this catchphrase is frequently referenced in the lyrics and on-screen comments.

### 2. Shift-JIS ASCII Art (AA) Culture
The graphic box in the article is a reproduction of a famous Japanese **Shift-JIS ASCII Art** template. 
In the late 1990s and 2000s on 2channel, users communicated through elaborate multi-line text drawings utilizing Japanese full-width typographical characters and Kana. 

This specific Doraemon AA—depicting Doraemon resting his chin on his hands with half-closed, deeply unimpressed eyes—became the standard reaction image posted whenever a forum user asked an extraordinarily stupid question or fell victim to an obvious troll.
