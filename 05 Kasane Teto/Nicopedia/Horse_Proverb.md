**"A horse coming out of a gourd"** (*Hyōtan kara koma ga deru*) is a traditional Japanese proverb.

It is frequently shortened to **"A horse from a gourd"** (*Hyōtan kara koma* / 瓢箪から駒), which serves as the active video tag on Nico Nico Douga.  
*(Note: The Kanji for "gourd" is historically written as 「瓢簞」, but in modern Japanese typography, the simplified Asahi glyph 「瓢箪」 is commonly used).*

---

## Overview

> **"Hey, hey! You know gourds, right? Those dried flask things old guys in samurai period dramas drink sake out of?"**  
> **"Wouldn't it be hilarious if a whole horse just suddenly popped right out of one?!"**

...And then, after joking around like an idiot, **a real horse actually popped out.**

From this imagery, the proverb describes **an astonishing, impossible thing emerging from an utterly mundane place**, or **something said purely in jest unexpectedly turning into concrete reality.**

Conversely, when used in the negative form—*"A horse never comes out of a gourd"* (*Hyōtan kara koma wa denai*)—it serves as a metaphor for an absolute, undeniable impossibility (akin to "when pigs fly").

### Literal Breakdown:
- **Hyōtan (瓢箪 / 瓢簞):** A bottle gourd that has been hollowed out, dried, and used as a canteen or container.
- **Koma (駒):** An archaic Japanese term for a **horse, steed, or pony**. It does *not* refer to a Japanese chess piece (Shogi *koma*) or a spinning top (which is pronounced *koma* but written 独楽).

## Appearances in Creative Works

### Uma Musume Pretty Derby

In Cygames' multimedia title *Uma Musume Pretty Derby*, players can earn the in-game epithet (*futatsuna*): **"An Uma Musume from a Gourd"** (*瓢箪からウマ娘*).  
- **Unlock Condition:** Win an elite G1 title using a trainee horse girl who inherited inspiration/stats from two parent horse girls who never won a single Graded stakes race (*jūshō*).

While this condition feels slightly different from the traditional proverb's definition, the in-universe lore of *Uma Musume* establishes that ordinary horses do not exist in their world; consequently, several real-world proverbs are adapted with altered meanings [1]. In this context, the title is used more like the proverb *"A kite giving birth to a hawk"* (*Tobi ga taka o umu*—mediocre parents unexpectedly producing an exceptional genius prodigy).

Furthermore, the game features several formidable rival horse girls during the climax of training scenarios who were originally written as completely fictional characters without any real-world racehorse namesakes. In 2022, however, real-world thoroughbred owners actually named their newly registered racehorses after these fictional anime characters—bringing real-life racehorses into existence from the game:
- **Happy Meek** → *Happy Meek* (Real-world Thoroughbred)
- **Little Cocon** → *Little Cocon* (Real-world Thoroughbred)
- **Bitter Glacé** → *Bitter Glacé* (Real-world Thoroughbred)

### Artistic Fireworks

At the renowned *Tsuchiura All Japan National Fireworks Competition*, an entry was once submitted in the Creative Fireworks division titled: **"A Horse Came Out of a Gourd."**

The display launched two mid-sized shells side-by-side to paint the silhouette of a gourd in the night sky. Right as the gourd began to fade, a smaller, vibrant shell was shot upward (or burst from within one of the shells) to depict the *koma* (horse) emerging into the sky.

## Cultural Context & Slang Guide: "A Horse from a Gourd"

### 1. Classical Origin: The Legend of Zhang Guolao (Chō Karō)
While modern Japanese speakers know the phrase as an everyday idiom, the proverb actually derives from classical Chinese Taoist folklore regarding **Zhang Guolao** (known as *Chō Karō* in Japan), one of the revered Eight Immortals (*Ba Xian*).

Legend says Zhang Guolao possessed a magical white mule capable of traveling thousands of miles a day without tiring. When he rested, he would fold the beast up like paper and slip it into a tiny gourd flask (*hyōtan*). When he was ready to ride again, he would squirt water or wine from his mouth onto the gourd, and the full-sized steed would magically leap back out. 

Over centuries in Japan, this bizarre visual image evolved into a colloquial proverb meaning:
- A truth born from a joke.
- An incredible, impossible development emerging from a tiny or ridiculous origin.

### 2. Connection to April Fool's & Niconico Culture
As seen in the earlier articles regarding **Kasane Teto**, **Gackpoid**, and **Nico Nico Douga Turning a Profit**, the tag **"A Horse from a Gourd" (瓢箪から駒)** is one of the most celebrated conceptual tags on Nico Nico Douga. 

Whenever a creator, software company, or community board cooks up an outrageous hoax that unexpectedly manifests as a genuine, life-changing cultural reality, netizens tag the video with:
- **瓢箪から駒** (*A Horse from a Gourd*)
- **嘘から出たまこと** (*A Truth Spawned from a Lie*)

Kasane Teto going from a throwaway 2channel forum prank into an official digital singer performing on stadium stages alongside Hatsune Miku is universally cited by Japanese netizens as the quintessential modern digital example of "a horse leaping out of a gourd."

### 3. *Uma Musume* and Linguistic Worldbuilding
In the hit franchise *Uma Musume Pretty Derby*, human girls with horse ears and tails run in competitive races. Crucially, **biological animals called "horses" (馬 - *uma*) do not exist in their universe**.

This creates a fascinating linguistic paradox: hundreds of traditional Japanese idioms and Kanji characters incorporate the "horse" radical (馬). The franchise playfully handles this through comedic worldbuilding adjustments:
- Words with the horse radical are given alternate in-universe folk etymologies.
- Proverbs like **馬子にも衣装** (*Mago ni mo ishō* / "Clothes make the man," literally referring to coarse stablehands looking handsome in fine robes) are rewritten using Katakana (マ子) to refer to young horse-girl children.
- In-game titles like *"An Uma Musume from a Gourd"* playfully remind the player that even the game's language bends around the absence of real horses.

### 4. "Tobi ga Taka o Umu" (鳶が鷹を生む)
The article notes that the *Uma Musume* achievement functions like the proverb **"A kite gives birth to a hawk."** 
- A **kite** (*tobi*) is a common, scavenging raptor seen everywhere across Japanese skies.
- A **hawk** (*taka*) is revered as a magnificent, noble bird of prey.

The phrase is the Japanese equivalent of humble, unexceptional parents producing a brilliant, prodigal child. Winning an elite Grade 1 racing championship using parents who never won a single trophy is the equine video-game realization of this proverb.
