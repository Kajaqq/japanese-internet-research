## Overview

*(From the Kasane Teto Official Site)*

"Second-Generation Teto" is the informal nickname given to one of the **rejected concept designs** submitted during the original creation of Kasane Teto.

### Origins of Her Creation

Kasane Teto was originally conceived on 2channel’s VIP board as an imaginary VOCALOID created as a bait-and-switch trolling prank for April Fool's Day 2008.  
When deciding upon her visual design, one of the candidate illustrations submitted to the thread was the character concept now referred to as "Second-Generation Teto."

This illustration survived all the way to the final candidate selection stage. Ultimately, however, the concept art submitted by artist **"Sen" (線)**—which represents the official Teto we know today—was chosen, and this runner-up proposal was discarded.

Because the artist closely emulated the aesthetic of KEI’s official Hatsune Miku artwork, the overall visual vibe leaned very heavily toward Miku: her hairstyle resembled Miku’s twin-tails with the tips twisted into subtle drills, and her outfit bore a distinct resemblance to MEIKO.  
Much like Sen’s winning design, this concept leaned aggressively into the aesthetic of being an obvious "bootleg knock-off VOCALOID"—a characteristic far more pronounced here than in later, more refined VIPPALOID characters.

### Why She Came to Be Called "Second-Generation"

Had history taken its normal course, this rejected sketch would have faded into complete obscurity.  
(After all, Kasane Teto herself was originally intended as nothing more than a throwaway, one-off joke for April Fool's Day; a rejected draft design had even less reason to survive.)

Her resurfacing was sparked by a joke image that artist Sen had posted at the very start of that fateful VIP thread: a bizarre, disproportionately tall Doraemon sporting drill hair.  
Over time, as Kasane Teto’s popularity exploded on Nico Nico Douga, that strange Doraemon monstrosity came to be celebrated as **"First-Generation Teto"** (*Shodai Teto*). Prominent MAD creators and music producers like Doriemon P and Tsuburaya P embraced the meme, earning it a devoted, cult-like following.

Naturally, the community turned its attention to the *next* chronological design submitted after the Doraemon sketch: that very same rejected runner-up concept. Fans affectionately dubbed her **"Second-Generation Teto"** (*Nidaime Teto*).

### Arrival as a 3D MMD Model

When First-Gen Teto was shockingly converted into a 3D MikuMikuDance (MMD) model—captivating audiences with its unapologetically *kimo-kawaii* (creepy-cute) appearance—community voices began asking: *"Can someone make an MMD model for Second-Generation Teto, too?"*  
However, unlike the First Generation, which possessed outrageous, unhinged comedic value as pure meme fuel, few 3D modelers saw much incentive to spend weeks modeling an obscure, discarded prototype.

Yet that wish was finally answered by a dedicated MMD craftsperson (*shokunin*).

## Cultural Context & Slang Guide: Second-Generation Teto

### 1. VIPPALOIDs (VIP系VOCALOID)
When Kasane Teto successfully fooled the internet on April 1, 2008, the VIPPERs felt guilty for leaving fans heartbroken that she wasn't a real singer. In response, they discovered Ameya's freeware voice synthesizer **UTAU** (released just weeks earlier in March 2008) and recorded voice samples to bring Teto to life as a real singing voicebank. 
As the article notes, later VIPPALOIDs were designed from scratch to look like polished, legitimate commercial software. 
Second-Gen Teto, by contrast, belonged to that raw first week when the goal was strictly to look like a suspicious, bootleg counterfeit (*pachimon*) of Miku and MEIKO.

### 2. "Kimo-kawaii" (キモカワイイ)
A classic 2000s Japanese slang portmanteau of *kimochi warui* (gross / creepy) and *kawaii* (cute). It refers to things that are simultaneously grotesque and strangely endearing—exemplified here by the tall, bootleg Doraemon drill mascot ("First-Gen Teto") that preceded Second-Gen Teto.

### 3. MMD "Shokunin" (MMD職人)
*Shokunin* literally means "artisan" or "master craftsman." On Nico Nico Douga, video creators, 3D modelers, and subtitle typographers who demonstrate extraordinary technical dedication are honored with the title *shokunin*. Modeling, rigging, and releasing an obscure rejected 2008 forum sketch 1,176 days after the fact is quintessential *shokunin* behavior.
