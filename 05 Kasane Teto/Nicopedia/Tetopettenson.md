# Tetopepetenson (テトペッテンソン

**Tetopepetenson** (テトペッテンソン) is a song broadcast on NHK's long-running educational music program *Minna no Uta* ("Everyone's Songs"). It originally debuted in October 2002 and has enjoyed several repeat broadcasts over the years.

---
## Overview

The song is celebrated for its strangely hypnotic, tongue-twisting nonsense lyrics and its distinctive stop-motion animation, in which simple rectangular wooden blocks glide, pivot, and interlock across the screen with the precision of a naval fleet maneuver. The vocals were performed by actor/singer Jun Inoue, accompanied by a children's chorus.

The music is adapted from **"Le beau tambour"** (*The Handsome Drum*), a 1986 children's song by Swiss singer-songwriter Henri Dès. The melody was rearranged by **Masaki Kurihara** (best known as the leader of the acoustic ensemble *Kuricorder Quartet*), while renowned media creator **Masahiko Sato** penned original Japanese lyrics.  
The Japanese lyrics carry no coherent semantic narrative; they are designed purely as phonetic wordplay.

*(For reference, the original French lyrics and their literal translation are as follows. In the Japanese adaptation, the song spans six distinct stanzas with varying nonsense syllables; in Dès's original, the following refrain repeats seven times)*:

> *J'ai reçu plan plan* (I made a plan)  
> *J'ai reçu plan plan* (I made a plan)  
> *J'ai reçu un beau tambour* (I got a handsome drum)  
>  
> *Et je joue plan plan* (And I execute the plan)  
> *Et je joue plan plan* (And I execute the plan)  
> *Et je joue quand il fait jour* (And I play it in the daylight)  
>  
> *Et quand il fait nuit* (And in the night)  
> *Et le mercredi* (And on Wednesdays)  
> *Et quand papa dort encore* (And when papa is still asleep)  
>  
> *Et pour les voisins* (And for the neighbors)  
> *Le dimanche matin* (On Sunday morning)  
> *Je vais dans le corridor.* (I march out into the hallway.)

The memorable animation was animated by Sora Matsumoto, directed overall by **Masahiko Sato** (who famously penned the viral sensation *Dango 3 Kyōdai*), with character and graphic design handled by art director **Norio Nakamura** (who previously collaborated with Sato on the legendary PlayStation puzzle game *I.Q: Intelligent Qube*).

### Trivia: Connection to Kasane Teto
As an aside: when the iconic UTAU virtual singer **Kasane Teto** was first being conceived via 2channel anonymous prompts (*anka*), her name was initially decided as **"Tetopepetenson,"** lifted directly from this song!  
To make the hoax convincing, users decided she needed a more authentic, commercial VOCALOID-style name. They appended the surname **Kasane** (重音, "Layered Sound") and shortened "Tetopepetenson" down to **Teto**, giving birth to her permanent name.

## Cultural Context & Slang Guide: Tetopepetenson

### 1. NHK's *Minna no Uta* (みんなのうた)
*Minna no Uta* ("Everyone's Songs") is a five-minute musical television and radio broadcast produced by Japan's public broadcaster NHK since 1961. It pairs original songs with independent animated films. Over six decades, it has introduced many of Japan's most famous children's and folk melodies, including *Dango 3 Kyōdai* and Kenshi Yonezu's *Paprika*.

### 2. Masahiko Sato (佐藤雅彦) & Kuricorder Quartet
The creative minds behind *Tetopepetenson* represent Japanese children's media royalty:
- **Masahiko Sato:** A legendary media theorist, game designer, and commercial director. He created the iconic PlayStation game ***I.Q: Intelligent Qube*** and developed NHK's universally acclaimed educational program ***PythagoraSwitch*** (*Pitagora Suitchi*), famed for its elaborate Rube Goldberg kinetic machines.
- **Kuricorder Quartet:** An acoustic ensemble famous for playing quirky arrangements using recorders, tubas, and melodicas. They composed the iconic theme song for *PythagoraSwitch*.

### 3. The Etymology of Kasane Teto
When 2channel VIP was inventing Teto's identity on March 30, 2008, one of the prompts asked for her first name. An anonymous user shouted out **"Tetopepetenson!"** after this viral NHK earworm. 
Had the thread not decided to abbreviate it, the virtual diva we know today would have been officially named **"Tetopepetenson Kasane"**!
