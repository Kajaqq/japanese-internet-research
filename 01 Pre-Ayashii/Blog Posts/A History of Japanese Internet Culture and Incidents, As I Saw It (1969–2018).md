# A History of Japanese Internet Culture and Incidents, As I Saw It (1969–2018)


![f:id:ohstuki_zunko:20180917184215j:plain](https://cdn-ak.f.st-hatena.com/images/fotolife/o/ohstuki_zunko/20180917/20180917184215.jpg "f:id:ohstuki_zunko:20180917184215j:plain")

## Nobody had compiled the history of internet culture and incidents, so I did

A few years ago it struck me: "You can learn Japan's history from a textbook, but the history of the internet hasn't really been compiled anywhere." So I decided to put together a chronological record of incidents and culture on the internet, and wrote it pretty much on impulse.

Since then I've been adding bits whenever I remembered something or readers sent me additions.

The original plan was to eventually turn it into a proper book. But having written it, the content isn't really worth charging money for, and since my sources are my own memory, Wikipedia and assorted other websites, I can't vouch for its accuracy.

More than anything, I figured it would be more fun for everyone to read it together and say "Oh yeah, that happened!" or "Hey, this is missing!" than to sell it, so I decided to publish it here.

Note that the incidents and culture are basically written from my own point of view, so the focus is on **2channel** and **niconico**. Some entries are based on hazy memories, and I've also included some world-shaking events that have nothing to do with the internet.

As for scope: I cover internet trends, culture and incidents, but that's an extremely broad field. If I started on Touhou, KanColle, THE IDOLM@STER and so on, it would expand endlessly, so unless something is really significant I've kept those to a minimum.

### Things I still lack information or dates for (info wanted)

I can't write about the following because I don't know when they happened or the details. Please tell me on [Twitter](http://twitter.com/ohtsuki_zunko).

> [!note]
> **Misakura-speak** is the distinctive, breathless ero-manga dialogue style of artist Misakura Nankotsu (e.g. 「らめぇ」). **Nijiura** (虹裏) is Futaba Channel's 2D-image board; see 2002.

## ARPANET and the Dawn of the Internet (1969–1990)

In 1969, the progenitor of the internet was born on planet Earth.

### 1969

- ARPANET, the origin of the modern internet, goes live in the USA. It started as a network linking four sites: UCLA, the Stanford Research Institute, UC Santa Barbara and the University of Utah. The first message sent was "lo" (it crashed while trying to type `login:`).

### 1981

- Tohoku University is connected to ARPANET, the first time ARPANET reached Japan.

### 1983

- ARPANET migrates to the internet.

> [!note]
> This refers to ARPANET's switch to the TCP/IP protocol suite on 1 January 1983, usually treated as the birth of the "internet" proper.

### 1984

- JUNET is born, connecting Keio University and the Tokyo Institute of Technology. This is the origin of the internet in Japan. The University of Tokyo and various other research institutions and universities later join.

> [!note]
> JUNET's first link also included the University of Tokyo; it was set up by Jun Murai, often called the "father of the Japanese internet".

### 1988

- Commercial internet begins in the USA.

### 1989

- Death of Emperor Shōwa; change of era name.

> [!note]
> Emperor Hirohito died on 7 January 1989 and the Shōwa era gave way to Heisei. Era names (年号) still frame how Japanese people talk about time, which is why the author includes this.

### 1990

- The bubble economy collapses.

## The Arrival of Windows 95 and the Appearance of *Ayashii World* (1994–1996)

![f:id:ohstuki_zunko:20180917213617j:plain](https://cdn-ak.f.st-hatena.com/images/fotolife/o/ohstuki_zunko/20180917/20180917213617.jpg "f:id:ohstuki_zunko:20180917213617j:plain")

After the internet reached Japan, Windows 95 went on sale and became a social phenomenon.

On the internet, a bulletin board called *Ayashii World* (あやしいわーるど, "Shady World") was born. It was the birthplace of many cultural staples, such as Giko-neko, *maatari* and *tsundere*.

### 1994

- Senrigan (千里眼), Japan's first search engine, appears.

### 1995

- Windows 95 is released. The internet spreads rapidly among the general public.
- The Tokyo subway sarin attack.
- The Great Hanshin–Awaji Earthquake.

> [!note]
> The Kobe earthquake (17 January) and the Aum Shinrikyo sarin attack (20 March) came two months apart and made 1995 a traumatic year in Japan. The Japanese release of Windows 95 on 23 November 1995 drew famous midnight crowds in Akihabara, shown in the first video below.

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FvfBJapb2pw)

[*Ohayō Asahi desu* on the day of the Great Hanshin–Awaji Earthquake (阪神淡路大震災当日『おはよう朝日です』)](https://youtube.com/watch?v=FvfBJapb2pw)

### 1996

- The bulletin board *Ayashii World* is created by Masayuki Shiba. It is the ancestor of the Japanese internet underground. It was originally a website set up to distribute a tasteless game based on the subway sarin attack. Much of the culture that arose on Ayashii World went on to have a big influence on the later internet.
- Tasteless games based on the sarin attack become popular: you scatter sarin on Eidan Subway lines (as it was then called) and compete over the casualty count.
- Net buzzwords: *maatari* (マターリ), ヽ(´ー｀)ノ, 『餃』『子』『の』『王』『将』, *moe* (萌え), *Warez*, Giko-neko (ギコ猫), *tsundere* (ツンデレ).

> [!note]
> - **マターリ (*maatari*)**: a drawn-out *mattari*, "laid-back, relaxed"; it became a byword for chill, friendly posting.
> - **ヽ(´ー｀)ノ**: one of the earliest widely used Japanese text emoticons (kaomoji) of the "carefree hurray" type.
> - **『餃』『子』『の』『王』『将』**: a joke format that splits *Gyōza no Ōshō* (a ramen/gyoza chain) into one quoted character per bracket.
> - **ギコ猫 (Giko-neko)**: an ASCII-art cat, (,,ﾟДﾟ), and one of the first great AA characters, later carried over to 2channel.
> - Several of these, *moe* and *tsundere* especially, are attributed to Ayashii World by the author, but their exact origins are disputed and most only became widespread in the 2000s.

## Ayashii World Closes, *Amezō Link* Is Born, and the Move to *2channel* (1997–1999)

![f:id:ohstuki_zunko:20180917213710j:plain](https://cdn-ak.f.st-hatena.com/images/fotolife/o/ohstuki_zunko/20180917/20180917213710.jpg "f:id:ohstuki_zunko:20180917213710j:plain")

*2channel*, which would greatly change the Japanese internet, is born.

In the real world, Japan saw the Kobe child murders, i-mode, the dot-com bubble and so on.

As the end of the 20th century, it felt like a pretty hectic time.

### 1997

- The bulletin board site *Amezō Link* (あめぞうリンク) is created by Amezō.
- The Kobe child murders (the "Sakakibara" case) take place. The real name of "Boy A" is posted on Ayashii World.

> [!note]
> The killer, who called himself "Seito Sakakibara" (酒鬼薔薇聖斗) in taunting letters, was a 14-year-old. Japanese law forbids publishing the names of juvenile offenders, so the leak of his name online was an early clash between the internet and media norms.

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BBMP13iMgTM)

[Live broadcast outside the police station at Seito Sakakibara's arrest (酒鬼薔薇聖斗逮捕時警察署前中継)](https://youtube.com/watch?v=BBMP13iMgTM)

### 1998

- Ayashii World closes. A large number of boards spun off from it appear, some of which were still running as of 2016.
- A thread-float style board (the format of today's 2channel), the Amezō board, is set up on *Amezō Link*. The first boards were "BBS News Flash" and "Stock News Flash".
- Among large bulletin board sites, which were considered hard to read, this system was groundbreaking at the time and became explosively popular.
- "Hiroyuki" appears around this time.
- Computers were still expensive and internet access was rare and costly, so users were limited to enthusiasts. Apart from chit-chat, topics are said to have been mostly specialised and advanced: stocks, economics, social affairs, computers and so on.

> [!note]
> **Thread-float (スレッドフロート型)** means that whichever thread gets a new reply jumps to the top of the board, the familiar "bumping" model of 2channel and later 4chan.
>
> **Hiroyuki** is Hiroyuki Nishimura (西村博之), founder of 2channel and later owner of 4chan (see 2015).

### 1999

- The golden age of text sites (テキストサイト).
- The bill for a Basic Act on Countermeasures Against Harmful Social Environments for Youth.
- Amezō's user numbers grow, making it hard to secure servers and bringing more trolls; the site runs into all sorts of operational problems.
- That same year, answering Amezō's call for someone to take the site over, one of its users, Hiroyuki, creates "2channel" (2ちゃんねる). At the time it was positioned as "Amezō's second channel". Around 10,000 visitors a day.
- The dot-com bubble.
- The otaku pop band "Tenchu" (天誅) makes a big breakthrough.
- NTT DoCoMo announces i-mode.
- The Toshiba complainer incident (東芝クレーマー事件).

> [!note]
> - **Text sites** were personal, humour-focused homepages built mostly from writing; the precursor of blogs.
> - **i-mode** let Japanese feature phones access the web years before smartphones, which is a big part of why Japan's mobile internet culture developed differently.
> - **Toshiba complainer incident**: a customer posted a recording of a Toshiba support employee calling him a *kureemaa* ("crank complainer"). It went viral and forced a public apology from Toshiba, an early example of an individual beating a big company through the internet.

## "If you can't tell lies are lies, it's hard (to use a bulletin board)" (2000–2003)

![f:id:ohstuki_zunko:20180917213925p:plain](https://cdn-ak.f.st-hatena.com/images/fotolife/o/ohstuki_zunko/20180917/20180917213925.png "f:id:ohstuki_zunko:20180917213925p:plain")

Because the perpetrator of the Nishitetsu bus hijacking had posted on 2ch as "Neo Mugicha" before the crime, 2channel started appearing in the news. The whole phenomenon of computers and the internet transforming society and spreading rapidly among people was dubbed the "IT revolution", which won the Buzzword of the Year award.

Meanwhile 2channel, which had become a major force on the internet, faced closure because it couldn't pay its server bills due to soaring traffic.

This is also when TDN, the progenitor of *Inmu* memes, had his appearance in a gay video SCOOOP!!!ed by a sports tabloid.

> [!note]
> **淫夢 (*Inmu*, "lewd dream")** refers to memes built from *Manatsu no Yo no Inmu* ("A Midsummer Night's Lewd Dream", 2001), a low-budget gay adult video. Its stilted dialogue became a vast, notoriously crude in-joke subculture on Niconico from around 2012. TDN is the nickname of one of the actors. "SCOOOP!!!" is itself written in Inmu-style emphasis.

It was also around this time that the September 11 attacks happened and the War on Terror waged by the USA and a coalition of allies began.

### 2000

- The Amezō board is wrecked by script trolling and closes the same year.
- The Nishitetsu bus hijacking. Because the perpetrator had posted on 2ch, Hiroyuki appears on TV for the first time.
- The IT revolution ("ITC revolution").

> 「(この掲示板は)匿名というのが前提になりますので、うそもあるし、ひどいことも書かれます。そういうのをうそはうそであるとか、見抜ける人でないと(掲示板を使うのは)難しいものがあるでしょう」
>
> "(This bulletin board) is premised on anonymity, so there are lies, and terrible things get written. Unless you're someone who can see through that, who can tell that lies are lies, I think it would be difficult (to use the board)."
> — Hiroyuki

> 「バーチャル・リアリティは悪であるということをハッキリと言う」
>
> "We should state clearly that virtual reality is evil."
> — National Commission on Educational Reform (Mori cabinet)

- Buzzwords: "IT revolution" (IT革命), *Ohhaa!* (おっはー)

> [!note]
> 「嘘を嘘と見抜ける人でないと（掲示板を使うのは）難しい」 became Hiroyuki's most famous line and is still quoted as a basic rule of internet literacy in Japan. The bus hijacker was a 17-year-old who posted under the handle ネオむぎ茶 ("Neo Barley Tea") shortly before the May 2000 hijacking. *Ohhaa!* was a greeting popularised by children's TV host Shingo Katori.

### 2001

- 1ch.tv is born as a rival to 2ch. Amezō and Kazuhiko Nishi are involved.
- *A Midsummer Night's Lewd Dream* (真夏の夜の淫夢) is released.
- The September 11 attacks.
- ADSL starts to spread: Year One of broadband.
- Around this time the dot-com bubble bursts.
- The Yoshinoya copypasta (吉野家コピペ) is born.
- Senkōsha (先行者) appears.
- Tashiro the God (田代神).
- The first *MAD GEAR SOLID* is released. "I'm overflowing with lust" (性欲をもてあます).
- 2ch's "August Crisis" hits due to soaring traffic. Although the site is pushed to the brink of closure, residents of the UNIX board cut traffic to 1/16 and it survives.
- The UD cancer research project using grid computing begins. Until its end in 2007, Team 2ch is a major contributor.
- Arrests over WinMX. 「かぎのおとが　おやがかぎわたしや　もうだめぽ」 ("the sound of keys / my parents gave them the key / it's over-po").
- Flash animations using Doraemon characters become popular (later known as "Ora sites" (オラサイト)).
- The Japanese Wikipedia is born.
- "Sha-mail" (写メール, picture mail) becomes popular.
- "Eight-head-tall Monar is creepy" (８頭身のモナーはキモイ).
- From Futaba Channel: the collage image *Masa ni gedō* ("Truly a fiend", まさに外道), using a panel from Gataro Man's *Jigoku Kōshien*, becomes popular.

> [!note]
> - **Yoshinoya copypasta**: a long rant about non-regulars ruining the beef bowl chain Yoshinoya ("They're not playing around at Yoshinoya!"). One of the most famous 2ch copypastas, and later loosely translated to English-language boards.
> - **Senkōsha**: a Chinese humanoid robot prototype (2000) whose design (complete with a cannon jutting from its crotch) was mocked on the text site *Samurai Damashii*, one of the first big viral hits on the Japanese web.
> - **Tashiro the God**: 2ch users mass-voted comedian Masashi Tashiro, then in disgrace over a voyeurism arrest, to the top of *Time* magazine's online Person of the Year poll, an early organised "raid".
> - **もうだめぽ (*mou dame po*)**: "It's hopeless", with a cutesy *-po* ending. The garbled, unconverted hiragana post was supposedly typed in panic by a WinMX user hearing someone at the door; *mou dame po* became a stock phrase for despair.
> - **Monar (モナー)** is a classic 2ch ASCII-art cat; an elongated "eight-head-tall" (八頭身) variant pursued him in a long-running gag.
> - **Ora sites**: Doraemon parody Flash animations, named after characters' *ora* ("hey!").

### 2002

- *Chinko Ondo* (ちんこ音頭, "Willy Dance") becomes popular.
- The euro enters circulation in Europe.
- Takara files a trademark application for Giko-neko (later withdrawn).

> [!note]
> - **Muneo House**: remixes of politician Muneo Suzuki's testimony during a 2002 Diet scandal ("Muneo House" was a nickname for a facility he allegedly steered funding to). One of the first Japanese audio-MAD phenomena.
> - **Giko-neko trademark**: a backlash similar to the later Nomaneko affair (2005); netizens objected to a company claiming a character created anonymously by the community.
> - **CAPTAIN System**: NTT's 1980s videotex service, a pre-internet online network.
> - **Shōnan Beach Clean-up OFF**: a large offline meetup where 2ch users gathered to clean beaches, which also earned 2ch some positive press. "OFF" (オフ会) means an offline meetup.
> - **Red & White Flash Battle**: a year-end Flash animation festival, named after NHK's *Kōhaku Uta Gassen* New Year's Eve singing contest.
> - **Tama-chan**: a bearded seal that wandered into the Tama River in Tokyo and became a national celebrity.
> - **ぬるぽ (*nurupo*)**: from Java's NullPointerException. Anyone who posts it gets "hit" with the reply ｶﾞｯ (*Ga!*), as in the familiar AA 「(　・∀・)　 | 　|　ｶﾞｯ」.
> - **Yutori education**: the "relaxed education" curriculum. "Yutori" later became a slur for the generation educated under it (see 2007).

> - **Miko Miko Nurse**: an adult game whose saccharine theme song got far bigger online than the game itself. Its singer's identity stayed secret until 2013.

## The Birth of News VIP and the *Densha Otoko* Boom (2004–2005)

![f:id:ohstuki_zunko:20180917214039j:plain](https://cdn-ak.f.st-hatena.com/images/fotolife/o/ohstuki_zunko/20180917/20180917214039.jpg "f:id:ohstuki_zunko:20180917214039j:plain")

A (probably) true love story from the internet was published as a book, made into a film and a TV drama as *Densha Otoko* ("Train Man"), and public perception of otaku changed dramatically. On 2channel, a "kuso function" was introduced that let users vote to exile the crap threads flooding the News Flash board. As the place of exile, "News Flash (VIP)" was created. It went on to give birth to much of today's internet culture, like "ｗｗｗｗｗｗｗｗ".

> [!note]
> **ｗ** stands for *warai* ("laugh"), the Japanese equivalent of "lol"; strings of it (ｗｗｗ) are read as "kusa" ("grass"), since they look like a lawn. **VIP** (ニュー速VIP) became the Japanese counterpart of 4chan's /b/.

YouTube was also born around this time. I seem to remember tons of TV anime being uploaded back then.

Everyone's beloved THE IDOLM@STER also dates from this period.

I didn't watch the *Densha Otoko* TV drama back then because I hated TV, but maybe I'll rent the DVD and give it a look.

### 2004

- The "Nandemo Jikkyō J" board (なんでも実況J, "Anything Live Commentary J") is born.
- "News Flash (VIP)" is born, created as a dumping ground for the crap-thread repellent function introduced on News Flash.
- VIPPERs develop a unique culture; early forms of what would later become "I tried making it" (作ってみた), "I tried singing it" (歌ってみた) and "I tried dancing it" (踊ってみた) start to appear.

## Niconico Douga and Twitter Are Born (2006–2007)

![f:id:ohstuki_zunko:20180917214211p:plain](https://cdn-ak.f.st-hatena.com/images/fotolife/o/ohstuki_zunko/20180917/20180917214211.png "f:id:ohstuki_zunko:20180917214211p:plain")

The culture of News VIP, then at its peak, flowed into Niconico Douga, and things like "ｗｗｗｗｗ" came to Niconico. Hatsune Miku was also born around this time.

VIP holds a "festival" to push its ending song to No. 1 on Oricon, and lots of "I tried dancing" videos of "Hare Hare Yukai" are uploaded to Niconico Douga and YouTube. The same thing happens the following year with "Motteke! Sailor Fuku", the OP of *Lucky☆Star*.

> - **思い出は億千万**: a line from the *Rockman 2* (Mega Man 2) remix song, a Niconico staple.
> - **た〜らこ〜**: the hypnotic song from a Kewpie *tarako* pasta-sauce ad.

## The Spread of Smartphones, the Akihabara Massacre, and the Great East Japan Earthquake (2008–2012)

![f:id:ohstuki_zunko:20180917214311j:plain](https://cdn-ak.f.st-hatena.com/images/fotolife/o/ohstuki_zunko/20180917/20180917214311.jpg "f:id:ohstuki_zunko:20180917214311j:plain")

Smartphones, which would greatly change the internet, began to spread, triggered by the iPhone.

As Niconico Douga gradually took off, the characters commonly used on the internet shifted from Monar to Yaruo, and from Yaruo to Yukkuri. MMD also appeared around this time. An amendment to the Copyright Act that attached criminal penalties to knowingly downloading illegal files caused controversy.

> [!note]
> The 2010 amendment (see 2010) made knowingly downloading illegally uploaded music and video unlawful but with no penalty. Criminal penalties were added by a further amendment in October 2012.

Yuzuki Yukari was born in the early 2010s.

### 2008

- Yaruo (やる夫) appears around this time.
- The *Charge Man Ken!* boom (チャー研) is covered by NHK.
- "Niconico Grand Conference 2008" (ニコニコ大会議2008) is held. Unlike today's Chōkaigi, it was more like an Apple product launch.
- Bossan (ぼっさん) disappears.
- MikuMikuDance is released.
- The Akihabara massacre (秋葉原通り魔事件).
- The iPhone 3G is released in Japan by SoftBank Mobile.
- "Anonymous", a group originating from 4chan, appears. Radical offshoots like LulzSec later emerge from it and go on to cause the PSN personal data breach.
- Trends: "I'm different from you" (あなたとは違うんです), "Rozen Asō" (ローゼン麻生), "Take it easy!!!" (ゆっくりしていってね!!!), "Mainichi Hentai Shimbun" (毎日変態新聞), "No, it's kefir." (いいえ、ケフィアです。), "XX? I raised 'em" (●●はわしが育てた), "big wave" (ビッグウェーブ).

> [!note]
> - **Yaruo**: an AA character (a round, smug face) who stars in *yaruo* stories, long serialised fiction told entirely in ASCII art.
> - **Yukkuri**: the "Yukkuri shiteitte ne!!!" ("Take it easy!!!") disembodied heads of Reimu and Marisa from Touhou, now famous as the synthetic-voice narrators of countless Japanese explainer videos.
> - **Charge Man Ken!**: a 1974 anime so cheaply made it became a cult comedy on Niconico.
> - **Akihabara massacre**: on 8 June 2008, Tomohiro Katō drove a truck into a crowd in Akihabara and stabbed people, killing seven. He had narrated his plans on a mobile phone bulletin board beforehand.
> - **Anonymous / PSN**: Anonymous did attack Sony in 2011, but responsibility for the massive PlayStation Network breach that year was never conclusively established. LulzSec did hack Sony Pictures.
> - **あなたとは違うんです**: PM Yasuo Fukuda's irritated retort to a reporter at his 2008 resignation press conference: "I can see myself objectively. I'm different from you."
> - **ローゼン麻生**: PM Tarō Asō, a self-declared manga fan, was rumoured to read the manga *Rozen Maiden*; the net loved him for it.
> - **毎日変態新聞**: the *Mainichi Shimbun*'s English site ran a column ("WaiWai") for years that presented lurid, often fabricated stories about Japanese sexual mores to foreign readers; the 2008 exposure led to this nickname.
> - **わしが育てた**: an AA of an old man claiming credit ("I raised them") for any famous person or success.
> - **ビッグウェーブ**: a man queuing for the iPhone 3G launch told TV 「乗るしかない、このビッグウェーブに」 ("I've got no choice but to ride this big wave").

## The Decline of *2channel* (2013–)

![f:id:ohstuki_zunko:20180917214451j:plain](https://cdn-ak.f.st-hatena.com/images/fotolife/o/ohstuki_zunko/20180917/20180917214451.jpg "f:id:ohstuki_zunko:20180917214451j:plain")

2channel's dominance, which peaked in the 2000s, starts to fade, and the centre of internet communities is gradually taken over by Twitter and other social networks. With smartphones spreading too, the internet, once the domain of a few eccentrics, suddenly goes mainstream. As a result, careless *enjō* over people bragging about their crimes are in the news daily.

For better or worse, the line between the internet and real society gradually blurs.

On the technical side, HTML5 is born. The major browsers start dropping Flash support, and the move away from Flash becomes certain. Topics like cryptocurrency and VR also start to stand out around this time.

### 2013

- Mr. 47, the developer of Winny, dies.
- The "2channel personal data leak" happens. From around this time people start whispering about 2ch's decline.
- Crime-bragging on Twitter ("*Bakattā*", バカッター) becomes a social problem.
- A Niconico *utaite* is arrested for sexual misconduct with an underage girl. "I knew it was bad, but I couldn't control my lust." (やばいと思ったが、性欲を抑えきれなかった)
- Former NSA employee Edward Snowden exposes the NSA's methods of collecting personal information.
- Windows Live Messenger shuts down.
- LINE passes 100 million registered users.
- Voice actress Kahoru Sasajima reveals that she was the singer of the theme song of *Miko Miko Nurse*, released in 2003.
- Penguin collages (ペンギンコラ).
- "When? Now!" (今でしょ！), "Double payback!" (倍返しだ！), "I'll exterminate them" (駆逐してやる), "Nyanpasu~" (にゃんぱすー).

> [!note]
> - **2channel data leak**: payment data and real names of users of 2ch's paid "●" (*Maru*) accounts were leaked, de-anonymising many posts, including some by public figures.
> - **バカッター (*Bakattā*)**: *baka* ("idiot") + Twitter, for young people posting photos of themselves in restaurant fridges and the like.
> - **やばいと思ったが、性欲を抑えきれなかった**: the confession-style phrase had already been a meme from earlier news reports; this case gave it a new round.
> - **今でしょ！**: cram-school teacher Osamu Hayashi's catchphrase. **倍返しだ！**: from the hit drama *Hanzawa Naoki*. **駆逐してやる**: Eren's line from *Attack on Titan*. **にゃんぱすー**: Renge's greeting from *Non Non Biyori*.

### 2014

- Jim kicks Hiroyuki out.
- The "2channel family feud" (2ちゃんねるお家騒動) takes place. Satoru Yano, who runs Jimaku.in, creates open2ch, and Hiroyuki, kicked out of 2ch, creates 2ch.sc. From then on, 2channel becomes a three-way battle.
- Assemblyman Nonomura's press conference.
- The offline meet with 0 attendees (オフ会0人).
- HTML5 becomes a W3C Recommendation.
- "Ganbare♥ ganbare♥" (がんばれ♥がんばれ♥)

> [!note]
> - **Jim**: Jim Watkins, the American owner of 2ch's server company, who took control of the 2ch.net domain from Hiroyuki in 2014. He later owned 8chan/8kun.
> - **open2ch**: the author dates its birth to 2012 above, but here credits Satoru Yano with creating it during the 2014 feud; both claims appear in the original.
> - **Nonomura**: Hyōgo assemblyman Ryūtarō Nonomura, questioned about expense fraud, burst into extraordinary wailing sobs at his press conference. The footage went around the world.
> - **オフ会0人**: syamu_game (see the "to add" list) held a fan meetup that nobody attended, then uploaded a video about it.

- "SUSHI Tabetai feat. Soy Sauce" (SUSHI食べたい feat.ソイソース).

> [!note]
> - **例の紐**: the blue string under the goddess Hestia's chest in *Is It Wrong to Try to Pick Up Girls in a Dungeon?* (DanMachi).
> - **Olympic emblem**: designer Kenjirō Sano's emblem was withdrawn after internet sleuths found it closely resembled a Belgian theatre logo and turned up other copied work.
> - **4chan**: Hiroyuki bought 4chan from its founder moot (Christopher Poole) in September 2015.
> - **Nohara Hiroshi's Lunchtime Ways**: a *Crayon Shin-chan* spin-off about Shin-chan's father's lunches. Readers joked that its gourmet-obsessed protagonist is so unlike the real Hiroshi that he must be "a stranger who thinks he is Hiroshi Nohara".

> [!note]
> - **保育園落ちた日本死ね**: an anonymous mother's furious post after her child was rejected from daycare. It was raised in the Diet and made the year's buzzword list.
> - **Emperor's statement**: Emperor Akihito's video message hinting at his wish to abdicate, which led to the 2019 abdication and the start of the Reiwa era.
> - **Yūsaku PSA series**: fake public-service warnings using a photo of an Inmu actor with a stern face.
> - **性の喜びおじさん**: a street preacher filmed on Christmas Eve ranting at couples: 「性の喜びを知りやがって！」 ("Ugh, you've learned the joy of sex!").
> - **ぽきたw魔剤ンゴ！？**: a parody of teen slang: *pokita* ("I woke up"), *mazai* ("magic drug", i.e. energy drinks like Monster), with Nan-J's *-nge* ending.
> - **PPAP**: Pikotarō's "Pen-Pineapple-Apple-Pen".

> [!note]
> - **5channel**: in October 2017 the 2ch.net site, controlled by Jim Watkins, was renamed 5ch.net after a dispute over the "2channel" trademark with Hiroyuki's camp.
> - **Salt guy**: Turkish chef Nusret Gökçe, "Salt Bae".
> - **ほう……炭酸抜きコーラですか**: a line from the *Baki* martial-arts manga, where a character is impressed that his opponent drinks flat cola for energy.

> - **Basilisk Time**: from the *Basilisk* pachislot machine's bonus round.
> - **熱盛**: TV Asahi's *Hōdō Station* flashed its "Atsumori!" ("Hot!") graphic by mistake, and the anchor's straight-faced apology, 「熱盛と出てしまいました」 ("'Atsumori' came up by accident"), became a meme.
