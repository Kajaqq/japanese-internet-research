## The History of the Japanese Internet You Won't Find in Textbooks ヽ(´ー｀)人(´ー｀)ノ

## Translator's Note

English translation of Barubora's (ばるぼら) timeline, first published 2002/09/30. Site names are given in English or romaji, with the original Japanese at first mention so they can still be searched for. All links point to the same Wayback Machine snapshots as the original. Recurring terms:

- **Pasokon tsūshin (パソコン通信)** – the pre-internet dial-up "PC communication" services, both commercial (NiftyServe, PC-VAN, ASCII-NET) and hobbyist.
- **Grassroots BBS (草の根BBS)** – a small, privately run dial-up BBS, as opposed to the big commercial networks. A **host program** (ホストプログラム, "hospro") is the software that runs one.
- **Forum / SIG / patio** – sub-communities and private rooms on the commercial networks.
- **UG / angura (アングラ)** – "underground": sites about hacking, cracking, warez, proxies, shock content and other grey-zone material.
- **Warez / ware (割れ)** – pirated software. **Serials** – stolen registration codes.
- **Kushi (串, "skewer")** – slang for a proxy server.
- **Arashi (荒らし)** – trolling, vandalising or flooding a message board. Tools that automate it are often named "…duke".
- **Chūbō (厨房)** – literally "kitchen", a pun on 中坊 (middle-schooler): immature newbie troublemakers.
- **Denpa (電波, "radio waves")** – sites by people who seem to be receiving transmissions: unhinged, crackpot or unintentionally surreal pages.
- **Tasteless (テイストレス)** – shock/gore content sites.
- **Shishobako (私書箱, "PO box")** – a culture of small, password-protected rental image boards ("boxes"), often used for trading porn, gore and warez.
- **Text site (テキストサイト)** – personal sites built around humorous essays and diaries. **Personal news site (個人ニュースサイト)** – one-person link-and-comment news blogs.
- **Antenna (アンテナ)** – an update-checker page that lists which sites on a watch list were updated, and when.
- **Net idol (ネットアイドル)** – a self-made internet celebrity, usually a young woman with a personal homepage.
- **Moe (萌え)** – fannish affection for (usually) fictional characters.
- **Thread-float (フロート型) board / "age"** – a board where a thread with a new reply jumps back to the top.
- **Tereho (テレホーダイ, "Telehōdai")** – NTT's flat-rate night-time phone plan (11 p.m.–8 a.m.), which shaped when the Japanese internet was busiest.

## An Overblown Preface

This HTML document is a timeline of a very small part of the history of the Japanese internet. Sadly, I don't have the talent to write entertaining prose that delights people, or clever prose that inspires deep insight. Still, I like to think it turned out somewhat better than the boring "internet theory" that's everywhere these days, the kind that keeps forcing grand overviews out of scant source material.

Someday, when a middle-school history teacher, textbook in hand, says "Once upon a time there was a closed network, limited to the Earth alone, called the Internet…", almost none of the things covered here will be mentioned. Maybe that's fine. Old stories are something to go with a drink. When was the time *you* had the most fun on the internet?

## A Very Personal Timeline of the Internet and Its Surroundings

### 1992 (Heisei 4)

**03/23**

Grassroots BBS [ZOB Station BBS](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.zob.ne.jp/) begins alpha-test operation (public test operation from 03/25). Host program: "mmm".

**03/30**

ASCII-NET, the largest commercial BBS, re-merges its four networks: ACS, PCS, MSX and DPI.

**04/06**

Windows 3.1 released.

**09/30**

**[Japan's first homepage](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.tsukuba.org/www/)**, the [KEK Entry Point](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.kek.jp/), is born. It was put online by Dr. Yōhei Morita of the Computing Research Center at the Ministry of Education's High Energy Accelerator Research Organization (KEK) in Tsukuba, Ibaraki. As you can tell from the content, it was very much an academic affair.

**11/05**

NiftyServe changes its message-board system.

> When you post to a board, your registered name (not necessarily your real name) is now displayed automatically.

So it goes. Along with this, "Free Talk" closed. In its place, *BBS#8 "Spirit (Kokoro) Corner"* started.

### 1993 (Heisei 5)

**02/xx**

Web browser *Mosaic* released. It's called "the internet's first killer app", and *WWW traffic reportedly grew by 341,634% in a single year*. The current mess that is HTML began with this browser's proprietary extensions.

**05/02**

The underground game "The Ambition of Sun Myung Moon, Reissue Edition" (文鮮明の野望、復刻版), in which you convert 25,000 Unification Church followers in the Kantō region and build a political (electoral) base against the Japanese state, is distributed, probably(?) on Nifty. The oldest underground game still circulating online.

**09/01**

Grassroots BBS *Center Net* (センターネット) opens. [Say Whatever You Like 120%](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.nurs.or.jp/%7Enspixp/nakaya/center_bth.txt) (言いたい放題１２０％). There is now a [Center Net alumni association](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://web.intaa.net/center/) site.

**10/xx**

"VS" (ver0.50) appears, a host program by Tamu (Takuya Yamazaki) that was as famous as "KTBBS". The official public version, ver1.0, followed about a year later on 1994/11/08. The final version, ver1.31β6, was distributed on 1996/12/29.

[TWICS](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.twics.co.jp/) launches Japan's first full internet connection service for individuals.

**11/xx**

[Internet Initiative Japan](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.iij.ad.jp/info/history.html) launches a dedicated-line TCP/IP connection service (the company was founded in '92).

**12/01**

Nippon Telegraph and Telephone (NTT) opens "What's New in Japan" (日本の新着情報).

### 1994 (Heisei 6)

- Was the internet populated only by intellectuals and university people? As of 1994/01/20, there were 14 institutions running WWW servers in Japan.

**01/14**

A couple in Akishima, Tokyo cause an uproar by trying to register their newborn son under the name "Akuma" (Devil).

**02/08?**

Media artist Kōichirō Etō's site "Eto Kouichirou's home page" (now [eto](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.eto.com/)) opens. At the time it was hosted on Keio SFC's server.

**03/xx**

Linux 1.0 released.

**04/15**

On PC-VAN, issue #1 of *Omanta Magazine* (おまんたマガジン) comes out. It's ridiculously funny; if you haven't read it, please do. The author is Anguirus of "Anguirus Net".

> It goes without saying that the greatest thrill of pasokon tsūshin lies in the arguments, fights and slanging matches that break out all over the SIGs. Pasokon tsūshin is a safari park where you can watch idiocy so extreme you'll rarely see it in normal society; a retirement home where you can spend your entire life with the brain of a primary-school kid; and a museum of hygiene that gives vulgar, filthy things new meaning and turns them into entertainment.

**04/26**

Toshihiro Takada's personal site [Alternative Toshi TAKADA](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.st.rim.or.jp/~takadat/) opens. At the time, Takada was at NTT's Basic Research Laboratories and was the driving force behind the NTT site mentioned above.

**04/xx**

Grassroots BBS *UL-net* opens, set up by a mail-order radio equipment shop called Aero Com.

> It shot to fame after the magazine SPA! featured it without permission, in a piece by Masaaki Aoyama (the kichiku-style writer famous for *Abunai Ichigō*). After that, its delicate balance collapsed, it eventually even produced arrests, and it shut down.

**06/01**

Fujitsu's internet service provider InfoWeb launches.

**08/09**

*Japan Edge*, a techno event info site, a.k.a. "The archive of Japanese street and underground culture", opens. There are both Japanese and English versions.

> A trio based at the [Kyushu Institute of Technology](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.kyutech.ac.jp/), who call themselves, of all things, [Analyzing Terrorists](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://digibi.com/AT/at.html), have opened [Japan Edge](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://digibi.com/index.html), a page packed with techno/club information, on the internet. This magazine got an invitation by e-mail too. Put simply, it's a fun project to broadcast techno/club information to the world over the international computer network.

**08/14**

The "Mika-chan Incident" (美香ちゃん事件) happens on NiftyServe. For details, see [UL-net SYSOP](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.nurs.or.jp/~nspixp/ul/sysop.html).

**08/31**

Issue #2 of *Omanta Magazine* comes out; it's the final issue. Issue #1 had been deleted by PC-VAN. Issues #3 onward were supposedly continued on "Anguirus Net", or maybe not.

**09/14**

On @Nifty, this is the day [Reikon CH (Spirit Channel)](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www2.plala.or.jp/tanpui/) (霊魂ＣＨ) began. You can also see the handle "Reikon" on early "Ayashii World". Rumour has it that R00T ZER0 of "DEF CON ZERO" was a Reikon too.

> Reikon is an intellectual collective that gathers in NiftyServe's CB (chat) room A-8 every Friday from 11 p.m. until dawn, all using the handle "Reikon", and holds high-level debates of every kind under cover of anonymity. (Really? lol)

**09/16**

The magazine *Backup Katsuyō Technique* (Sansai Books) is renamed *Game Lab* (ゲームラボ).

**09/17**

Impress launches [iNTERNET magazine](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://internet.impress.co.jp/) (preview issue 1994/07/27). The first internet information magazine.

**09/xx**

UL-net shuts down after an arrest for drug dealing.

Individual ISP service *[Bekkoame](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.bekkoame.ne.jp/)* (ベッコアメ) launches.

> The internet back then offered no firm vision of what you could actually do with it, yet just connecting was extremely expensive. I was angry at the prices and thought, I'll start a company that lets people connect much more cheaply. So first I asked to place an ad in the first issue of Internet Magazine, then came back and thought about setting up the company.

**10/01**

Joi Ito's site *Tomigaya* (富ケ谷) officially opens as version 2.0 (ver1.0 had been around a little earlier). The TRATTORIA and Chibarei sites used to live here too. Ito's current site is [Joi Ito's Web](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://joi.ito.com/jp/).

**10/27**

HotWired's website launches in the US, carrying an AT&T ad at 470 x 76 dpi. *The beginning of banner advertising.* Sorry, I know this has nothing to do with Japanese history.

**10/xx**

[RimNet](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.rim.or.jp/) starts ISP service for individuals.

**11/02**

Personal site "HIROSHIGE Takuji's Home Page" opens. On 11/04 of the same year, "Club HARU SION" (春紫苑; now [Club HAL](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.tky.3web.ne.jp/~thir/)) starts. Famous for legal drugs. (Huh?)

**11/08**

Host program "VS" (ver1.0) officially released.

**11/18**

The Rolling Stones broadcast their concert in Dallas, Texas live over the internet. The Stones' site opened in early September 1996.

**11/27**

Personal site "Koichi Matsuda's Home page" opens. Its "Nethackers" page was popular. Its "Shōjin Nikki" (精進日記, Devotion Diary), started 1995/02/06, was reportedly the fourth site ever registered on the famous "Tsuda Diary Links".

**11/xx**

Taketo Tamura starts "Searcher in Waseda". Renamed *Senrigan* (千里眼, Clairvoyance) on 1995/11/06.

**12/30**

Yasuyuki Sugiura completes host program [MPNBBS](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://member.nifty.ne.jp/sugisugi/) (Ver0.00). Full operation began with Ver0.10 on 1995/01/04. The final version, Ver3.00, was released on 1996/10/01, and Ver3.10, which integrated sei-t's Z-MODEM protocol, was distributed on 1999/07/18.

**12/xx**

Web browser *Netscape 1.0* released.

**Undated**

Online software guide site *Akiu Mado* (秋保窓) opens; it later became [Mado no Mori](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.forest.impress.co.jp/) (窓の杜, Forest of Windows). This site (well, mailing list) regularly reported new software and version updates, and is the origin of the Japanese news site. Am I overstating it?

### 1995 (Heisei 7)

- The internet is an empty cave.
- NHK-BS airs "NewBreed", a programme about the internet, with appearances by Timothy Leary and John C. Lilly. TURN ON, TUNE IN, "TAKE OVER".
- Shōeisha publishes *Net Travelers '95*.

**01/17**

Personal site "Nohtomi's HomePage" opens. At first it was mostly a ramen diary. Its link collection of girls' pages only, *[We Love Network Beauty](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.st.rim.or.jp/~nohtomi/n-beauty.html)* (We Love 電脳美女), started on 08/20.

**01/19**

The "Chiba Reiko Kaibunsho" (an anonymous smear letter about Reiko Chiba) circulates on Nifty. See also [To the Otaku Bastard Who Wrote the Chiba Reiko Letter](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.nurs.or.jp/~nspixp/chibarei/chibarape2.txt). Reiko Chiba is, as you know, a "digital creator" (lol). She retired from showbiz in '95 and founded Cherry Babe Inc. For more on this, see her official site [CherryBabe](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.cherrybabe.co.jp/).

> It might also be good to throw in little titbits, like how, for the column she used to write in ASAHI Pasokon, she couldn't write Japanese so she sent it in English every time, driving her editor crazy, and on top of that sent a huge amount of copy for a half-page and then made outrageous demands like "don't cut anything."

**01/xx**

SoftBank launches the internet magazine *Internet User*. Suspended with the September 1997 issue (on sale August 1997).

**02/16**

[IBM Japan](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.ibm.com/jp/) launches IBM Global Network (IBM internet connection service).

**02/22**

NEC launches the provider service "mesh".

**03/19**

★Ashura♪ "prophesies" on PC-VAN that a police raid is about to hit Kamikuishiki village (where Aum was holed up).

> Hiroshi Kume of News Station says: "How could he have known about the raid in advance?"

**03/27**

Japan's first adult site, *[Tokyo Topless](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.tokyotopless.com/)* (formerly "UTAMARO - Virtual Tokyo Night -"), opens.

**03/31**

[nozomi Ohmori SF page](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.ltokyo.com/ohmori/) opens. Nozomi Ōmori is an SF translator and critic.

**03/xx**

"MixJuice", a website carrying the contents of the various information magazines published by [Recruit Co.](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.recruit.co.jp/), opens.

**04/01**

[CLUBKING](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.clubking.com/), the website of Club King Inc., famous for the free paper *dictionary*, opens.

**04/xx**

Grassroots BBS "Chijō no Rakuen" (地上の楽園, Paradise on Earth) opens. "Completely anonymous, post anything, no deletions."

[BNN Inc.](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.bnn.co.jp/) launches *MEDIA FRONT*, a general magazine on the internet and other media (suspended with issue 9 in February of the following year, 1996). On MEDIA FRONT, see [Media Front (99.7.23)](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www02.so-net.ne.jp/~cbx/enjoy990723.html). It included things like "Anatero Tsūshin" by Kyushu's "analyzing terrorist".

**05/18?**

"Tsuda Diary Links" (津田日記リンクス) opens.

> Then another clever student had the idea of making a diary link collection that anyone could join. Open-participation link collections had existed for quite a while, like the "Link Free Club". The second stage of web diaries began when someone tried to turn one into a diary link collection.

**05/xx**

Legendary UG site **Chikadō Iriguchi** (地下道入口, "Underpass Entrance"; known as "Chikadō") opens (moved to RimNet in August).

> A homepage run by Jun Arai. Japan's first serious hardcore underground site, with an extensive collection of links to underground pages and newsgroups, both Japanese and foreign. It closed for various reasons, beginning with the arrest of CC, to the great regret of many. Many of the Japanese underground pages still around today were influenced by this site.

**07/01**

The final version, 6.21, of *KTBBS (Kuki Turbo Bulletin Board System)*, the representative host program for grassroots BBSes, is released. KTBBS was a modification, by the Kuki PC Users Club, of "Turbo Bulletin Board System (TBBS)", originally written in 1985 by Canadian Robert H. Maxwell. Judging by the version history, the oldest release, Version 1.21, was distributed on 1987/06/24.

**07/25?**

*[Internet Rescue](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.rescue.ne.jp/)* (インターネットレスキュー) opens. Anyone who knows message boards has passed through here at least once. Wasn't the very first board CGI script they handed out "WWW Keijiban (wwwbbs)"?

**08/01**

Mac news site *[MacTree](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.sannet.ne.jp/mactree/)* opens (called "WelcomeHome" until the 15th).

**08/20**

Within "Nohtomi's HomePage", the girls'-pages-only link collection *[We Love Network Beauty](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.st.rim.or.jp/~nohtomi/n-beauty.html)* starts.

**08/xx**

Web browser Internet Explorer 1.0 released.

*Telehōdai* 1800 (local calls) begins. Telehōdai 3600 (adjacent areas) from 1995/10. INS Telehōdai from 1996/02.

**09/01**

*[Nandemo Ranking](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://203.138.101.131/rank/)* (何でもランキング, Rank Anything) opens. One of the few hit sites in an era when the internet had little entertainment.

**09/25**

The Yomiuri Shimbun runs articles headlined "A Rush of 'Construction' on the Internet" and "Shopping in a Shopping Street on Your PC Screen".

**09/xx**

Japanese search service Japan Search Engine launches (founded 5/17); it later became DRAGON. It started as a project by students in Kyoto and was incorporated in May '96. In October, the translation search service Dragon Search Engine also launched.

**10/15**

On a NiftyServe board, the "Kanae Miyata Incident" (宮田かなえ事件), apparently a copycat of last year's "Mika-chan Incident", takes place. For details, see [Kansai Jitsuwa: Naniwa Cyber Detectives Compilation](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.nichikon.co.jp/jitu1-12.html). Shiba of "Ayashii World" also recalls the scene.

> Loads of men fell for it beautifully, and once again their lewd, pervy e-mails got posted on the BBS. I happened to be on CB (chat) at the time, so I could watch the posts pile up on the BBS in real time. Needless to say, I downloaded every single one.

(Author's note: "once again" means "just like in the Mika-chan Incident".)

**10/21**

[Matt's Script Archive](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.scriptarchive.com/), the most famous CGI script distribution site overseas, releases *[WWWBoard](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.scriptarchive.com/wwwboard.html)*, a tree-style message board used all over the world.

**10/27**

The University of Tokyo launches the search engine ODIN.

**11/01**

On NiftyServe, **Mega Nikki** (メガ日記, Mega Diary) begins: "a project to collect the diaries of many people in one place for 100 days".

> During the Great Hanshin Earthquake, what we saw on TV was the view looking down from a helicopter, with a death-toll counter on the screen, and the number kept shooting up. It was the number of people who had died, but I had no real sense that thousands of people were dead. And that scared me. If it were somewhere abroad I could understand it, but it wasn't that far away and yet it felt completely unreal.
>
> So I thought maybe that's because I can't actually feel the lives of thousands of people. Then let's at least train ourselves to perceive the daily lives of 100 people. I started collecting diaries to learn the volume, the quantity, of everyday life.

"Kinoboards" begins distributing the Kinoboards tree-style board, one of the oldest of Japan's message-board CGIs (now renamed [Kinobozu](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.jin.gr.jp/~nahi/kb/)).

Search engine review site [Search Desk](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.searchdesk.com/) (検索デスク) opens ([apparently opened to the public from 1995/12/31](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.searchdesk.com/history2.htm)).

**11/22**

In NiftyServe's "Lies Conference Room" (嘘情報会議室), it is announced that the World Society for Perceptual Cognition, meeting in Switzerland, has approved *the "hanage" (鼻毛, "nose hair") as the international unit of pain*.

**11/22?**

Kowloon Kurosawa's grassroots BBS *Tokyo Isoternet* (東京イソターネット) opens. Kowloon Kurosawa shot to fame with the book *Sawayaka Internet* (Shūwa System, November 1995) and was one of the very earliest UG writers on the net.

> Isoter was Kowloon's place, wasn't it? I'm pretty sure he started it right around when he finished writing that book, "Sawayaka Internet".

**11/23**

Windows 95 Japanese edition released. An explosive boom.

Music-related personal site [routine](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.znet.or.jp/~masahiro/) opens. DJ.

*Benri-kun* (べんりくん, "Handy-kun"), the original agent for fetching sites' last-updated times, appears.

[INTERNET Watch](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.watch.impress.co.jp/internet/), Japan's first commercial e-mail newspaper, has its pre-launch. **12/31**

*Hated People & Hated BBS List '95* (嫌われ者＆嫌われＢＢＳリスト９５) by [Trumpman](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.nurs.or.jp/~nspixp/trampman/) is uploaded to FILER FIRST on "Chijō no Rakuen".

"Yui Chat" creates the file-upload CGI *Yui Note* (ゆいのーと). Probably the very first CGI in Japan with a file-upload function. File upload is defined in RFC-1867 (Form-Based File Upload in HTML) and sends data files from an HTML FORM. The feature originally came with [Netscape 3.0](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://www.watch.impress.co.jp/pc/docs/article/960819/ns30.htm), which extended the FORM element with the attribute `ENCTYPE="multipart/form-data"` (["EXTENSIONS TO HTML 3.0"](https://web.archive.org/web/20021204222537/http://wp.netscape.com/assist/net_sites/html_extensions_3.html "Netscape's proprietary HTML extensions")). Internet Explorer implemented it by patching 3.0 and adopted it officially from 4.0, making it a standard feature in both of the so-called big two browsers.

> The "original Yui Note" only worked in Netscape (because of the developer's net setup). In IE you could only upload a limited set of extensions like .txt. It seems it was made more or less for fun and was released and distributed unfinished. The "Ψ Pirate Edition Ψ" (Ψ海賊版Ψ), made general-purpose and feature-rich in the "ぁ" world, is what came into wide use. It's also the basis of the uploaders used today. For details, see the "Zurubon Uploader Support Page".

## An Overblown Afterword

I think the culture of the Japanese internet from 1992 to 2002 was truly made by individuals. Before long it will be hemmed in by laws and regulations, everything you do will cost money, and you'll be bound by age and job title. That future is coming soon, but however many decades the history of the internet goes on, let me leave a record here that there was an era when the people who started something new, something interesting, were always somebody, somewhere.

There are still loads of sites and events I couldn't fit in or couldn't research this time, so I'll keep adding them as I go. The coverage of tools in general, and of everything from 2000 onward, is especially thin, so I expect the next update will focus there. This time only, the date mattered (exactly ten years, you see), so it ended up being published half-finished like this, blah blah.

(2002/09/30)
